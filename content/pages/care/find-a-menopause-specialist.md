A menopause specialist is a clinician with extra, tested training in menopause care, such as a Menopause Society Certified Practitioner (MSCP) [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. Ask your primary care doctor for a referral, or search The Menopause Society's directory by zip code [@menopause-society-practitioner-directory]. Specialist help matters most when hormone therapy may not be safe for you [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## How do I get a referral to a menopause specialist?

Start with your primary care doctor, OB/GYN or nurse practitioner: tell them your symptoms and ask to be referred to a clinician with menopause expertise. Then check whether your insurance needs that referral in writing. A referral is a written order from your primary care doctor to see a specialist [@healthcare-gov-referral-glossary].

- **Bring a short summary**: your symptoms, how long you've had them, your period pattern, and anything you've already tried
- **Name what you want**: "I'd like a referral to someone with menopause expertise, such as an MSCP"
- **Ask who they'd suggest**: your doctor may know local clinicians with menopause experience
- **If you feel brushed off**: see [what to say if your doctor dismisses your symptoms](/care/when-your-doctor-dismisses-you/)

Our list of [questions to ask your doctor about perimenopause](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/) can help you prepare.

## Does my insurance need a referral?

It depends on your plan type. In many HMOs, you need a referral before you can get care from anyone except your primary care doctor, and the plan may not pay if you skip it [@healthcare-gov-referral-glossary]. The main plan types work like this [@healthcare-gov-plan-types]:

- **HMO**: usually covers only doctors who work for or contract with the plan, and generally not out-of-network care except emergencies
- **POS**: needs a referral from your primary care doctor to see a specialist
- **PPO**: lets you see doctors outside the network without a referral, for an extra cost
- **EPO**: covers only doctors and specialists in the plan's network

Call the number on your insurance card and ask whether you need a referral, and whether the specialist you've found is in network. Our [insurance and menopause care guide](/care/insurance/) covers what else to ask.

## How do I find a certified menopause practitioner?

Use The Menopause Society's "Find a Menopause Practitioner" directory, which you can search by US zip code, by country, or for telehealth by US state [@menopause-society-practitioner-directory]. Listings show which clinicians hold the MSCP credential [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner].

A few things to know about the directory:

- **It isn't complete**: it lists only Society members and MSCPs who asked to be included, and leaves out those not accepting new patients [@menopause-society-practitioner-directory]
- **It isn't an endorsement**: the Society says it does not recommend the clinicians listed [@menopause-society-practitioner-directory]
- **Not everyone listed is an MSCP**: listings include MDs, DOs, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, nurse midwives, pharmacists, nurses, physical therapists and social workers, so check each listing's credential if that matters to you [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]

## What does "MSCP" mean?

MSCP stands for **Menopause Society Certified Practitioner**, a credential for licensed healthcare professionals who pass an exam showing expertise in midlife women's health [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. It lasts **3 years** and has to be renewed by a new exam or continuing education [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. So a current MSCP has kept up with the field, not just passed a test once.

## When does a specialist matter most?

A specialist matters most when standard treatment may not be safe for you or the right choice isn't clear. UK guidance (NICE) suggests considering a referral to someone with menopause expertise when [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- There are menopause symptoms plus a reason you **shouldn't take HRT** (a contraindication)
- There's **real uncertainty** about which treatment fits your symptoms
- You've had **coronary heart disease or a stroke** and are thinking about HRT: NICE says HRT should be discussed with you by someone with menopause expertise

If your situation is straightforward, a well-informed primary care doctor or OB/GYN may be all you need. See [who HRT may suit, and who needs specialist advice](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/) for more on these situations.

## How do I choose a menopause clinician?

Pick someone you can get to, who takes your insurance, and who listens. The Menopause Society suggests weighing location, whether they accept commercial insurance or Medicare, whether they offer telemedicine, and their specialty [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. It also suggests looking for someone who listens to your concerns, answers your questions, and respects your decisions [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner].

If no specialist is nearby, the directory's telehealth search can help [@menopause-society-practitioner-directory]. Our [menopause telehealth comparison](/compare/) covers what each service says it offers. A local clinician can still handle in-person exams and tests.

## If you're in the UK

If you're in the UK: start with your GP and ask about a referral. NICE guidance says to consider referring people to a healthcare professional with menopause expertise if they can't take HRT or if the best treatment for their symptoms is unclear [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
