Not every primary care doctor or OB/GYN has deep, current training in menopause care — it's a genuine gap in general medical education. If your usual doctor isn't giving you confident, specific answers, looking for someone with dedicated menopause expertise is a reasonable next step.

## What "MSCP" actually means

**Menopause Society Certified Practitioner (MSCP)** is awarded to licensed healthcare professionals who pass a competency examination demonstrating expertise in midlife women's health [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. It isn't a permanent label — the credential is valid for **3 years** and has to be renewed through re-examination or continuing education [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. That renewal requirement is actually useful information: it means a current MSCP has recently engaged with the field, not just passed a test once years ago.

## Where to look

The Menopause Society maintains a searchable **"Find a Menopause Clinician"** database that includes a range of credential types — MDs, DOs, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, nurse midwives, pharmacists, nurses, physical therapists, and social workers who provide care for women from perimenopause onward [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. Not every listed clinician holds the MSCP credential specifically, so it's worth checking which credential each listing has if that distinction matters to you.

## When this matters most

NICE guidance specifically recommends considering a referral to someone with menopause expertise in situations where [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- You have **contraindications to HRT** that make the standard approach uncertain
- There's **genuine uncertainty** about which management option fits your symptoms and health history

If your situation is straightforward, a well-informed primary care doctor or OB/GYN may be all you need. If you've been told "I don't really prescribe HRT" or your history is complicated (clotting risk, cancer history, early menopause), a specialist search is worth the extra step. See [who HRT may suit, and who needs specialist advice](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/) for the specific situations that call for this.

## If a specialist isn't available nearby

Telehealth menopause specialists can work well, especially paired with a local provider who can handle in-person exams, bloodwork, or prescriptions that need to be filled locally. Our [menopause telehealth comparison](/compare/) covers options by situation.
