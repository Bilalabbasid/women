What HRT costs depends on the form, the dose, the pharmacy, and your insurance. As of November 2026, GoodRx listed a month of generic estradiol patches at about $37 to $69 without insurance [@goodrx-estradiol-pricing-2026]. Check the price for your exact prescription before you fill it.

## What the medication itself costs (without insurance)

As of **November 2026**, GoodRx listed US retail (non-insurance) prices for a one-month supply of **generic estradiol**:

- **Once-weekly transdermal patches**: roughly **$37 to $69**, depending on dose [@goodrx-estradiol-pricing-2026]
- **Estradiol gel packets**: far less consistent: from about **$39 up to $545** depending on the exact dose and quantity, with some specific strengths priced unusually high [@goodrx-estradiol-pricing-2026]

The wide gel range is useful to know before you fill a prescription. The *same drug* at a *different dose or packet count* can cost very different amounts. Check the price for the exact product, dose, and pharmacy on your prescription, rather than assuming one "typical" price. These are **retail prices without insurance**, so what you pay with insurance or a discount program may be different.

## What a telehealth visit costs

If you're thinking about a menopause-focused telehealth service instead of (or alongside) your regular doctor, these were the self-pay prices each company listed as of **November 2026**:

- **[Midi Health](/compare/midi-review/)**: $250 for a first visit, $150 for follow-ups, not including labs or medications, no membership required; Midi says insured patients pay about $50 per visit on average when in-network [@midi-health-pricing-2026; @midi-health-own-site-2026]
- **[Evernow](/compare/evernow-review/)**: a membership starting at **$35/month** (includes ongoing messaging and optional insurance-eligible video visits), or a one-time insurance-eligible visit with no membership [@evernow-pricing-2026; @evernow-own-site-2026]
- **[Alloy](/compare/alloy-review/)**: does not bill insurance; HSA or FSA funds can be used, and some patients get reimbursed by PPO insurance afterward. We could not confirm a specific current consult price on Alloy's public pages [@alloy-insurance-2026; @alloy-own-site-2026]

See our full [telehealth comparisons](/compare/) for how these models differ beyond price.

## Why your real cost will differ from any number on this page

- **Insurance coverage varies a lot**: see our [insurance and menopause care guide](/care/insurance/) for what the research shows about access gaps by insurance type
- **Brand-name vs. generic** versions of the same hormone can cost different amounts
- **Dose and formulation** (patch vs. gel vs. pill vs. vaginal insert) price very differently from each other
- **Prices change**, and this page shows what we checked on the date noted, so check the current price before you commit

## What to do with this information

- Ask your pharmacy to compare the **cash price** with your insurance copay
- Ask your prescriber whether a **different form** (patch, gel, or pill) would work as well for you, since prices differ between forms
- If considering telehealth, compare its **current self-pay price** against your actual in-network out-of-pocket cost before assuming either is cheaper
- Use our [treatment conversation builder](/tools/treatment-questions/) to prepare these cost questions before your appointment
