The best sleepwear for night sweats is made from specialized moisture-wicking synthetic microfibers, bamboo viscose, or lightweight modal rather than traditional cotton, as highlighted in our [daily menopause care guides](/care/) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. While cotton absorbs perspiration well, it retains water in its fibers, leaving you lying in cold, damp pajamas that trigger violent shivering twenty minutes after a night sweat passes.

## What sleepwear can and cannot do

Before investing in specialized sleepwear, understand what it is designed to achieve:

- **What it can do**: It pulls moisture rapidly away from your skin, accelerates evaporation, dries quickly, and eliminates the drenched, clammy feeling that jolts you awake.
- **What it cannot do**: It does not lower your internal core body temperature, stop hot flashes, or fix the underlying hormonal fluctuations driving night sweats [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

To explore root-cause medical treatments, read our primary guide on [night sweats in perimenopause and menopause](/symptoms/night-sweats/).

## Fabric comparison: which works best?

| Fabric | Moisture absorption | Drying speed | Breathability | Overall night sweat rating |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Technical synthetic microfibers** | High capillary pull | Very fast (evaporates in minutes) | High | **Best choice**: keeps skin dry and prevents chills |
| **Bamboo viscose / modal** | High | Moderate (faster than cotton) | Very high | **Good choice**: exceptionally soft and naturally cool to touch |
| **100% Traditional cotton** | Very high | Slow (holds moisture in fibers) | Moderate | **Avoid for heavy sweats**: stays wet and cold against skin |
| **Polyester fleece / flannel** | Low | Slow | Very low | **Avoid entirely**: traps body heat and accelerates sweating |

## How to choose the right fit and design

When shopping for sleepwear to manage nighttime temperature spikes, prioritize these design features:

1. **Loose, non-restrictive cut**: Tight-fitting waistbands, elastic cuffs, and high necklines trap warm air against your torso. Choose relaxed silhouettes, wide armholes, and V-neck or scoop tops.
2. **Tagless labels and flatlock seams**: Sweating increases skin sensitivity; scratchy tags and raised interior seams cause friction and itching against sensitized skin.
3. **Short sleeves or sleeveless tops**: Exposing your neck, shoulders, and wrists allows surface blood vessels to dissipate heat rapidly during a flush.
4. **Machine washable and fast-drying**: You will be laundering sleepwear frequently; choose durable materials that retain wicking properties after dozens of wash cycles without requiring delicate hand washing.

## Pairing sleepwear with your sleep environment

Sleepwear is only one component of nighttime temperature management. For optimal rest:
- Pair wicking pajamas with lightweight, breathable bed sheets (such as percale cotton or eucalyptus).
- Keep bedroom ambient temperature between 65°F and 68°F (18°C to 20°C).
- For severe nighttime sweats where passive sleepwear is not enough, investigate [bed cooling fans and climate systems](/does-it-work/cooling-products/).
