If a doctor brushes off your menopause symptoms, ask directly whether perimenopause or menopause has been considered, and what specifically rules out treatment. Guidelines say care should be tailored to you, and that HRT should be offered for hot flashes unless there's a reason not to [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. If you still get no clear answer, a second opinion is reasonable.

Feeling brushed off is frustrating, and it doesn't mean your symptoms don't matter. Below: why it happens, the words to use, and what to do if HRT is refused.

## Why do doctors dismiss menopause symptoms?

Often because many doctors had little menopause training. In a 2017 survey of US residents in family medicine, internal medicine and OB/GYN, 20% had received no menopause lectures during their training, and only about 7% felt well prepared to care for women in menopause [@kling-resident-menopause-knowledge-2019].

The same survey found that about a third of these doctors in training would not offer hormone therapy to a newly menopausal woman with bothersome symptoms and no reason to avoid it [@kling-resident-menopause-knowledge-2019]. It was a small survey with a low response rate, so treat the numbers as a sign of the gap, not an exact figure [@kling-resident-menopause-knowledge-2019].

Other times the doctor is being careful for a real reason, such as a past blood clot or breast cancer, which you deserve to have explained [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. And some advice still reflects the old HRT warning label: in November 2025 the FDA asked for heart disease, breast cancer and probable dementia to be removed from the boxed warning, and it approved the first new labels in February 2026 [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. Our guide to the [HRT boxed warning change](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/) explains what changed.

## What to say if your doctor dismisses your symptoms

Specific, calm questions are harder to wave away than general frustration. You could try:

- **"Could this be perimenopause or menopause? Can we go through my symptoms and cycle changes?"** This puts the question on the table directly.
- **"What specifically about my health history makes you cautious about hormone therapy?"** You'll either get a real reason or learn that the hesitation isn't about you.
- **"Can you note in my record that we discussed these symptoms and this treatment, and what we decided?"** A written record helps if you later want a second opinion.
- **"Would a referral to someone with menopause expertise make sense for me?"** UK guidance suggests this when the best option isn't clear [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

UK guidance says care should be tailored to the person at all times, and adapted as symptoms change [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. A blanket "that's just aging" that skips your actual symptoms falls short of that.

## What to do if your doctor won't prescribe HRT

Ask for the specific reason, then check it against the guidance. For healthy women under 60, or within 10 years of their last period, The Menopause Society says the benefits of hormone therapy generally outweigh the risks, and UK guidance says to offer HRT for hot flashes and night sweats [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Ask which reason applies to you**: the main reasons not to use HRT are unexplained vaginal bleeding, liver disease, a past estrogen-sensitive cancer such as breast cancer, past heart disease, stroke, heart attack or blood clot, or a high inherited risk of clots [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Ask whether it's about your age or timing**: starting more than 10 years after menopause, or after 60, carries more risk, so the balance is less favorable [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Ask whether a different type would suit you**: UK guidance suggests patches or gels rather than pills for people at higher risk of blood clots, including those with a BMI over 30 [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Ask for a referral**: UK guidance suggests referral to a menopause expert when there are reasons HRT may not be safe, or when the best option is unclear [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Ask about other treatments**: if HRT really isn't right for you, there are non-hormone options, including menopause-focused CBT and fezolinetant [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. See [non-hormonal treatments for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/).

For a fuller look at who hormone therapy may suit, read [is HRT right for me?](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/)

## Do I need a blood test to prove it's menopause?

Usually not if you're 45 or older. UK guidance says that in otherwise healthy people of that age, perimenopause can be recognized from recent hot flashes or sweats plus changes in your periods, without lab tests, and that hormone tests such as estradiol and AMH shouldn't be used to identify it [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [blood tests for perimenopause](/perimenopause/blood-tests/) for when tests do help.

## How do I get a second opinion or see a menopause specialist?

Ask your doctor for a referral, or look one up yourself. The Menopause Society's directory lists clinicians by US zip code, and you can also search for those who offer telehealth in your state [@menopause-society-practitioner-directory]. Clinicians marked MSCP (Menopause Society Certified Practitioner) have passed an exam in midlife women's health [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner].

A referral matters most when there's a reason HRT may not be safe for you, or when the right treatment is unclear [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Our guide on [how to find a menopause specialist](/care/find-a-menopause-specialist/) covers referrals and insurance.
