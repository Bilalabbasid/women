Feeling brushed off when you raise perimenopause or menopause symptoms is a common, frustrating experience — and you're not imagining it. A 2025 survey of 627 women found that the most common reasons they turned to the internet for menopause information were exactly the gaps many describe experiencing in appointments: unclear symptom information, limited guidance for their specific situation, and a lack of transparency about where the information came from [@jmir-online-menopause-information-2025].

## What to say, specifically

Vague frustration is hard for anyone to act on — specific, direct language works better:

- **"I'd like to specifically discuss whether this could be perimenopause or menopause. Can we go through my symptoms and cycle changes against the criteria for that?"**
- **"What specifically about my health history makes you cautious about hormone therapy for me?"** (This either gets you a real, specific answer, or reveals that the hesitation isn't based on your individual case.)
- **"Can you document in my chart that we discussed [symptom] and [treatment option], and what we decided?"** (This creates a record and often shifts the conversation to be more thorough.)
- **"I'd like a referral to someone with menopause expertise, given [your specific complicating factor]."**

NICE guidance is explicit that care should be **tailored to the individual at all times**, and adapted as symptoms change [@nice-ng23-recommendations] — a blanket "that's just aging" without engaging with your specific symptoms and history falls short of that standard, and it's reasonable to say so.

## Why this happens

It's rarely personal. Some clinicians have limited current training in menopause management — this is a genuine, documented gap in general medical education, not a reflection of how seriously your symptoms deserve to be taken. Others are being appropriately cautious because of something specific in your history, which is worth understanding rather than assuming is dismissal.

## When to seek a second opinion or referral

NICE recommends considering a referral to someone with menopause expertise specifically when there's **uncertainty about the right management option**, or when **contraindications to HRT** make your situation more complex [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. If you've asked specific questions and gotten vague or no answers, that's a reasonable trigger to seek a second opinion — see our guide on [how to find a menopause specialist](/care/find-a-menopause-specialist/).

## Bring evidence, not just a feeling

Walking in with a clear, written pattern changes the conversation.
