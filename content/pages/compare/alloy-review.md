Alloy is an online, cash-pay menopause service: you fill in a medical form, a doctor reviews it, and medicine is mailed to you. It doesn't bill insurance. Its main offer, FDA-approved estradiol (hormone therapy), is the treatment with the strongest evidence for hot flashes [@alloy-bioidentical-2026; @alloy-insurance-2026; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

We wrote this review from Alloy's own website and help center, checked in November 2026. Nobody pays to be reviewed here, and Alloy did not see this page.

## How does Alloy work?

Alloy works by online form, not a scheduled video call. Alloy says the medical intake takes 5 to 10 minutes, a board-certified doctor it describes as menopause-specialized reviews it, and a treatment plan typically follows within about 12 hours [@alloy-own-site-2026]. Its help center gives a longer guide: you can usually expect to hear from your doctor within 1 to 2 business days [@alloy-after-signup-2026].

- **Messaging**: Alloy says you can message the care team at any time [@alloy-own-site-2026].
- **Payment first**: hormone prescriptions don't ship until you review and approve the payment in your account [@alloy-after-signup-2026].
- **Billing**: refills bill and ship automatically every 3 months, and you can change or cancel them from your dashboard [@alloy-cost-help-2026].
- **Pharmacy**: Alloy says it can't send a new prescription to your local pharmacy; it goes to Alloy's partner pharmacy. After at least one order, refills can be moved, but you must cancel your Alloy subscription [@alloy-pharmacy-transfer-2026].
- **No consult for some products**: skincare, sexual health, hair and gut-health products can be ordered at checkout without buying a doctor consultation [@alloy-after-signup-2026].

## How much does Alloy cost?

Alloy charges for each medicine, plus a consultation for hormone treatment, and does not bill insurance. Its products page listed these prices in November 2026. Alloy's pages that we checked don't state the consultation fee; it shows at checkout [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026; @alloy-after-signup-2026].

| Treatment | Alloy's listed price (Nov 2026) |
|---|---|
| Estradiol pill | From $39.99 a month [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026] |
| Estradiol spray (Evamist) | $69.99 a month [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026] |
| Estradiol gel | $69.99 a month [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026] |
| Estradiol patch | $100 a month [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026] |
| Progesterone (to protect the uterine lining) | From $23 a month [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026] |
| Paroxetine (non-hormonal, for hot flashes) | $34.99 a month [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026] |
| Estradiol vaginal cream | $119.97 for 3 months [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026] |

You can pay with HSA or FSA funds, and Alloy says some patients get reimbursed by a PPO plan after they pay [@alloy-insurance-2026]. Prices and promotions change, so check the total at checkout. For what the same medicines can cost elsewhere, see [how much HRT costs](/care/hrt-cost/).

## What Alloy offers for each symptom, and how we grade it

Below is what Alloy's own pages say for each concern. These are the company's own descriptions, not proof that a product works. After each, we show how this site grades the underlying treatment, where we have a grade.

### Alloy for HRT (hot flashes, night sweats and vaginal dryness)

For hormone therapy, Alloy offers estradiol as a pill, patch, gel or spray, progesterone to take alongside it, a low-dose birth control pill for perimenopause, estradiol vaginal cream, and non-hormonal paroxetine for hot flashes [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026]. Alloy says its estradiol is FDA-approved and bioidentical (the same molecule the body makes) [@alloy-bioidentical-2026].

- **Hormone therapy for hot flashes and night sweats**: :grade{id="mht-vms"}. The Menopause Society says the benefits outweigh the risks for most healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/).
- **Vaginal estrogen for dryness and painful sex**: :grade{id="vaginal-estrogen-gsm"}. See [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/).
- **Paroxetine and similar medicines for hot flashes**: :grade{id="ssri-snri-vms"}. See [SSRIs and SNRIs](/treatments/nonhormonal/ssris-snris/).

### Alloy for mood and anxiety

Alloy doesn't list a mood or anxiety treatment. Its anxiety page suggests seeing a doctor if anxiety gets in the way of daily life most days for 6 months, finding a therapist, and lifestyle steps, and says more mood offerings are "coming soon" [@alloy-symptom-anxiety-2026]. Its mood swings page points to hormone therapy as one option [@alloy-symptom-mood-swings-2026].

How this site grades the main options:

- **Talking therapy (CBT) for low mood and irritability in menopause**: :grade{id="cbt-mood"}
- **Hormone therapy for low mood that starts along with other menopause symptoms**: :grade{id="mht-depressive-symptoms"}
- **Usual depression care for diagnosed depression**: :grade{id="depression-care"}

Read more in [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/) and [low mood and depression](/symptoms/depression/).

### Alloy for skin and aging

For skin, Alloy sells prescription creams built around estriol (a weak form of estrogen), plus tretinoin, dark-spot and rosacea creams, with the M4 estriol face cream listed at $150 for 3 months [@alloy-skincare-2026; @alloy-solutions-pricing-2026]. Alloy bases its results claims on its own 12-week studies, one of them placebo-controlled with 86 people [@alloy-skincare-2026]. We haven't reviewed those studies and don't grade estriol skin creams.

On hormone therapy taken for skin, a 2025 review found many studies link it to better skin, but results are mixed and guidelines don't support starting it for skin alone [@viscomi-menopausal-skin-hrt-2025]. See [dry and itchy skin in menopause](/symptoms/dry-itchy-skin/).

### Alloy for hair loss

For hair loss, Alloy sells low-dose oral minoxidil and oral spironolactone, each listed at $99 for 3 months [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026]. Its hair loss page also names minoxidil, spironolactone and hormone therapy as options [@alloy-symptom-hair-loss-2026].

- **Minoxidil applied to the scalp for female pattern hair loss**: :grade{id="minoxidil-fphl"}. A review lists it as the first treatment to try [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022]. Our grade covers the scalp form; we haven't graded the low-dose pill or spironolactone.
- **Hormone therapy for female pattern hair loss**: :grade{id="mht-hair"}. The same review found too little evidence that HRT helps this kind of hair loss [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022].

See [hair thinning in menopause](/symptoms/hair-thinning/).

### Alloy for joint pain

Alloy doesn't sell a joint pain treatment. Its joint pain page suggests staying active, low-impact exercise and drinking water, and says women who take estrogen report less joint pain, pointing to its hormone products [@alloy-symptom-joint-pain-2026].

The evidence for that is modest. In a later look at a large US trial, women taking estrogen alone reported joint pain a little less often after a year than women on placebo (76.3% vs 79.2%), while joint swelling was slightly more common (42.1% vs 39.7%) [@chlebowski-whi-estrogen-joint-symptoms-2013]. We don't have a grade for HRT for joint pain. See [joint and muscle pain in menopause](/symptoms/joint-pain/).

## Alternatives to Alloy

Other online menopause services we've reviewed are Evernow, Hers, Midi Health and Winona, and you can also see a menopause specialist in person. They're listed alphabetically here, not ranked. The main differences are whether they bill insurance and how you're seen.

- **Evernow**: offers a one-time visit it calls insurance-eligible, or a membership from $35 a month with messaging and optional insurance-covered video visits [@evernow-pricing-2026]. See our [Evernow review](/compare/evernow-review/) and [Alloy vs. Evernow](/compare/alloy-vs-evernow/).
- **Hers**: part of Hims & Hers Health, a large telehealth company. Its site blocked our checks, so we couldn't confirm its menopause prices or insurance policy [@hims-hers-health-public-company-2026]. See our [Hers review](/compare/hers-review/).
- **Midi Health**: virtual visits with clinicians, in-network with most (not all) major PPO plans, or $250 for a first visit and $150 for a follow-up if you pay yourself [@midi-health-own-site-2026; @midi-health-pricing-2026]. See our [Midi Health review](/compare/midi-review/) and [Midi vs. Alloy](/compare/midi-vs-alloy/).
- **Winona**: doesn't bill insurance; its own site labels its cream and pill hormones as compounded, which differs from FDA-approved products [@winona-own-site-2026]. See our [Winona review](/compare/winona-review/).
- **In person**: your own doctor or gynecologist, or a clinician with menopause training. See [how to find a menopause specialist](/care/find-a-menopause-specialist/) and [does insurance cover menopause care?](/care/insurance/)

For all of them side by side, see [menopause telehealth companies compared](/compare/).

## Who Alloy's model may suit, and who it may not

Alloy's model fits people who are paying cash anyway and are comfortable with an online form instead of a live visit [@alloy-insurance-2026; @alloy-own-site-2026]. It fits less well if you want care billed to insurance, want prescriptions sent to your local pharmacy from the start, or have a complex health history you'd rather talk through live [@alloy-pharmacy-transfer-2026].

## What we didn't check

We couldn't check clinical outcomes, the consultation fee, or how Alloy's doctors handle complex histories. This review describes Alloy's own stated model, not the quality of its care. We also left out Alloy's marketed results figures, because we couldn't check how they were measured.

## When an online form isn't enough

An online service can't examine you. Get in-person care for these:

:::urgent
- **Any bleeding after menopause**, even a small amount or once: see a doctor to have it checked [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting**: get emergency help; in the US, call 911 [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **Thoughts of harming yourself**: call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time [@lifeline-988].

If you're in the UK: call 999 in an emergency, or Samaritans on 116 123 [@samaritans-contact].
:::
