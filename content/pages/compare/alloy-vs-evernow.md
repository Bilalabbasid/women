Alloy and Evernow are both online menopause services, but you pay them in different ways. Evernow takes major insurance plans for video visits and sells a membership from $35 a month; Alloy doesn't bill insurance and charges a one-time $49 consult plus the price of each medicine [@evernow-faq-2026; @alloy-insurance-2026; @alloy-estradiol-pill-2026].

All details below come from each company's own pages, checked in November 2026. Nobody pays to be listed here, and this isn't a recommendation of either.

## Does Alloy or Evernow take insurance?

Evernow takes insurance for video visits and Alloy doesn't take insurance at all. Evernow says it works with major national commercial plans, including UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, Anthem and Blue Cross Blue Shield, and covers video visits regardless of plan type; it doesn't currently support Medicare or Medicaid [@evernow-faq-2026]. Evernow's membership fee isn't covered by insurance, though it can be paid with HSA or FSA funds [@evernow-faq-2026].

Alloy says it doesn't bill insurance for any service or prescription, but some patients get reimbursed by a PPO plan after paying, and HSA or FSA funds can be used [@alloy-insurance-2026]. For what plans usually cover, see [does insurance cover menopause care?](/care/insurance/)

## Which is cheaper, Alloy or Evernow?

It depends on your insurance, how often you need care, and which medicine you're prescribed. Evernow's pricing page says membership starts at $35 a month [@evernow-pricing-2026], and its FAQ lists these prices [@evernow-faq-2026]:

- **Pay per visit**: your usual copay and deductible if your plan is eligible, or $150 a visit self-pay
- **Membership, month to month**: $49 a month
- **Membership, 3 months**: $129 (about $43 a month)
- **Membership, 12 months**: $420 (about $35 a month)

Evernow's pay-per-visit option includes a 20-minute consult and 90 days of access to your care plan and prescriptions [@evernow-membership-vs-ppv-2025]. Medicines are charged separately [@evernow-faq-2026].

Alloy charges a one-time $49 consult fee before hormone treatment [@alloy-estradiol-pill-2026], then prices each medicine and usually bills every 3 months [@alloy-cost-help-2026]. Its listed prices include an estradiol pill from $39.99 a month, gel or spray at $69.99 a month, a patch at $100 a month and progesterone from $23 a month [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026]. To compare, add up a year of visits, fees and medicine for each. Our [HRT cost guide](/care/hrt-cost/) shows what the same medicines can cost at a regular pharmacy.

## What's an Alloy visit like, and what's an Evernow visit like?

Evernow uses live video visits, while Alloy works through an online form by default. With Alloy, you fill in a 5 to 10 minute medical form, a doctor reviews it, and you can message the care team afterward [@alloy-own-site-2026]. Alloy says hormone prescriptions don't ship until you approve the payment [@alloy-after-signup-2026].

With Evernow, you meet a clinician by video; members also get 24/7 secure messaging, and Evernow says messages are often answered within a day [@evernow-faq-2026]. If you want to talk through a complex history in real time, a video visit allows that; if you'd rather not schedule a call, a form may suit you.

## How fast can you start?

Alloy's homepage says a treatment plan typically comes within about 12 hours, while its help center says you'll usually hear from a doctor within 1 to 2 business days [@alloy-own-site-2026; @alloy-after-signup-2026]. Evernow says it can often fit in a video visit within 24 hours [@evernow-faq-2026]. These are the companies' own figures, and we haven't tested them.

## What do Alloy and Evernow each treat?

Both treat menopause symptoms with hormone therapy, and both reach beyond it. Alloy lists estradiol pills, patches, gels and sprays, progesterone, vaginal estradiol cream and non-hormonal paroxetine, plus skincare, sexual health, weight, hair and gut products [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026; @alloy-own-site-2026]. Alloy says its estradiol is FDA-approved [@alloy-bioidentical-2026] and that its doctors follow guidelines from ACOG and The Menopause Society [@alloy-solutions-pricing-2026].

Evernow says its clinicians can prescribe hormone therapy, GLP-1 weight-loss medicines, hair loss treatment, prescription skincare, vaginal products and anxiety medicines, and that lab tests are needed only for some medicines [@evernow-faq-2026]. It also says its clinicians are trained in perimenopause and menopause care and follow ACOG and Menopause Society guidelines [@evernow-faq-2026]. Whichever service you use, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats :grade{id="mht-vms"} [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/).

## Can I use my local pharmacy?

With Evernow, yes; with Alloy, not at first. Evernow says medicines can go to your preferred local pharmacy or its partner pharmacies, though some cash-pay-only medicines must come from its delivery pharmacy [@evernow-faq-2026]. Alloy sends new prescriptions to its partner pharmacy; refills can be moved after at least one order, but you must cancel your Alloy subscription and lose its doctor messaging [@alloy-pharmacy-transfer-2026].

## Things that may matter to you

- **If you have commercial insurance**: Evernow says it bills major plans for video visits; Alloy doesn't bill any plan [@evernow-faq-2026; @alloy-insurance-2026].
- **If you have Medicare or Medicaid**: Evernow doesn't support these plans, and Alloy doesn't bill insurance [@evernow-faq-2026; @alloy-insurance-2026].
- **If you want a live conversation**: Evernow's visits are by video; Alloy's intake is a form [@evernow-faq-2026; @alloy-own-site-2026].
- **If you want your local pharmacy**: Evernow allows it; Alloy's first prescription goes to its partner pharmacy [@evernow-faq-2026; @alloy-pharmacy-transfer-2026].
