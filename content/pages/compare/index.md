**Quick answer:** If you want a live video visit covered by major health insurance with a clinician trained in menopause care, **Midi Health** and **Allara Health** lead for insurance-backed, synchronous care. If you prefer transparent, one-off cash-pay appointments with an independent doctor without a recurring membership, **Sesame Care** lets you book single visits upfront. If you want asynchronous prescription fulfilment for standard FDA-approved estradiol or vaginal estrogen without insurance, **Alloy** offers direct cash-pay prescribing, while **Hers** provides asynchronous subscription care.

Many women turn to online menopause clinics because finding a local specialist who is up to date on current [menopause treatment guidelines](/treatments/) can take months [@jmir-online-menopause-information-2025]. Below is how we compare the main telehealth options strictly by **clinician credentials**, **insurance and cost structure**, and **adherence to clinical guidelines**—never by affiliate commission.

## How we evaluate online menopause clinics (our 4 criteria)

We never rank a telehealth provider based on whether they pay a referral fee. Instead, we check every service against clinical standards from The Menopause Society and NICE [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations]:

1. **Clinician credentials and visit format:** Do you meet live by video with an OB/GYN, physician, or nurse practitioner trained in midlife care (such as a Menopause Society Certified Practitioner, MSCP), or is the assessment an asynchronous text questionnaire?
2. **Insurance acceptance vs cash-pay subscriptions:** Does the clinic bill major US health insurance plans like a normal specialist office visit, or do you pay out-of-pocket membership and medication markups?
3. **Guideline-backed prescribing (FDA-approved vs compounded):** Does the service prioritize standard, regulated transdermal estradiol, micronized progesterone, [low-dose vaginal estrogen](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/), and [Level I non-hormonal prescriptions](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]? Can they send prescriptions to your local pharmacy so you can use your own prescription drug coverage?
4. **No unnecessary lab markups:** For otherwise healthy women aged 45 and older, clinical guidelines advise identifying perimenopause from menstrual changes and vasomotor symptoms rather than selling costly salivary or serial hormone panels [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. We favour clinics that order blood work only when clinically indicated (for example, under age 45, or to rule out thyroid disease, anaemia, or liver abnormalities before starting [fezolinetant](/symptoms/hot-flashes/)).

## Detailed look at each service model

### 1. Insurance-covered synchronous video clinics (Midi Health, Allara Health)
- **Evidence grade for clinical model:** **Strong evidence** (aligns with standard specialist consultation and NAMS/NICE prescribing pathways [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations]).
- **Why this model ranks highest:** A live video conversation allows a clinician to review your personal and family medical history, blood pressure, [FDA boxed warning considerations](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/), and differential diagnoses (such as thyroid dysfunction or iron deficiency). Because prescriptions can be sent to your neighbourhood pharmacy, you pay your standard insurance copay for generic estradiol patches or micronized progesterone rather than a bundled direct-to-consumer markup.
- **Limitations:** Wait times for an initial video slot can range from a few days to a few weeks depending on your state, and you must verify that your specific insurance plan is in-network before booking.

### 2. Upfront cash-pay appointment marketplaces (Sesame Care)
- **Why consider it:** Unlike subscription telehealth brands that lock you into a monthly fee, a direct-care marketplace lets you filter by OB/GYN credentials, see the exact single-visit fee upfront, and have your prescription sent to a local pharmacy.
- **Limitations:** Because each clinician on a marketplace practices independently, their depth of menopause-specific training varies. Bring our [doctor appointment question list](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/) and your [two-week symptom report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) to keep the visit focused.

### 3. Direct-to-consumer asynchronous prescribing (Alloy, Hers)
- **Why consider it:** Asynchronous intake forms are fast and convenient if you already understand your options, have no contraindications, and prefer home shipping without taking time off work for a video call.
- **Limitations:** Text intakes are less suited to complex symptom pictures (such as overlapping [joint pain](/symptoms/), heavy or erratic bleeding, or multiple chronic conditions). In addition, paying cash for ongoing medication shipments is often substantially more expensive over a year than filling generic FDA-approved prescriptions through your health insurance at a local pharmacy.

## When telehealth is NOT appropriate (see a doctor in person)

Telehealth clinicians cannot perform a physical examination, listen to your heart and lungs, or conduct pelvic imaging. You need an **in-person medical assessment** if you have:

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding, spotting, or brown discharge after 12 consecutive months without a period** [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Heavy bleeding that soaks through pads or tampons every 1 to 2 hours, bleeding between periods, or bleeding after sex** [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or persistent heart palpitations** [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **New or worsening pelvic pain, a breast lump, or unexplained weight loss.**

If you are in emotional crisis or having thoughts of self-harm, call or text **988** in the US or **116 123** (Samaritans) in the UK, or visit our [urgent help page](/urgent-help/).
:::

## Questions to ask before booking an online menopause visit

:::questions
- Is my visit a live video consultation with a licensed clinician trained in menopause care, or a text-only review?
- Do you bill my health insurance directly, and what is the total out-of-pocket fee if my plan is out of network?
- Can you send FDA-approved prescriptions to my local pharmacy so I can use my prescription drug insurance?
- If I am on systemic estrogen and still have a uterus, how will you provide endometrial protection (such as micronized progesterone)?
- How is follow-up handled if I need a dose adjustment or experience unscheduled spotting in the first 3 months?
:::

## How to prepare for your telehealth appointment

1. **Log 14 days of symptoms first:** Telehealth visits typically last 15 to 30 minutes. Use our free, on-device [symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) or [perimenopause symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/) so you can read out your exact frequency and severity scores.
2. **Know your recent blood pressure and screening dates:** Online clinicians will almost always ask for a recent blood pressure reading and the date of your last mammogram and cervical screening before prescribing systemic hormone therapy [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
3. **Review practical care options:** Explore our [getting care hub](/care/) and [evidence-based menopause books](/care/menopause-books/) to understand what guidelines say before your visit.
