Menopause telehealth companies differ mainly in how you pay and how you're seen. Midi and Evernow say they bill insurance for virtual visits, while Alloy and Winona don't. Whichever you use, hormone therapy prescribed by a clinician is the most effective treatment for hot flashes [@midi-health-own-site-2026; @evernow-pricing-2026; @alloy-insurance-2026; @winona-own-site-2026; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Below are the five services we've reviewed, using what each company says on its own site, checked in November 2026. We don't rank them, and nobody pays to be listed.

## What to check when comparing online menopause clinics

We look at the same four things for every service, based on guidance from The Menopause Society and NICE [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations]:

1. **How you're seen**: a live video visit with a clinician, or an online form reviewed later.
2. **How you pay**: whether the clinic bills your insurance, or you pay cash for visits and medicines.
3. **What it prescribes**: hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and vaginal dryness [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy], and The Menopause Society recommends some non-hormonal medicines and CBT for people who can't or don't want to use it [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. ACOG advises against routinely using compounded "bioidentical" hormones when FDA-approved ones exist [@acog-compounded-bioidentical-2023]. See [bioidentical vs. compounded hormones](/treatments/hormone-therapy/bioidentical-vs-compounded/).
4. **Testing**: for healthy people 45 and over, NICE says perimenopause is identified from symptoms and period changes, not hormone blood tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## How much does online menopause care cost?

Published prices range from a membership starting at $35 a month (Evernow) to $250 for a first self-pay visit (Midi), before medicines [@evernow-pricing-2026; @midi-health-pricing-2026]. Alloy and Winona don't bill insurance, so you pay their prices yourself, though HSA or FSA funds can be used [@alloy-insurance-2026; @winona-own-site-2026]. Insurance can lower visit costs at clinics that bill it [@midi-health-own-site-2026]. For medicine prices at a regular pharmacy, see [how much HRT costs](/care/hrt-cost/).

## Is telehealth as good as seeing a doctor in person?

Care from a clinician who prescribes guideline-based treatment, by telehealth or in person, is graded :grade{id="clinician-led-care"} on this site. That grade is about the model of care, not any company. A video visit or online form can't include a physical or pelvic exam, so some problems need an in-person visit. To find someone local, see [how to find a menopause specialist](/care/find-a-menopause-specialist/), or ask your own doctor.

## When telehealth isn't enough

Get in-person care for any of these:

:::urgent
- **Any bleeding after menopause**, even a small amount or once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Bleeding between periods or after sex, or heavy periods that affect your life** [@nhs-heavy-periods].
- **Palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting**: get emergency help; in the US, call 911 [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **Thoughts of harming yourself**: call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time [@lifeline-988]. See our [urgent help page](/urgent-help/).

If you're in the UK: call 999 in an emergency, or Samaritans on 116 123 [@samaritans-contact].
:::

## Questions to ask before booking an online menopause visit

:::questions
- Is my visit a live video call with a clinician trained in menopause care, or an online form?
- Do you bill my insurance, and what will I pay in total if you don't?
- Can you send prescriptions to my local pharmacy so I can use my drug coverage?
- If I take estrogen and still have a uterus, how will you protect my uterine lining (for example with progesterone)?
- How is follow-up handled if I need a dose change or have unexpected spotting?
:::

## How to prepare for your telehealth appointment

1. **Track your symptoms for two weeks**: use our free [symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) or [perimenopause symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/) so you can describe how often and how badly symptoms affect you.
2. **Have your health details ready**: a recent blood pressure reading, your medicines, and the dates of your last mammogram and cervical screening, in case the clinician asks.
3. **Read up first**: our [getting care hub](/care/), [questions for your doctor](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/) and [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) cover what guidelines say.
