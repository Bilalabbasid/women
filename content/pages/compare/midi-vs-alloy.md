**Quick answer:** If you have insurance Midi is in-network with, start there — it's built around billing insurance for a live video visit. If you're cash-pay anyway, or want to skip scheduling a video call entirely, Alloy's asynchronous intake is faster to get started, though you'll want to confirm its current price at checkout.

## The core difference: insurance vs. cash-pay

This is the single biggest decision point between these two services, based on what each states on their own site:

- **Midi Health:** States it is in-network with most, though not all, major PPO plans. Self-pay pricing was listed at **$250 for a first visit, $150 for follow-ups** (not including labs or medications), with insured patients reportedly paying around $50 out-of-pocket per visit on average [@midi-health-own-site-2026]. See our full [Midi Health review](/compare/midi-review/).
- **Alloy:** Explicitly markets itself as **cash-pay**, positioned as avoiding insurance billing and pre-authorization delays entirely. FSA/HSA cards are accepted [@alloy-own-site-2026]. See our full [Alloy review](/compare/alloy-review/).

If your insurance plan is in-network with Midi, that will typically beat paying cash anywhere. If you're uninsured, have a high-deductible plan, or simply prefer not to deal with insurance, Alloy's cash-pay model removes that friction — but confirm the current price at checkout, since we could not verify a specific consult price on Alloy's public pages.

## Visit format: live video vs. asynchronous intake

- **Midi:** Live video visits with clinicians (MDs, NPs).
- **Alloy:** An asynchronous medical intake (Alloy states 5–10 minutes) reviewed by a physician, with a treatment plan typically delivered within about 12 hours, plus 24/7 messaging access afterward [@alloy-own-site-2026].

If you want a real-time conversation to walk through your history, Midi's format is built for that. If you'd rather fill out a form on your own schedule and get a plan back without a call, Alloy's model is faster to start.

## What each actually treats

Both offer hormone therapy. Alloy's site also sells a broader catalog — skincare, sexual health, GLP-1 weight care, hair/lash/brow, and gut-health products — beyond menopause hormone care specifically [@alloy-own-site-2026]. If you're looking for menopause care specifically rather than a wider wellness catalog, keep that distinction in mind when browsing either site.

## Who each suits

- **Choose Midi if:** You want to try billing insurance first and your plan might be in-network, or you want a live conversation with a clinician.
- **Choose Alloy if:** You're cash-pay regardless of provider, want to avoid scheduling a video call, or you're interested in Alloy's broader product catalog alongside hormone therapy.
