**What it is:** Winona is a direct-to-consumer telehealth company offering what it describes as bioidentical hormone replacement therapy, with treatment prescribed by board-certified doctors based on symptoms rather than hormone testing. We reviewed Winona's own site directly; this isn't a paid placement, and nobody pays to be reviewed here.

## The most important thing to know: most formats are compounded, not FDA-approved

Winona's own treatments page labels its **body cream, vaginal cream, and pill** formats as **"Compounded"** [@winona-own-site-2026]. Only the **patch** format appears without that label on the same page, suggesting it may be a standard, FDA-approved product while the other formats are compounded preparations made by a compounding pharmacy.

This matters because of a specific, well-documented concern: ACOG's 2023 clinical consensus recommends **against routinely prescribing compounded bioidentical hormone therapy** when FDA-approved formulations exist, specifically because compounded products don't go through the same standardized testing for purity, potency, and sterility [@acog-compounded-bioidentical-2023]. See our full explainer on [bioidentical vs. compounded hormones](/treatments/hormone-therapy/bioidentical-vs-compounded/) for why this distinction matters, independent of this specific review.

This doesn't mean Winona's products are unsafe — it means they haven't gone through the same regulatory testing pathway that FDA-approved alternatives have, and that's worth knowing and asking about directly before choosing a format.

## What else Winona offers

Winona's own site states it does not require blood or saliva hormone testing before prescribing, citing that The Menopause Society and ACOG don't require this for typical cases, and that its physicians adjust treatment based on symptom severity [@winona-own-site-2026] — this part of its approach is consistent with mainstream clinical guidance, separate from the compounding question above.

## Cost and insurance, as of November 2026

Winona's own site states it does **not** work directly with insurance companies and cannot bill on your behalf, though HSA/FSA receipts may be submitted for reimbursement [@winona-own-site-2026]. We were not able to independently confirm a specific, current treatment price on Winona's public pages; check their site directly for current cost, since pricing can change and may depend on the specific hormone and format you're prescribed.

## Who this may suit

If the patch format meets your needs, this may be a standard FDA-approved product delivered through a convenient telehealth model.

## Who this may not suit

If you're specifically looking for FDA-approved cream or pill formulations, Winona's labeled offerings in those categories are compounded rather than FDA-approved — ask directly whether an FDA-approved alternative in your preferred format is available, from Winona or elsewhere, before assuming "bioidentical" means "standard."
