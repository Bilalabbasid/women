**Our verdict: Not recommended as a treatment for hot flashes (Level II).**

Acupuncture is commonly recommended as a non-hormonal option for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/), and it's one of the more frequently studied complementary approaches. After reviewing the randomized trial literature in its **2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement**, The Menopause Society placed **acupuncture** in the **not recommended** category for vasomotor symptoms (Level II) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Why trials haven't supported it

A key issue in acupuncture research is the comparison group: well-designed trials typically compare real acupuncture against **sham acupuncture** (placement that mimics the experience without targeting the specific traditional points). When studies make that comparison, acupuncture for hot flashes hasn't consistently outperformed the sham version by a meaningful margin [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

This doesn't mean acupuncture is unsafe — performed by a licensed practitioner, it's generally low-risk. The verdict is specifically about whether it's an effective *treatment* for reducing hot flash frequency or severity, and the trial evidence doesn't support that.

## What actually has evidence behind it

- **[Hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/)**: The most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms in women without contraindications [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **[CBT and clinical hypnosis](/treatments/nonhormonal/cbt-and-hypnosis/)**: Level I, with no drug interactions [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **[Non-hormonal prescription options](/treatments/nonhormonal/)**: SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, and NK3 antagonists, all Level I [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
