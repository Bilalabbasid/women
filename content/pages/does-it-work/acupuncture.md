**Our verdict:** :grade{id="acupuncture-vms"} for hot flashes. When trials compared real acupuncture with fake (sham) needling, hot flashes improved about as much in both groups [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013; @ee-acupuncture-hot-flashes-rct-2016]. For hot flashes, hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Does acupuncture help hot flashes?

Not more than a convincing fake treatment does. Women who get acupuncture often have fewer [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) afterward, but women given sham acupuncture improve by a similar amount [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013; @ee-acupuncture-hot-flashes-rct-2016]. That points to expectation, attention and time rather than the needles themselves.

After reviewing the trials, The Menopause Society placed acupuncture in its "not recommended" group for hot flashes and night sweats in 2023, at Level II [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. "Not recommended" means the expert panel would not advise it for hot flashes; some readers take it as "not a first choice" rather than "proven useless" [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## What did the studies find?

| Study | What was compared | What it found |
|---|---|---|
| Cochrane review, 2013: 16 trials, 1,155 women [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013] | Acupuncture vs. sham, vs. hormone therapy, or vs. no treatment [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013] | No clear difference from sham in how often hot flashes happened; about 3 more hot flashes a day than with hormone therapy; fewer and milder than with no treatment. Evidence was low or very low quality [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013] |
| Australian trial, 2016: 327 women [@ee-acupuncture-hot-flashes-rct-2016] | 10 sessions over 8 weeks of real needling vs. sham needles that did not go into the skin [@ee-acupuncture-hot-flashes-rct-2016] | Hot flash scores were almost the same at the end: 15.4 with acupuncture, 15.0 with sham [@ee-acupuncture-hot-flashes-rct-2016] |
| US trial (AIM), 2016: 209 women [@avis-acupuncture-aim-trial-2016] | Up to 20 sessions over 6 months vs. a waiting list, with no sham group [@avis-acupuncture-aim-trial-2016] | Hot flashes fell 37% with acupuncture and rose 6% on the waiting list [@avis-acupuncture-aim-trial-2016] |

The sham comparison is the fair test, because it shows whether the needling itself does anything beyond the ritual of treatment. When the Cochrane authors set aside studies of breast cancer survivors, the small advantage they had seen for hot flash severity disappeared too [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013].

## Why do some people say it worked for them?

Their hot flashes often did get better. In the US trial, women who had acupuncture had about a third fewer hot flashes after 6 months, and the drop held at 29% a year in [@avis-acupuncture-aim-trial-2016]. Because that trial had no sham group, it can't tell whether the needles, the regular visits or expectation did the work. The Cochrane authors said results from trials without a sham or placebo group should be read with great caution [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013].

## How many sessions did studies use?

Trials used about 10 sessions over 8 weeks [@ee-acupuncture-hot-flashes-rct-2016] or up to 20 sessions over 6 months [@avis-acupuncture-aim-trial-2016]. In the US trial, change showed up after 3 sessions and was largest after a median of 8 [@avis-acupuncture-aim-trial-2016]. These are research schedules, not a recommended course.

## Is acupuncture safe?

Serious problems are uncommon, but they do happen. The NIH's complementary health center says relatively few complications have been reported, and that nonsterile needles or poor technique have caused infections and punctured organs [@nccih-acupuncture-effectiveness-safety-2022]. In the Australian trial, no serious side effects were reported [@ee-acupuncture-hot-flashes-rct-2016], though the Cochrane review found too little side-effect data to judge [@dodin-cochrane-acupuncture-hot-flushes-2013].

- **Needles**: The FDA treats acupuncture needles as medical devices that must be sterile and for single use only [@nccih-acupuncture-effectiveness-safety-2022].
- **Practitioners**: Most states license acupuncturists, but the requirements vary from state to state [@nccih-acupuncture-effectiveness-safety-2022].
- **Other care**: Don't let acupuncture delay a visit to a doctor about a health problem, and tell your clinicians about any complementary treatment you use [@nccih-acupuncture-effectiveness-safety-2022].

## Does insurance cover acupuncture for hot flashes?

Medicare doesn't. Part B pays for acupuncture only for chronic low back pain, at up to 12 sessions in 90 days and no more than 20 a year [@medicare-acupuncture-coverage]. Treatment for other conditions, hot flashes included, is not covered [@medicare-acupuncture-coverage]. We haven't found reliable published data on private plans, so check your own policy.
