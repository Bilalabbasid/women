**Our verdict: Not supported by clinical guidelines for hot flashes.**

Black cohosh (*Actaea racemosa* / *Cimicifuga racemosa*) is one of the most heavily marketed botanical supplements for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/). However, in its **2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement**, The Menopause Society concluded that over-the-counter supplements and herbal remedies are **not recommended** for vasomotor symptoms (Levels I–II) because randomized controlled trials have not shown consistent benefit over placebo [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Why people experience relief in unblinded use (the placebo effect)

In menopause clinical trials, placebo groups routinely experience a noticeable reduction in hot flash frequency simply from tracking symptoms and participating in a study [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @gunter-menopause-manifesto]. When black cohosh is tested head-to-head against a matching placebo pill in large, independent double-blind trials, it does not reliably outperform placebo [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Furthermore, over-the-counter botanical extracts vary widely in purity and standardization [@gunter-menopause-manifesto].

Because our evidence grade for black cohosh is **Not supported**, MenoVerdict never places affiliate links for black cohosh supplements on this page. See [how we grade evidence](/evidence/how-we-grade/) and [does it work? directory](/does-it-work/).

## What has proven evidence instead

If you want to manage hot flashes without hormones, explore the **Level I–II options** recommended by The Menopause Society [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]:

- [Non-hormonal prescription and behavioral treatments for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/) (including CBT, clinical hypnosis, SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, oxybutynin, [Veozah](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/), and [Lynkuet](/treatments/nonhormonal/elinzanetant/))
