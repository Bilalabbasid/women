**Our verdict:** :grade{id="calcium-fractures"}. After menopause, women need 1,200 mg of calcium a day, ideally mostly from food [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023]. But for preventing fractures in women living at home, the USPSTF recommends against low-dose calcium and vitamin D supplements, which also slightly raise the risk of kidney stones [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-fractures-2018; @uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-falls-fractures-draft-2024].

[Menopause](/menopause/) speeds up bone loss, so calcium pills are a common recommendation. Below: how much calcium you need, the best food sources, what the big trials and the US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) found about supplements, and what has stronger evidence for protecting your bones (see also our [bone health](/postmenopause/bone-health/) guide).

## How much calcium do you need after menopause?

Women aged 51 to 70 need 1,200 mg of calcium a day, and so does everyone 71 and older; that's 200 mg more than women under 51 [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023]. The amount counts food, drinks and supplements together. The body absorbs and keeps less calcium after menopause, which over time can lead to fragile bones [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023].

There is also an upper limit: 2,000 mg a day from all sources for adults 51 and older [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023]. The limit includes supplements, so count them along with what you eat.

## Which foods are high in calcium?

You can get the recommended amount by eating a variety of foods [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023]:

- **Dairy**: milk, yogurt and cheese are the main sources for most people in the US.
- **Fish with bones**: canned sardines and canned salmon with the bones in.
- **Vegetables**: kale, broccoli and Chinese cabbage (bok choy).
- **Fortified foods**: some fruit juices, soy and almond drinks, tofu and breakfast cereals have calcium added; check the label.

## What does the USPSTF say about calcium supplements?

For **postmenopausal women** living at home (not in a care facility), taking calcium together with vitamin D [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-fractures-2018]:

- **Recommends against** daily supplements of **1,000 mg or less** of calcium (with 400 IU or less of vitamin D) to prevent fractures. This is a **Grade D** recommendation, meaning moderate certainty of **no net benefit**.
- Found the evidence **insufficient** to judge benefits and harms at **higher doses**.

That 2018 statement is being updated. A December 2024 draft, not yet final, recommends against vitamin D with or without calcium for preventing fractures in postmenopausal women and men 60 and older, across a broad range of doses [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-falls-fractures-draft-2024]. Pooling seven trials with more than 88,000 people, it found no difference in hip fractures [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-falls-fractures-draft-2024]. The draft does not apply to people with osteoporosis, a past osteoporotic fracture or diagnosed vitamin D deficiency [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-falls-fractures-draft-2024].

## What did the big Women's Health Initiative trial find?

In the Women's Health Initiative, 36,282 healthy postmenopausal women took 1,000 mg of calcium with 400 IU of vitamin D, or a placebo, for an average of 7 years [@jackson-whi-calcium-vitamin-d-fractures-2006]:

- **Bone density**: hip bone density was about 1% higher with the supplements [@jackson-whi-calcium-vitamin-d-fractures-2006].
- **Fractures**: there was no significant drop in hip, spine or total fractures [@jackson-whi-calcium-vitamin-d-fractures-2006].
- **Kidney stones**: they were 17% more common with the supplements [@jackson-whi-calcium-vitamin-d-fractures-2006].

Women who kept taking their pills had fewer hip fractures than those on placebo [@jackson-whi-calcium-vitamin-d-fractures-2006]. Looking back across the WHI trials, its investigators said the results don't support routinely recommending calcium plus vitamin D for all postmenopausal women, but that supplements are appropriate for women who don't get enough from their diet [@manson-whi-trials-clinical-practice-2024].

## Does calcium help menopause symptoms?

No. In the same trial, calcium plus vitamin D made no difference to hot flashes, night sweats, sleep, energy or mood compared with placebo [@leblanc-whi-calcium-vitamin-d-menopause-symptoms-2015].

## Calcium carbonate or citrate: which is better?

These are the two main forms in supplements, and they are absorbed differently [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023]:

- **Calcium carbonate**: absorbed best when taken with food. Some antacids, such as Tums and Rolaids, contain it.
- **Calcium citrate**: absorbed well with or without food, and more easily by people with low stomach acid, which is more common in older people.
- **Splitting the dose**: calcium is absorbed best in amounts of 500 mg or less at a time, so 1,000 mg a day from supplements is better taken as two smaller doses.

## Side effects and risks of calcium supplements

- **Kidney stones**: the USPSTF found adequate evidence that vitamin D and calcium supplements increase the risk of kidney stones; its 2024 draft rated this harm as small, about 2 more people with stones per 1,000 [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-fractures-2018; @uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-falls-fractures-draft-2024].
- **Stomach effects**: gas, bloating and constipation. Spreading the dose through the day, taking it with meals or switching forms can help [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023].
- **Heart**: some studies suggest calcium supplements might raise heart disease risk and others show no effect; overall, experts believe calcium intake doesn't affect heart disease risk [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023].

## Calcium and medication interactions

Yes, with several [@ods-calcium-consumer-2023]:

- **Levothyroxine (thyroid hormone)**: not absorbed well within 4 hours of a calcium carbonate supplement.
- **Quinolone antibiotics** (such as ciprofloxacin): not absorbed well within 2 hours of a calcium supplement.
- **Dolutegravir (an HIV medicine)**: take it 2 hours before or 6 hours after calcium.
- **Lithium**: taken long term or with calcium supplements, it can lead to too much calcium in the blood.

Tell your doctor and pharmacist about any calcium you take, including antacids.

## What protects bones better after menopause?

- **[Hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/)**: prevents bone loss and fractures [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Strength and impact training**: :grade{id="strength-training-bone"}. In one trial, 8 months of supervised high-intensity resistance and impact training twice a week improved spine and hip bone density in postmenopausal women with low bone mass [@liftmor-trial-bone-density].
- **Bone density screening**: the USPSTF recommends osteoporosis screening for women 65 and older, and for younger postmenopausal women at higher fracture risk [@uspstf-osteoporosis-screening-2025].
