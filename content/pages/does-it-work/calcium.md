**Our verdict: Not recommended at typical doses specifically for fracture prevention; evidence is insufficient (not proven, not disproven) at higher doses, and supplementation carries a real downside.**

Calcium supplements are commonly recommended for bone health as [menopause](/menopause/) accelerates bone loss (see our [bone health](/postmenopause/bone-health/) guide). The **US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF)** reviewed the trial evidence on this specific question — calcium supplements for fracture prevention — and reached a more cautious conclusion than common advice suggests.

## What the USPSTF actually concludes

For **postmenopausal women**, taking calcium together with vitamin D [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-fractures-2018]:

- **Recommends against** daily supplementation with **1,000mg or less** of calcium (combined with 400 IU or less of vitamin D) for fracture prevention — a **Grade D** recommendation, reflecting moderate certainty of **no net benefit**.
- Concludes the evidence is **insufficient** to judge benefits and harms at **higher doses**.

## The tradeoff worth knowing about

This isn't simply "no benefit, no harm, so why not." The USPSTF found **adequate evidence that vitamin D and calcium supplementation together increases the risk of kidney stones** [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-fractures-2018]. That's a real, documented downside to weigh against a fracture-prevention benefit the same review couldn't confirm at typical supplement doses.

## What this doesn't mean

This verdict is specifically about **supplement pills** for fracture prevention. It isn't a recommendation to avoid calcium-rich foods as part of a normal diet, which is a separate nutritional question with its own, uncontroversial rationale. If you're specifically taking a calcium supplement because you believe it protects your bones, that's the claim this verdict addresses directly.

See our companion verdict on [vitamin D](/does-it-work/vitamin-d/), since the two are almost always considered together, and our [bone health after menopause](/postmenopause/bone-health/) guide for what does have strong evidence instead — hormone therapy and high-intensity resistance training.
