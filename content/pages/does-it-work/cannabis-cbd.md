**Our verdict:** :grade{id="cannabis-cbd"} for menopause symptoms. The Menopause Society does not recommend cannabis-based products, including CBD, for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy], and no trial evidence we found shows they help menopause sleep or mood. The most effective treatment for hot flashes is hormone therapy [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Does CBD help menopause symptoms?

It hasn't been shown to. After reviewing the trials, The Menopause Society placed cannabinoids in its "not recommended" group for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and night sweats, at Level II [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. CBD (cannabidiol) and THC are two of these compounds from the cannabis plant [@fda-cannabis-cbd-regulation].

The FDA has not approved cannabis for any disease or condition. Its only approved CBD medicine, Epidiolex, treats certain rare seizure disorders, not menopause [@fda-cannabis-cbd-regulation].

## Why do so many women try cannabis for menopause?

Mostly for sleep and worry. In a US survey of 258 women around menopause, most of whom already used cannabis, 79% said they used it for menopause symptoms, most often poor sleep (67%) and mood or anxiety (46%) [@dahlgren-medical-cannabis-menopause-survey-2022]. In a Canadian survey of 1,485 women aged 35 and over, a third were current users, and their top reasons were sleep (65%), anxiety (45%) and muscle or joint aches (33%) [@babyn-cannabis-menopause-survey-2023].

Three in four current users in the Canadian survey said cannabis helped [@babyn-cannabis-menopause-survey-2023]. Surveys like these show what people do and believe, not whether a product works better than a placebo, and both research teams called for proper trials [@dahlgren-medical-cannabis-menopause-survey-2022; @babyn-cannabis-menopause-survey-2023].

## Can CBD help with menopause sleep or anxiety?

The evidence is thin and not specific to menopause. The NIH's complementary health center says the effect of cannabis products on sleep in people without other illnesses is uncertain, and that only a small amount of evidence suggests they might ease anxiety [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019].

For [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) with hot flashes, the best-tested option is CBT for insomnia. In pooled data from four US trials of 546 women, it improved insomnia scores more than any other treatment tested [@guthrie-msflash-insomnia-pooled-2018]. For worry and low mood, see [anxiety during menopause](/symptoms/anxiety/).

## Does the label match what's in the bottle?

Often not. When researchers tested 84 CBD extracts bought online, only 31% held an amount of CBD within 10% of the label; about 26% had less and 43% had more [@bonn-miller-cbd-labeling-2017]. THC turned up in 21% of them, so a product sold as CBD may not be THC-free [@bonn-miller-cbd-labeling-2017].

The FDA says CBD and THC products fall outside the legal definition of a dietary supplement, and unapproved products haven't been checked for side effects or drug interactions [@fda-cannabis-cbd-regulation]. Store-bought products can also contain contaminants [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019].

## Side effects of CBD and cannabis

- **CBD**: Diarrhea, sleepiness, lower alertness, mood changes and abnormal liver tests have been seen in studies. CBD has also been linked to liver injury and male reproductive harm [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019].
- **Cannabis with THC**: It can cause dizziness when you stand up, which may lead to fainting or falls, and it is linked to a higher risk of car crashes [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019].
- **Dependence**: Some people develop cannabis use disorder, with craving, withdrawal and loss of control over use [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019].

## Drug interactions to ask about

CBD can interact with other medicines [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019], and unapproved CBD products have not been tested for how they combine with prescription drugs [@fda-cannabis-cbd-regulation]. If you take any regular medicine, show the product label to a pharmacist before you start. Mention it to whoever prescribes your hot flash, mood or sleep treatment too.

## Who should avoid it?

- **Pregnant or breastfeeding**: The FDA warns of possible harm to the baby from THC-containing products [@fda-cannabis-cbd-regulation].
- **Anyone driving or at risk of falls**: Cannabis is linked to car crashes, and dizziness on standing can lead to falls [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019].
- **People with liver problems or on several medicines**: CBD has been linked to liver injury and drug interactions, so get a doctor or pharmacist to check first [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019].

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if hot flashes, broken sleep or low mood are wearing you down, because treatments with real evidence exist. Talk to someone too if you notice craving, withdrawal or trouble cutting back on cannabis [@nccih-cannabis-cannabinoids-2019]. If you're having thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the US, or 911 in an emergency.

If you're in the UK: call 999 in an emergency, NHS 111, or Samaritans on 116 123.
