**Our verdict: Limited evidence — reasonably good general evidence for skin, but not menopause-specific; no sourced claim we can make for joints or bone.**

Collagen supplements are heavily marketed to women around menopause, often for skin, joint comfort, and bone density together. The actual evidence doesn't line up neatly with that bundle, so we're separating it out.

## What the evidence shows for skin

A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled **26 randomized controlled trials (1,721 participants)** testing oral hydrolyzed collagen for skin aging. Compared with placebo, collagen supplementation was associated with a **statistically significant improvement in both skin hydration and elasticity** (p<0.00001 for both) [@pu-collagen-skin-aging-metaanalysis-2023]. That's a genuinely solid signal across a reasonably large pooled sample.

The important caveat: this is **general adult population research**, not a menopause-specific trial. The reviewers themselves also flagged several sources of bias across the included studies and called for larger, more rigorous trials to confirm the effect [@pu-collagen-skin-aging-metaanalysis-2023]. The biological story — skin collagen content declines with age and with the drop in estrogen after menopause — is a reasonable one, but it's our inference connecting a general finding to the menopause context, not something this meta-analysis tested directly in menopausal women specifically.

## What we couldn't verify: joints and bone

Collagen is also commonly marketed for joint comfort and bone density around menopause, often alongside the skin claims. We looked for research directly testing collagen for **menopause-related joint pain or bone density** and did not find a study we could verify well enough to cite a specific claim here. That's an honest evidence gap, not a negative finding — we simply don't have research to point to either way for this specific use, so we're not making a claim on it. If you're exploring these claims elsewhere, that absence of solid sourcing is worth noticing.

## What to consider

- **For skin specifically**, the general evidence is reasonably encouraging, even though it isn't menopause-specific
- **For joint pain or bone health specifically**, we don't have a verified claim to make — see our [joint and muscle pain](/symptoms/joint-pain/) page for what does have direct evidence for that symptom
- **Collagen source and dose varied** across the pooled trials, so there's no single confirmed "best" product or dose to point to

If bone density specifically is your concern, see our [probiotics](/does-it-work/probiotics/) verdict, which has stronger menopause-specific evidence for that outcome, or compare every option in our [does it work? evidence checker](/tools/does-it-work-checker/).
