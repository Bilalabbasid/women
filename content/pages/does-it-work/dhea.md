**Our verdict: Depends entirely on which DHEA you mean — oral supplement (Limited/Not supported for general symptoms) vs. prescription vaginal insert (Moderate to Strong, for vaginal dryness and painful sex specifically).**

"DHEA" covers two genuinely different products marketed for menopause, and conflating them is one of the most common mistakes in this space. Here's the evidence for each, kept separate on purpose.

## Oral DHEA supplements (for general menopause symptoms, energy, libido, "anti-aging")

A Cochrane systematic review pooled **28 trials covering 1,273 menopausal women** [@scheffers-dhea-cochrane-2015]. The findings were not encouraging for the broad claims these supplements are often sold on:

- **No evidence DHEA improves overall quality of life** compared with placebo (moderate-quality evidence)
- **Associated with androgenic side effects**, mainly acne, significantly more than placebo
- **Unclear whether it affects general menopausal symptoms** (hot flashes, etc.) — trial results were too inconsistent to pool into one answer
- **May slightly improve sexual function** overall, but when the oral-only trials were looked at separately from the one intravaginal trial, the oral subgroup's effect on sexual function was **not itself statistically significant** [@scheffers-dhea-cochrane-2015]

In plain terms: the broad "DHEA for menopausal wellbeing" claim that supplement marketing often makes isn't backed by this evidence. The one area with a hint of benefit (sexual function) is driven more by the intravaginal data than the oral data.

## Prescription vaginal DHEA (prasterone / Intrarosa) for vaginal dryness and painful sex

This is a different story. A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled **5 unique randomized controlled trials (1,611 women)** testing **intravaginal DHEA**, a nightly vaginal insert, specifically for vulvovaginal atrophy (vaginal dryness and painful sex after menopause) [@lemos-intravaginal-dhea-meta-analysis-2026]. Compared with placebo, it produced:

- A **significant improvement in vaginal dryness** (mean difference -0.23, 95% CI -0.35 to -0.11) [@lemos-intravaginal-dhea-meta-analysis-2026]
- A **significant improvement in dyspareunia** (painful sex) (mean difference -0.40, 95% CI -0.66 to -0.15) [@lemos-intravaginal-dhea-meta-analysis-2026]
- **No major safety concerns**, with only mild and infrequent adverse effects [@lemos-intravaginal-dhea-meta-analysis-2026]

This product is FDA-approved under the brand name **Intrarosa**, available by prescription, and works locally in vaginal tissue rather than as a general hormone supplement. It's a genuinely different evidence picture from the oral capsule sold in supplement aisles.

## The bottom line

| Form | Use | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oral DHEA capsule/supplement** | General wellbeing, energy, libido, "anti-aging" | Limited — no proven quality-of-life benefit, linked to acne, unclear symptom effect [@scheffers-dhea-cochrane-2015] |
| **Prescription vaginal DHEA (prasterone/Intrarosa)** | Vaginal dryness, painful sex | Moderate to strong — significant, replicated benefit for this specific use [@lemos-intravaginal-dhea-meta-analysis-2026] |
