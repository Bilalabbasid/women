**Our verdict: Limited evidence — one genuinely positive, well-designed trial, not yet independently replicated.**

Equelle's ingredient, S-equol, is one of the more scientifically interesting entries in the menopause supplement aisle, and the one real trial behind it is worth taking seriously — while still being honest about what "one trial" does and doesn't prove.

## The biology behind it

About 20–30% of people (and a smaller share of non-Asian populations) have gut bacteria that convert daidzein, a compound in soy, into **S-equol**, which has estrogen-like activity. People who don't produce it naturally are called "equol non-producers." Equelle's premise is to supply S-equol directly, bypassing the need for that gut conversion — which is why the key trial specifically recruited women who don't make it naturally.

## What the trial actually found

A multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial recruited **160 postmenopausal Japanese women** who were confirmed equol non-producers and who experienced at least one hot flush per day. They took either **10 mg/day of natural S-equol** or a placebo for 12 weeks [@aso-s-equol-hot-flash-trial-2012]. Results:

- Hot flush frequency dropped **58.7%** in the S-equol group versus **34.5%** with placebo — a statistically significant difference (p=0.009)
- **Hot flush severity** and **neck or shoulder muscle stiffness** also decreased significantly more than placebo
- **No serious adverse effects** were reported [@aso-s-equol-hot-flash-trial-2012]

The researchers themselves describe this as "the first trial" to show this effect, which is an important detail: a real, well-designed, statistically significant result, but a first result, not a settled one [@aso-s-equol-hot-flash-trial-2012].

## Why we're grading this Limited, not higher

Our evidence grades reflect the overall weight of evidence a treatment has built up, not just whether one trial succeeded. This is a single trial, in one population (postmenopausal Japanese women), with a 12-week follow-up. Good trial design (randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled) is a real strength, but it hasn't yet been confirmed by **independent replication** in other populations, including the non-Asian equol non-producers who make up much of this product's target market in the US.

That's a meaningfully better starting point than several other supplements on our [does it work? directory](/does-it-work/) — this isn't a "not supported" verdict — but it's not yet the kind of broadly replicated, guideline-reviewed evidence base that earns a Moderate or Strong grade.

- **Not yet independently replicated** across broader populations
