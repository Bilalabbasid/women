Many women turn to non-prescription supplements and alternative remedies seeking natural relief, but clinical trial results frequently contradict marketing promises [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. 

In this directory, every option is graded based on peer-reviewed randomized controlled trials (RCTs), systematic reviews, and consensus guidelines from major medical societies.

## How we evaluate options

We examine each product and ingredient using four criteria:

1. **Human clinical trial data**: Did double-blind, placebo-controlled trials demonstrate measurable symptom reduction?
2. **Safety profile**: Are there known risks of hepatotoxicity, drug interactions, or bleeding complications?
3. **Consistency of effect**: Have positive findings been replicated in independent studies, or only in brand-funded trials?
4. **Regulatory status**: Is the substance subject to manufacturing standardization and purity testing?

## Common supplements evaluated

- [Does magnesium help menopause symptoms?](/does-it-work/magnesium/): An honest review of sleep and relaxation benefits, trial limits, and optimal forms.
- **Black cohosh**: One of the most studied herbs for hot flashes; evidence remains mixed with notable placebo response rates.
- **Red clover and soy isoflavones**: Phytoestrogens with modest, variable effects on mild hot flashes.
- **Ashwagandha and adaptogens**: Small trials suggest stress and cortisol modulation, but long-term safety data in menopause is limited.

## Commercial integrity notice

Our evidence grades are established strictly from clinical trial literature before any commercial links are considered. If a product is graded **Not supported** or **Harm concern**, that grade will never be altered by commercial partnerships.
