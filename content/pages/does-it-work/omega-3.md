**Our verdict: Not recommended as a treatment for hot flashes, sleep, or mood.**

Omega-3 fatty acids (commonly taken as fish oil) are widely marketed for menopause symptoms, including [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/). A well-designed, double-blind randomized controlled trial from the **MsFLASH research network** — a well-regarded NIH-funded clinical trials network focused specifically on menopause symptoms — tested this directly in 355 women [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].

## What the trial actually found

Women took either 1.8 grams per day of omega-3 (EPA and DHA) or a placebo for 12 weeks. The results were clear [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]:

- **Hot flash frequency:** No significant difference from placebo (a relative difference of just 0.3 fewer hot flashes per day, which was not statistically significant).
- **Hot flash bother:** No significant difference from placebo.
- **Sleep and mood:** No improvement in self-reported sleep quality or mood compared with placebo.

This wasn't a small or poorly controlled study — it was double-blind and reasonably well-powered, which makes this a fairly solid null result rather than just "not enough evidence yet."

## Why this one is worth taking seriously as a "no"

A lot of supplement verdicts on this site land on "evidence is mixed" or "too early to tell." This one is more clear-cut: a trial specifically designed to test this question, in a relevant population, found no benefit across every outcome it measured [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].
