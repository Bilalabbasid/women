**Our verdict:** :grade{id="omega-3-vms"}. In a double-blind trial of 355 women, omega-3 fish oil worked no better than a placebo for hot flashes, sleep or mood [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]. For hot flashes, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Omega-3 fatty acids (commonly taken as fish oil) are widely marketed for menopause symptoms, including [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/). A double-blind randomized controlled trial from the **MsFLASH research network** tested this directly in 355 women with hot flashes and night sweats [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].

## What the trial actually found

Women took either 1.8 grams per day of omega-3 (EPA and DHA) or a placebo for 12 weeks [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]. Compared with placebo, omega-3 showed:

- **Hot flash frequency**: No significant difference (about 0.3 fewer hot flashes per day, which could easily be chance) [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].
- **Hot flash bother**: No significant difference [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].
- **Sleep and mood**: No improvement in self-reported sleep quality or mood [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].

This wasn't a small or loosely controlled study. It was double-blind, with 177 women on omega-3 and 178 on placebo, so this is a fairly solid "no" rather than "not enough evidence yet" [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].

## Why this one is worth taking seriously as a "no"

A lot of supplement verdicts on this site land on "evidence is mixed" or "too early to tell." This one is more clear-cut: a trial designed to test this question, in women with hot flashes, found no benefit on any of these outcomes [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].

## What has stronger evidence

For hot flashes, The Menopause Society recommends CBT, clinical hypnosis, SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin and fezolinetant (Level I) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
