**Our verdict:** :grade{id="omega-3-vms"}. In a double-blind trial of 355 women, omega-3 fish oil worked no better than a placebo for hot flashes, sleep or mood [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]. For hot flashes, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Omega-3 fatty acids, most often taken as fish oil, are widely marketed for menopause symptoms, including [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/). Below: what the main menopause trial found, what omega-3 may and may not do for mood, eyes, joints and heart, how much people take, and the side effects to know about.

## What the trial actually found

The **MsFLASH research network** tested omega-3 directly in 355 women with hot flashes and night sweats [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]. Women took either 1.8 grams a day of omega-3 (EPA and DHA) or a placebo for 12 weeks [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]. Compared with placebo, omega-3 showed:

- **Hot flash frequency**: no significant difference (about 0.3 fewer hot flashes a day, which could easily be chance) [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].
- **Hot flash bother**: no significant difference [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].
- **Sleep and mood**: no improvement in self-reported sleep quality or mood [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].

## Why this one is worth taking seriously as a "no"

Many supplement verdicts on this site land on "evidence is mixed" or "too early to tell." This one is more clear-cut. The trial was double-blind, with 177 women on omega-3 and 178 on placebo, and it was designed to answer this exact question in women who had hot flashes [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]. So it counts as a fairly solid "no" rather than "not enough evidence yet."

## Does omega-3 help menopause mood or depression?

Not in menopause studies so far. The MsFLASH trial found no mood benefit [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014]. More broadly, a 2021 review of 35 studies of omega-3 for depression concluded that any effect may be too small to be meaningful, and the quality of evidence was low or very low [@nccih-omega3-2024]. If low mood is affecting your daily life, talk to a doctor; our [depression](/symptoms/depression/) page covers what can help.

## Does fish oil help dry eyes or joint pain?

Dry eyes and achy joints are both common in midlife, and fish oil is often suggested for them. The evidence is weak for both:

- **Dry eyes**: in the NIH-funded DREAM trial, 535 people with moderate-to-severe dry eye took 3,000 mg of fish-derived omega-3 or an olive oil placebo for a year. Symptoms improved about equally in both groups [@dream-omega3-dry-eye-2018]. See our [dry eyes](/symptoms/dry-eyes/) page for other options.
- **Joint pain**: in rheumatoid arthritis, people taking omega-3 with their usual treatment may need less pain medicine, but it's not clear whether it reduces pain, swelling or stiffness [@ods-omega3-consumer-2022]. We haven't found trials of omega-3 for menopause-related [joint pain](/symptoms/joint-pain/).

## Is omega-3 good for women over 50 for heart health?

Eating fish helps; supplements are mainly for people at higher heart risk. The American Heart Association recommends one to two servings of seafood a week, and about 1 g a day of EPA plus DHA for people with heart disease, but it does not recommend omega-3 supplements for people without high cardiovascular risk [@ods-omega3-consumer-2022]. A 2020 review of 86 studies found more EPA and DHA slightly lowered the risk of coronary events and coronary death [@nccih-omega3-2024]. For some conditions, the evidence for eating seafood is stronger than for taking supplements [@nccih-omega3-2024]. Our [heart health after menopause](/postmenopause/heart-health/) guide covers other risk factors.

## How much omega-3 do you need?

There is no set daily amount for EPA and DHA, the omega-3s in fish oil; a recommended amount exists only for ALA, the plant form (1.1 g a day for women) [@ods-omega3-consumer-2022]. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans advise adults to eat 8 ounces or more of a variety of seafood a week [@nccih-omega3-2024]. FDA advises no more than 5 g a day of EPA and DHA combined from supplements [@ods-omega3-consumer-2022]. For comparison, the menopause trial used 1.8 g a day [@cohen-omega3-vms-rct-2014].

## Side effects and interactions

Side effects are usually mild [@ods-omega3-consumer-2022; @nccih-omega3-2024]:

- **Common side effects**: unpleasant taste, bad breath, heartburn, nausea, stomach discomfort, diarrhea, headache and smelly sweat [@ods-omega3-consumer-2022].
- **Blood thinners**: high doses may cause bleeding problems when taken with warfarin or other anticoagulant medicines [@ods-omega3-consumer-2022].
- **Fish liver oils**: cod liver oil also contains vitamins A and D in varying amounts, and both can be harmful in excess [@nccih-omega3-2024].

Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before starting fish oil if you take a blood thinner or other regular medicines.

## What has stronger evidence

For hot flashes, The Menopause Society rates CBT, clinical hypnosis, SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin and fezolinetant as Level I options [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
