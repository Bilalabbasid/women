**Our verdict:** :grade{id="red-clover"}. In a large Cochrane review, red clover extract worked no better than a placebo for hot flashes [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. If hot flashes are your main problem, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Red clover extract (sold under names like Promensil) is a phytoestrogen supplement, a plant product with estrogen-like compounds, marketed for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. A **2013 Cochrane systematic review** — examining 43 randomized trials of phytoestrogens in 4,364 women — pooled the 5 trials that used red clover extract and found **no significant difference in how often hot flashes happened** compared with placebo (mean difference -0.93, 95% CI -1.95 to 0.10) [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013].

## The review's bottom line, and one nuance worth knowing

The Cochrane authors' overall conclusion, across all phytoestrogen types examined: **"no conclusive evidence shows that phytoestrogen supplements effectively reduce the frequency or severity of hot flushes and night sweats"** [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013].

One nuance: 4 separate trials (not combined in the main analysis) using extracts with **high levels of genistein** (above 30mg a day) showed more consistent drops in how often hot flashes happened, which the review's authors said deserves more study [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. Those trials were separate from the pooled red clover trials, so they don't change the verdict on red clover, but they are a reason this area of research isn't entirely closed.

## Why it can feel like it's working

The same review noted a **strong placebo effect** across most trials, with hot flashes dropping by 1% to 59% in the placebo groups [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. That's a meaningful part of why red clover and similar supplements can feel effective in everyday use even when controlled trials don't show a real benefit over placebo.

## Is it at least safe?

The review found **no evidence that these supplements acted like estrogen on the womb lining or vagina, and no other side effects**, in trials lasting up to two years [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. However, people with a personal history of breast cancer were excluded from the underlying trials, so this doesn't establish safety for that group — see our [menopause after breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/) guide, and check with your oncologist before taking any phytoestrogen supplement.

## What has stronger evidence instead

See our [soy and isoflavones](/does-it-work/soy-isoflavones/) and [black cohosh](/does-it-work/black-cohosh/) verdicts for other commonly marketed plant remedies. For hot flashes, The Menopause Society recommends CBT, clinical hypnosis, SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin and fezolinetant (Level I) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]; see our [non-hormonal treatments overview](/treatments/nonhormonal/).
