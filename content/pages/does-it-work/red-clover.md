**Our verdict: Not recommended as a treatment for hot flashes.**

Red clover extract (marketed under names like Promensil) is one of the most studied botanical supplements for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/), because it contains isoflavones similar to those in soy. A comprehensive **2013 Cochrane systematic review** — examining 43 randomized trials of phytoestrogens in 4,364 women — pooled the 5 trials that used red clover extract and found **no significant difference in hot flush frequency** compared with placebo (mean difference -0.93, 95% CI -1.95 to 0.10) [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013].

## The review's bottom line, and one nuance worth knowing

The Cochrane authors' overall conclusion, across all phytoestrogen types examined: **"no conclusive evidence shows that phytoestrogen supplements effectively reduce the frequency or severity of hot flushes and night sweats"** [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013].

One nuance: 4 separate trials (not combined into the main pooled analysis) using extracts with **high levels of genistein** (another isoflavone, above 30mg/day) showed more consistent reductions in hot flush frequency, which the review's authors said deserves further investigation [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. That's a different, more concentrated extract than standard red clover products — it doesn't change the verdict on red clover itself, but it's a reason this area of research isn't entirely closed.

## Why it can feel like it's working

The same review noted a **strong placebo effect** across most trials, with hot flush reductions of 1% to 59% in placebo groups [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. That's a meaningful part of why red clover and similar supplements can feel effective in everyday use even when controlled trials don't show a real benefit over placebo.

## Is it at least safe?

The review found **no evidence of estrogenic stimulation of the endometrium or vagina, or other adverse effects**, in trials using these supplements for up to two years [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. However, people with a personal history of breast cancer were excluded from the underlying trials, so this doesn't establish safety for that group — see our [menopause after breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/) guide, and check with your oncologist before taking any phytoestrogen supplement.
