**Our verdict: Not recommended as a clinical treatment for hot flashes (Level II).**

Soybeans contain plant compounds called **isoflavones** (such as genistein and daidzein) that bind weakly to estrogen receptors, and some people have gut bacteria that convert daidzein into **S-equol**. However, after reviewing the randomized trial literature in its **2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement**, The Menopause Society placed **soy foods and extracts, as well as equol**, in the **not recommended** category for treating [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) (Level II) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Nuance: whole food nutrition vs hot flash pills

As noted in our source registry [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy], "not recommended" in the 2023 guideline means the evidence is too inconsistent or modest to recommend soy isoflavones or equol as an effective therapy for vasomotor symptoms. A 2013 Cochrane systematic review of 43 trials across all major phytoestrogen types reached the same conclusion, while also noting a strong placebo effect of 1% to 59% reduction that likely explains why these supplements can feel effective in everyday use [@lethaby-cochrane-phytoestrogens-2013]. See our [red clover](/does-it-work/red-clover/) verdict for how that specific extract fared in the same review.

Enjoying whole soy foods (such as tofu, tempeh, or edamame) as part of a nutritious diet is very different from buying concentrated over-the-counter isoflavone capsules expecting prescription-level relief.

If you have a personal history of hormone-sensitive [breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/), always check with your oncologist before taking any concentrated phytoestrogen or isoflavone supplement.

## Proven options to compare instead

- **[Menopausal hormone therapy (HRT)](/treatments/hormone-therapy/)**: Most effective option for moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms in women without contraindications [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **[Level I non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/)**: Including CBT, clinical hypnosis, SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, and NK3 antagonists [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
