**Our verdict:** :grade{id="yoga-vms"}. In the largest trial, yoga did not reduce hot flashes, though it modestly improved sleep and day-to-day quality of life [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014; @reed-yoga-menqol-rct-2014]. The Menopause Society does not recommend yoga for hot flashes; hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for them [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

**Yoga for menopause at a glance**

- **Hot flashes**: No change in the largest trial: in a 12-week study of 249 women, those who did yoga had as many hot flashes as those who carried on as usual, and found them just as bothersome [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014]. The Menopause Society does not recommend yoga for hot flashes (Level II) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Sleep**: Insomnia symptoms improved slightly in that trial; in pooled data from four trials, CBT for insomnia helped most [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014; @guthrie-msflash-insomnia-pooled-2018].
- **Stress and mood**: Reviews found benefits for stress in healthy adults and for anxiety symptoms [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].
- **Better than other exercise?**: Not clearly; a 2018 review found broadly similar effects [@cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018].
- **How much was tested**: One 90-minute class a week for 12 weeks, plus daily practice at home [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014].
- **Is it safe?**: Generally, yes. Beginners should skip extreme poses such as headstands, and hot yoga can cause overheating and dehydration [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].

## Does yoga help menopause symptoms?

A little, for some symptoms, but not as a hot flash treatment. Trials suggest yoga can ease sleep problems and lift overall well-being slightly, about as much as other exercise does [@reed-yoga-menqol-rct-2014; @cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018]. For [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) themselves, The Menopause Society placed yoga, along with mindfulness, in its "not recommended" group in 2023, at Level II [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Does yoga reduce hot flashes?

Not in the best-run trial. In a 12-week US study of 249 women, those who took a weekly yoga class and practiced at home had the same number of hot flashes as those who carried on as usual, and found them just as bothersome [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014].

| Study | Who and what | Result |
|---|---|---|
| MsFLASH trial, 2014: 249 women [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014] | 12 weekly 90-minute classes plus daily home practice vs. usual activity [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014] | No change in how often hot flashes came or how much they bothered women; insomnia symptoms improved slightly [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014] |
| Same trial, quality of life, 2014: 355 women [@reed-yoga-menqol-rct-2014] | Yoga vs. aerobic exercise vs. usual activity [@reed-yoga-menqol-rct-2014] | Yoga modestly improved menopause quality of life scores; the authors said the clinical value was uncertain [@reed-yoga-menqol-rct-2014] |
| Review, 2018: 13 trials, 1,306 people [@cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018] | Yoga vs. no treatment or other exercise [@cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018] | Fewer symptoms than with no treatment, including hot flashes. Against other exercise, only hot flashes differed, and only slightly [@cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018] |

The review's authors found their results did not hold up when they checked for some kinds of trial bias, such as dropouts and unblinded scoring [@cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018]. Weighing all the trials in 2023, The Menopause Society still did not recommend yoga for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Can yoga help with menopause sleep?

Slightly. Yoga improved [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) symptoms in the MsFLASH trial [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014]. When data from four trials in 546 women with hot flashes and poor sleep were pooled, yoga gave a small gain, while CBT for insomnia gave the largest drop in insomnia scores [@guthrie-msflash-insomnia-pooled-2018]. NIH reviewers note that yoga has helped sleep in several groups, including women with sleep problems [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].

## Can yoga help with anxiety, mood and stress?

It may help with everyday stress and worry. The NIH's complementary health center reports that reviews found benefits for stress in healthy adults and for anxiety symptoms, though evidence for diagnosed anxiety disorders is limited [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023]. Compared with no treatment, yoga eased emotional menopause symptoms in the 2018 review [@cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018]. If worry or low mood is persistent, see our page on [anxiety during menopause](/symptoms/anxiety/).

## Is yoga better than other exercise for menopause?

Not clearly. The 2018 review found yoga's effects broadly comparable to other exercise, with a small edge only for hot flashes [@cramer-yoga-menopause-metaanalysis-2018], and NIH reviewers say it seems at least as effective as other types [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023]. For bone strength after menopause, the best evidence is for strength and impact training, graded :grade{id="strength-training-bone"}; see [strength training through menopause](/does-it-work/exercise/).

## How much yoga did studies use?

The main US trial used one 90-minute class a week for 12 weeks, plus daily practice at home [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014]. That is the amount tested, not a prescription, and the benefits it produced were modest [@newton-yoga-vms-rct-2014; @reed-yoga-menqol-rct-2014].

## Is yoga safe during menopause?

Yes, for most healthy people. Yoga is generally considered safe, with a lower injury risk than high-impact activity; most injuries are sprains and strains, and serious ones are rare [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023]. Injury rates are higher in people 65 and older [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].

- **Beginners**: Learn with a qualified teacher and skip extreme poses such as headstands and shoulder stands [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].
- **Hot yoga**: It carries a risk of overheating and dehydration [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].
- **Health conditions**: Old injuries, lower back or spine disease, severe high blood pressure, balance problems and glaucoma may call for changes to your practice; check with your clinician and tell your teacher [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if hot flashes or night sweats are disturbing your sleep, work or mood despite lifestyle changes, because proven treatments exist [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Before starting yoga, talk to your clinician if you have a condition from the safety list above [@nccih-yoga-effectiveness-safety-2023].
