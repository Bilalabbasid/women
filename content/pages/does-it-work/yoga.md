**Our verdict: Not recommended as a treatment for hot flashes (Level II).**

Yoga is one of the most commonly suggested lifestyle approaches for perimenopause and menopause symptoms. After reviewing the randomized trial literature in its **2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement**, The Menopause Society placed **yoga** (alongside mindfulness) in the **not recommended** category specifically for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) (Level II) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## A narrower finding than it might sound

This verdict is specifically about **vasomotor symptoms as a measured trial outcome** — it doesn't mean yoga has no value during this life stage. Many women find real benefit from yoga for general stress, flexibility, sleep quality, and overall wellbeing, even if it doesn't move the needle on hot flash frequency itself in controlled trials. If you already enjoy yoga, there's no reason to stop; the point is simply not to rely on it as your primary strategy if hot flashes themselves are disrupting your life.

For general exercise benefits during menopause — including what's actually well-supported for bone density, mood, and weight — see our dedicated [exercise](/does-it-work/exercise/) verdict, which is graded differently from this specific hot-flash question.

## What actually has evidence for hot flashes specifically

- **[Hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/)**: Most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms without contraindications [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **[CBT and clinical hypnosis](/treatments/nonhormonal/cbt-and-hypnosis/)**: Level I, with real stress-and-sleep benefits layered on top [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **[Non-hormonal prescriptions](/treatments/nonhormonal/)**: SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, NK3 antagonists [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
