In health publishing, bold claims are easy to make but difficult to prove. We evaluate treatments, supplements, and lifestyle interventions against a rigorous hierarchy of scientific evidence.

## The evidence hierarchy

When reviewing options, we prioritize sources in the following order:

1. **Regulatory approvals and prescribing labels**: FDA, EMA, and MHRA evaluations based on pivotal Phase 3 trials.
2. **Society consensus guidelines**: The Menopause Society (formerly NAMS), the International Menopause Society (IMS), and the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE).
3. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses**: Cochrane reviews and independent pooled analyses of randomized controlled trials.
4. **Individual randomized controlled trials (RCTs)**: Well-powered, placebo-controlled human studies.
5. **Observational and preliminary research**: Cohort and animal studies (clearly labelled as exploratory).

## Our evidence grades explained

- **Strong evidence**: Proven efficacy replicated across multiple randomized controlled trials or adopted into formal clinical guidelines.
- **Moderate evidence**: Supported by at least one high-quality randomized trial or consistent observational data, but with minor limitations in sample size or duration.
- **Limited evidence**: Conflicting findings, small study populations, or benefits restricted to subjective self-reports without objective physiological changes.
- **Not supported**: Robust clinical trials demonstrate no significant benefit compared to placebo.
- **Harm concern**: Credible evidence indicates that potential adverse effects (e.g., hepatotoxicity, increased thrombosis risk) outweigh potential benefits.
- **Per option**: Used on symptom hub pages where multiple treatment choices are compared side-by-side in a graded summary table.
