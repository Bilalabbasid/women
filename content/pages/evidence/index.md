MenoVerdict is a free menopause guide where every medical claim links to a published source that we opened and read. Medicine labels and medical society guidelines count most, and small or brand-funded studies count least. Each treatment gets a plain-language evidence grade, and no doctor has checked our pages yet.

## What "evidence-based" means on this site

Here, evidence-based means every medical statement comes from a published source you can check yourself, listed under References at the end of the page. If we can't confirm a claim from a source we've read, we take it out rather than guess. AI tools help us read, summarize and grade the evidence, and they can make mistakes, which is why every claim points back to its source.

## Where our information comes from

We weigh sources in this order, strongest first:

1. **Medicine labels and regulators**: prescribing information and safety notices from the US FDA and other regulators such as the UK MHRA.
2. **Medical society guidelines**: advice from expert groups such as The Menopause Society, NICE in the UK, ACOG and the International Menopause Society.
3. **Systematic reviews**: studies that pool the results of many trials, such as Cochrane reviews.
4. **Randomized controlled trials**: studies where people are assigned by chance to a treatment or a comparison, such as a placebo.
5. **Cohort and other studies**: research that follows groups of people over time and looks for links between health and later outcomes.
6. **Small studies, preprints and brand-funded research**: listed separately and weighed with caution.

We also use patient information pages from health services such as MedlinePlus and the NHS. You can see every source we use, grouped this way, on [our sources page](/evidence/sources/).

## How we grade the evidence

Each grade applies to one treatment for one use, for example hormone therapy for hot flashes, not to a treatment in general. The grade words mean:

- **Strong evidence**: the benefit is shown in several randomized trials, or is part of formal clinical guidelines.
- **Moderate evidence**: at least one good randomized trial or consistent observational data, with some limits such as size or length.
- **Limited evidence**: conflicting results, small studies, or benefits seen only in how people rate their own symptoms.
- **Comfort only**: it may make you more comfortable, but it doesn't treat the problem.
- **Not supported**: good trials found no real benefit over placebo.
- **Harm concern**: credible evidence suggests the risks outweigh the benefits.

## What we don't do

- **No ads or sponsors**: there are no ads or sponsors, and nobody pays to be mentioned or graded. See [how MenoVerdict is funded](/about/how-we-make-money/).
- **No diagnosis or prescribing**: our pages and tools explain options and guidelines, and never tell you what you have or what to take. See our [editorial policy](/evidence/editorial-policy/).
- **No doctor's sign-off yet**: no doctor has checked our pages. If one does, their name, credentials and the date will appear on that page. See [our medical review process](/evidence/medical-review/).
