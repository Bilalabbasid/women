Every piece of menopause advice on this site comes from a published source listed on this page: medicine labels, medical society guidelines, research reviews, clinical trials and health service pages. They're ranked strongest first, and each one links to the original and to the pages on this site that cite it.

## Where does our menopause advice come from?

From published sources only, never from personal stories. Each kind of source does a different job:

- **Medicine labels and regulator statements**: what a medicine is approved for, its warnings and its side effects, from bodies such as the US FDA.
- **Society and clinical guidelines**: what expert groups such as The Menopause Society and NICE advise doctors to do, after weighing the research.
- **Systematic reviews and randomized trials**: the research that tests whether a treatment works better than a placebo or another option.
- **Cohort studies**: large studies that follow women over many years, which tell us how long symptoms tend to last and what changes with age.
- **Patient information pages**: plain-language health pages from services such as MedlinePlus and the NHS.
- **Other sources**: commentary, narrative reviews and companies' own pages, used for facts such as published prices.
- **Small studies, preprints and brand-funded research**: kept in their own groups and weighed with caution.

Unclear sourcing is a real problem with menopause information online. In a 2025 UK survey, 92.7% of participants had used the internet for menopause information, and they named outdated content and a lack of source transparency among the gaps they found [@jmir-online-menopause-information-2025]. This page is our answer to that: you can check every source yourself.

## How to check a claim on any page

1. Open the page and scroll to its References section at the end.
2. Find the source here, or follow its link to the original publisher page or DOI.
3. See which other pages cite the same source, listed under each entry.

## How to read this list

Sources are grouped the way our [evidence grading method](/evidence/how-we-grade/) orders them: regulator labels and statements first, then society and clinical guidelines, then systematic reviews and randomized controlled trials, then cohort studies, patient information pages and other sources. Small studies, preprints and brand-funded research come last, in their own groups, so you can weigh them accordingly. Within each group, the newest come first.

Each entry links to the original source and lists which published pages on this site cite it. A source with a funding conflict shows it directly, not in fine print elsewhere.
