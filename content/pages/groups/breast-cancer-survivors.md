Chemotherapy, ovarian suppression, and endocrine therapies (such as tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors) frequently trigger sudden, severe [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/), [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/), and [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/). Because systemic hormone therapy is generally contraindicated after breast cancer, management focuses on proven [non-hormonal options](/treatments/nonhormonal/) coordinated with your oncology team.

## 1. Evidence-backed non-hormonal options for hot flashes

In its 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement, The Menopause Society identified several **Level I–II non-hormonal treatments** that reduce vasomotor symptom frequency or distress in randomized trials [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]:

- **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and clinical hypnosis (Level I):** Reduce how much hot flashes and night sweats interfere with daily life and sleep, with zero drug interactions [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Selected SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, and oxybutynin (Levels I–II):** Prescription medications shown to reduce hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. *Crucial medication interaction check:* If you take **tamoxifen**, certain SSRIs (such as paroxetine and fluoxetine) inhibit the CYP2D6 enzyme that activates tamoxifen, so your oncologist and prescriber must choose an option that does not interfere with your endocrine therapy.
- **Neurokinin 3 (NK3) antagonists (Level I):** Non-hormonal options such as [fezolinetant (Veozah)](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]; discuss safety and required liver blood monitoring with your oncologist.

By contrast, over-the-counter herbal remedies and [menopause supplements](/does-it-work/) are **not recommended** by The Menopause Society for hot flashes (Levels I–II) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] and can interact with cancer treatments.

## 2. Managing vaginal dryness and painful sex

Both The Menopause Society (2020) and ACOG (2021) recommend **regular non-hormonal vaginal moisturisers** (used several times per week for continuous tissue hydration) alongside **personal lubricants** during sexual activity as first-line care [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. If severe symptoms persist despite consistent non-hormonal care, any discussion of low-dose local vaginal therapy must take place directly with your oncologist [@nams-2020-gsm].
