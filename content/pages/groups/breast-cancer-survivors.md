Hot flashes, night sweats, and vaginal dryness are common after breast cancer treatment, and UK guidance says to stop systemic hormone therapy (HRT) when breast cancer is diagnosed [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Non-hormonal options with the strongest evidence include talking therapy (CBT), clinical hypnosis, and some prescription medicines [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. For vaginal dryness, moisturizers and lubricants come first [@nams-2020-gsm].

Chemotherapy, ovarian suppression, and hormone-blocking treatments such as tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors can all bring on [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/), [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/), and [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/). Any hormone use after breast cancer is a decision to make with your oncology team [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy], so most care focuses on [non-hormonal options](/treatments/nonhormonal/).

## 1. Non-hormonal options for hot flashes

In its 2023 position statement, The Menopause Society listed several non-hormonal treatments that reduced how often hot flashes happened, or how much they bothered people, in randomized trials [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. These are the options:

- **CBT and clinical hypnosis** (:grade{id="cbt-hypnosis-vms"}): Reduce how much hot flashes and night sweats disrupt daily life and sleep, and involve no medicine [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Some antidepressants (SSRIs/SNRIs), gabapentin, and oxybutynin**: Prescription medicines shown to reduce hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. If you take **tamoxifen**, some SSRIs, especially paroxetine and fluoxetine, can block the enzyme (CYP2D6) your body uses to activate tamoxifen. The Menopause Society names venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, escitalopram, or citalopram as safer choices with tamoxifen [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy], so your oncologist and prescriber should choose together.
- **NK3 blockers** (:grade{id="nk3-vms"}): Non-hormonal medicines such as [fezolinetant (Veozah)](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Its label carries a warning about liver injury and requires liver blood tests before starting and during the first months of treatment [@veozah-prescribing-information].

Over-the-counter herbal remedies and [menopause supplements](/does-it-work/) are **not recommended** by The Menopause Society for hot flashes (:grade{id="otc-supplements-vms"}) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. UK NICE guidance also warns that St John's wort can interact with medicines including tamoxifen [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## 2. Managing vaginal dryness and painful sex

The Menopause Society (2020) and ACOG (2021) both recommend **regular vaginal moisturizers** (used several times a week) plus **lubricants** during sex as the first step [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. NICE says the same for people with a history of breast cancer [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

If symptoms continue, NICE says low-dose vaginal estrogen can be considered, and that people taking aromatase inhibitors should decide this with a breast cancer specialist [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The Menopause Society says there isn't enough data yet to confirm vaginal estrogen is safe after breast cancer, and that it should be prescribed together with your oncologist [@nams-2020-gsm; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## When to see a doctor

- **Liver warning signs on fezolinetant**: The label warns of liver injury, so contact your prescriber promptly if you have signs such as yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, or pain in the upper right belly [@veozah-prescribing-information].
- **Any vaginal bleeding after menopause**: Get it checked by a doctor, even if it's light or happens once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
- **Symptoms that disrupt your sleep, work, or relationships**: There are proven options to try, so ask your oncology or menopause team rather than waiting it out [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
