Menopause can be natural, early (before 45), premature (before 40, also called premature ovarian insufficiency or POI), or induced, when surgery or cancer treatment stops the ovaries working [@nhs-early-menopause; @menopause-society-glossary-2026]. If yours started early or suddenly, the advice on hormones and long-term health is different.

Most menopause articles assume a gradual change in your late 40s or early 50s. In a 2025 survey of 627 people looking for menopause information online, a frequent complaint was how little trustworthy guidance exists for medical, early or surgical menopause [@jmir-online-menopause-information-2025]. This section fills that gap.

## What are the types of menopause?

There are four main types, grouped by age and cause:

- **Natural menopause**: periods stop on their own, at 52 on average in the US, after a few years of perimenopause [@menopause-society-glossary-2026].
- **Early menopause**: periods stop before 45 [@nhs-early-menopause]. In a US national survey, 11.8% of women with natural menopause reported it at ages 40 to 45 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021].
- **Premature menopause or POI**: periods stop, or become very infrequent, before 40 [@nhs-early-menopause; @nice-ng23]. A 2019 review of 31 studies worldwide put POI at about 3.7% of women and early menopause at about 12% [@golezar-poi-early-menopause-prevalence-2019].
- **Induced menopause**: both ovaries are removed (surgical menopause) or permanently damaged by cancer treatment [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. There's no perimenopause run-up; that only happens with natural menopause [@menopause-society-glossary-2026].

## Does a hysterectomy cause menopause?

A hysterectomy only causes menopause if both ovaries are removed as well. If you keep your ovaries, you typically won't go through menopause after the operation, though your periods stop [@cleveland-clinic-menopause-after-hysterectomy-2025]. Whether the surgery brings natural menopause forward is unsettled: one small study found an earlier average age at menopause after hysterectomy, and another found no real difference [@ahn-hysterectomy-ovarian-function-2002; @atay-hysterectomy-ovarian-preservation-2007].

When both ovaries are removed, symptoms can start very soon after surgery. They're the same symptoms as natural menopause but may be more severe [@cleveland-clinic-menopause-after-hysterectomy-2025].

## How likely is menopause after chemotherapy?

It depends on the drugs and your age. In a review of 45 studies of young cancer survivors, the share whose periods stopped after chemotherapy ranged from 0% to 83% depending on the treatment; certain drugs (alkylating agents) and being older at treatment raised the chance most [@overbeek-chemotherapy-ovarian-function-2017]. All the studies had limitations, so your oncologist is the best guide to your own odds [@overbeek-chemotherapy-ovarian-function-2017].

## Why does early or sudden menopause need different care?

Starting menopause early means more years with low estrogen, which raises the chance of bone and heart problems, so replacing hormones matters if menopause starts before 45 [@nhs-early-menopause]. The main options are HRT or the combined contraceptive pill, and HRT is usually advised until at least age 51 [@nhs-early-menopause; @nhs-when-to-take-hrt]. NICE also recommends offering psychological support to people who are distressed by an early menopause diagnosis [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## What if hormone therapy isn't an option?

After breast cancer, and especially hormone receptor-positive cancer, doctors generally don't recommend menopausal hormone therapy [@acs-mht-after-breast-cancer-2025]. The Menopause Society recommends several non-hormonal treatments for hot flashes with the strongest level of evidence: CBT, clinical hypnosis, certain antidepressants (SSRIs and SNRIs), gabapentin and fezolinetant [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Some antidepressants, particularly paroxetine and fluoxetine, can weaken tamoxifen, so the choice matters [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. For a history of endometriosis, a 2025 European guide advises against estrogen-only HRT even after a hysterectomy [@emas-endometriosis-menopause-guide-2025].
