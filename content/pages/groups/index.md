Standard menopause articles usually assume a gradual transition in your late 40s or early 50s. In a 2025 survey of 627 people searching online for menopause information, one of the most common frustrations was the lack of trustworthy guidance for medical, early, or surgical menopause [@jmir-online-menopause-information-2025].

This hub gathers our evidence-based guides for situations where standard rules — and standard treatment trade-offs — change.

## Specialist situation guides

- **[Early menopause (ages 40-45)](/groups/early-menopause/)**: How common it is, what's linked to earlier timing, and why bone and heart health need extra attention.
- **[Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI) — under age 40](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/)**: How POI is diagnosed, why hormone replacement before the average age of natural menopause plays a different role than standard midlife HRT [@nice-ng23], and what to ask a specialist.
- **[Surgical menopause (after ovary removal)](/groups/surgical-menopause/)**: What happens when bilateral oophorectomy causes an immediate drop in ovarian hormones rather than a gradual transition, and how clinicians manage sudden vasomotor symptoms and bone health.
- **[Menopause after breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/)**: Why systemic hormone therapy is generally contraindicated after hormone-sensitive breast cancer, and which Level I [non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] and local vaginal care options to discuss with your oncology and menopause team.
