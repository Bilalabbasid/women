If hot flashes, disrupted sleep, or brain fog are affecting how you function at work, you're dealing with something real and common — and something most women never actually raise with their employer.

## What the data actually shows

A 2026 US survey of **1,011 women aged 40-65** who were employed (or had recently stopped working) and experiencing vasomotor symptoms — averaging **7.5 hot flashes or night sweats per day** — measured the real workplace impact [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]:

- **36%** reported some degree of activity impairment at work [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]
- **32%** reported presenteeism (being at work but functioning below capacity) [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]
- **6%** reported absenteeism (missing work entirely) [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]
- All three increased alongside symptom severity [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]

Despite this, **81.3% had never received any treatment** for their symptoms, and **almost 80% had never discussed their symptoms with their employer** at all. Only **18.1%** felt their employer was actually supportive around this [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026].

A note on this source: the survey was funded by Astellas Pharma, which makes a prescription treatment for vasomotor symptoms, and several of the study's authors are Astellas employees or paid consultants. We're citing it because the workplace-impact data itself (how women experience and cope with symptoms at work) doesn't depend on which treatment, if any, is "the answer" — but it's worth knowing who funded it.

## What actually helps, according to the women surveyed

The most common coping strategies reported were:

- **Controlling the temperature** around them (71.0%) [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]
- **Wearing layered, breathable clothing** (46.0%) [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]

When asked what workplace accommodations would help most, the top answers were:

- **Changes to benefit packages** (27.9%) — such as insurance coverage for menopause-related care [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]
- **More general workplace awareness** (25.7%) — so symptoms aren't misread or stigmatized [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]
- **More flexible working arrangements** (23.2%) — such as remote options on difficult days or flexible start times after a disrupted night [@kapoor-vms-workplace-survey-2026]

## Practical steps if you're considering raising this

- **Treat your symptoms as a health matter first.** Given how few women in this survey had received treatment, that's often the highest-leverage step — see our [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [does it work? directory](/does-it-work/) for evidence-based options to discuss with a clinician.
- **Decide what you actually need, not just what you're comfortable with.** Common practical requests include desk fans or temperature control, access to a private space, flexibility around early mornings after a disrupted night, or breathable uniform/dress-code adjustments.
- **Check what your employer or HR already offers.** Some employers have specific menopause policies or benefits; many don't, and workplace awareness about this is genuinely still low, per the survey above.
- **Workplace rights and protections vary** by location and employer, and this is a legal question we can't answer generically — if you believe you're being treated unfairly because of menopause-related symptoms, a conversation with HR or an employment law resource specific to your location is the right next step, not something we can advise on here.
