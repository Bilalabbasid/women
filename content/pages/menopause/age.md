The average age of menopause in the US is about 51 to 52, and most people reach it between 45 and 55 [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq; @nia-what-is-menopause; @medlineplus-menopause-2026]. You've reached menopause once you've gone 12 months in a row without a period [@nia-what-is-menopause]. Before 45 counts as early menopause and before 40 as premature, and both are worth checking with a doctor [@nhs-early-menopause; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

## What is the average age of menopause?

The average age of menopause in the US is 51 according to the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), and 52 according to the National Institute on Aging [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq; @nia-what-is-menopause]. A large national survey that counted only natural menopause found a slightly lower average, 49.9 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021].

| Source | Average age of menopause | Usual range |
|---|---|---|
| ACOG (US) | 51 (age of the last period) [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] | Perimenopause often runs from about 45 to 55 [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] |
| National Institute on Aging (US) | 52 [@nia-what-is-menopause] | Most begin the transition between 45 and 55 [@nia-what-is-menopause] |
| MedlinePlus (US) | Not stated [@medlineplus-menopause-2026] | Most common age is 45 to 55 [@medlineplus-menopause-2026] |
| NHS (UK) | Not stated [@nhs-early-menopause] | Usually between 45 and 55 [@nhs-early-menopause] |
| US national survey, 2015-2018 | 49.9 for natural menopause [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021] | Not reported [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021] |

The numbers differ a little because the sources measure age in different ways. The survey asked women aged 40 to 74 when their periods had stopped naturally, so it left out menopause caused by surgery or medical treatment [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021].

To see where you are against these averages, and your 12-month date if your periods have stopped, try the free [menopause age calculator](/tools/menopause-age-calculator/).

## What age does menopause start?

Menopause itself is one point in time: 12 months after your last period, which for most people falls between 45 and 55 [@nia-what-is-menopause; @medlineplus-menopause-2026]. The run-up to it, perimenopause, usually starts in your 40s and can last several years [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. The FDA notes that some people reach menopause in their early 40s and others as late as their 60s [@fda-home-menopause-test].

You only know you've reached menopause looking back, once a full year has passed without a period or spotting [@nia-what-is-menopause]. For when the first changes usually appear, see [what age perimenopause starts](/perimenopause/age/).

## Has the average age of menopause changed?

Yes, it has crept later. A US national survey covering 60 years found the average age of natural menopause rose from 48.4 in 1959-1962 to 49.9 in 2015-2018 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. Over the same time, the average reproductive life span (from first period to menopause) grew from 35.0 to 37.1 years [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021].

## What's linked to earlier or later menopause?

After adjusting for other factors, the national survey found [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]:

- **Linked to earlier menopause**: Black or Hispanic race/ethnicity, poverty, current or former smoking, and hormone therapy use.
- **Linked to later menopause**: more years of education, and use of the birth control pill.

The National Institute on Aging also notes that smoking is linked to earlier menopause, and that Black women are more likely than White women to reach menopause early [@nia-what-is-menopause]. UK guidance reminds clinicians that people from some ethnic minority backgrounds and people with some lifelong conditions may reach menopause younger [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. These are patterns across large groups, not predictions for any one person.

## What counts as early or premature menopause?

Menopause before 45 is called early menopause, and menopause before 40 is called premature menopause [@nhs-early-menopause; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Premature menopause is also known as primary ovarian insufficiency (POI), and both can happen on their own or after medical treatment [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

| Term | Age | How common |
|---|---|---|
| Early menopause | Before 45, or 40 to 44 [@nhs-early-menopause; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026] | 11.8% of US women with natural menopause had it at 40 to 45 (2015-2018) [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]; about 12.2% worldwide [@golezar-poi-early-menopause-prevalence-2019] |
| Premature menopause (POI) | Before 40 [@nhs-early-menopause; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026] | About 3.7% worldwide [@golezar-poi-early-menopause-prevalence-2019] |

The worldwide figures come from a 2019 review that pooled 31 studies [@golezar-poi-early-menopause-prevalence-2019]. More detail is in our guides to [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/) and [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).

## Early menopause symptoms

The main sign of early or premature menopause is periods that become irregular or stop completely before age 45 [@nhs-early-menopause]. The other symptoms are the same as menopause at any age [@nhs-early-menopause]:

- **Hot flashes and night sweats**: called hot flushes in the UK.
- **Vaginal dryness**: which can make sex uncomfortable.
- **Sleep trouble**: difficulty getting to sleep or staying asleep.
- **Mood changes**: low mood or anxiety.
- **Lower sex drive**: less interest in sex than usual.
- **Memory or focus problems**: trouble remembering things or concentrating.

Having these symptoms doesn't confirm early menopause by itself. A doctor will ask about your age, symptoms and family history to see whether the menopause transition is the likely cause [@nia-what-is-menopause].

## What causes early menopause?

Sometimes there's no clear cause [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Known reasons include:

- **Family history**: your chance is higher if early menopause runs in your family [@nhs-early-menopause; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].
- **Lifestyle and body factors**: smoking, being underweight, or having started your periods early [@nhs-early-menopause].
- **Medical treatment**: chemotherapy or radiotherapy, certain hormone medicines, or surgery to remove the ovaries [@nhs-early-menopause; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].
- **Some health conditions**: autoimmune conditions such as thyroid disease, and genetic conditions such as Turner syndrome [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

If treatment caused your menopause, see [surgical menopause](/groups/surgical-menopause/) or [menopause caused by chemotherapy](/groups/chemotherapy-induced-menopause/).

## How is early menopause diagnosed?

Early menopause is usually diagnosed from your symptoms and cycle history, with blood tests playing a bigger role the younger you are [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. UK guidance says a follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) blood test may be considered between 40 and 45 when you have symptoms and cycle changes [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

Under 40, the same guidance diagnoses POI from menopause symptoms, including no periods or infrequent periods, plus raised FSH on 2 blood tests taken 4 to 6 weeks apart, never from a single test [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [how menopause is diagnosed](/menopause/diagnosis/) for the full picture by age.

## Why early menopause matters for your health

Reaching menopause early raises your chance of osteoporosis (thinning bones) and heart and blood vessel disease [@nhs-early-menopause]. Years without estrogen also raise the risk of diabetes [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

- **Hormone treatment**: the main treatments are hormone replacement therapy (HRT) or the combined birth control pill, and replacing hormones matters when menopause starts before 45 [@nhs-early-menopause]. For POI, UK guidance offers either one unless there's a reason not to, usually continued until at least the typical age of natural menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **When hormones aren't an option**: some people who have had certain cancers, such as some breast cancers, may not be able to take hormone treatment [@nhs-early-menopause].
- **Fertility**: early or premature menopause can make it harder to get pregnant, though IVF with your own frozen eggs or donor eggs may be possible [@nhs-early-menopause].
- **Support**: counseling and support groups can help with the emotional side [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

## When to see a doctor

Tell your doctor if your periods stop or become irregular before age 45 [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. It's also worth booking a visit if you notice symptoms that might be menopause, at any age, and want to know your options [@nhs-early-menopause].

- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
