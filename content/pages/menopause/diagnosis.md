For most women aged 45 and over, confirming natural [menopause](/menopause/) does not require a blood test or ultrasound. Both the international **STRAW+10 criteria** [@straw-10-2012] and **UK NICE guideline NG23** [@nice-ng23-recommendations] confirm natural menopause retrospectively after **12 consecutive months without a menstrual period**, provided you are not using hormonal contraception and there is no other medical cause.

You can track that 12-month window privately on your device with our [12-month period-free counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) or explore how earlier cycle changes are classified in the [STRAW+10 perimenopause stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/).

## Why hormone blood tests are not routinely used at 45 and over

For otherwise healthy people aged 45 or over, NICE NG23 advises identifying menopause from a 12-month absence of periods — or from vasomotor symptoms in people who do not have a uterus — **without laboratory tests** [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. At 45 and over, NICE specifically advises clinicians **not** to use anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), inhibin A or B, oestradiol, antral follicle count, or ovarian volume to diagnose perimenopause or menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

For a closer look at when follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) testing *is* considered — such as between ages 40 and 45, or under age 40 when [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/) is suspected — read [Is there a blood test for perimenopause?](/perimenopause/blood-tests/) and check our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/).

## Questions to ask your clinician

:::questions
- Given my age and the date of my last period, can we confirm menopause from my history alone?
- Since I use a hormonal IUD (or hormonal contraception), how should we assess whether I have passed menopause and when is it safe to stop contraception?
- What symptoms or preventive health checks (such as bone density) should we review now that my periods have stopped?
:::

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See our guide on [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::
