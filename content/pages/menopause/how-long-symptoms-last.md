Menopause symptoms often last for years. In a large US study, frequent hot flashes and night sweats lasted a median of 7.4 years in total [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015], while vaginal dryness tends to stay unless it is treated [@nams-2020-gsm]. Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for both [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Reaching 12 months without a period is a calendar milestone for [menopause](/menopause/), not a switch that turns symptoms off. UK NICE guideline NG23 notes that menopause symptoms can range from mild to severe and can last for **short or long periods** [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Different symptoms also follow different timelines.

## 1. Hot flashes and night sweats (vasomotor symptoms)

In the STRAW+10 staging framework, the late menopausal transition — when periods skip for 60 days or more — lasts an average of **1 to 3 years** before the final menstrual period [@straw-10-2012], and [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) are most pronounced during late perimenopause and the first years of [postmenopause](/postmenopause/) [@straw-10-2012].

For most women, hot flashes and night sweats fade in time, but that can take years. The SWAN study, which followed more than 3,300 US women for up to 17 years, found that frequent hot flashes and night sweats lasted a median of 7.4 years in total, including a median of 4.5 more years after the final period itself [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. Duration varied: women whose frequent symptoms started earliest (premenopausal or early perimenopausal) had the longest course, with a median over 11.8 years, while those who didn't start until after menopause had the shortest, at a median of 3.4 years [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. When hot flashes disrupt sleep or work, both [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy] and [non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/) (plus practical comfort aids evaluated in [Do cooling products help hot flashes?](/does-it-work/cooling-products/)) can help while symptoms last.

## 2. Vaginal and urinary symptoms (genitourinary syndrome)

Unlike hot flashes, symptoms of the genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) — including [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) and [painful sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/) — are caused by low estrogen in the tissues of the vagina, vulva and bladder area, and typically do not go away on their own without ongoing care [@nams-2020-gsm].

Clinical guidelines from The Menopause Society and ACOG recommend regular non-hormonal vaginal moisturizers (used several times a week) and personal lubricants during sex as first-line care, and low-dose local [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) when moderate-to-severe symptoms persist [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].

## Track your own pattern over time

Because symptom duration varies so widely from person to person [@nice-ng23-recommendations], logging your episodes for a few weeks in our [private symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) gives you and your clinician a clear baseline.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if symptoms are affecting your sleep, work or daily life, since effective treatments exist [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See our guide on [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::
