Menopause is when your periods stop for good. It is confirmed once you have gone 12 months in a row without a period, if you are not using hormonal contraception [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations], and it most often happens between ages 45 and 55 [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. For hot flashes and night sweats, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

In everyday talk, "menopause" often means the whole midlife transition. Strictly, it is one point in time: the years before it are [perimenopause](/perimenopause/), and the years after it are [postmenopause](/postmenopause/).

## What is the medical definition of menopause?

Natural menopause is your final menstrual period, confirmed only looking back, once 12 months in a row have passed without a period while you are not using hormonal contraception [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. After it, you can no longer get pregnant [@medlineplus-menopause-2026].

It happens because the ovaries permanently make much less estrogen and progesterone [@straw-10-2012]. The US National Institute on Aging describes it as a normal part of aging, not a disease [@nia-what-is-menopause]. For how clinicians confirm it, see [how menopause is diagnosed](/menopause/diagnosis/).

## What are the three stages of menopause?

The three stages are perimenopause, menopause and postmenopause [@straw-10-2012]:

- **Perimenopause**: Starts when cycle length begins to change by 7 days or more, and ends 12 months after your final period [@straw-10-2012]. It usually begins in your 40s and can last several years [@medlineplus-menopause-2026].
- **Menopause**: The final period itself, a single point in time that is confirmed at the 12-month mark [@straw-10-2012].
- **Postmenopause**: Every year after that point [@straw-10-2012].

For a side-by-side comparison, read [perimenopause vs menopause](/perimenopause/vs-menopause/), or see which stage your period pattern resembles with the [stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/).

## What age does menopause happen?

Most people reach menopause between 45 and 55 [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. In the US, the average is about 50: a national survey found an average age of 49.9 years in 2015 to 2018 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. The same survey found 11.8% of women reached natural menopause between 40 and 45 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021].

Read more about the [average age of menopause](/menopause/age/), [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/), or [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/) if your periods stopped before 40.

## Can you get pregnant after menopause?

No. Once you have reached menopause, you can no longer get pregnant [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. Before that point, while you still have periods, even irregular ones, pregnancy is still possible, so read about [pregnancy in perimenopause](/perimenopause/pregnancy/) and [contraception in perimenopause](/perimenopause/contraception/).

## What are the symptoms of menopause?

Besides changes to your periods, UK NICE guidance lists these symptoms, which can range from mild to severe and last for short or long periods [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **Hot flashes and sweats**: [Hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/).
- **Vaginal and urinary symptoms**: For example [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/), sometimes with [painful sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/).
- **Mood changes**: For example low mood or [depression](/symptoms/depression/).
- **Joint and muscle pain**: See [joint and muscle pain](/symptoms/joint-pain/).
- **Sexual difficulties**: For example low sexual desire.

Lower estrogen after menopause can also reduce bone density [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]; see [bone health after menopause](/postmenopause/bone-health/). Our symptom guides also cover [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/), [anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/) and [brain fog](/symptoms/brain-fog/).

## How long do menopause symptoms last?

Hot flashes and night sweats often last for years. In a large US study, frequent hot flashes or night sweats lasted a median of 7.4 years in total [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. Vaginal symptoms behave differently from hot flashes, and [how long menopause symptoms last](/menopause/how-long-symptoms-last/) explains both.

## Does a hysterectomy cause menopause?

Not on its own. Removing only the uterus stops your periods, but it does not cause menopause unless both ovaries are also removed [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. Surgery to remove the ovaries, and chemotherapy or hormone treatment for breast cancer, can bring on menopause sooner [@medlineplus-menopause-2026].

See [surgical menopause](/groups/surgical-menopause/), [hysterectomy with ovaries kept](/groups/hysterectomy-ovaries-retained/) and [chemotherapy-induced menopause](/groups/chemotherapy-induced-menopause/).

## What helps menopause symptoms?

You may not need any treatment if symptoms are mild [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. When they bother you, [hormone therapy (HRT)](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) is the most effective treatment for hot flashes, night sweats and vaginal symptoms, and it prevents bone loss. The Menopause Society says its benefits outweigh its risks for healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

If you can't or prefer not to use hormones, talking therapy (CBT), clinical hypnosis and several prescription medicines also reduce hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Our guides to [non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/) and [exercise](/does-it-work/exercise/) grade the evidence for each option.
