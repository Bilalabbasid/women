Yes, perimenopause can start at 44, and that's a very common time for it: most people notice the first changes, such as irregular periods, sometime in their 40s [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. It's defined by changes in your cycle, not a birthday, so some people start in their 30s and others in their 50s [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

## Can perimenopause start at 44?

Yes. Starting perimenopause at 44 is normal and sits right in the usual window. Mayo Clinic says signs often appear sometime in the 40s, and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) says perimenopause usually begins in the mid-40s and may run from about 45 to 55, though timing varies from person to person [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026; @acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq].

The first sign is usually a change in your cycle. In the clinical STRAW+10 staging system, perimenopause begins when your cycle lengths start to differ by 7 days or more from one cycle to the next, and keep doing so [@straw-10-2012]. Hot flashes, night sweats or sleep changes may come along with it.

From 45, UK guidance says clinicians can identify perimenopause from new hot flashes or night sweats plus any change in your cycle, without blood tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Between 40 and 45, the same guidance says a follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) blood test may be considered to confirm menopause in people with symptoms and cycle changes [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [is there a blood test for perimenopause?](/perimenopause/blood-tests/) for what testing can and can't show.

## What is the average age perimenopause starts?

There's no single agreed average age for the start of perimenopause, because it begins gradually and is defined by cycle changes. The sources below agree it usually starts in the 40s, with menopause itself (12 months without a period) typically arriving around 50.

| Source | When perimenopause usually starts | Average age at menopause |
|---|---|---|
| Mayo Clinic | Sometime in the 40s; some notice changes in their 30s or as late as their 50s [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026] | Not stated [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026] |
| ACOG | Usually the mid-40s, often running from about 45 to 55 [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] | 51 in the US [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] |
| National Institute on Aging | Most begin the transition between 45 and 55 [@nia-what-is-menopause] | 52 in the US [@nia-what-is-menopause] |
| US national survey, 2015-2018 | Not measured [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021] | 49.9 for natural menopause, up from 48.4 in 1959-1962 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021] |

The survey counted only natural menopause, as reported by the women themselves [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. The sources measure age in different ways, so their numbers differ slightly, but they all put perimenopause mostly in the 40s.

## How long does perimenopause last?

Perimenopause usually lasts several years, but the length varies a lot. The late stage, when gaps between periods reach 60 days or more, lasts 1 to 3 years on average [@straw-10-2012]. The National Institute on Aging says menopause-related symptoms can last between 2 and 8 years [@nia-what-is-menopause]. For more detail, see [how long perimenopause lasts](/perimenopause/how-long/).

## What counts as early or premature menopause?

Menopause between ages 40 and 44 is called early menopause, and menopause before 40 is called premature menopause, also known as primary ovarian insufficiency (POI) [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Both can happen on their own or after medical treatment, such as surgery to remove both ovaries [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

Early menopause isn't rare. In a US national survey, 11.8% of women with natural menopause in 2015-2018 said it happened between 40 and 45, the study's own definition of early menopause [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. For them, perimenopause would have started in their late 30s or early 40s.

Under 40, diagnosis works differently. UK guidance says POI is diagnosed from menopause symptoms, including no periods or infrequent periods, plus raised follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) on 2 blood tests taken 4 to 6 weeks apart, and never from a single blood test [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See our guides to [perimenopause in your 30s](/perimenopause/in-your-30s/) and [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).

## What can make perimenopause start earlier?

Sometimes there's no clear reason [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Known factors linked with an earlier start include:

- **Smoking**: menopause happens 1 to 2 years earlier in people who smoke [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- **Family history**: a family history of early menopause makes early menopause more likely [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].
- **Cancer treatment**: chemotherapy and pelvic radiation have been linked to early menopause [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- **Surgery**: removing both ovaries brings on menopause, and a hysterectomy that keeps the ovaries can sometimes lead to earlier menopause [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026; @mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- **Some health conditions**: such as thyroid disease, rheumatoid arthritis or other autoimmune conditions, and genetic conditions such as Turner syndrome [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026; @owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

## What should I do if perimenopause starts early?

If your periods stop or become irregular before 45, tell your clinician; blood tests are most useful for checking this before age 40 [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Getting it checked matters because years without estrogen raise the risk of bone loss, heart disease and diabetes [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

- **Ask about treatment**: hormone therapy may help relieve symptoms and protect your bones, and you and your clinician can decide together whether it's right for you [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. For POI, UK guidance offers hormone therapy or a combined hormonal contraceptive unless there's a reason not to, usually continued until at least the typical age of natural menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Ask about birth control**: pregnancy is still possible while you're having periods [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. See [contraception in perimenopause](/perimenopause/contraception/).
- **Get support**: learning that menopause is coming early can bring grief, especially about fertility, and counseling and support groups can help [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Our [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/) guide has more.
