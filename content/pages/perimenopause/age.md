There's no single age that marks the start of [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) — it's defined by changes in your menstrual cycle, not a birthday. In the clinical STRAW+10 framework, the transition begins when your cycle lengths start varying by 7 days or more from one cycle to the next, whatever age that happens [@straw-10-2012]. See our [stages of perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/) guide for how that framework works.

That said, population data gives a useful sense of the typical range. A US national survey tracking women from 1959-1962 through 2015-2018 found the average age at natural menopause (the point when perimenopause ends) rose from 48.4 to 49.9 years over that 60-year period — a small but statistically significant increase, alongside a longer average reproductive life span (35.0 to 37.1 years) [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. Since perimenopause typically lasts several years before that point, most women's first symptoms appear somewhere in their 40s.

## Can perimenopause start earlier than that?

Yes, and it's not as rare as it might feel. The same study found that 11.8% of US women with natural menopause in the most recent survey period reported what the researchers defined as "early menopause" — natural menopause occurring between ages 40 and 45 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. For those women, the perimenopause years leading up to it would have started in their late 30s or early 40s.

If symptoms start well before 40, that's a different clinical picture — see our page on [perimenopause in your 30s](/perimenopause/in-your-30s/) and our [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/) guide, since NICE guidance uses a different, lab-test-based diagnostic approach under age 40 [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See our companion page on the [average age of menopause itself](/menopause/age/) for how this age data extends past the perimenopause years.
