There's no single menopause test, blood or urine, that can confirm perimenopause on its own, because FSH and other hormones rise and fall from month to month [@straw-10-2012; @fda-home-menopause-test]. At 45 and over, UK guidance says clinicians can usually identify perimenopause and menopause from your symptoms and periods, without a blood test [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Is there a menopause test?

Not one that settles it by itself: clinicians mainly use your age, symptoms and period pattern, and add blood tests only in certain situations [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Even when an FSH test is used, the FDA notes that doctors wouldn't rely on it alone; they combine it with your medical history, a physical exam and other lab tests [@fda-home-menopause-test].

Menopause itself is confirmed looking back, after 12 months in a row without a period when you're not using hormonal contraception [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. In the STRAW+10 staging system, period changes are the main way to stage the transition, and hormone results only support them, partly because lab tests aren't standardized [@straw-10-2012]. Read more on [how menopause is confirmed](/menopause/diagnosis/).

## Do I need a menopause blood test? What NICE NG23 advises

Usually not if you're 45 or over and otherwise healthy: UK guideline NG23 says perimenopause can be identified when hot flashes or sweats (hot flushes in the UK) have recently started along with any change in your periods, without lab tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. A blood test is mainly considered at younger ages:

| Your situation | What NICE NG23 advises |
|---|---|
| 45 or over, otherwise healthy, with menopause symptoms | Identify perimenopause and menopause from symptoms and periods, without lab tests (recommendation 1.3.1) [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| 45 or over | Don't use AMH, inhibin A or B, estradiol (oestradiol), antral follicle count or ovarian volume to identify perimenopause or menopause (1.3.4) [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| 40 to 45, with symptoms including a change in periods | Consider an FSH blood test to confirm menopause (1.3.6) [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| Under 40, menopause suspected | Diagnose premature ovarian insufficiency from symptoms plus raised FSH on 2 blood samples 4 to 6 weeks apart, never a single test (1.7.2, 1.7.3) [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| Using combined estrogen and progestogen contraception or high-dose progestogen | Don't use an FSH blood test to identify menopause (1.3.5) [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |

In the US, the Office on Women's Health advises telling your provider if your periods stop or become irregular before 45, and says blood tests are most useful before 40 [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. In the UK, the NHS says a GP may sometimes do blood tests if you're under 45 and getting menopause symptoms [@nhs-early-menopause].

Hormonal contraception such as the progestogen-only pill, hormonal IUD (coil), implant or injection can make periods irregular or stop them, which makes it harder to tell when perimenopause or menopause has started [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].

### Under 40: testing for premature ovarian insufficiency

Under 40, blood tests are part of the diagnosis. NICE advises diagnosing [premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/) from symptoms, including no or infrequent periods, together with raised FSH on two blood samples taken 4 to 6 weeks apart, and not from a single blood test [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## FSH levels and menopause: a chart of typical ranges

FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) is made by the pituitary gland, and levels rise when the ovaries stop working [@fda-home-menopause-test]. The ranges below are the ones MedlinePlus lists, plus what the STRAW+10 criteria say about the transition years. Labs use slightly different ranges, so read your result against the range printed on your own report [@medlineplus-fsh-blood-test].

| Stage | Typical FSH level |
|---|---|
| Still having periods | 4.7 to 21.5 mIU/mL [@medlineplus-fsh-blood-test] |
| Early menopausal transition (perimenopause begins) | Raised but variable early in the cycle, with no fixed cutoff [@straw-10-2012] |
| Late menopausal transition | Sometimes in the menopausal range, sometimes in the range of earlier years; above 25 IU/L on a random blood draw is typical of this stage [@straw-10-2012] |
| After menopause | 25.8 to 134.8 mIU/mL [@medlineplus-fsh-blood-test] |

MedlinePlus gives the postmenopause range as the same numbers in IU/L [@medlineplus-fsh-blood-test]. A high FSH result isn't only a sign of menopause: it can also happen with hormone therapy, certain pituitary tumors or Turner syndrome [@medlineplus-fsh-blood-test].

## What are normal FSH levels in perimenopause?

There isn't a reliable "perimenopause range," because FSH swings during the transition: one result can sit in the normal range and the next in the menopausal range [@straw-10-2012]. The FDA also notes that as you get older, FSH may rise and fall during your cycle [@fda-home-menopause-test].

In the late transition, STRAW+10 describes FSH that is sometimes in the menopausal range and sometimes back in the reproductive range, especially when estradiol is high [@straw-10-2012]. So a normal result doesn't rule perimenopause out, and a single high result doesn't prove it. If you still have periods, you may be asked to have the test on certain days of your cycle [@medlineplus-fsh-blood-test].

## Do home menopause tests work?

Home menopause tests can show whether the FSH in your urine is raised, but the FDA says they don't detect menopause or perimenopause [@fda-home-menopause-test]. Here's what the FDA says about them:

- **What they measure**: FSH in urine, as a yes-or-no answer about raised FSH rather than a level. They pick up FSH accurately about 9 out of 10 times [@fda-home-menopause-test].
- **What can change the result**: whether you used your first morning urine, drinking a lot of water before the test, and using or recently stopping pill or patch contraception, HRT or estrogen supplements [@fda-home-menopause-test].
- **A positive result**: you may be in a stage of menopause, so see a doctor. Don't stop contraception because of it, since you could still get pregnant [@fda-home-menopause-test].
- **A negative result**: it doesn't mean you haven't reached menopause, especially if you have symptoms [@fda-home-menopause-test].
- **Fertility**: these tests can't reliably tell you whether you can become pregnant [@fda-home-menopause-test].

NICE's recommendations don't mention home kits, but they advise that at 45 and over, hormone tests aren't needed to identify perimenopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. A few months of notes on your periods and symptoms usually tell a clinician more.

## When to see a doctor

- **You think you're getting menopause symptoms** and want to know your options [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **You're under 45** and your periods have become irregular or stopped [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026; @nhs-early-menopause].
- **Your bleeding pattern has changed** and you're bleeding more, not less, than before, or you have palpitations (a fast or pounding heartbeat) [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **Any vaginal bleeding after 12 months without a period**, even a small amount or once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
