If you're still having periods — even irregular ones — [pregnancy remains possible during perimenopause](/perimenopause/pregnancy/), which means contraception is still a real, active decision for many women in this stage, not something to assume you've aged out of.

## No method is ruled out by age alone

A 2025 multidisciplinary expert consensus, developed by the Italian Society of Contraception through a systematic review and a formal voting process (95–99% agreement across all 7 final statements), set out to guide exactly this decision for women over 40 [@cagnacci-contraception-over-40-consensus-2025]. The consensus treats **cardiovascular safety as the primary factor** in choosing a method at this age [@cagnacci-contraception-over-40-consensus-2025] — which tracks with NICE guidance recommending that people with menopause-associated symptoms be given information specifically about contraception for women over 40 [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

Key findings from the consensus:

- **Progestogen-only methods** are considered **safe for cardiovascular risk** [@cagnacci-contraception-over-40-consensus-2025]
- **Combined hormonal contraceptives** using **natural estrogens or 20mcg ethinylestradiol with non-androgenic progestins** are considered **more advantageous for cardiovascular risk** compared with other combined formulations [@cagnacci-contraception-over-40-consensus-2025]

Your personal cardiovascular risk factors (blood pressure, smoking status, clotting history, migraine with aura) still matter enormously here — this is guidance to discuss with a clinician, not a one-size answer.

## A dual-purpose option: managing bleeding at the same time

If [irregular](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) or [heavy periods](/perimenopause/heavy-periods/) are also a problem, some contraceptive options double as bleeding management. The consensus identifies the **levonorgestrel IUD**, **estradiol valerate/dienogest** combined pills, and the **combined vaginal ring** as the **most effective options for menstrual bleeding management** among the choices studied [@cagnacci-contraception-over-40-consensus-2025].

## What else the consensus found

- **Sexual function**: hormone type and route of administration can influence sexual function, with **implants and vaginal rings** showing **positive effects** specifically [@cagnacci-contraception-over-40-consensus-2025]
- **Bone density**: **combined hormonal contraceptives** showed a **protective effect** on bone density, while **progestogen-only methods** had a **neutral** effect [@cagnacci-contraception-over-40-consensus-2025]

## When to actually stop

There's no single date that applies to everyone. The right time to stop contraception depends on your age, your specific method, and your personal health history — exactly the kind of individualized decision NICE points toward dedicated guidance for, rather than a symptom-based guess [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Our [12-month counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) tracks the one firm marker that does apply broadly: 12 consecutive months without a period, which confirms menopause itself.
