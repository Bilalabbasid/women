The earliest sign of perimenopause is usually a change in your periods: cycles may first get a little shorter, then the time between periods starts to vary by a week or more [@straw-10-2012]. Hot flashes, poor sleep and mood swings can start too [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. Writing down the day each period starts, for a few months, is the simplest way to spot the pattern.

These changes often start while periods still come every month, so it's easy to blame stress or a busy life. Knowing what the official definitions say can help you make sense of what you're noticing. For the bigger picture, see our [perimenopause guide](/perimenopause/). Other early changes can include problems with memory or concentration, and muscle aches [@nhs-menopause-symptoms; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Sign 1: Changes in your cycle

STRAW+10 is the staging system researchers and clinicians use to describe the years around menopause. It uses your bleeding pattern as the main marker [@straw-10-2012]:

- **Subtly shorter cycles (late reproductive stage)**: Before perimenopause officially begins, periods are still regular, but cycles often become a little shorter [@straw-10-2012].
- **Early transition (the start of perimenopause)**: The length of one cycle differs from the next by **7 days or more**, and this happens again within 10 cycles. For example, a 26-day cycle followed by a 34-day cycle, with similar swings recurring [@straw-10-2012].
- **Late transition**: You go **60 days or longer** without a period. This stage lasts 1 to 3 years on average, and hot flashes are likely during this stage [@straw-10-2012].
- **Changes in flow**: Periods may come more or less often, and bleeding may get heavier or lighter [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].

Perimenopause ends 12 months after your final period [@straw-10-2012]. If you've had a hysterectomy or endometrial ablation (surgery to remove the uterus, or a procedure to remove its lining), your periods can't be used to stage perimenopause [@straw-10-2012]. Hormonal treatments, such as those for heavy bleeding, can also make menopause harder to identify [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. For more on what each stage looks like, see [the stages of perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/) and [irregular periods](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/).

## Other early signs

Besides changes to your cycle, the NHS and the UK NICE menopause guideline list these symptoms [@nhs-menopause-symptoms; @nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **Hot flashes and night sweats**: Sudden heat in the face, neck and chest, or sweating at night [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. See [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/).
- **Sleep problems**: Trouble getting to sleep or staying asleep [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. See [waking at 3 a.m.](/symptoms/waking-at-3am/) and [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/).
- **Mood changes**: Mood swings, low mood or depression [@nhs-menopause-symptoms; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [irritability](/symptoms/irritability/).
- **Memory and concentration**: Problems with memory, concentration or "brain fog" [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. See [brain fog](/symptoms/brain-fog/).
- **Muscle aches and joint pain**: Listed by both the NHS and NICE [@nhs-menopause-symptoms; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [joint and muscle pain](/symptoms/joint-pain/).
- **Other changes**: A faster or more noticeable heartbeat (palpitations), headaches or migraines that are worse than usual, vaginal dryness, more urinary tract infections, and lower sex drive [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].

Symptoms can range from minor to severe, and can last a short or long time [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## How a doctor confirms it

The NICE guideline sets out how clinicians identify perimenopause at different ages:

- **Age 45 or over**: Perimenopause can be identified without blood tests in otherwise healthy people who have recently started having hot flashes or night sweats and any change in their cycle [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. NICE advises against using hormone tests, such as AMH or estradiol, to identify perimenopause at this age [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Age 40 to 45**: A blood test for FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) may be considered to confirm menopause in people with menopause symptoms, including a change in their cycle [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Under 40**: Menopause symptoms, including no periods or infrequent periods, can be a sign of premature ovarian insufficiency (POI). NICE says POI is diagnosed from symptoms plus raised FSH levels on 2 blood samples taken 4 to 6 weeks apart, not from a single test [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).

Read more in [blood tests for perimenopause](/perimenopause/blood-tests/).

## What else could cause these symptoms?

Some common, treatable conditions can feel like early perimenopause. A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- **Underactive thyroid**: Can cause tiredness, low mood, joint or muscle pain, weight gain, and heavier, irregular periods [@medlineplus-hypothyroidism].
- **Overactive thyroid**: Can cause anxiety, nervousness, sleep problems, a racing heartbeat, sweating, feeling too hot, and irregular periods [@medlineplus-hyperthyroidism].
- **Low iron from heavy periods**: Heavy, long or frequent periods can leave your body short of iron (iron deficiency anemia), which can make you feel weak or tired and make it hard to concentrate [@medlineplus-iron-deficiency-anemia].
- **Other causes of bleeding changes**: Fibroids, polyps, thyroid problems, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), bleeding disorders, some medicines such as blood thinners, and pregnancy can all change your bleeding pattern [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]. You can still get pregnant during perimenopause: see [pregnancy in perimenopause](/perimenopause/pregnancy/).

## When to see a doctor

Make an appointment if:

- you think your symptoms could be due to perimenopause and want to know your options [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]
- you're under 40 and your periods have become infrequent or stopped, or you have other menopause symptoms [@nice-ng23-recommendations]
- your bleeding pattern has changed and you're bleeding more, not less, than before [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]
- you have signs of heavy bleeding: changing a pad or tampon after less than 2 hours, needing to double up on pads, periods lasting more than 7 days, or clots the size of a quarter or larger [@cdc-heavy-menstrual-bleeding]
- you bleed between periods or after sex, or your periods have been heavier or longer than usual for 3 or more cycles [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]
- you notice palpitations (a fast or pounding heartbeat) [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]

:::urgent
**Contact a doctor promptly** if:

- you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for 2 to 3 hours in a row
- bleeding is heavy enough to make you feel weak or lightheaded
- you're bleeding and you are or could be pregnant
- you have severe pain, or a fever with lower belly pain [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]

**Any bleeding after 12 months without a period** should be checked, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-menopause-symptoms; @nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::
