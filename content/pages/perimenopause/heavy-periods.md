Heavier, longer, or less predictable flow is a common complaint during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/), as [irregular cycles](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) and less predictable ovulation change how your periods behave [@straw-10-2012]. Heavier bleeding on its own isn't automatically a red flag — but there's a clear line between "heavier than before" and bleeding that needs medical attention.

## When to get it checked

NHS guidance on heavy periods recommends seeing a GP if [@nhs-heavy-periods]:

- Heavy periods **affect your daily life** or have **lasted some time**
- You have **severe pain** during your periods
- You **bleed between periods or after sex**
- Heavy periods come with **other symptoms**, such as pain when peeing, having a bowel movement, or having sex

:::urgent
Seek urgent medical care if you are **soaking through pads or tampons every hour or two for several hours**, **passing large clots repeatedly**, or feeling **dizzy, lightheaded, or faint** from blood loss.
:::

## What can help while you get assessed

Once more serious causes have been ruled out, options for managing heavy perimenopausal bleeding — from hormonal approaches to procedures — are a conversation for your clinician, since the right choice depends on your bleeding pattern, whether you still want contraception, and your personal medical history. Our [treatment conversation builder](/tools/treatment-questions/) can help you organize what to ask.

## Track it before your appointment

Logging the number of heavy days, how often you're changing products, and any pain or clotting in our [private symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/) gives your clinician something concrete to work from, rather than a general impression of "heavier than before."
