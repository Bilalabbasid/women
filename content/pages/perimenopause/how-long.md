Perimenopause lasts about four years on average, but it can last anywhere from a few months to 10 years [@owh-menopause-basics-2026; @cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]. It ends 12 months after your final period [@straw-10-2012]. Because the end only shows in hindsight, tracking your periods tells you most about where you are.

## How long does perimenopause last on average?

Perimenopause lasts about four years on average, according to the US Office on Women's Health, but it can last up to 10 years before menopause [@owh-menopause-basics-2026]. Cleveland Clinic gives the same average and notes that some people are in it for only a few months, while others take several years [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]. A large Massachusetts study of about 2,570 women also found it lasted nearly 4 years [@mckinlay-mwhs-menopause-transition-1992].

Why the range is so wide: [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) starts with the first lasting changes in your cycle and only ends once a full year has passed without a period [@straw-10-2012]. Some studies stop counting at the last period instead of a year later, so their figures run a little shorter [@mckinlay-mwhs-menopause-transition-1992].

## How long does each stage of perimenopause last?

Clinicians split perimenopause into an early and a late stage, based on your periods [@straw-10-2012]. Here's how long each part tends to last:

| Stage | What you notice | How long it lasts |
|---|---|---|
| Early perimenopause | Cycle lengths keep differing by 7 days or more [@straw-10-2012] | Varies; perimenopause as a whole averages about 4 years [@owh-menopause-basics-2026] |
| Late perimenopause | 60 days or more between periods [@straw-10-2012] | 1 to 3 years on average [@straw-10-2012] |
| The final year | No periods at all [@straw-10-2012] | 12 months, ending at menopause [@straw-10-2012] |
| Early postmenopause (after perimenopause) | Hormone levels settle [@straw-10-2012] | About 5 to 8 years [@straw-10-2012] |

See the [stages of perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/) for the full staging picture.

### How long does late perimenopause last?

Late perimenopause lasts 1 to 3 years on average [@straw-10-2012]. It starts when you first go 60 days or more without a period, and hot flashes are likely during this stage [@straw-10-2012]. It ends with your final period, which you can only confirm a year later [@straw-10-2012].

## When does perimenopause start?

Perimenopause usually starts in your mid-40s, but it can begin as early as your mid-30s or as late as your mid-50s [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]. In the Massachusetts study, the median age it began was 47.5 [@mckinlay-mwhs-menopause-transition-1992]. Most people begin the transition between ages 45 and 55 [@nia-what-is-menopause]. See [perimenopause age](/perimenopause/age/) for more.

## What can make perimenopause longer or shorter?

No one can predict the length for one person, but studies point to a few patterns:

- **Smoking**: Smokers tended to have an earlier and a shorter perimenopause, and to reach menopause earlier [@mckinlay-mwhs-menopause-transition-1992; @mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- **Family history and medical treatment**: A family history of early menopause, chemotherapy or pelvic radiation, and hysterectomy are linked with earlier onset [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- **Some health conditions**: Thyroid disease, rheumatoid arthritis and other autoimmune diseases are also linked with earlier onset [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

In the Massachusetts study, women with longer transitions saw doctors most often [@mckinlay-mwhs-menopause-transition-1992].

## How do you know perimenopause is ending?

You can't know for sure until afterward. Going 60 days or more between periods means you've likely reached the late stage, which lasts 1 to 3 years on average [@straw-10-2012]. Perimenopause is over, and you've reached menopause, once you've gone 12 months without a period [@straw-10-2012].

Blood tests usually can't answer this. UK NICE guidance says not to use hormone tests such as AMH or estradiol (oestradiol in the UK) to diagnose perimenopause or menopause in people 45 or over [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. If you use hormonal birth control or have had a hysterectomy, your periods can't be used to stage you; a doctor can help instead [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. Our [12-month counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) tracks the time since your last period, and the [stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/) estimates your stage from your cycle pattern.

## How long do perimenopause symptoms last?

Symptoms often outlast perimenopause itself. The NHS says menopause and perimenopause symptoms usually last 7 to 9 years, sometimes longer, and that they can change, with hot flashes (hot flushes in the UK) sometimes giving way to low mood or anxiety [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. In the US SWAN study, frequent hot flashes and night sweats lasted a median of 7.4 years, including 4.5 years after the final period [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015].

For other symptoms, such as anxiety, nausea or breast pain, studies haven't measured how long they last in perimenopause. In the Massachusetts study, symptom reports rose during perimenopause and fell again after menopause [@mckinlay-mwhs-menopause-transition-1992]. See [how long menopause symptoms last](/menopause/how-long-symptoms-last/) for a symptom-by-symptom timeline.

## When to see a doctor

See a healthcare professional (your GP in the UK) if any symptoms bother you [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. Also get checked if your periods stop or become irregular before age 45 [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026], or if bleeding is very heavy, lasts longer than 7 days, happens between periods, or your periods are usually less than 21 days apart [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** needs a doctor's check, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::
