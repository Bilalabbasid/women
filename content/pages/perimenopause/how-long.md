There's no fixed length for [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) — it varies a lot between individuals — but clinical staging gives a useful general picture. In the STRAW+10 framework, the **late menopausal transition** (when you go 60 days or more between periods) lasts **1 to 3 years on average**, and perimenopause as a whole is only confirmed to have ended **12 months after your final period** [@straw-10-2012]. See our [stages of perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/) guide for the full staging picture, including the earlier stage that often comes first.

That means the total time from your first noticeable cycle changes to reaching menopause can easily add up to several years, not months.

## Why it can feel longer than the "official" stages suggest

The staging framework describes cycle changes, but [hot flashes and night sweats](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) don't always follow the same timeline — and they don't necessarily stop the day you reach menopause. The SWAN study, a long-running US study of over 3,300 women followed for up to 17 years, found that frequent vasomotor symptoms lasted a median of 7.4 years in total, with a median of 4.5 more years after the final period itself [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. Timing mattered: women who started having frequent symptoms while still premenopausal or in early perimenopause had the longest duration (median over 11.8 years), while those who didn't start until after menopause had the shortest (median 3.4 years) [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. See our dedicated page on [how long menopause symptoms last](/menopause/how-long-symptoms-last/) for more on this distinction between the cycle-based stages and the symptom timeline.

## How to track where you are

Because the end of perimenopause is only confirmed retrospectively, tracking helps more than guessing:

- Our [12-month counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) tracks the time since your last period toward the 12-month menopause mark.
- Our [stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/) estimates which stage your current cycle pattern suggests, based on STRAW+10 criteria.

## When to get checked

:::urgent
If you reach 12 months without a period and then experience any vaginal bleeding or spotting afterward, that always needs a doctor's check — see [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::
