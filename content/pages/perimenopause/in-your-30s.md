New or changing symptoms in your 30s — irregular [periods](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/), [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/), or [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) — can understandably make you wonder about perimenopause. It's uncommon at this age, but it isn't impossible, and the right next step is testing, not assuming either way.

## Why this age range gets tested, not guessed

NICE guideline NG23 uses different rules depending on age [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **Age 45 and over:** Perimenopause can usually be identified from symptoms and cycle changes alone, without lab tests.
- **Age 40 to 45:** A clinician may use an FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) blood test to help confirm what's happening.
- **Under 40:** Symptoms alongside infrequent or absent periods and FSH testing can diagnose **premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)** — but NICE is explicit that POI should never be diagnosed from a single blood test; it requires two elevated FSH readings taken 4 to 6 weeks apart [@nice-ng23].

That last point matters most if you're in your 30s: a one-off blood test, whatever the result, isn't enough on its own to confirm or rule out anything.

## What this means for you

If you're having perimenopause-like symptoms in your 30s, the useful move is asking your clinician specifically about **FSH testing done correctly** — twice, weeks apart — rather than accepting a single test result or a guess based on symptoms alone. Read more on our [blood tests for perimenopause](/perimenopause/blood-tests/) page, and see our dedicated guide to [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/) if testing points that way, or [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/) for the broader 40-45 picture. Once a diagnosis is clear, our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) grades specific symptom-relief options rather than leaving you to guess from marketing claims.

The STRAW+10 cycle-based staging framework that defines perimenopause by cycle-length changes was developed and validated mainly in midlife cohorts [@straw-10-2012], which is another reason symptoms at this age deserve a proper workup rather than self-diagnosis against that framework.
