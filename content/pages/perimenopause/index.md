Perimenopause is the biological transition phase preceding menopause, typically beginning between ages 40 and 45 and lasting four to eight years on average [@straw-10-2012]. During this interval, ovarian follicle depletion causes erratic surges and drops in estrogen and progesterone, rather than a steady decline.

Understanding where you are in this transition helps you make sense of daily changes and choose effective treatments.

## The two stages of the menopause transition

Clinical consensus defined by the STRAW+10 criteria classifies the transition into two distinct stages [@straw-10-2012]:

1. **Early transition**: Menstrual cycles remain regular or vary by seven or more days between consecutive periods. You might experience subtle mood changes, lighter or heavier bleeding, or initial sleep disruptions.
2. **Late transition**: Skipped cycles of 60 days or more occur. Vasomotor symptoms like hot flashes and night sweats usually become more frequent and pronounced during this phase.

## Recognizing early symptoms

Symptoms often appear while periods are still relatively regular. Common early indicators include:

- Shorter cycle length (for instance, dropping from 28 days to 23 days).
- Sudden waking at 3 a.m. with rapid heartbeat or sweating.
- Premenstrual mood swings or anxiety that feel more intense than past years.
- Slower cognitive processing or word retrieval during busy workdays.

Read our detailed breakdown in the [Early signs of perimenopause guide](/perimenopause/early-signs/).

## Diagnosing perimenopause: do you need a blood test?

A common question is whether a blood test can prove you are in perimenopause. Major clinical guidelines, including NICE and The Menopause Society, advise against routine hormone blood tests (such as FSH or estradiol) in women aged 45 and over [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. 

Because hormone levels fluctuate widely from day to day and even hour to hour during perimenopause, a single blood draw does not provide a reliable diagnosis. Instead, diagnosis is based on your age, cycle history, and symptom patterns [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
