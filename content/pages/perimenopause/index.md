Perimenopause is the transition to menopause: the years when estrogen rises and falls unevenly and your periods start to change [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026; @cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]. It usually starts in your mid-40s, lasts about four years on average, and ends 12 months after your final period [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause; @straw-10-2012]. If symptoms bother you, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for hot flashes [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## What is perimenopause?

Perimenopause is the stretch of time that begins when your menstrual cycle starts to change and other menopause-related symptoms appear, and ends one year after your final period [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. It's also called the menopause transition [@menopause-society-glossary-2026; @nia-what-is-menopause]. Being "perimenopausal" simply means you're in this stage.

During perimenopause, your ovaries make less estrogen and progesterone, but not in a smooth line: estrogen rises and falls along the way [@nia-what-is-menopause; @mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. These hormone changes may explain the symptoms many people notice [@nia-what-is-menopause].

Menopause itself is a single point in time: your final period, confirmed once you've gone 12 months in a row without one [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. Perimenopause happens only with natural menopause [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. If surgery removes both ovaries and you don't take hormones, periods stop and menopause symptoms can start right away [@nia-what-is-menopause].

## Premenopausal vs perimenopausal vs postmenopausal: what's the difference?

Premenopause is the years of periods before any changes start, perimenopause is the transition, and postmenopause is the rest of life after your final period [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. Here's how the stages compare:

| Stage | When it is | Your periods | Can you get pregnant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premenopause | From your first period until perimenopause begins [@menopause-society-glossary-2026] | Usually regular [@straw-10-2012] | Yes; fertility ends only at menopause [@menopause-society-glossary-2026] |
| Perimenopause | From the first lasting cycle changes until 12 months after your final period [@menopause-society-glossary-2026; @straw-10-2012] | Shorter or longer, heavier or lighter, or skipped [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] | Less likely, but still possible [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause] |
| Menopause | One point in time: your final period, confirmed after 12 months without one [@menopause-society-glossary-2026] | Stopped for good [@menopause-society-glossary-2026] | No; it marks the permanent end of fertility [@menopause-society-glossary-2026] |
| Postmenopause | The rest of your life after menopause [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024] | None; any bleeding should be checked [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding] | No; the ovaries stop releasing eggs [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024] |

So if your periods have started to change, or you've begun having symptoms such as hot flashes, you've moved from premenopause into perimenopause [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. In postmenopause, hot flashes often get milder or go away, though some people still have them for years [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. For more detail, see [perimenopause vs menopause](/perimenopause/vs-menopause/) and [life after menopause](/postmenopause/).

## The two stages of perimenopause

The STRAW+10 staging system, used by researchers and clinicians, splits perimenopause into two stages based on your periods [@straw-10-2012]:

- **Early perimenopause**: the length of your cycle keeps differing by 7 days or more from one cycle to the next [@straw-10-2012]. For example, one cycle might be 24 days and the next 33.
- **Late perimenopause**: you go 60 days or more without a period. This stage lasts 1 to 3 years on average, and hot flashes are likely during it [@straw-10-2012].

Perimenopause then ends 12 months after your final period [@straw-10-2012]. Read more in [the stages of perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/), or check your own pattern with the [perimenopause stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/).

## What are the signs of perimenopause?

A change in your periods is often the first sign: they may come closer together or further apart, get heavier or lighter, or skip a month [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq; @nia-what-is-menopause]. Other common signs include:

- **Hot flashes and night sweats**: hot flushes in the UK. A sudden feeling of heat in the face and upper body, which usually lasts from 30 seconds to 10 minutes; night sweats are hot flashes during sleep [@nia-what-is-menopause].
- **Sleep problems**: trouble falling or staying asleep [@nhs-menopause-symptoms; @cleveland-clinic-perimenopause].
- **Mood changes**: irritability, mood swings, low mood or depression [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause; @nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **Brain fog**: problems with memory or concentration [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **Vaginal and bladder changes**: vaginal dryness, discomfort during sex, needing to pee more often, and more urine infections [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause; @nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **Aches and headaches**: muscle aches, joint pains, and headaches or migraines that are worse than usual [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **Lower sex drive**: less interest in sex than before [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause; @nhs-menopause-symptoms].

Symptoms vary a lot: some people have few or mild ones, while others have several that last for years [@nia-what-is-menopause]. See the [early signs of perimenopause](/perimenopause/early-signs/), what to expect [year by year](/perimenopause/year-by-year/), or record yours with the [symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/).

## How long does perimenopause last?

Perimenopause lasts about four years on average, but it can last up to eight years [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]. The late stage, with 60 or more days between periods, lasts 1 to 3 years on average [@straw-10-2012].

Hot flashes can outlast perimenopause itself. In a large US study, frequent hot flashes and night sweats lasted a median of 7.4 years in total, and continued for a median of 4.5 years after the final period [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. See [how long perimenopause lasts](/perimenopause/how-long/) for the full picture.

## When does perimenopause start?

Perimenopause usually starts in your mid-40s, but it can begin as early as your mid-30s or as late as your mid-50s [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]. The National Institute on Aging says most women begin the transition between 45 and 55 [@nia-what-is-menopause]. Smoking and a family history of early menopause are linked with an earlier start [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

If your periods stop or become irregular before 45, tell your clinician [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. See [perimenopause age](/perimenopause/age/) and [perimenopause in your 30s](/perimenopause/in-your-30s/).

## How is perimenopause diagnosed?

Usually from your age, symptoms and period pattern, without a blood test [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. UK guidance from NICE says that in otherwise healthy people aged 45 and over, perimenopause can be identified without lab tests when hot flashes or sweats have recently started along with any change in the cycle [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

The same guidance advises against using hormone tests such as estradiol or anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) to identify perimenopause at 45 or over [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. A doctor may suggest a blood test in some cases, for example if periods stopped at an early age [@nia-what-is-menopause]. See [is there a blood test for perimenopause?](/perimenopause/blood-tests/)

## Can you get pregnant during perimenopause?

Yes. Pregnancy is less likely during perimenopause, but it's still possible [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]. Mayo Clinic advises using birth control until you've gone 12 months without a period if you don't want to get pregnant [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. See [pregnancy in perimenopause](/perimenopause/pregnancy/) and [contraception in perimenopause](/perimenopause/contraception/).

## What helps with perimenopause symptoms?

Many women find they don't need treatment [@nia-what-is-menopause]. If symptoms bother you, these are the main options:

- **Everyday changes**: NHS guidance suggests lightweight clothing, a cool bedroom, cutting back on triggers such as caffeine, alcohol and spicy food, and regular exercise [@nhs-menopause-things-you-can-do]. The Menopause Society didn't find good enough evidence that these reduce hot flashes, so think of them as comfort measures [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **CBT and clinical hypnosis**: :grade{id="cbt-hypnosis-vms"}. Talking and hypnosis therapies that don't use medicine and can reduce how much hot flashes bother you [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. See [CBT and hypnosis](/treatments/nonhormonal/cbt-and-hypnosis/).
- **Hormone therapy (HRT)**: :grade{id="mht-vms"}. The most effective treatment for hot flashes and vaginal symptoms, and it prevents bone loss [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. For women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause with no reason to avoid it, the benefits generally outweigh the risks [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. If you have a uterus, a progestogen is needed along with estrogen [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/).
- **Hormonal birth control**: in perimenopause, birth control pills can steady hormone levels and often relieve symptoms [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause].
- **Non-hormone prescription medicines**: :grade{id="nonhormonal-rx-vms"}. The FDA has approved low-dose paroxetine, fezolinetant and elinzanetant for moderate to severe hot flashes; fezolinetant carries a warning about rare, serious liver injury [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. See [non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/).
- **Supplements**: :grade{id="otc-supplements-vms"}. The Menopause Society does not recommend supplements or herbal remedies for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Our [does it work?](/does-it-work/) guides review the trials.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor (a GP in the UK) if your symptoms bother you or get in the way of daily life [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause; @nhs-menopause-symptoms]. Irregular periods are common in perimenopause, but get checked if [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026; @cleveland-clinic-perimenopause]:

- Your periods are very heavy, with large clots, or you need a new pad or tampon every 1 to 2 hours [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause].
- Your periods come less than 21 days apart or last longer than 7 days [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- You bleed or spot between periods or after sex [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq].
- Your periods stop or become irregular before 45 [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].
- You have heart palpitations [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].

:::urgent
- **Any bleeding after 12 months without a period**: even a small amount, or once, needs checking [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Very heavy bleeding**: soaking through one or more pads or tampons every hour for several hours in a row [@cdc-heavy-menstrual-bleeding]. Contact a clinician right away.
- **Chest pain**: call 911 for crushing, squeezing or pressure in the chest, pain spreading to the jaw or left arm, or chest pain with shortness of breath, sweating or a racing heart [@medlineplus-chest-pain].
- **Thoughts of suicide or self-harm**: call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time [@lifeline-988].

If you're in the UK: call 999 if a life is at risk, NHS 111 for urgent mental health help, or Samaritans on 116 123 [@nhs-urgent-help-mental-health].
:::
