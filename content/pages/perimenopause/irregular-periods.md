Periods that come closer together, further apart, lighter, heavier, or get skipped altogether are a normal part of [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. A few months of tracking shows your pattern best. Bleeding between periods or after sex, very heavy bleeding, or periods usually less than 21 days apart should be checked by a doctor [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq; @mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

## What's a normal period pattern in perimenopause?

In perimenopause, your periods may get shorter or longer, the gap between them may grow or shrink, flow may get heavier or lighter, and you may skip some [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. This happens because the ovaries release an egg in some months and not in others [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. Before perimenopause, a typical cycle is 24 to 38 days, with a period lasting up to 8 days [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq].

Clinicians use two markers to describe the stages. In the early stage, your cycle lengths keep differing by 7 days or more from one cycle to the next; in the late stage, you go 60 days or more without a period [@straw-10-2012]. See the [stages of perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/) for more.

| Change in your periods | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Cycle lengths vary by 7 days or more | Typical of early perimenopause [@straw-10-2012] |
| 60 days or more between periods | Typical of late perimenopause [@straw-10-2012] |
| Skipped periods, lighter or heavier flow | Common in perimenopause [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] |
| Periods usually less than 21 days apart | See a healthcare professional [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026] |
| Bleeding for more than 7 days | See a healthcare professional [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026] |
| Bleeding or spotting between periods or after sex | Not normal; get it checked [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] |
| Bleeding heavier or longer than your usual | Not normal; get it checked [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] |
| Any bleeding after 12 months with no period | Always get it checked [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding] |

## When should I see a doctor about irregular periods?

See a doctor if your bleeding is very heavy, lasts more than seven days, happens between periods or after sex, or your periods usually come less than 21 days apart [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026; @acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. Irregular timing on its own is usually nothing to worry about and is fine to track [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

These are routine appointments, not emergencies, but don't put them off: abnormal bleeding can sometimes be a sign of a health problem that needs treating [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. UK guidance gives similar reasons to see a GP, including heavy periods that affect your life and periods with pain when peeing, pooing or having sex [@nhs-heavy-periods]. For more on booking and what to bring, see [when to see a doctor about perimenopause](/perimenopause/when-to-see-a-doctor/).

:::urgent
Contact a clinician right away if you soak through one or more pads or tampons every hour for several hours in a row [@cdc-heavy-menstrual-bleeding], or bleeding makes you feel weak or lightheaded [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]. Any bleeding after you've gone 12 months without a period also needs checking, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::

## Is it normal to have a period every two weeks in perimenopause?

Cycles can get shorter in perimenopause, but periods that usually come less than 21 days apart are a reason to see a healthcare professional [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. A doctor can check whether this is the hormone changes of perimenopause or something else, such as a polyp [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. If heavy flow is the main problem, see [heavy periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/heavy-periods/).

## Can you skip periods for months and then get one?

Yes. Going 60 days or more without a period is typical of late perimenopause, and a period can still come after that [@straw-10-2012]. You've only reached menopause once you've gone 12 months in a row without a period [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. Any bleeding after that point needs checking [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. The [12-month counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) keeps track for you.

## How long do irregular periods last before menopause?

It varies. The late stage, when gaps reach 60 days or more, lasts 1 to 3 years on average [@straw-10-2012]. Perimenopause ends 12 months after your final period [@straw-10-2012]. See [how long perimenopause lasts](/perimenopause/how-long/).

## What else can cause irregular bleeding?

Less regular ovulation as you approach menopause is a common cause of irregular bleeding, but it isn't the only one [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]. A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- Polyps, fibroids or adenomyosis (the uterine lining growing into the muscle wall) [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]
- A thickened lining of the uterus (endometrial hyperplasia) [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]
- Thyroid conditions or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]
- Pregnancy, infection or a bleeding disorder [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]
- An IUD or medicines such as blood thinners [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]
- Less often, cancer of the uterine lining, which is why unusual bleeding should be checked [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]

## Can you get pregnant with irregular periods in perimenopause?

Yes. Pregnancy gets harder as ovulation becomes less regular, but it's still possible while you're having periods [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. If you don't want to get pregnant, use birth control until you've gone 12 months without a period [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. See [can you get pregnant during perimenopause?](/perimenopause/pregnancy/)

## What tests might a doctor do?

For the cause of abnormal bleeding, a doctor will usually ask about your health and family history, may examine you, and may suggest tests such as a pelvic ultrasound or an endometrial biopsy (a small sample of the uterine lining) [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. To confirm perimenopause itself at 45 or over, UK guidance says blood tests usually aren't needed when hot flashes or night sweats have started along with cycle changes [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [is there a blood test for perimenopause?](/perimenopause/blood-tests/)

## Track your pattern

Because staging depends on comparing cycle lengths over time, a few months of dates tells you more than any single cycle [@straw-10-2012]. Log the first day of each period, how many days you bleed, and any spotting in between. The [perimenopause stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/) uses your pattern to estimate where you are.
