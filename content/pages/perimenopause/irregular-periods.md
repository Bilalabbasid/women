Periods that arrive closer together, further apart, lighter, heavier, or just less predictably are one of the earliest and most common signs of [perimenopause](/perimenopause/). In the STRAW+10 clinical staging framework, this is formally described as **early menopausal transition** — a persistent difference of 7 days or more between consecutive cycle lengths [@straw-10-2012]. UK guideline NICE NG23 also uses menstrual cycle changes, alongside new vasomotor symptoms, to help identify perimenopause without needing blood tests in people 45 and over [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## What "irregular" actually covers

Perimenopausal cycle changes can go in more than one direction, and both are expected:

- **Shorter cycles**, sometimes with periods arriving closer together than before
- **Longer cycles**, with gaps stretching out as ovulation becomes less frequent
- **Skipped periods**, which become more common as you move toward [late perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/)
- **Lighter or heavier flow** than your usual pattern (see our dedicated page on [heavy periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/heavy-periods/) if flow itself is the main issue)

## When irregularity needs a doctor's check

Most cycle irregularity in perimenopause is fine to simply track. A smaller set of patterns needs medical attention instead [@nhs-heavy-periods]:

:::urgent
See a doctor for an assessment if you have **bleeding between periods, bleeding after sex, periods that are unusually heavy or prolonged**, or **any bleeding after you have gone 12 consecutive months without a period** (which is always worth checking — see [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/)).
:::

## Track your pattern

Because the staging itself depends on comparing cycle lengths over time, a few months of tracking is more useful than trying to judge a single cycle. Our [perimenopause stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/) uses your logged pattern to estimate where you likely are, and our [12-month counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) picks up once your periods actually stop.

If you've had unexplained bleeding changes and want more clarity before your appointment, see [is there a blood test for perimenopause?](/perimenopause/blood-tests/) for what testing can and can't tell you.
