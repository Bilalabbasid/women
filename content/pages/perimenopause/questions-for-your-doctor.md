The most useful questions about hormone therapy (HRT) cover whether it suits your health history, which type fits you, your personal risks, what side effects to expect, and how long you'd take it. Bring a short symptom log and your period dates. Guidance says HRT's balance of benefits and risks is most favorable when started before 60 or within 10 years of menopause [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Questions to ask your doctor about HRT

Pick the questions that matter most to you; you won't need all of them in one visit. To build and print your own list, use the [HRT and treatment question builder](/tools/treatment-questions/).

### Is HRT right for me?

Ask this first. For people under 60 or within 10 years of menopause who have no reason to avoid it, the benefits of hormone therapy generally outweigh the risks; starting later carries greater absolute risks of heart disease, stroke, blood clots and dementia [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The FDA's labeling recommendation is to start systemic HRT within 10 years of menopause or before age 60 [@hhs-mht-fact-sheet-2025].

- Given my age, how long since my last period, and my health and family history, am I a good candidate for HRT?
- Is there anything in my history that rules HRT out, or that means we should get specialist advice first?
- Which of my symptoms is HRT likely to help, and which might need something else?

See [who HRT may suit, and who needs specialist advice](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/).

### Which type and form would suit me?

Estrogen for the whole body comes as pills, skin patches, gels, sprays and a vaginal ring [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. If you have a uterus, you also need progesterone or a progestin to protect its lining, while estrogen-only HRT is offered after a total hysterectomy [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. Certain forms of estrogen may carry a higher risk of blood clots, so the form matters [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].

- Do I need a progestogen, and which one would you suggest?
- Would a patch or gel suit me better than a pill, given my risk of blood clots?
- My main problem is vaginal dryness or pain during sex. Would vaginal estrogen alone be enough? (Vaginal estrogen treats these symptoms [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].)
- Is the product you're suggesting FDA-approved?

See [HRT patch vs. pill](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) and [types of HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/types/).

### What are the risks for me?

HRT labeling describes risks such as blood clots, stroke, heart attack and certain cancers, and your own risk depends on your age, health history, and the type, dose and form you use [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. In February 2026 the FDA approved label changes for six HRT products that removed heart disease, breast cancer and probable dementia from the boxed warning [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. The FDA did not seek to remove the boxed warning about endometrial cancer on estrogen-alone products [@hhs-mht-fact-sheet-2025].

- What are my personal risks of blood clots, stroke and breast cancer on HRT, compared with not taking it?
- Does my family history change those risks?
- I have had breast cancer (or am at high risk). Should we involve my cancer care team first? (Guidance says to talk with them before any hormone therapy, including vaginal estrogen [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].)

See [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/), [HRT and blood clot risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/blood-clot-risk/) and [the HRT boxed warning](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/).

### What side effects should I expect?

Vaginal bleeding is a common side effect in the first 6 months of HRT, or within 3 months of changing the dose or type; UK guidance says to seek medical help promptly for unscheduled bleeding outside those timeframes [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The same guidance uses the lowest dose that works [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- What side effects are common at first, and how long do they usually last?
- What bleeding is expected, and what bleeding should make me call you?
- When will we review how it's going?

See [HRT side effects](/treatments/hormone-therapy/side-effects/).

### How long would I take it, and how would I stop?

There's no single time limit for HRT; you and your clinician review your symptoms, health and preferences on a regular schedule [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. UK guidance says the possible length of treatment should be discussed at the start, that symptoms may come back when HRT stops, and that tapering off or stopping straight away makes no difference to symptoms in the longer term [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- How long do you expect I'd take HRT, and how often will we review it?
- If I want to stop, should I taper off or stop at once?
- What happens if my symptoms come back after stopping?

See [starting and stopping HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/starting-and-stopping/).

### What if HRT isn't for me?

The FDA has approved three nonhormone medicines for moderate to severe hot flashes: low-dose paroxetine, fezolinetant and elinzanetant [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. Fezolinetant carries an FDA warning about rare, serious liver injury [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and clinical hypnosis can also reduce how much hot flashes bother you [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].

- If I can't or don't want to take HRT, which nonhormone medicines could I try?
- Would CBT or clinical hypnosis be worth trying for my hot flashes or sleep?
- If I take fezolinetant, how will we check my liver?

See [non-hormonal treatments for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/).

### What will it cost?

- Is this covered by my insurance, and is there a generic version?
- If cost is a problem, is there a cheaper form that would work as well for me?

See [how much HRT costs](/care/hrt-cost/).

## Questions about perimenopause itself

- Based on my age, cycle changes and symptoms, does this look like perimenopause?
- Should we check for other causes of my symptoms, such as thyroid problems?
- Do I still need birth control, and for how long?

From age 45, UK guidance says perimenopause can be identified from new hot flashes or night sweats plus cycle changes, without blood tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Pregnancy is still possible while you're having periods [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

## How to use a short appointment

Appointments can feel rushed, so plan the order:

1. **Lead with your main problem**: for example, "Night sweats wake me four nights a week and it's affecting my work."
2. **Hand over your record**: a one-page summary from the [symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) or the completed [perimenopause symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/).
3. **Ask your top three questions**: from the lists above, and agree on next steps before you leave.

## What if your doctor says you're "too young"?

You're not too young to ask. Signs of perimenopause often start sometime in the 40s, and some people notice changes in their 30s [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. US guidance says to tell your provider if your periods stop or become irregular before 45 [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].

Blood tests aren't how perimenopause is usually identified. At 45 or over, UK guidance says not to use hormone tests such as estradiol or anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) for this, and to go by symptoms and cycle changes instead [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Between 40 and 45, an FSH test may be considered [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

If you feel dismissed:

- **Describe the impact**: "Even if my results are normal, these night wakings and cycle changes are affecting my daily life."
- **Ask for a note**: "Could we record in my chart that we discussed these symptoms and the plan?"
- **Ask about a specialist**: The Menopause Society lists clinicians who hold its certified practitioner credential (MSCP) [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. See [how to find a menopause specialist](/care/find-a-menopause-specialist/) and [what to say if your doctor dismisses your symptoms](/care/when-your-doctor-dismisses-you/).

## Checklist to bring

- **Your symptom summary**: how often symptoms happen and how they affect sleep and work.
- **Your period dates**: the first day of each of your last few periods, since clinical staging is based on changes in cycle length [@straw-10-2012].
- **Your medicines and supplements**: including over-the-counter products and doses.
- **Your health history**: including personal or family history of breast cancer, blood clots, stroke or heart disease, because HRT risks depend on it [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].
