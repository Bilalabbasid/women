Perimenopause is the few years when periods turn irregular and hot flashes often start. Menopause is one point in time: your final period, confirmed after 12 months in a row without one [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. If you're 45 or over, a doctor can usually tell your stage from your periods and symptoms, with no blood test needed [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

People often say "going through menopause" to mean all of these years, but guidelines separate [perimenopause](/perimenopause/), [menopause](/menopause/) and [postmenopause](/postmenopause/), the years after the final period [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Side-by-side comparison (STRAW+10 & NICE NG23)

| Stage | How clinical criteria define it | What happens with periods and symptoms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Early perimenopause** (STRAW+10 Stage −2) | Begins when consecutive cycle lengths vary by **7 days or more** (recurring within 10 cycles) [@straw-10-2012] | Periods still come, but cycle length shifts |
| **Late perimenopause** (STRAW+10 Stage −1) | Gaps of **60 days or more** without a period; lasts **1 to 3 years** on average [@straw-10-2012] | Skipped periods; hot flashes and night sweats are likely in this stage [@straw-10-2012] |
| **Menopause** | Confirmed looking back, after **12 consecutive months** without a period (when not using hormonal contraception) [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations] | Periods have stopped for good |
| **Postmenopause** (STRAW+10 Stage +1/+2) | All the years after the final period; the early stage lasts about 5 to 8 years [@straw-10-2012] | No periods. Hot flashes are most likely in the first years; vaginal dryness becomes more common later [@straw-10-2012]. Any bleeding needs a doctor's check [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding] |

## How doctors tell which stage you're in

- **If you're 45 or over**: NICE advises that perimenopause can be identified without lab tests when hot flashes or sweats have recently started along with any change in your cycle, and menopause after 12 months without a period if you aren't using hormonal contraception [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Blood tests usually aren't needed**: For people 45 or over, NICE advises against using hormone tests such as AMH, inhibin or estradiol, or ultrasound measures of the ovaries (antral follicle count or ovarian volume) to identify perimenopause or menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [blood tests for perimenopause](/perimenopause/blood-tests/).
- **If you're under 45**: NICE suggests an FSH blood test may be considered to confirm menopause in people aged 40 to 45 with symptoms and cycle changes, and in people under 40 when menopause is suspected [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/) and [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).
- **If periods can't guide you**: Hormonal treatments can make menopause hard to identify, and after a hysterectomy NICE advises identifying it from your symptoms instead [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [hysterectomy with ovaries retained](/groups/hysterectomy-ovaries-retained/).

## Symptoms across the stages

Menopause-related symptoms are not only about periods. NICE lists hot flashes and sweats, vaginal dryness and urinary symptoms, mood changes such as low mood, joint and muscle pain, and sexual difficulties such as low desire. They can be mild or severe, and short-lived or long-lasting [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Hot flashes and night sweats**: Likely in the late transition and most likely in the first years after the final period [@straw-10-2012]. In the large US SWAN study, frequent hot flashes or night sweats lasted a median of 7.4 years in total and continued for a median of 4.5 years after the final period [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. More in [how long menopause symptoms last](/menopause/how-long-symptoms-last/).
- **Vaginal dryness**: Becomes increasingly common in later postmenopause [@straw-10-2012].

## When to see a doctor

- **Heavy or unusual bleeding before menopause**: See a doctor if heavy periods affect your life, if you have severe period pain, or if you bleed between periods or after sex [@nhs-heavy-periods]. See [heavy periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/heavy-periods/).
- **Symptoms affecting your life**: Our guide on [when to see a doctor in perimenopause](/perimenopause/when-to-see-a-doctor/) covers what to raise and how to prepare.

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even if it's a small amount or happens only once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. See [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::
