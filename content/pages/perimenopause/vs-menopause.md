faq:
  - q: Is perimenopause the same as menopause?
    a: No. Perimenopause is the multi-year transition when hormone levels fluctuate and cycles shift. Menopause is the milestone reached once you have gone 12 consecutive months with no menstrual period.
  - q: Can I get hot flashes in perimenopause while I still have periods?
    a: Yes. Hot flashes and night sweats frequently begin during the early or late menopausal transition while periods are still happening or skipping.

Although people often say "going through menopause" to describe several years of changing periods and symptoms, medical guidelines distinguish clearly between **[perimenopause](/perimenopause/)**, **[menopause](/menopause/)**, and **[postmenopause](/postmenopause/)** [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Side-by-side comparison (STRAW+10 & NICE NG23)

| Stage | How clinical criteria define it | What happens with periods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Early perimenopause** (STRAW+10 Stage −2) | Begins when consecutive cycle lengths vary by **7 days or more** (recurring within 10 cycles) [@straw-10-2012] | Periods still happen, often shifting shorter or longer |
| **Late perimenopause** (STRAW+10 Stage −1) | Intervals of **60 days or more** without a period; lasts **1 to 3 years** on average [@straw-10-2012] | Skipped periods for 2 to 11 months; vasomotor symptoms often peak |
| **Menopause** | Confirmed retrospectively after **12 consecutive months** without a period (when not using hormonal contraception) [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations] | Periods have stopped permanently |
| **Postmenopause** (STRAW+10 Stage +1/+2) | All the years following that 12-month mark [@straw-10-2012] | No menstrual bleeding; any bleeding or spotting needs a doctor's check [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding] |
