See a doctor if perimenopause symptoms bother you or affect daily life, or if your bleeding changes in certain ways, such as bleeding between periods or after sex [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026; @acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. Most symptoms aren't urgent, but any bleeding after 12 months without a period always needs checking [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].

## When should I see a doctor about perimenopause?

Book an appointment when any perimenopause symptom bothers you, when you want to talk about treatment, or when your bleeding pattern changes in a way listed below. You don't need to wait until symptoms are severe: Mayo Clinic's advice is simply to see a healthcare professional if any symptoms bother you [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

Good reasons to book a routine appointment:

- **Symptoms affect your life**: hot flashes, sleep problems, mood changes or others are getting in the way of work, sleep or relationships [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- **You want to know your options**: lifestyle changes and medicines can help, and a doctor can go through them with you [@nia-what-is-menopause; @nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **Your periods changed before 45**: tell your clinician if periods stop or become irregular before 45 [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].
- **You're bleeding more, not less**: heavier bleeding than before is worth checking [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **You have heart palpitations**: these are a listed reason to contact a doctor [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. See [heart palpitations](/symptoms/heart-palpitations/).
- **You need birth control advice**: pregnancy is still possible while you're having periods [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. See [contraception in perimenopause](/perimenopause/contraception/).

## Do irregular periods in perimenopause need a doctor?

Usually not on their own. Irregular periods are common in perimenopause and most of the time aren't a concern [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026]. Book a routine appointment if bleeding is very heavy, lasts more than seven days, happens between periods, or your periods usually come less than 21 days apart [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

ACOG adds bleeding or spotting after sex, and bleeding that's heavier or lasts more days than usual, to the list of changes that aren't normal [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. A doctor can check for causes such as polyps or a thickened lining of the uterus (endometrial hyperplasia) [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. Our page on [irregular periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) explains what's typical.

## Which symptoms are urgent?

Most perimenopause symptoms can wait for a routine appointment. These can't:

:::urgent
- **Very heavy bleeding**: soaking through one or more pads or tampons every hour for several hours in a row [@cdc-heavy-menstrual-bleeding], or bleeding heavy enough to make you feel weak or lightheaded [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]. Contact a clinician right away.
- **Any bleeding after 12 months without a period**: even a small amount, or once, needs checking [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Chest pain**: call 911 for crushing, squeezing or pressure in the chest, pain spreading to the jaw or left arm, or chest pain with shortness of breath, sweating or a racing heart [@medlineplus-chest-pain].
- **Thoughts of suicide or self-harm**: call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time [@lifeline-988].

If you're in the UK: call 999 for palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath or fainting [@nhs-heart-palpitations], and NHS 111 for urgent mental health help [@nhs-urgent-help-mental-health].
:::

## What kind of doctor should I see for perimenopause?

Start with your primary care clinician or an ob-gyn. ACOG advises talking with an ob-gyn about bleeding changes as you near menopause [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. If you'd like someone with extra menopause training, The Menopause Society awards a certified practitioner credential (MSCP) and keeps a searchable directory that includes doctors, nurse practitioners, physician assistants and nurse midwives [@menopause-society-choosing-practitioner]. See [how to find a menopause specialist](/care/find-a-menopause-specialist/).

## When should I ask for a referral to a menopause specialist?

Ask for a referral if treatment hasn't helped your symptoms or side effects keep going. That's the point at which UK guidance recommends referral to a clinician with expertise in menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The same guidance describes these specialists as the people who handle:

- **Complex health problems** that affect which menopause treatments are safe to use [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Cancer risk or history**: a raised risk of breast or ovarian cancer, or a past hormone-dependent cancer [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Doubt about premature ovarian insufficiency**: if it's unclear whether you have it under 40, referral to a menopause or reproductive specialist is advised [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

In the US, check whether your insurance plan needs a referral from your primary care clinician before you book a specialist; see [does insurance cover menopause care?](/care/insurance/). If you feel your symptoms aren't being taken seriously, see [what to say if your doctor dismisses your symptoms](/care/when-your-doctor-dismisses-you/).

## Will I need blood tests?

Often not. From age 45, UK guidance says perimenopause can be identified from new hot flashes or night sweats plus cycle changes, without lab tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Between 40 and 45 an FSH blood test may be considered, and under 40 two FSH tests 4 to 6 weeks apart are used to check for premature ovarian insufficiency [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [is there a blood test for perimenopause?](/perimenopause/blood-tests/)

## What to bring to your appointment

A focused appointment gets you further than a vague one. Bring:

- **Your period dates**: the first day of each of your last few periods, since clinical staging is based on how much cycle lengths change [@straw-10-2012]. The [symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/) helps you note which symptoms to raise.
- **A short symptom log**: how often symptoms happen and how they affect sleep or work. The [symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) makes a one-page summary.
- **Your medicines and supplements**: including over-the-counter products and doses.
- **Your health history**: including personal or family history of cancer, blood clots, stroke or heart disease, because hormone therapy risks depend on your health history [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026], and any family history of early menopause [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].
- **Your questions, written down**: build them with the [treatment question builder](/tools/treatment-questions/), or start from [what to ask your doctor about perimenopause](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/).
