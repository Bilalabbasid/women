Most [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) symptoms are a normal part of the transition and don't need urgent attention. But some patterns are worth a routine appointment, and a few deserve prompt care. Here's how to tell the difference.

## See a doctor soon if...

- Symptoms (hot flashes, sleep problems, mood changes, or others) are **affecting your work, relationships, or daily life**
- You want to **discuss treatment options**, including hormone therapy or non-hormonal alternatives
- Your periods have become **irregular in a way you want evaluated**, especially if you also want to talk about [contraception](/perimenopause/pregnancy/)
- You have **contraindications to hormone therapy**, or are unsure which management options fit your health history — NICE guidance specifically recommends considering a referral to a menopause specialist in this situation [@nice-ng23-recommendations]

## Get checked more urgently if...

:::urgent
- **Heavy periods** that soak through pads or tampons every hour or two, affect your daily life, or come with severe pain, bleeding between periods, bleeding after sex, or pain when peeing, having a bowel movement, or having sex [@nhs-heavy-periods]
- **Any vaginal bleeding after you've gone 12 consecutive months without a period** — this always needs checking, even once or lightly [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]
- **Chest pain, severe palpitations, shortness of breath, or fainting** — see our [heart palpitations](/symptoms/heart-palpitations/) page for what counts as an emergency
:::
