Bone loss speeds up around your last period, because lower estrogen means your body breaks down bone faster than it rebuilds it [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @greendale-swan-bone-loss-fmp-2012]. It causes no symptoms, so a bone density scan is the only way to check [@niams-osteoporosis-2022]. Strength and impact training and hormone therapy have the strongest evidence for protecting bone [@liftmor-trial-bone-density; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Why does menopause cause bone loss?

Your skeleton is always being broken down and rebuilt. Estrogen helps keep those two processes in step; after [menopause](/menopause/), permanently lower estrogen tips the balance toward breakdown, raising the risk of thin bones (osteopenia), osteoporosis and fractures [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @medlineplus-osteoporosis]. US health agencies list the drop in estrogen at menopause as one of the main causes of bone loss in women [@medlineplus-osteoporosis; @niams-osteoporosis-2022].

Other things add to the risk. They include a family history of osteoporosis, a small or thin build, low calcium or vitamin D intake, smoking, heavy drinking, being inactive, and long-term use of some medicines, such as steroid tablets [@niams-osteoporosis-2022; @medlineplus-osteoporosis]. About half of women over 50 will break a hip, wrist or spine bone during their lifetime [@medlineplus-osteoporosis].

## How fast do you lose bone after menopause?

Bone loss is fastest from about a year before your final period until two years after it, then slows down but doesn't stop. That was the pattern in SWAN, a large US study that followed women through menopause [@greendale-swan-bone-loss-fmp-2012]. Over the 10 years around the last period, women lost about 10.6% of their spine bone density and 9.1% at the hip, and most of the spine loss (7.4%) happened in that three-year window [@greendale-swan-bone-loss-fmp-2012].

The rate wasn't the same for everyone. Women with a higher body weight and African American women lost bone more slowly, while Chinese and Japanese women lost it faster [@greendale-swan-bone-loss-fmp-2012]. Because the steepest loss starts before periods stop, the years of [postmenopause](/postmenopause/) just after your last period are a good time to think about bone.

## When should I get a bone density scan?

The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening for osteoporosis in all women 65 and older, and in younger postmenopausal women whose risk factors put them at higher risk of fracture [@uspstf-osteoporosis-screening-2025]. The test is a DEXA scan: a low-radiation x-ray, usually of the spine and hip [@medlineplus-osteoporosis].

The scan shows whether you have bone loss or osteoporosis and helps predict your risk of a future fracture [@medlineplus-osteoporosis]. Because osteoporosis has no early symptoms, many people only find out after they break a bone [@medlineplus-osteoporosis]. Our [postmenopause health checklist](/postmenopause/health-checklist/) lists this alongside other screening tests.

## What protects bone after menopause?

These are the options with the clearest research behind them:

- **Strength and impact training** :grade{id="strength-training-bone"}: in the LIFTMOR trial, women with low bone density or osteoporosis did 8 months of supervised, twice-weekly, 30-minute sessions of heavy resistance and impact training. They gained spine and hip bone density, strength and function compared with a gentle home program [@liftmor-trial-bone-density]. See [does exercise help menopause symptoms?](/does-it-work/exercise/)
- **Hormone therapy**: systemic [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) prevents bone loss and fracture. The benefit-risk balance is most favorable for women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause who have no reason to avoid it [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Calcium and vitamin D from your diet**: guidance is 1,200 mg of calcium a day for women 51 and older [@medlineplus-calcium-diet] and 600 IU of vitamin D a day up to age 70, then 800 IU [@medlineplus-vitamin-d]. Supplements are meant to fill a gap in your diet, not replace food [@medlineplus-osteoporosis].
- **Not smoking, limiting alcohol and preventing falls**: smoking and heavy drinking both weaken bone, and alcohol also makes falls more likely [@niams-osteoporosis-2022; @medlineplus-osteoporosis].

### What kind of exercise is best for bones?

US guidance points to three types of exercise for bone health [@niams-exercise-bone-health-2023]:

- **Weight-bearing**: brisk walking, jogging, racket sports, stair climbing or dancing.
- **Resistance**: free weights, weight machines, resistance bands or bodyweight moves such as pushups.
- **Balance**: tai chi, step-ups or lunges, which matter more as you get older.

If you already have osteoporosis or low bone density, talk to a provider before starting a new program [@niams-exercise-bone-health-2023]. Avoid exercise with a risk of falling, and high-impact moves that could cause a fracture in older bones [@medlineplus-osteoporosis]. LIFTMOR's training was supervised throughout [@liftmor-trial-bone-density].

### Do calcium and vitamin D supplements prevent fractures?

Not at low doses in women who aren't short of them. The USPSTF recommends against daily supplements of 400 IU or less of vitamin D and 1,000 mg or less of calcium for preventing fractures after menopause, and says there's too little evidence to judge higher doses [@uspstf-vitamin-d-calcium-fractures-2018]. Researchers from the Women's Health Initiative trials conclude that supplements make sense for women who don't get enough from food, rather than for everyone [@manson-whi-trials-clinical-practice-2024].

Calcium is absorbed best in doses of no more than 500 mg at a time [@medlineplus-calcium-diet]. Up to 2,000 mg a day is considered safe for adults over 50, but high intake over a long time can raise the risk of kidney stones in some people [@medlineplus-calcium-diet]. More detail: [calcium after menopause](/does-it-work/calcium/) :grade{id="calcium-fractures"} and [vitamin D](/does-it-work/vitamin-d/) :grade{id="vitamin-d-fractures"}.

Probiotics are a newer idea. A 2024 review of 12 trials found small gains in spine and hip bone density, more so in women with osteopenia, though the authors want more trials [@wang-probiotics-bone-health-metaanalysis-2024]. See [probiotics](/does-it-work/probiotics/) :grade{id="probiotics-bone"}.

## Can you rebuild bone after menopause?

Some bone density can be regained. In LIFTMOR, supervised high-intensity training increased spine and hip bone density in women who already had osteopenia or osteoporosis [@liftmor-trial-bone-density]. Some osteoporosis medicines, such as denosumab, teriparatide and abaloparatide, also increase bone density [@medlineplus-osteoporosis].

## What medicines treat osteoporosis?

Medicines are used when a bone density scan shows osteoporosis and your fracture risk is high, or when you've broken a bone and the scan shows thin bones [@medlineplus-osteoporosis]. The main options are [@medlineplus-osteoporosis]:

- **Bisphosphonates**: the main medicines for preventing and treating osteoporosis after menopause, taken by mouth or by IV.
- **Denosumab**: an injection under the skin that slows bone loss and raises bone density.
- **Teriparatide or abaloparatide**: man-made versions of a natural hormone that increase bone density.
- **Romosozumab**: a newer medicine for more severe bone thinning.
- **Raloxifene**: an estrogen receptor modulator.

How long you take a medicine depends on your fracture risk. US guidance gives 5 years of tablets or 3 years of IV treatment for lower risk, and 10 years of tablets or 6 years of IV treatment for higher risk [@medlineplus-osteoporosis].

## What if menopause came early?

If your periods stopped early, especially before 40 ([premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/)), ask about bone protection sooner rather than later. UK guideline NG23 covers how to manage premature ovarian insufficiency, including the effects of HRT on long-term health [@nice-ng23]. See also [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/).

## When to see a doctor about your bones

Ask about a bone density scan if you're 65 or older, or younger and past menopause with risk factors such as a family history, a low body weight, smoking or long-term steroid use [@uspstf-osteoporosis-screening-2025; @niams-osteoporosis-2022]. See a doctor if you notice new back pain, lost height or a stooped posture. These can be signs of spine fractures, which often happen without a clear injury [@niams-osteoporosis-2022; @medlineplus-osteoporosis].
