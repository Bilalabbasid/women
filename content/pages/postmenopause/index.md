Postmenopause is every year after menopause: it begins once you've had no period for 12 months in a row and lasts for the rest of your life [@menopause-society-glossary-2026; @cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Hot flashes usually fade over time, but vaginal dryness tends to linger. Any bleeding after menopause needs a doctor's check [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].

This section covers what changes in your body after the final period, which symptoms settle and which don't, and the health checks that matter more from here on. Each guide below goes deeper on one part of it.

## What does postmenopausal mean?

Being postmenopausal means you're past menopause: 12 months have passed since your last period, your periods have stopped for good, and your ovaries no longer release eggs [@menopause-society-glossary-2026; @cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Menopause itself is just one moment, the final period, and you can only know it happened by looking back after a full year [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. The run-up with irregular periods, perimenopause, ends at that same 12-month mark [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Typical age**: natural menopause happens at 52 on average in the US, so most people become postmenopausal in their early to mid 50s [@menopause-society-glossary-2026; @owh-menopause-basics-2026].
- **Induced menopause**: removing both ovaries, or cancer treatment that permanently damages them, ends periods at any age, without a gradual run-up [@menopause-society-glossary-2026]. See [menopause in specific situations](/groups/).
- **When the 12-month rule doesn't work**: if you've had a hysterectomy or use hormonal birth control, your periods can't show you when menopause happened [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. Symptoms and a clinician's judgment fill the gap.

To work out your own 12-month date, use the [12-month period-free counter](/tools/12-month-counter/).

## How long does postmenopause last?

Postmenopause lasts for the rest of your life; it's a life stage, not a phase that ends [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Women now spend about 40% of their lives postmenopausal [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. What changes over those years is which symptoms you have, and that differs a lot from person to person.

## What are the symptoms of postmenopause?

The most common postmenopause symptoms are hot flashes that taper off over several years, and vaginal and bladder changes that tend to stay or slowly worsen [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015; @da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. Here is what tends to happen:

- **Vaginal dryness and painful sex**: lower estrogen makes vaginal tissue thinner and drier [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Between 50% and 70% of postmenopausal women report these symptoms (genitourinary syndrome of menopause), and unlike hot flashes they tend to get worse without treatment [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. Regular vaginal moisturizers and lubricants come first; low-dose vaginal estrogen helps persistent symptoms [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
- **Bladder urgency and repeat UTIs**: low estrogen can bring on urgency and makes repeat urinary infections more likely [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026].
- **Low mood**: in the large US SWAN study, the odds of high depressive-symptom scores were about 1.5 times higher after menopause than before perimenopause began [@kravitz-swan-postmenopause-depression-2022].
- **Weight around the middle**: many women gain weight around menopause; regular exercise can help reduce weight and waist size [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026].

## Do hot flashes stop after menopause?

For most people hot flashes fade gradually after the last period, but often not quickly. In SWAN, frequent hot flashes or night sweats continued for a median of 4.5 years after the final period [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. In another US study, moderate or severe hot flashes peaked in the first 2 years after the last period, and a third of women followed for 10 or more years still had them [@freeman-poas-long-term-hot-flashes-2014].

People whose hot flashes started early in perimenopause tended to have them longest [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. Hot flashes that appear for the first time many years after menopause should be checked for other causes [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. For how long each symptom tends to last, see [life after menopause](/postmenopause/life-after-menopause/).

## Can you get pregnant after menopause?

Once you're truly postmenopausal you can't get pregnant naturally, because your ovaries have stopped releasing eggs [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Until a clinician confirms you're no longer ovulating, Cleveland Clinic advises keeping up birth control [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Home menopause test kits only show whether FSH, a hormone that rises around menopause, is raised; the FDA says not to use them to judge whether you could become pregnant [@fda-home-menopause-test].

## What health risks go up after menopause?

Bone loss and heart disease are the two big ones: you lose bone faster after menopause, and heart disease risk rises [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026].

- **Bones**: the US Preventive Services Task Force recommends osteoporosis screening for all women 65 and over, and for younger postmenopausal women at higher fracture risk [@uspstf-osteoporosis-screening-2025]. In the LIFTMOR trial, supervised heavy strength and impact training twice a week improved spine and hip bone density in postmenopausal women with low bone mass [@liftmor-trial-bone-density]. See [does exercise help after menopause?](/does-it-work/exercise/)
- **Heart**: the share of women at intermediate-to-high heart risk nearly doubles between ages 51 and 55 [@khalifey-hrt-cardiovascular-2026]. Hormone therapy protects bone but isn't recommended to prevent heart disease or stroke [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026].

## When to see a doctor

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** needs a doctor's check, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. It usually isn't serious, but it can be a sign of cancer, so don't wait for it to happen again [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Thoughts of harming yourself**: call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the US, any time [@lifeline-988].
:::

Book a routine appointment if vaginal dryness, painful sex or bladder symptoms are bothering you, since these are treatable and tend not to clear on their own [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021; @nams-2020-gsm]. Do the same for hot flashes that start years after your last period [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy], or low mood that lasts.
