**Postmenopause** begins once **12 consecutive months** have passed since your final menstrual period [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations] and describes all the years that follow. While menstrual periods and cyclic hormonal swings are behind you, early postmenopause often still includes vasomotor symptoms, and long-term priorities shift toward genitourinary comfort, pelvic floor strength, and bone health.

## What changes in postmenopause

1. **Vasomotor symptoms gradually settle, but genitourinary symptoms often persist:** While [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) usually ease over the years after the final period, [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/), [painful sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/), and [recurrent UTIs](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/) often require ongoing local care such as regular [vaginal moisturisers and lubricants](/care/moisturizers-and-lubricants/) or low-dose [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
2. **Bone and muscle preservation move to the forefront:** Lower estrogen accelerates bone remodeling. Alongside medical options, twice-weekly supervised high-intensity resistance and impact training improved spine and hip bone mineral density in postmenopausal women in the LIFTMOR randomized trial [@liftmor-trial-bone-density] — see [Does exercise help menopause symptoms?](/does-it-work/exercise/).

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. Most causes are benign, but a clinician needs to rule out endometrial hyperplasia or cancer [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
:::
