After menopause, you're in postmenopause for the rest of your life [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Hot flashes usually ease over a few years, but vaginal dryness tends to get worse without treatment, and bone loss and heart risk rise [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015; @da-silva-gsm-management-2021; @owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026]. Treating dryness and strength training for bones both have good evidence [@nams-2020-gsm; @liftmor-trial-bone-density].

## What happens after menopause?

After menopause your periods have stopped for good and your estrogen stays low, which affects your bones, heart and bladder as well as how you feel day to day [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026; @medlineplus-menopause-2026]. Some changes ease with time, and some don't:

| Change | What usually happens after menopause |
|---|---|
| Hot flashes and night sweats | Often carry on for a few years, then ease: a median of 4.5 years after the final period in the SWAN study [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015] |
| Vaginal dryness and painful sex | Tend to get worse without treatment and rarely clear up on their own [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021] |
| Bladder | Urgency and repeat UTIs become more likely [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026] |
| Bones | Bone loss speeds up, raising the risk of osteoporosis and broken bones [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026] |
| Heart | The risk of heart disease rises [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026] |
| Body shape | The shift to more fat and less muscle levels off about 2 years after the final period [@greendale-swan-body-composition-2019] |

## How long does postmenopause last?

Postmenopause lasts for the rest of your life: it starts once you've gone 12 months in a row without a period [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. A 2021 review estimated that women now spend about 40% of their lives postmenopausal [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. That's why long-term health matters at this stage, not just symptoms. Your ovaries no longer release eggs, so you can't get pregnant [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024; @medlineplus-menopause-2026].

Not sure you've reached the 12-month mark yet? The [12-month menopause counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) counts it from your last period.

## Do hot flashes stop after menopause?

For most women hot flashes do ease, but often not right away. In the SWAN study of 3,302 women, frequent hot flashes and night sweats lasted a median of **7.4 years** in total, and **4.5 years** of that came after the final period [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015].

How long they last varies a lot. Women whose hot flashes started early in the transition had them longest (median over 11.8 years), while women whose hot flashes started only after menopause had them for the shortest time (median 3.4 years) [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015]. If they still affect your sleep or your day, treatment is an option at this stage too:

- **Hormone therapy (HRT)**: :grade{id="mht-vms"}. The Menopause Society calls it the most effective treatment for hot flashes. The balance of benefits and risks is less favorable when it's started more than 10 years after menopause or after age 60 [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Non-hormonal prescription medicines**: :grade{id="nonhormonal-rx-vms"}. Options such as fezolinetant, low-dose SSRIs and SNRIs, and gabapentin are recommended for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

See [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) for what helps and what doesn't.

## Does vaginal dryness get better after menopause?

Usually not on its own. Unlike hot flashes, vaginal dryness and related symptoms (genitourinary syndrome of menopause, or GSM) tend to get worse over time if they aren't treated [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. GSM affects an estimated 27% to 84% of postmenopausal women and is often not diagnosed or treated, even though most cases can be managed well [@nams-2020-gsm].

- **Vaginal moisturizers and lubricants**: :grade{id="vaginal-moisturizers-gsm"}. A moisturizer used regularly, plus a lubricant during sex, is the usual first step [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
- **Low-dose vaginal estrogen**: :grade{id="vaginal-estrogen-gsm"}. For symptoms that don't settle, it is highly effective and generally safe [@nams-2020-gsm].

The same low estrogen makes bladder urgency and repeat UTIs more likely [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026]. Read more in [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) and [sexual health after menopause](/postmenopause/sexual-health/).

## What happens to your bones and heart after menopause?

Bone loss speeds up after menopause and the risk of heart disease rises, so both need attention for the long term [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026]. What the evidence supports:

- **Bone density test**: the US Preventive Services Task Force recommends osteoporosis screening for all women 65 and over, and for postmenopausal women under 65 who are at higher risk of fractures [@uspstf-osteoporosis-screening-2025].
- **Strength and impact training**: :grade{id="strength-training-bone"}. In the LIFTMOR trial, 8 months of supervised twice-weekly training improved spine and hip bone density in postmenopausal women with low bone mass [@liftmor-trial-bone-density].
- **Hormone therapy**: it helps prevent bone loss, but it is not recommended to prevent heart disease or stroke [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026].
- **Everyday habits**: being active, not smoking and getting enough calcium from food all help [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026].

See [bone health after menopause](/postmenopause/bone-health/) and [heart health after menopause](/postmenopause/heart-health/) for more.

## Will I gain weight after menopause?

Many women gain weight around menopause, and regular exercise can help reduce weight and waist size [@owh-menopause-and-your-health-2026]. In SWAN, the faster fat gain and muscle loss that began with the menopause transition continued until about 2 years after the final period, then leveled off [@greendale-swan-body-composition-2019].

## Can menopause affect your mood years later?

It can. In SWAN, women were more likely to have high levels of depressive symptoms after menopause than before it (about 1.5 times the odds) [@kravitz-swan-postmenopause-depression-2022]. Women who already had moderate or high depressive symptoms before their final period were the most likely to have them afterward [@kravitz-swan-postmenopause-depression-2022]. Low mood that doesn't lift is worth raising with a doctor; see [depression and menopause](/symptoms/depression/).

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if symptoms are bothering you and you want to know your options, and always for any bleeding after 12 months without a period [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. Keep up with routine screening too. The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends a mammogram every 2 years from 40 to 74 [@uspstf-breast-cancer-screening-2024], and says cervical screening can stop after 65 for women who have had adequate screening before and aren't at high risk [@uspstf-cervical-cancer-screening-2018]. The [long-term health checklist](/postmenopause/health-checklist/) lists the tests that matter at each age.

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** needs a doctor's check, however many years it has been, even if it's light or happens only once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. It usually isn't serious, but it can be a sign of cancer [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **If you're thinking about harming yourself**, call or text **988** (US), free and confidential, any time [@lifeline-988].
- **If you're in the UK:** call Samaritans free on **116 123**, day or night [@samaritans-contact].
:::

## Keeping track over the years

Symptoms, screenings and priorities shift over time rather than all at once. Logging how you feel now and then in the [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/), not only during a flare-up, gives you and your doctor a clearer picture to work from.
