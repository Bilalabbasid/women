Acne can start or come back in perimenopause, usually as red spots and deeper bumps around the chin and jaw, because falling estrogen lets androgens (male hormones) have more effect [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. Most women don't need blood tests [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. Treatments you put on your skin come first, and spironolactone is the main pill option [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

## Is perimenopause acne normal?

Yes. Acne around perimenopause or menopause, usually in women 45 and older, is a recognized type that doctors call menopausal acne [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. Adult acne is common: studies find it in 20% to 40% of adult women, and one found it in 26.3% of women in their 40s [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

US dermatologists list hormone shifts in perimenopause and menopause, and starting or stopping birth control pills, among the causes of adult acne [@aad-adult-acne].

## What does menopausal acne look like?

It usually shows up as red, sometimes pus-filled spots along the jaw and chin [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. In perimenopause it is often deeper, tender bumps around the mouth, and after 60 it appears more on the chest and back and can be harder to treat [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

These spots can leave lasting redness, dark marks and scars, and they can weigh on mood and quality of life [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

## Why does acne happen in perimenopause and menopause?

The main driver is a shift in the balance of hormones: as estrogen falls faster than androgens, the *relative* balance tips toward androgens, even without androgen levels rising [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. The same shift can bring [new facial hair](/symptoms/facial-hair/) or thinning scalp hair [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

Other things can add to it:

- **Other hormone conditions**: thyroid problems, high prolactin and insulin resistance [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].
- **Daily life**: genes, stress, diet changes, poor sleep and less exercise [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. Stress makes the body produce more androgens [@aad-adult-acne].
- **Products**: some skin and hair products can trigger breakouts, so look for ones labeled non-comedogenic or oil-free [@aad-adult-acne].
- **Medicines and supplements**: doctors should ask about steroids, androgens and hormone therapy [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. In trials, [DHEA](/does-it-work/dhea/) supplements were linked to acne more than placebo [@scheffers-dhea-cochrane-2015].

## What treats menopause acne?

For mild acne, long-term treatment applied to the skin is the main approach, and a pill such as spironolactone is added if needed [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. Dermatologists often combine two or more treatments [@aad-adult-acne].

| **Spironolactone** (prescription pill) | First-choice anti-androgen pill in the review [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]; suggested as an option in the guideline [@reynolds-aad-acne-guideline-2024] | Side effects rise with dose: tiredness, peeing more, headache and dizziness [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019] |
| **Combined birth control pills** | Suggested as an option in the guideline [@reynolds-aad-acne-guideline-2024] | Relatively unsuitable after menopause [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019] |

## Can HRT help or cause acne?

Menopausal hormone therapy isn't an acne treatment: it isn't among the options in the 2024 US acne guideline [@reynolds-aad-acne-guideline-2024]. A dermatology review lists hormone therapy among the medicines a doctor should ask about when acne appears, and notes that estrogen in older women raises concerns such as heart disease and blood clots [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

If you take hormone therapy and your skin changes, raise it with your prescriber; see [HRT side effects](/treatments/hormone-therapy/side-effects/).

## Does menopause acne go away?

The research doesn't say how long it lasts. A 2019 review notes that adult acne tends to come back, so ongoing (maintenance) treatment is needed once it is under control, and that most adult women with acne have had it on and off since their teens [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

## Skin care that helps

Gentle care matters because older skin dries and irritates more easily [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

:::try
1. Use a moisturizer and sunscreen labeled non-comedogenic (won't clog pores) or oil-free [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019; @aad-adult-acne].
2. Replace old makeup and skip oil-based foundations [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].
3. For oily skin, a salicylic acid or glycolic acid face wash can help [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].
4. Support your skin with sleep, less stress, exercise and not smoking [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].
:::

If dryness is the bigger problem, see [dry, itchy skin in menopause](/symptoms/dry-itchy-skin/).

## Do I need blood tests for acne in my 40s or 50s?

Usually not. Most women with a handful of spots and no other symptoms have **normal androgen levels** and don't need extensive lab work [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019]. A testosterone test becomes more useful when acne is severe or comes with new facial hair, thinning at the crown, or irregular periods, which can point to conditions like polycystic ovary syndrome or an inherited adrenal gland condition (late-onset congenital adrenal hyperplasia) [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

The review also says doctors should check for related conditions such as obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure and high cholesterol [@khunger-mehrotra-menopausal-acne-2019].

## When to see a doctor about menopause acne

See a dermatologist if nothing you've tried clears your acne [@aad-adult-acne], or if spots are deep, painful or leaving scars, since stronger treatments are recommended for scarring acne [@reynolds-aad-acne-guideline-2024].

:::urgent
See a doctor soon if acne comes with fast-growing facial or body hair, a deeper voice, more muscle, male-pattern thinning on your scalp, an enlarging clitoris or smaller breasts [@medlineplus-hirsutism]. These can be signs of too much male hormone that need checking [@medlineplus-hirsutism].
:::
