Needing to pee urgently, more often, or with little warning is a common but rarely discussed change after [menopause](/menopause/). It frequently overlaps with [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) and [recurrent UTIs](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/) as part of **genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)** — falling estrogen thins and changes the tissue of the vagina, urethra, and bladder [@nams-2020-gsm].

NICE guideline NG23 specifically addresses this overlap: for people with genitourinary symptoms *and* an overactive bladder, it points to the dedicated NICE guideline on managing urinary incontinence and pelvic organ prolapse (NG123) for choosing bladder medicines, alongside the use of vaginal estrogen for the underlying tissue changes [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Urgency, or incontinence?

These overlap but aren't identical:

- **Bladder urgency** is the sudden, strong need to urinate, sometimes with little warning.
- **[Incontinence](/symptoms/incontinence/)** is actual leakage, which can be linked to urgency (urge incontinence) or to physical strain like coughing or exercise (stress incontinence).

If you have any leakage, our dedicated [incontinence](/symptoms/incontinence/) page covers pelvic floor training in more detail.

:::urgent
Seek prompt medical care if urinary urgency or frequency comes with fever, chills, flank or back pain, nausea, confusion, or visible blood in your urine — these can signal a urinary tract infection or kidney infection that needs treatment, not just symptom management. See our [recurrent UTIs](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/) page for more on telling the two apart.
:::

## What can help (graded by evidence)

| Option | Evidence grade | What guidelines say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low-dose local [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/)** | **Strong** | Restores vaginal and urethral tissue health in GSM; NICE cross-references its use alongside overactive bladder care [@nams-2020-gsm; @nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| **Supervised pelvic floor muscle training** | **Strong** | NICE's incontinence guideline recommends a supervised programme of at least 3 months, with at least 8 contractions 3 times a day, as first-line care for stress or mixed incontinence [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence] |
| **Ruling out infection first** | **Essential** | Urgency and burning can occur with GSM alone, with no infection present, so a urine culture helps clarify what you're actually treating [@nams-2020-gsm] |

See our [pelvic floor exercises guide](/care/pelvic-floor-exercises/) for how to do the training correctly — technique matters more than most people expect.
