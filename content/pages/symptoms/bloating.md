Feeling puffy, swollen, or uncomfortably full in the stomach is a widely reported complaint during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) — and also one of the harder symptoms to pin down scientifically. A 2009 literature review found that gastrointestinal symptoms, including **bloating, abdominal discomfort, and altered bowel patterns**, increase during the perimenopause period and around menstruation, in women both with and without irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009].

## What the evidence actually says — and doesn't

The same researchers propose that **declining or fluctuating ovarian hormone levels** (estrogen and progesterone) may contribute to this pattern, directly or indirectly [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009]. But it's worth being honest about where the evidence stands: the review itself is explicit that this is a **proposed mechanism**, not an established one, and it specifically calls for **confirmatory studies** to properly understand the role of ovarian hormones in GI symptoms [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009]. Framings like "estrogen dominance" that sometimes appear in popular menopause content go well beyond what this research actually shows, and aren't a recognized medical diagnosis.

## Other things worth considering

Bloating has many possible contributors beyond hormones, and most aren't specific to perimenopause:

- **Changes in diet, fiber intake, or physical activity**
- **Underlying or undiagnosed IBS**, which the research above suggests may flare more around hormonal shifts
- **Constipation**, which can itself cause a feeling of fullness and swelling
- **Food intolerances** that may become more noticeable with age
- **[Weight changes](/symptoms/weight-gain/)** happening around the same time, which can compound the sense of abdominal fullness

## When bloating needs a doctor's check

:::urgent
See a doctor for bloating that is **new, persistent, or worsening**, especially if it comes with **unexplained weight loss, loss of appetite, feeling full quickly, a change in bowel habit lasting several weeks, pelvic pain, or abnormal vaginal bleeding**. These combinations warrant evaluation rather than being assumed to be a normal part of perimenopause.
:::
