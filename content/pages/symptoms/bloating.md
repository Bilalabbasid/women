Bloating is common in perimenopause: research finds digestive symptoms such as bloating become more frequent then, and around periods [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009]. Eating smaller meals and cutting back on fizzy drinks can help [@nhs-bloating]. Bloating that lasts 2 weeks or more and isn't normal for you needs a doctor's check [@cdc-ovarian-cancer-symptoms].

## Why am I so bloated in perimenopause?

Shifting hormones may play a part, but the exact reason isn't known. A 2009 review found that digestive symptoms, including bloating, belly discomfort, and changes in bowel habits, increase during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and around periods, in women with and without irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009].

The researchers suggest that falling or changing levels of estrogen and progesterone may be one cause. They are clear that this is an idea that still needs testing, not an established fact [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009]. Claims about "estrogen dominance" in popular menopause content go well beyond what this research shows.

## What else causes bloating?

Most bloating comes from gas in the gut, from certain foods and drinks or from swallowing air [@nhs-bloating]. A doctor can check for other causes, such as [@nhs-bloating; @niddk-gas-symptoms-causes]:

- **Swallowed air**: Eating fast, chewing gum, fizzy drinks, and smoking add to it.
- **Hard-to-digest foods**: Gut bacteria make gas as they break down some sugars, starches, and fiber.
- **Constipation**: Being backed up can make you feel full and swollen.
- **Food intolerances**: Lactose (milk sugar) or fructose (a fruit sugar) can cause gas and bloating.
- **Digestive conditions**: IBS, celiac disease, and bacterial overgrowth in the small intestine are some examples.

Less often, ongoing bloating can be a sign of something more serious, such as ovarian cancer [@nhs-bloating; @niddk-gas-symptoms-causes]. That's covered below.

## How can I get rid of bloating?

Small changes to how and what you eat help many people. The NHS and the US National Institutes of Health suggest [@nhs-bloating; @niddk-gas-treatment]:

- **Eat smaller meals more often**: Big meals, especially late at night, make bloating worse.
- **Swallow less air**: Eat slowly, sit down to eat, chew with your mouth closed, and skip chewing gum and straws.
- **Cut back on fizzy drinks, alcohol, and caffeine**.
- **Notice gassy foods**: Beans, lentils, cabbage, broccoli, and sugar-free sweets with sweeteners ending in "-ol" can add gas.
- **Ease constipation**: Drink plenty of water and add soluble fiber, such as oats or linseed.
- **Keep moving**: Regular exercise helps, even on days you feel bloated.

:::try
1. Have a smaller dinner, and eat it slowly and sitting up [@nhs-bloating; @niddk-gas-treatment].
2. Swap fizzy drinks for water [@nhs-bloating].
3. Take a walk or do some gentle exercise [@nhs-bloating].
4. Gently massage your belly [@nhs-bloating].
5. Write down what you ate and where you are in your cycle when bloating strikes.
:::

Talk with your doctor before trying supplements, probiotics, or other remedies for gas and bloating [@niddk-gas-treatment]. Our [probiotics evidence review](/does-it-work/probiotics/) covers what studies after menopause have tested, mainly for bone health.

## Is bloating a sign of ovarian cancer?

Usually not, but ongoing bloating can be one of its signs, so it's worth knowing the pattern. The CDC lists these signs of ovarian cancer [@cdc-ovarian-cancer-symptoms]:

- Bloating
- Pain or pressure in the pelvis, or belly or back pain
- Feeling full too quickly, or trouble eating
- Needing to pee more often or urgently, or constipation
- Vaginal bleeding (especially after menopause) or discharge that isn't normal for you

If any of these last 2 weeks or longer and aren't normal for you, see a doctor. They are often caused by something else, but a doctor can find out [@cdc-ovarian-cancer-symptoms].

## When should I see a doctor about bloating?

See a doctor if you feel bloated regularly, or you've changed your diet and still keep feeling bloated [@nhs-bloating]. Also see one if gas or bloating changes suddenly or comes with belly pain, constipation, diarrhea, or weight loss [@niddk-gas-symptoms-causes].

:::urgent
**See a doctor soon** if bloating:

- Lasts 2 weeks or longer and isn't normal for you, especially with pelvic or belly pain, feeling full quickly, or needing to pee more often [@cdc-ovarian-cancer-symptoms]
- Comes with weight loss you can't explain or blood in your poop [@nhs-bloating]

**See a doctor right away** for vaginal bleeding that isn't normal for you, including any bleeding after menopause [@cdc-ovarian-cancer-symptoms]. Get same-day advice if bloating comes with vomiting, diarrhea or constipation, stomach pain, a fever, a lump in your belly, or you can't pee, poop, or pass gas [@nhs-bloating].

**Call 911** if your belly is swollen and you have sudden, severe stomach pain, you're vomiting blood, or you're struggling to breathe [@nhs-bloating].

**If you're in the UK:** call **999** or go to A&E for those emergency signs, and call **NHS 111** or ask for an urgent GP appointment for the same-day signs [@nhs-bloating].
:::
