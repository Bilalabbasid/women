Losing your train of thought mid-sentence, struggling to recall a familiar name, or walking into a room and forgetting why you are there is widely known as **menopause brain fog**. During [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and early [menopause](/menopause/), fluctuating estradiol interacts with chronic sleep disruption from [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) and [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/), making working memory and attention feel sluggish [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## What else a clinician should check

Before blaming every cognitive change on hormones, a clinician will typically check reversible contributors:

- **Sleep quality:** Untreated night sweats or obstructive sleep apnea
- **Blood panels:** Thyroid function (TSH), vitamin B12, folate, and iron/ferritin stores
- **Mood and medications:** [Menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/), low mood, or sedating medications (such as antihistamines or certain sleep aids)

## What helps — and what guidelines warn against

1. **Treat sleep disruption and vasomotor symptoms:** When night sweats and [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) are waking you repeatedly, treating them with [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy] or [Level I non-hormonal options](/treatments/nonhormonal/) (such as CBT [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]) helps restore restorative sleep.
2. **Do not take HRT solely to prevent dementia:** While systemic HRT has a favorable benefit-risk ratio for vasomotor symptoms under age 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset, The Menopause Society notes it is not indicated to prevent dementia, and starting more than 10 years after menopause or after age 60 increases absolute dementia risk [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
3. **Skip "brain booster" menopause supplements:** Over-the-counter botanical blends are not recommended by guidelines [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] — check our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/).
