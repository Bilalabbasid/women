Menopause brain fog is the trouble finding words, focusing or remembering that many women notice in perimenopause. Studies suggest the dip is small and tends to ease after menopause [@greendale-swan-cognition-2009; @maki-jaff-brain-fog-2024]. Sleeping better, including treating night sweats that wake you, is the most practical place to start [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## What does menopause brain fog feel like?

Brain fog feels like your thinking has slowed: you lose your train of thought, blank on a familiar name, or walk into a room and forget why. Problems with memory or concentration are a recognized symptom of [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/) [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. Researchers note that it affects more than memory, touching a wider range of thinking skills such as attention [@maki-jaff-brain-fog-2024].

## What causes brain fog in menopause?

Several things usually pile up at once:

- **Changing hormones**: in a large US study, women in late perimenopause stopped showing the usual improvement on repeated memory and speed tests, a change that age alone didn't explain [@greendale-swan-cognition-2009; @maki-jaff-brain-fog-2024].
- **Broken sleep**: lack of sleep can make you more forgetful than normal [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause]. In a sleep-lab study of 34 women in perimenopause, about 7 in 10 night-time hot flashes came with an awakening [@de-zambotti-hot-flashes-sleep-2014]. See [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) and [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/).
- **Mood and stress**: depression, anxiety and stress can all cause memory problems [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging]. See [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/).

## How long does menopause brain fog last?

For most women it seems to be temporary. The Study of Women's Health Across the Nation (SWAN) followed 2,362 women for 4 years: learning on repeated tests stalled during perimenopause and returned to premenopausal levels after menopause, which the authors read as a sign the problem may be time-limited [@greendale-swan-cognition-2009]. Studies can't yet predict how long it will last for any one person.

## Is it brain fog or early dementia?

Brain fog usually means slips that are annoying but don't stop you from managing your life. Average declines during the transition are small, and scores stay within normal limits for all but a very small number of women [@maki-jaff-brain-fog-2024]. The National Institute on Aging suggests talking with a doctor if you notice signs like these [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging]:

- asking the same questions over and over
- getting lost in places you know well
- having trouble following recipes or directions
- becoming more confused about time, people and places
- not taking care of yourself

Our page on [memory problems in menopause](/symptoms/memory-problems/) goes into more detail.

## What else can a doctor check?

Brain fog has many possible causes, so a doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- **Thyroid problems**: an underactive thyroid can cause difficulty concentrating, tiredness and low mood [@nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms].
- **Low vitamin B12**: low levels of some nutrients can cause memory problems [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging].
- **Medicine side effects, alcohol, or kidney or liver problems**: all are on the list of treatable causes [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging].
- **Sleep apnea**: breathing that stops and starts at night causes daytime sleepiness, which can lead to problems focusing [@nhlbi-sleep-apnea-symptoms].

These causes usually improve once they're treated [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging].

## Does HRT help brain fog?

Hormone therapy isn't a treatment for brain fog itself. The Menopause Society says hormone therapy is not recommended at any age to prevent or treat a decline in thinking or dementia, and three large trials found it had a neutral effect on thinking when used early after menopause [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Starting it more than 10 years after menopause or after 60 carries greater absolute risks, including dementia [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Where it can help is sleep. Hormone therapy reduces night-time waking in women with bothersome night sweats :grade{id="mht-sleep-vms"} [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/).

## What helps menopause brain fog?

These everyday habits are suggested by the National Institute on Aging for memory slips.

:::try
1. Keep a daily routine and use to-do lists, a calendar and notes [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging].
2. Put your keys, glasses and phone in the same place every time [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging].
3. Aim for enough sleep and keep a regular sleep schedule [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging; @nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
4. Get regular exercise and limit alcohol [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging].
:::

If night sweats are waking you, treating them is the next step:

- **Talking therapy (CBT)**: :grade{id="cbt-sleep-vms"}. Recommended by The Menopause Society for hot flashes, including when they disrupt sleep [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. See [non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/).
- **Non-hormone prescriptions**: :grade{id="nonhormonal-rx-sleep-vms"}. Options such as certain antidepressants, gabapentin and fezolinetant are recommended for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **"Brain booster" supplements**: The Menopause Society doesn't recommend supplements or herbal remedies for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy], and pills that promise to improve memory may be unsafe or a waste of money [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging]. See our [Does It Work? directory](/does-it-work/).

## When to see a doctor about brain fog

See a doctor if memory problems last more than a few weeks, keep getting worse, or make everyday things hard [@nia-memory-forgetfulness-aging]. Before the visit, tick your symptoms on the [printable symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/) and note when the fog is worst in the [private symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/).
