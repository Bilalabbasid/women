A persistent burning, scalded, or tingling sensation on the tongue, lips, or roof of the mouth — without anything visibly wrong when you look — is the hallmark of **burning mouth syndrome (BMS)**. It's an under-discussed symptom, but one with a real, studied link to the perimenopausal period.

## What the evidence actually says

A study of dental-clinic patients found BMS prevalence of **13.8%**, and identified a significant association between menopause and BMS, with an odds ratio of **4.45** — meaning menopausal status was associated with a substantially higher likelihood of BMS in this sample [@baharvand-burning-mouth-syndrome-2013]. BMS is considered a **diagnosis of exclusion**: it's defined by the burning sensation existing *without* any visible lesion or abnormality on oral examination, so a clinician first needs to rule out other explanations before BMS is the working diagnosis [@baharvand-burning-mouth-syndrome-2013].

## Other causes a dentist or doctor will check first

Because BMS has no single visible sign, ruling out these treatable causes usually comes first:

- **Dry mouth (xerostomia)**, which can itself cause a burning feeling and is common around menopause
- **Nutritional deficiencies**, particularly B vitamins, iron, or zinc
- **Oral candidiasis (thrush)** or other infections
- **Ill-fitting dentures or an allergic reaction** to a denture material or mouthwash
- **Acid reflux**, which can irritate the mouth and throat
- **Certain medications**, which can cause dry mouth or altered taste as a side effect

## What to do about it

Because the study we're citing establishes the association, not a tested treatment, we aren't making a sourced claim about specific remedies here. The reasonable first step is a dental or medical visit to rule out the causes above — many of which have straightforward fixes once identified. If those are ruled out and BMS is the working diagnosis, your clinician can discuss symptom-focused options with you directly.

This symptom often overlaps with other mucosal-dryness complaints at menopause, including [dry eyes](/symptoms/dry-eyes/), [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/), and [dry, itchy skin](/symptoms/dry-itchy-skin/) — worth mentioning together if you're seeing more than one.
