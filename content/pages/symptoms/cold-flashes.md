Yes, menopause can cause "cold flashes": some women feel chilled or start to shiver as a hot flash fades. Mayo Clinic lists feeling chilled as the hot flash lets up as a hot flash symptom and puts it down to the body losing heat [@mayo-hot-flashes-symptoms-2026], and the National Institute on Aging says heavy sweating and cold shivering can follow a hot flash [@nia-what-is-menopause]. Chills that come on their own, without a hot flash, have other common causes, such as an infection with a fever, an underactive thyroid or anemia, and are worth checking [@medlineplus-chills; @nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms; @nhlbi-anemia-symptoms].

**Cold flashes at a glance**

- **What it is**: Feeling chilled as a hot flash lets up, because the body has lost heat [@mayo-hot-flashes-symptoms-2026].
- **At night**: You may feel a wave of heat during a night sweat, then feel cold when the sweating stops [@cleveland-clinic-night-sweats-2026].
- **How common**: Nobody knows. In interviews with 20 US women with hot flashes, cold sweats and chills were among the key symptoms described, but no large study has counted how many women get them [@haberland-vms-experiences-qualitative-2025].
- **Other causes**: Infection and fever, an underactive thyroid, anemia and panic attacks can all cause chills [@medlineplus-chills; @nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms; @nhlbi-anemia-symptoms; @nhs-panic-disorder].
- **What helps**: No treatment has been studied for the chills themselves; if they follow hot flashes, hormone therapy is the most effective hot flash treatment [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **When it's urgent**: Shivering or feeling very cold with a fever, confusion or fast breathing can be a sign of sepsis, a medical emergency [@cdc-sepsis-about-2026; @nhs-sepsis-2026].

## Are cold flashes a sign of menopause?

They can be, when they come straight after a hot flash. "Cold flash" isn't a medical term, but the experience is described by major health bodies: Mayo Clinic lists "feeling chilled as the hot flash lets up" among the symptoms of a hot flash [@mayo-hot-flashes-symptoms-2026], and the National Institute on Aging says a hot flash can bring flushing, red blotches on the chest, back and arms, then heavy sweating and cold shivering [@nia-what-is-menopause].

So if your chills arrive a few minutes after a rush of heat, especially in your 40s or 50s and alongside changes to your periods, they are likely to be part of your [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/). Chills on their own are a different matter, and the research on them is thin (see below).

## Why do I feel cold after a hot flash?

Because a hot flash is your body getting rid of heat fast. During one, the face and neck may flush and you may sweat heavily, mostly on the upper body; once it passes, some people feel chilled because they have lost body heat [@mayo-hot-flashes-symptoms-2026; @nia-what-is-menopause].

There may be more to it. In a small laboratory study, women with hot flashes had almost no gap between the body temperature that set off sweating and the one that set off shivering (0.0°C, measured rectally), while women without hot flashes had a gap of 0.4°C. The researchers concluded that small rises in body temperature acting within this narrow zone may trigger hot flashes [@freedman-thermoneutral-zone-1999]. The study involved 20 women and did not measure chills after a hot flash, so it doesn't prove why cold flashes happen.

## Can you get chills instead of hot flashes?

Possibly, but very little research has looked at it. The best evidence comes from Japan:

- In a study of Japanese women aged 45 to 55, 29.3% reported chilliness, more than reported hot flushes (3.0% to 22.1%). How common it was differed between premenopausal women and women at later stages, and it went along with other symptoms linked to estrogen, which led the author to call it an important vasomotor (hot flash type) symptom in Japan [@melby-chilliness-japan-2007].
- But in a menopause clinic study of 475 Japanese women aged 40 to 65, severe chilliness (found in 28.4%) was not related to menopause stage. Anxiety was the only background factor linked with it [@terauchi-chilliness-anxiety-2020].

Both studies were in one country, took a single snapshot in time and can't show cause and effect. We found no studies in US or European women that measured chills without hot flashes. So if you feel cold or shiver but don't have hot flashes first, don't assume it's menopause; the causes in the next sections are worth ruling out.

## Why do I get chills at night?

Most often in midlife, because of night sweats. A night sweat is a hot flash that happens during sleep [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do], and you may feel a sudden wave of heat, especially in your neck, chest and upper body, then feel cold when the sweating stops [@cleveland-clinic-night-sweats-2026]. Our [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) guide covers how to sleep cooler.

Night chills need a closer look when they don't follow a sweat, or when they come with other signs of illness. The NHS advises seeing a doctor if night sweats come with a very high temperature or you feel hot, cold or shivery, a cough or diarrhea, or weight loss you can't explain [@nhs-night-sweats].

## What else can cause chills?

A doctor may want to check for these, especially if your chills don't follow hot flashes:

- **Infection and fever**: Chills are your muscles tightening and relaxing quickly to make heat, and they often come just before a fever. Any infection can cause them [@medlineplus-chills]. A kidney infection, for example, can cause chills and shaking, a fever above 101°F (38.3°C), and pain in the side, back or groin [@medlineplus-uti-adults]. Menopause raises the risk of urinary infections; see [recurrent UTIs](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/).
- **An underactive thyroid**: Feeling cold more than usual is a symptom, along with tiredness, weight gain, low mood, constipation, and irregular or heavy periods [@nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms; @medlineplus-hypothyroidism]. Many of these symptoms are common and have other causes, so it is worth asking a GP, who can check with a blood test [@nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms].
- **Anemia**: Chills are among the general symptoms of anemia, along with weakness, paleness, tiredness, shortness of breath and dizziness [@nhlbi-anemia-symptoms]. Heavy, long or frequent periods are a common cause of the blood loss behind iron deficiency anemia [@medlineplus-iron-deficiency-anemia]; see [heavy periods](/perimenopause/heavy-periods/).
- **Panic attacks and anxiety**: Chills, trembling and hot flushes are all listed panic attack symptoms, and most attacks last 5 to 20 minutes [@nhs-panic-disorder]. In the Japanese clinic study, anxiety was the factor most linked with severe chilliness [@terauchi-chilliness-anxiety-2020]. See [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/).
- **A cold room**: Being in a cold environment is the simplest cause of all [@medlineplus-chills].

## Cold flashes treatment: what helps

No treatment has been tested for menopause chills on their own, so we can't grade any. Here is what the evidence does support.

**If your chills follow hot flashes**, the aim is fewer hot flashes. Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for them: compared with placebo, it cut how often hot flashes happened by about 75% [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The Menopause Society also recommends several non-hormone prescriptions [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Our [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) guide grades each option. No study we found has measured whether treating hot flashes also reduces the chills that follow them.

**If your chills come with a fever**, that points to an illness rather than menopause. MedlinePlus advises against bundling up in blankets, or using fans or air conditioning, if you have a high temperature, because both can make the chills worse and may make the fever rise [@medlineplus-chills].

:::try
1. Dress in layers you can take off at the start of a hot flash [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do], and keep one nearby to put back on as it fades.
2. At night, layer your bedding so you can push a layer off or pull it back as needed [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do].
3. For 2 to 4 weeks, note when chills happen, whether a hot flash came first, and your temperature if you feel unwell, in the [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/). Take it to your appointment.
:::

## When to see a doctor about chills

Book an appointment if:

- you get chills without hot flashes, or they're new and you can't explain them
- you feel cold more than usual and are also tired, putting on weight or having heavier periods [@nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms; @medlineplus-iron-deficiency-anemia]
- night chills come with a high temperature, a cough, diarrhea or weight loss you can't explain [@nhs-night-sweats]
- hot flashes and the chills after them bother you or disturb your sleep, so you can talk about treatment [@nia-what-is-menopause]

Contact a doctor promptly if chills come with a bad cough, shortness of breath, belly pain, or burning or frequent peeing; if you have a stiff neck; or if a fever stays above 103°F (39.4°C) after 1 to 2 hours of home treatment or lasts more than 3 days without improving [@medlineplus-chills]. Back or side pain with chills, fever or vomiting can mean a kidney infection and needs a doctor right away [@medlineplus-uti-adults].

:::urgent
**Get emergency help now if shivering or chills come with any of these**: confusion, slurred speech or not making sense; very fast breathing or shortness of breath; blue, pale, grey or blotchy skin, lips or tongue; a rash that doesn't fade when you press it; a high heart rate or weak pulse; or extreme pain [@nhs-sepsis-2026; @cdc-sepsis-about-2026]. These can be signs of sepsis, a life-threatening emergency that needs fast treatment [@cdc-sepsis-about-2026].

In the US, call **911** or go to the emergency room. In the UK, call **999** or go to A&E [@nhs-sepsis-2026].
:::
