Yes, perimenopause is a time of higher risk for low mood and depression [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018]. If you've felt low or lost interest most days for two weeks or more, talk to a doctor [@nimh-depression-booklet]. Talking therapy and antidepressants are the first-line treatments [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018].

## Can perimenopause cause depression?

Perimenopause doesn't cause depression in everyone, but it is a time of higher risk for both depressive symptoms and full depression, according to guidelines endorsed by The Menopause Society [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018]. UK guidance also lists low mood among the main symptoms linked to menopause, alongside hot flashes and sleep problems [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

A 2026 review of 12 studies in about 9,900 women found that women with more [hot flashes and night sweats](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) were more likely to report depressive symptoms [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026]. The reviewers rated this evidence low certainty. Most studies were one-time snapshots, so they show the two often go together, not that one causes the other.

## What does menopause depression feel like?

The US National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) lists these common signs of depression [@nimh-depression-booklet]:

- Feeling sad, anxious, or "empty" much of the time
- Losing interest or pleasure in things you used to enjoy
- Feeling tired or low on energy
- Trouble concentrating, remembering, or making decisions
- Trouble sleeping, waking too early, or sleeping too much
- Thoughts of death or suicide

Doctors diagnose depression when symptoms last most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks [@nimh-depression-booklet]. Some menopause symptoms, such as poor sleep and trouble concentrating, overlap with these signs [@nhs-menopause-symptoms], which can make the two hard to tell apart [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018]. That's one reason a proper assessment helps.

## Depressive symptoms, or depression?

UK guideline NICE NG23 draws an important distinction [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **Depressive symptoms that don't meet the criteria for depression**: If they start around the same time as other menopause symptoms, HRT or menopause-specific CBT may help.
- **Depression that is suspected or already diagnosed**: This should be treated using both menopause guidance and the standard depression guideline. The two are meant to work together.

Only a clinician can tell which applies to you, so new or worsening low mood deserves an assessment rather than a guess.

## Who is more likely to get depressed in perimenopause?

Having had depression before is the biggest clue: most women who have a major depressive episode in perimenopause have had one earlier in life [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018]. In a long-running US study of midlife women (SWAN), these factors before the final period were linked to more depressive symptoms after menopause [@kravitz-swan-postmenopause-depression-2022]:

- Earlier low mood or depressive symptoms
- High anxiety and sleep problems
- Frequent hot flashes
- Childhood trauma and less social support
- Younger age and poorer physical functioning

## How long does menopause depression last?

Studies haven't pinned down a typical length, and low mood doesn't always lift once periods stop. In the SWAN study, women had about 1.5 times the odds of high depressive symptoms after menopause compared with before it [@kravitz-swan-postmenopause-depression-2022]. So it's worth getting help now rather than waiting for it to pass.

## Does HRT help with menopause depression?

HRT isn't approved to treat depression, but it may help some people in perimenopause. A Menopause Society guideline found evidence that estrogen can ease depression in perimenopausal women, especially those who also have hot flashes; evidence for estrogen combined with a progestogen is limited and unclear [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018]. NICE says HRT may be considered for depressive symptoms that start alongside other menopause symptoms [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. For depression itself, antidepressants and talking therapy remain the first-line treatments [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018].

## When to see a doctor

Book an appointment if low mood or loss of interest has lasted most of the day, nearly every day, for two weeks or more [@nimh-depression-booklet]. If worry or a short temper are part of the picture, our pages on [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/) and [irritability](/symptoms/irritability/) cover those too.

:::urgent
**If you are thinking about suicide or feel unable to stay safe, get help now.**

- **US:** Call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), free and confidential, 24/7 [@lifeline-988; @nimh-depression-booklet]. In a life-threatening situation, call **911** [@nimh-depression-booklet].

**If you're in the UK:** call **999** or go to A&E if a life is at risk, or call **NHS 111** and choose the mental health option for urgent advice [@nhs-urgent-help-mental-health]. Samaritans are on **116 123**, free, any time [@samaritans-contact].
:::
