Low mood that feels new, heavier, or harder to shake can appear during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/), sometimes alongside [anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/), [irritability](/symptoms/irritability/), or disrupted sleep. UK guideline NICE NG23 lists effects on mood — including depressive symptoms — among the core symptoms associated with menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

A 2026 systematic review pooling 12 observational studies in roughly 9,900 peri- and postmenopausal women found that women with heavier [hot flash and night sweat](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) burden had meaningfully higher odds of depressive symptoms — adjusted odds ratios ranged from 1.67 to 2.99 across the studies that reported them [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026]. The evidence was rated low certainty, and because most studies were cross-sectional snapshots, they cannot prove that vasomotor symptoms cause depression — only that the two tend to occur together, sometimes through shared pathways like poor sleep.

## Depressive symptoms, or depression?

NICE NG23 draws an important distinction [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **Depressive symptoms that don't meet the criteria for a diagnosis of depression**, with onset around the same time as other menopause symptoms, may respond to HRT or menopause-specific CBT.
- **Depression that is suspected or already diagnosed** should be managed using both menopause guidance and the standard depression-treatment guideline — the two are meant to work together, not instead of each other.

Only a clinician can tell which situation applies to you, so a new or worsening low mood deserves an assessment rather than a guess.

## What can help (graded by evidence)

| Option | Evidence grade | What guidelines say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Hormone therapy (HRT)](/treatments/hormone-therapy/)** | **Moderate** | NICE NG23 says HRT may be considered for depressive symptoms (not full depression) with onset alongside other menopause symptoms [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)** | **Strong** | Level I evidence from The Menopause Society (2023); also a NICE-recommended option, alone or alongside other treatments [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| **Standard depression care** (if diagnosed) | **Strong** | NICE recommends following its depression-in-adults guideline together with menopause-specific care [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| **Over-the-counter herbal remedies, cannabinoids** | **Not supported** | Not recommended by The Menopause Society (2023), Levels I–II [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |

See our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) before trying any supplement marketed for mood or "hormone balance."

## Getting help now

:::urgent
If you are having thoughts of harming yourself or feel unable to stay safe, you don't have to wait for an appointment:

- **US:** Call or text **988**, free and confidential, 24/7 [@lifeline-988]
- **UK and Ireland:** Call **Samaritans on 116 123**, free from any phone, 24/7 [@samaritans-contact]
:::
