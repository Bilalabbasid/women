Constipation, looser stools, reflux, or a flare-up of an existing digestive condition — many women notice some kind of shift in digestion around [perimenopause](/perimenopause/). It's a genuinely reported pattern, though the research explaining *why* is still an open question, and this symptom overlaps closely with [bloating](/symptoms/bloating/), which we cover in more depth separately.

## What the evidence actually says — and doesn't

A 2009 literature review examined gastrointestinal symptoms in women with and without irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) across the menstrual cycle and the perimenopause period. It found that GI symptoms — including bloating, abdominal discomfort, and altered bowel patterns — tend to increase during perimenopause in both groups [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009].

The same researchers propose that **declining or fluctuating ovarian hormones** (estrogen and progesterone) may contribute, directly or through their effects on gut motility and sensitivity [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009]. But they're explicit that this is a **proposed mechanism**, not a proven one — the review specifically calls for confirmatory studies to clarify the role ovarian hormones actually play [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009]. If you already have IBS, this research suggests perimenopause may be a time when flare-ups become more noticeable, rather than a time a new separate condition appears.

## Other things worth considering

Digestive changes in your 40s and 50s have plenty of possible contributors that have nothing to do with hormones directly:

- **Diet, fiber, and hydration changes**, which shift naturally with life changes around this age
- **Reduced physical activity**, which can slow transit time and contribute to constipation
- **Medications**, including some used for other menopause symptoms, pain, or mood
- **An underlying condition** such as IBS, celiac disease, or thyroid dysfunction, which can either start or become more noticeable in this decade
- **[Weight changes](/symptoms/weight-gain/)** happening around the same time

## When digestive changes need a doctor's check

:::urgent
See a doctor for digestive symptoms that are **new, persistent, or worsening over several weeks**, especially with **unexplained weight loss, blood in the stool, severe abdominal pain, or a significant change in bowel habit that's new for you**. This matters particularly from your late 40s onward, when routine colorectal screening also becomes relevant — don't assume new digestive symptoms are "just menopause" without ruling out other causes first.
:::

Because the research links this to a broader, individually variable hormonal and digestive pattern rather than one single cause, logging your symptoms against your cycle, diet, and stress in our [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/) can help your clinician see whether a hormonal pattern, an underlying condition, or something else is driving it.
