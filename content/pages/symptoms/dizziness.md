A spinning, lightheaded, or off-balance feeling is a symptom many women first notice during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/), often alongside [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) or [heart palpitations](/symptoms/heart-palpitations/). It's a real and researched association — but dizziness has many possible causes, and the evidence linking it specifically to hormone changes is still developing.

## What the evidence actually says

A 2014 study looking at peri- and postmenopausal women found that dizziness and related equilibrium complaints were commonly reported alongside vasomotor symptoms, and explored how closely hot flash severity tracked with dizziness and vertigo scores [@owada-suzuki-dizziness-menopause-2014]. A 2024 narrative review of vestibular symptoms during the menopause transition proposes a biological basis for this: estrogen and progesterone receptors exist in the inner ear and vestibular system, so hormone decline is a plausible contributor [@castillo-bustamante-vestibular-menopause-2024].

That same review is careful to flag an important complication: the dizziness reported around perimenopause overlaps heavily with **vestibular migraine**, which also becomes more common around this life stage and can be mistaken for a purely hormonal symptom [@castillo-bustamante-vestibular-menopause-2024]. That overlap means dizziness during perimenopause isn't a diagnosis in itself — it's a symptom that still needs sorting out.

## Other common causes to rule out

Dizziness in your 40s and 50s is frequently **not** hormonal at all. Causes worth asking your doctor about include:

- **Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)**, a common inner-ear cause of brief spinning with head movement
- **Vestibular migraine**, which can cause dizziness with or without a headache
- **Low blood pressure or dehydration**, especially if dizziness happens on standing
- **Medication side effects**, including some blood pressure or sleep medications
- **Inner-ear infections or Meniere's disease**
- **Anxiety**, which can produce a similar lightheaded sensation to vasomotor-linked dizziness

## When dizziness needs urgent care

:::urgent
Get emergency care immediately if dizziness comes with **chest pain, slurred speech, sudden weakness or numbness on one side, double vision, a severe sudden headache unlike any before, fainting, or a fall**. These are not menopause symptoms and need immediate evaluation.
:::

For dizziness without those red flags, still raise it with your doctor if it's new, frequent, or getting worse — vestibular causes like BPPV are often very treatable once correctly identified.

Logging when dizziness happens — with or without a hot flash, time of day, and whether it comes with a headache — in our [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/) can help your clinician tell a vasomotor pattern apart from a vestibular or migraine one.
