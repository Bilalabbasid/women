Gritty, scratchy, or persistently dry-feeling eyes are a common complaint after menopause, and there's real research behind why.

## What the evidence actually says

A case-control study of 44 postmenopausal women — 22 with **severe evaporative dry eye syndrome** and 22 without — measured serum sex hormone levels and several eye-surface markers. Oestradiol, oestrone, and total testosterone were all **significantly lower** in the dry eye group compared with the group without dry eye [@gagliano-dry-eye-hormones-2014]. Within the dry eye group specifically, lower levels of all three hormones correlated strongly with **higher tear film osmolarity** — a measure of how concentrated (and therefore less stable) the tear film is — with correlation coefficients of -0.70 (oestradiol), -0.88 (oestrone), and -0.81 (testosterone) [@gagliano-dry-eye-hormones-2014].

The likely mechanism: sex hormones help regulate the **meibomian glands**, tiny oil-producing glands along the eyelid margin that secrete the lipid layer preventing tears from evaporating too quickly. When those hormones drop, meibomian gland function can decline, making the "evaporative" type of dry eye — where tears evaporate too fast rather than being under-produced — more likely.

## What this study can and can't tell you

This was a **case-control study**: it compared two existing groups of women to find an association, not a trial that tested whether raising hormone levels fixes dry eyes. It's good evidence for *why* dry eye becomes more common after menopause, but it doesn't establish that hormone therapy is an effective *treatment* for dry eye on its own [@gagliano-dry-eye-hormones-2014].

## What has evidence behind it for dry eye itself

This source doesn't evaluate specific treatments, so we won't make a sourced claim about remedies here. Standard, widely used first steps for dry eye that an eye doctor can tailor to you include artificial tears, warm compresses to support meibomian gland function, and — for more persistent cases — prescription anti-inflammatory eye drops. Discuss which fits your situation with an optometrist or ophthalmologist, especially if symptoms are affecting your vision or daily comfort.

This often shows up alongside other mucosal-dryness symptoms, including [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) and [burning mouth](/symptoms/burning-mouth/), which share a similar hormonal backdrop.
