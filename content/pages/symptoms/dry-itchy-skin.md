Skin that feels drier, thinner, or itchier than it used to is a real physiological change of [menopause](/menopause/), not just a cosmetic inconvenience. A 2025 dermatology review explains that declining estrogen reduces **collagen production, elasticity, and moisture retention** in the skin, leading to dryness and, for some women, wrinkling [@viscomi-menopausal-skin-hrt-2025].

## Does HRT fix it?

This is a common question, and the honest answer is: **not reliably enough to be a recommended reason on its own.** The same review found that most studies report a positive association between hormone therapy and improved menopausal skin quality, but the findings are **inconsistent**, and current clinical guidelines **do not support using HRT solely for estrogen-deficient skin**, because robust, skin-specific clinical trials are still lacking [@viscomi-menopausal-skin-hrt-2025]. If you're already considering [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) for hot flashes or other symptoms, improved skin quality may follow as a side benefit — it's just not established evidence to start HRT for skin alone.

Some women describe a crawling, tingling, or prickling sensation on the skin, sometimes called **formication**, alongside or instead of simple dryness. This is reported anecdotally around the menopause transition, though the evidence specific to this exact sensation is thinner than for dryness itself — it's reasonable to mention to your clinician alongside your other skin symptoms rather than assume it's unrelated.

## What else could be going on

Dry, itchy skin has plenty of non-hormonal contributors worth ruling out or addressing alongside any hormonal piece:

- **Over-washing or hot showers**, which strip natural oils
- **Harsh soaps or fragranced products**
- **Dehydration and low humidity**, especially in winter
- **Underlying skin conditions** such as eczema, or systemic causes like thyroid disease or kidney or liver conditions, if itching is severe, widespread, or doesn't respond to basic skin care

## Practical steps with good general support

- **Fragrance-free moisturizer, applied right after bathing**, while skin is still slightly damp
- **Shorter, lukewarm showers** instead of long hot ones
- **A humidifier** in dry indoor air
- **Gentle, fragrance-free cleansers** instead of harsh soaps

## When to see a doctor rather than just moisturizing

:::urgent
See a doctor if itching lasts more than a few weeks, affects your whole body, or comes with other symptoms (unexplained weight change, extreme fatigue, yellowing skin) — these can point to a cause beyond menopause that needs its own evaluation.
:::
