Menopause can cause dry, itchy skin: as estrogen falls, skin makes less collagen and holds less moisture, so it dries out [@viscomi-menopausal-skin-hrt-2025], and dry skin is a common cause of itching [@medlineplus-itching]. A mild cleanser and moisturizer put on right after bathing are the first steps dermatologists suggest [@aad-skin-care-menopause].

## Can menopause cause itching?

Yes. Lower estrogen reduces collagen, elasticity and moisture in the skin, leading to dryness [@viscomi-menopausal-skin-hrt-2025], and dry skin is one of the most common reasons for itching [@medlineplus-itching]. In menopause, skin loses some of its ability to hold water, which is most noticeable in dry air [@aad-skin-care-menopause].

Skin also gets more sensitive. Around age 50 the skin's pH changes, so rashes and irritation become more likely, and existing eczema or rosacea may flare [@aad-skin-care-menopause]. Aging skin is thinner and makes less natural oil, which adds to dryness [@medlineplus-dry-skin].

If the itching is around the vagina or vulva, see our [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) page.

## What is the crawling feeling on my skin?

A crawling or prickling feeling on the skin is called formication, and it has been linked with menopause. In a survey of 2,100 nurses aged 40 to 55 in China, being further through menopause was linked with formication, along with other symptoms like hot flashes [@liu-menopausal-symptoms-nurses-2015].

That is one survey, so the evidence is thinner than for dryness. It's worth mentioning to a clinician with your other symptoms; our [tingling](/symptoms/tingling/) page covers it in more detail.

## What helps menopause itching?

Simple daily skin care is the first step, and it's backed by US dermatology and health guidance:

:::try
1. Wash with a mild, fragrance-free cleanser instead of soap, and skip deodorant bars [@aad-skin-care-menopause; @medlineplus-dry-skin].
2. Keep baths and showers short and lukewarm, and pat your skin dry rather than rubbing [@medlineplus-dry-skin; @medlineplus-itching].
3. Put on a fragrance-free moisturizer right after bathing and whenever skin feels dry [@aad-skin-care-menopause].
4. Try a cold compress on itchy spots instead of scratching, and wear cool, loose clothes rather than wool [@medlineplus-itching].
:::

- **Moisturizer ingredients**: creams with hyaluronic acid or glycerin [@aad-skin-care-menopause], or with urea and lactic acid [@medlineplus-dry-skin], can help dry skin.
- **Over-the-counter relief**: hydrocortisone cream or an oral antihistamine may ease itching for a while; antihistamines can cause drowsiness [@medlineplus-itching].
- **Dry air**: itching tends to be worse in cold, dry winter air and with indoor heating or cooling [@medlineplus-dry-skin].

## Does HRT help itchy skin?

Possibly, but it isn't a recommended reason to start HRT on its own. A 2025 review found that most studies link hormone therapy with better skin quality, but the results are mixed, and clinical guidelines do not support using HRT just for skin because strong skin-specific trials are missing [@viscomi-menopausal-skin-hrt-2025].

If you're already considering [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) for hot flashes or other symptoms, your skin may improve as a side benefit.

## Do collagen supplements help menopause skin?

The evidence is limited and not specific to menopause. A review of 26 trials in adults in general found collagen supplements improved skin moisture and elasticity compared with a placebo, but the trials had several weaknesses and the authors called for larger studies [@pu-collagen-skin-aging-metaanalysis-2023]. Itching itself wasn't the focus. See our [collagen evidence review](/does-it-work/collagen/).

## What else can cause dry, itchy skin?

Not all itching is menopause. A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- **Skin habits and weather**: long or frequent baths, some soaps and detergents, and dry winter or indoor air [@medlineplus-dry-skin].
- **Skin conditions**: such as eczema or psoriasis [@medlineplus-dry-skin].
- **Thyroid problems**: an underactive thyroid can cause dry skin, along with tiredness, feeling cold and weight gain [@medlineplus-hypothyroidism].
- **Other health conditions**: diabetes can cause dry skin [@medlineplus-dry-skin], and liver disease with yellowing skin (jaundice) or kidney disease can cause itching all over [@medlineplus-itching].
- **Medicines**: some creams and tablets can dry the skin [@medlineplus-dry-skin].

## Does menopause itching go away?

We found no studies that tracked how long menopause itching lasts. Because it is tied to drier, thinner skin that comes with lower estrogen and aging [@viscomi-menopausal-skin-hrt-2025; @medlineplus-dry-skin], it may not simply fade, but the skin care steps above can help keep it under control.

## When should I see a doctor about itching?

See a doctor if your itching:

- is severe, doesn't go away, or has no clear cause [@medlineplus-itching]
- covers your whole body, or keeps coming back [@medlineplus-itching]
- comes with other symptoms you can't explain [@medlineplus-itching]
- comes with a rash that lasts or a skin condition that's getting worse (a board-certified dermatologist can help) [@aad-skin-care-menopause]
