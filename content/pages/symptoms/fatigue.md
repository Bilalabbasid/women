Feeling drained no matter how much you sleep is one of the most common, and most dismissed, complaints of [perimenopause](/perimenopause/). A 2026 clinical review describes perimenopause as a transition marked by fluctuating hormone levels that produce overlapping symptom clusters — fatigue, sleep disturbance, and psychological or cognitive concerns frequently occur together rather than in isolation [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026].

One reason fatigue and mood symptoms tend to travel together: a 2026 systematic review of 12 observational studies found that the link between [hot flash and night sweat](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) burden and mood symptoms was, in several studies, partly explained by fatigue or poor sleep rather than hormones acting on mood directly [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026]. In other words, night sweats can fragment sleep without fully waking you, leaving you exhausted the next day without an obvious cause.

## Other causes worth ruling out

Fatigue is a nonspecific symptom, and perimenopause is rarely the only explanation. Before assuming it's hormonal, ask your clinician about:

- **Thyroid disease** (both underactive and overactive thyroid cause fatigue)
- **Iron-deficiency anemia**, especially if you've had [heavy perimenopausal periods](/perimenopause/)
- **Sleep apnea**, which becomes more common around menopause and is often missed
- **Vitamin D or B12 deficiency**
- **Depression**, which can present mainly as low energy rather than low mood — see [menopause depression](/symptoms/depression/)
