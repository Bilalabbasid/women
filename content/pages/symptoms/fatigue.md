Menopause fatigue is often hormonal, but mostly in an indirect way: changing hormones bring night sweats, broken sleep and mood changes, and these tend to travel with tiredness [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026]. Improving sleep is the best first step, and hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for night sweats [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Is menopause fatigue hormonal?

Partly, yes, though usually through sleep rather than as a direct effect. In perimenopause, rising and falling hormone levels bring overlapping symptoms, and fatigue often shows up together with poor sleep and mood or memory changes [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026]. Long-term studies link sleep problems with changes in the hormones FSH and estradiol, beyond the effect of getting older [@baker-sleep-menopausal-transition-review-2018].

Many hot flashes at night are linked with waking, and women often under-report them, because brief wake-ups are easy to forget [@baker-sleep-menopausal-transition-review-2018]. So [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) can leave you exhausted without an obvious cause.

Tiredness may also connect hot flashes and mood. A 2026 review of 12 observational studies found that, in several of them, the link between hot flashes and low mood was partly explained by fatigue or poor sleep [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026].

## What else could be making me so tired?

Fatigue has many possible causes, and menopause is rarely the only one. A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- **Thyroid problems**: both an underactive and an overactive thyroid can cause fatigue [@medlineplus-fatigue]. An underactive thyroid can also bring heavier, irregular periods, weight gain, feeling cold and dry skin, which are easy to mistake for perimenopause [@medlineplus-hypothyroidism].
- **Low iron or anemia**: heavy, long or frequent periods are a common cause, and early signs include feeling tired or weak and trouble concentrating [@medlineplus-iron-deficiency-anemia]. Low iron can cause fatigue even before it becomes anemia [@medlineplus-fatigue].
- **Sleep apnea**: breathing that stops and starts during sleep becomes more common after menopause [@young-menopause-sleep-disordered-breathing-2003]. In women it often shows up as fatigue, headaches or insomnia, while loud snoring is more typical in men [@nhlbi-sleep-apnea-symptoms].
- **Depression**: fatigue is one of its signs, alongside low mood, loss of interest and sleep changes [@nimh-depression-booklet]. See [menopause depression](/symptoms/depression/).
- **Medicines and alcohol**: some sedatives, antihistamines, blood pressure medicines, steroids and water pills can cause tiredness, as can alcohol [@medlineplus-fatigue].
- **Other health conditions**: such as diabetes, heart failure, liver or kidney disease, and infections [@medlineplus-fatigue].

## How long does menopause fatigue last?

There is no clear answer, because we found no studies that tracked how long fatigue itself lasts around menopause. Since it so often follows broken sleep from night sweats, it may ease as those ease. In a large US study, frequent hot flashes and night sweats lasted a median of 7.4 years in total [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015].

That is an average, not a forecast. If fatigue goes on for months or affects daily life, it's worth checking rather than waiting it out.

## What helps menopause fatigue?

The best-supported approach is to treat what is breaking up your sleep and to rule out other causes [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026].

:::try
1. Go to bed and get up at the same times each day, and skip late-afternoon or evening naps [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
2. Avoid caffeine late in the day, and keep in mind that even small amounts of alcohol make it harder to stay asleep [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
3. Keep the bedroom quiet and at a comfortable temperature, with no TV, computer or phone in bed [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
4. Get regular exercise, but not close to bedtime [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
:::

Caffeine and other stimulants are not a treatment for fatigue itself [@medlineplus-fatigue]. More sleep tips are on our [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) page.

## Does HRT help with menopause fatigue?

It can help fatigue that comes from night sweats, because hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for them [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. We did not find trials that tested HRT for tiredness on its own, so it isn't a proven fatigue treatment if your sleep isn't being broken by night sweats.

Whether HRT suits you depends on your health history; see [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/).

## When to see a doctor about fatigue

See a doctor if tiredness is affecting your work, relationships or daily life, so they can look at both menopause and other causes together [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026]. Ask about a sleep study if you snore, gasp at night or wake unrefreshed [@nhlbi-sleep-apnea-symptoms].

:::urgent
Contact a doctor right away if tiredness comes with confusion or dizziness, blurred vision, little or no urine, or new swelling and weight gain [@medlineplus-fatigue]. If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline); in a life-threatening emergency, call 911 [@nimh-depression-booklet].
:::
