A gradual thinning over the crown and front of the scalp — while the hairline itself stays put — is one of the more under-discussed changes of [menopause](/menopause/). A 2022 dermatology review found a measurable reduction in the proportion of growth-phase (anagen) hairs in postmenopausal women, most noticeable over the frontal scalp rather than the back of the head [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022].

## What this usually is: female pattern hair loss

The most common pattern — **female pattern hair loss (FPHL)**, also called androgenetic alopecia — presents as thinning over the **frontovertical scalp with the frontal hairline itself preserved** [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022]. It commonly presents around menopause, though it can start earlier. Importantly, the review notes that the **vast majority of women with FPHL have normal androgen (testosterone) levels** — reduced estrogen is thought to be the more relevant factor, not excess male hormones [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022].

## When testing is actually needed

Unless hair loss is **severe or sudden in onset**, blood tests usually aren't required to diagnose FPHL [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022]. Testing for testosterone and prolactin becomes relevant specifically if you also have other signs of excess androgens — such as new facial hair growth, severe acne, or irregular periods — which would point toward a different underlying cause worth investigating [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022]. The review also lists chronic telogen effluvium (widespread shedding, often tied to a triggering event) and diffuse alopecia areata as other conditions to rule out if the pattern doesn't fit typical FPHL.

## What has evidence behind it — and what doesn't

| **Hormone therapy (HRT)** | :grade{id="mht-hair"} | Insufficient evidence to support a role specifically for female pattern hair loss [@kamp-menopause-hair-disorders-2022] |
| **"Hair growth" or collagen supplements** | :grade{id="hair-supplements"} | Not addressed by this evidence base as an effective treatment; check any specific product in our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) |

Hair-care habits — flat irons, frequent coloring, tight styles — can also make existing thinning more visible by weakening the hair shaft itself, independent of the underlying hormonal pattern.
