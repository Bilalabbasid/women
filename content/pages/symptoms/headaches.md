Headaches often change in perimenopause. The best-studied link is with migraine, which can become more frequent and less predictable as estrogen and progesterone rise and fall unevenly [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]. Keeping a headache diary is a useful first step, and a sudden, severe or new kind of headache needs prompt medical care [@medlineplus-headache].

## Can menopause cause headaches?

Yes, changing hormones can bring on headaches or change their pattern, though the research mostly covers migraine. Migraine affects women about three times as often as men, and shifting estrogen levels help explain this; during perimenopause, unstable hormone levels can make attacks more frequent [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].

Research focused on women in [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and after menopause is still limited [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]. Not every new headache is hormonal, so it helps to look at other causes too [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026].

## Is it a headache or a migraine?

The two feel different, and the difference matters for treatment and for hormone therapy decisions:

- **Tension headache**: the most common type, usually on both sides of the head, with dull or squeezing pain and often a tight or sore neck, shoulders or jaw [@medlineplus-headache].
- **[Migraine](/symptoms/migraines/)**: throbbing or pounding pain, usually on one side, that gets worse when you move. It often comes with nausea, vision changes, or sensitivity to light or sound, and some people get warning signs first, called an aura [@medlineplus-headache].

If you're not sure which you have, that's a fair question to bring to a clinician.

## Do headaches go away after menopause?

For many women with migraine they improve, but not for everyone. Large population studies show migraine tends to get better after menopause, with a possible rise during perimenopause first; improvement is more likely after natural menopause than after surgery to remove the ovaries [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015].

Migraine without aura often improves after menopause, while migraine with aura tends to continue [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]. Studies of people seen at headache clinics found no improvement, or even worsening, so a lasting pattern is worth getting checked [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015].

## Can poor sleep cause headaches?

It can play a part. Lack of sleep is a known migraine trigger [@medlineplus-headache], and night sweats often break up sleep around menopause [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause]. Sleep apnea, where breathing stops and starts at night, can cause headaches too, and in women it more often shows up as headaches, fatigue or insomnia than as loud snoring [@nhlbi-sleep-apnea-symptoms].

See [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) for ways to sleep better.

## What helps menopause headaches?

Start by finding your pattern, then treat what feeds it. A 2026 review recommends looking at how much a symptom bothers you, how it affects daily life, and what else could cause it before choosing a treatment [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026].

:::try
1. Keep a headache diary: when it started, what you ate and drank, how you slept, what you were doing and how long it lasted [@medlineplus-headache].
2. At the first sign of a migraine, drink water, rest in a quiet, dark room and put a cool cloth on your head [@medlineplus-headache].
3. Keep regular sleep and wake times, and avoid caffeine late in the day [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
4. If you need pain medicine 3 or more days a week, talk to a clinician, since frequent use can cause rebound headaches [@medlineplus-headache].
:::

- **Over-the-counter pain relief**: US health guidance lists acetaminophen, aspirin or ibuprofen for tension headaches [@medlineplus-headache].
- **Treating night sweats and poor sleep**: this may help when broken sleep is a trigger [@medlineplus-headache].
- **Migraine treatment**: standard migraine medicines remain central during the menopause transition; see [menopause migraines](/symptoms/migraines/) [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].
- **Skipping unproven "hormone balance" supplements**: check any product in our [Does It Work? directory](/does-it-work/) first.

## Is it safe to take HRT if I get headaches?

It can be, but the type of hormone therapy matters, so discuss your headache history with a clinician first. A 2026 review found that estrogen taken as a pill, especially at higher doses, may make migraine worse, while low-dose estrogen through the skin (patch or gel) appears safer and better tolerated [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].

In observational studies pulled together in a 2015 review, hormone therapy after menopause was linked with migraine getting worse [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015]. If you get migraine with aura, there are added stroke-risk points to discuss; see [migraines and HRT](/symptoms/migraines/) and [patch vs. pill](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/).

## When to see a doctor about a headache

See a clinician if your headaches are new, changing or affecting daily life, and before starting or changing hormone therapy. New headaches after age 50 are also worth checking [@medlineplus-headache].

:::urgent
Call 911 or go to the emergency room for a headache that comes on suddenly and is explosive or violent, or is the worst you have ever had [@medlineplus-headache]. Get emergency help for a headache with sudden weakness or numbness on one side, slurred speech, confusion, vision changes or loss of balance, as these can be signs of a stroke [@cdc-stroke-signs-2026]. Also get urgent care for a headache with fever, stiff neck or vomiting, one that follows a head injury, or one that keeps getting worse over 24 hours [@medlineplus-headache].
:::
