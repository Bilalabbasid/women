Headaches often change in perimenopause. The best-studied link is with migraine, which can become more frequent and less predictable as estrogen and progesterone rise and fall unevenly [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]. Tracking when your headaches happen is a useful first step, and new, severe, or changing headaches are worth checking with a clinician.

Migraine affects women more often than men, and a 2026 review of migraine across the menopause transition found that changing estrogen levels help explain this [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]. The same review notes that research focused on women in [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and after menopause is still limited [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].

## Headache or migraine?

Not every headache is a migraine, and the distinction matters for safety and treatment:

- **Tension-type and other everyday headaches**: usually a dull, tight-band feeling, without nausea, vision changes, or strong sensitivity to light.
- **[Migraines](/symptoms/migraines/)**: a separate condition of the brain and nerves, often one-sided and throbbing, sometimes with an "aura" first (vision changes, tingling, or other odd sensations), and often with nausea or sensitivity to light and sound. Migraine with aura carries specific safety considerations around hormone therapy — see our [menopause migraines](/symptoms/migraines/) page.

If you're not sure which you have, that's a reasonable question to bring to a clinician rather than guess at.

:::urgent
Get urgent medical care for a headache that is the "worst of your life," comes on suddenly and severely, follows a head injury, or comes with fever, stiff neck, confusion, weakness on one side, slurred speech, or vision loss. These are not typical perimenopausal headaches and need immediate evaluation.
:::

## What can help

A 2026 review of non-hormonal care in perimenopause recommends looking at how much a symptom bothers you, how it affects daily life, and what else could be causing it before choosing a treatment, rather than assuming every new headache is hormonal [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026]. In practice, that means:

- **Tracking your pattern**: noting timing against your cycle, sleep, caffeine, and stress helps tell a hormonal pattern apart from other triggers.
- **Treating poor sleep and night sweats**: these can trigger or worsen headaches; see [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/).
- **Checking in with a clinician**: do this before starting or changing hormone therapy if headaches are new, changing, or severe, because the right approach differs for migraine and other headaches. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/).
- **Skipping unproven "hormone balance" supplements**: check any specific product in our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) first.
