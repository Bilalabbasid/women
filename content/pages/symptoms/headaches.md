Many women notice their headache pattern shifting during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) — more frequent, less predictable, or simply different from the headaches they're used to. A 2026 clinical review on headache disorders across the menopausal transition notes that unstable estradiol and progesterone levels during perimenopause can worsen headache frequency and predictability, and that headache disorders disproportionately affect women in part because of this hormonal link [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].

## Headache or migraine?

Not every headache is a migraine, and the distinction matters for safety and treatment:

- **Tension-type and other everyday headaches** are typically dull, band-like, and not accompanied by nausea, visual disturbance, or severe light sensitivity.
- **[Migraines](/symptoms/migraines/)** are a distinct neurological condition, often one-sided and throbbing, sometimes preceded by an "aura" (visual changes, tingling, or other sensory symptoms), and often accompanied by nausea or sensitivity to light and sound. Migraine with aura carries specific safety considerations around hormone therapy — see our [menopause migraines](/symptoms/migraines/) page.

If you're not sure which you have, that's a reasonable question to bring to a clinician rather than guess at.

:::urgent
Get urgent medical care for a headache that is the "worst of your life," comes on suddenly and severely, follows a head injury, or comes with fever, stiff neck, confusion, weakness on one side, slurred speech, or vision loss. These are not typical perimenopausal headaches and need immediate evaluation.
:::

## What can help

A 2026 review on non-hormonal perimenopause management recommends a structured assessment of symptom burden, functional impact, and alternative causes before choosing a treatment path, rather than assuming every new headache is hormonal [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026]. In practice, that means:

- **Tracking your pattern** — timing relative to your cycle, sleep, caffeine, and stress — helps distinguish a hormonal pattern from other triggers.
- **Treating poor sleep and night sweats**, which can themselves trigger or worsen headaches; see [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/).
- **Checking in with a clinician** before starting or changing hormone therapy if headaches are new, changing, or severe — the right approach differs for migraine versus non-migraine headache. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/).
- **Skipping unproven "hormone balance" supplements** for headache relief — check any specific product in our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) first.
