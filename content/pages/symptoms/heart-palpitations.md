Heart palpitations (a heartbeat that feels racing, pounding, fluttering, or irregular) can be a sign of menopause and are usually harmless [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. They can also come from other causes a doctor can check. Get emergency help if they don't stop or come with chest pain, breathlessness, or fainting.

:::urgent
**When to get emergency help:** Call **911** or go to the emergency room right away if heart palpitations **do not go away**, or if they come with **chest pain, shortness of breath, or feeling faint or fainting** [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. Don't drive yourself. If those symptoms happened but have now stopped, ask your doctor's office for an **urgent appointment today** [@nhs-heart-palpitations].

If you're in the UK: call 999 or go to A&E for the symptoms above. If they have stopped, ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment or call NHS 111 [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
:::

## Why palpitations can happen in perimenopause

During [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/), many women notice brief episodes of a pounding, fluttering, or racing heartbeat, sometimes around a [hot flash](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) or [night sweat](/symptoms/night-sweats/), or alongside [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/). The NHS lists menopause among the possible causes of palpitations, and NICE lists hot flashes and sweats among the core symptoms of menopause [@nhs-heart-palpitations; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

It's still best not to assume palpitations are "just hormones," because the NHS lists several other common causes [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- A heart rhythm problem (arrhythmia) or another heart condition, such as heart valve disease
- An overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism)
- Iron-deficiency anemia, especially if you have had heavy periods
- Stress, lack of sleep, hard exercise, caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, or a medicine you take

Over-the-counter [menopause supplements](/does-it-work/) shouldn't be used to self-treat heart palpitations.

## When to see a doctor

If you've had palpitations and feel well, see a doctor if they keep coming back or are happening more often, last longer than a few minutes, or you have a heart condition or a family history of heart problems [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. A doctor may suggest an ECG (a quick, painless test of your heart's electrical signals) to look for the cause [@nhs-heart-palpitations].

## What to record for your doctor

Log when your palpitations happen in our [private symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/), noting how long they last, whether they happen at the same time as hot flashes, and whether caffeine or poor sleep came first.
