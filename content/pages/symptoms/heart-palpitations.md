:::urgent
**When to get emergency help:** Call **911** (US) or **999** (UK) or go to the emergency room immediately if heart palpitations **do not go away**, or if they come with **chest pain, shortness of breath, or feeling faint or fainting** [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. If those symptoms happened but have now stopped, ask your doctor's office for an **urgent appointment today** (or call 111 in the UK) [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
:::

## Why palpitations can happen in perimenopause — and why they always need a medical check

During [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/), many women notice brief episodes of a pounding, fluttering, or racing heartbeat — often right as a [hot flash](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) or [night sweat](/symptoms/night-sweats/) begins, or alongside a wave of [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/) [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

However, **you should never assume heart palpitations are "just hormones" without a medical assessment** [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. Palpitations can also be caused by:

- Heart rhythm conditions (arrhythmias such as atrial fibrillation or supraventricular tachycardia)
- An overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism)
- Iron-deficiency anemia (especially if you have had heavy perimenopausal periods)
- Dehydration, caffeine, alcohol, fever, or medication side effects

Over-the-counter [menopause supplements](/does-it-work/) should never be used to self-treat heart palpitations.

## What to record for your doctor

Once emergency symptoms are ruled out, log when your palpitations happen in our [private symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) — noting how long they last, whether they happen at the same time as hot flashes, and whether caffeine or poor sleep preceded them.
