A hot flash is a sudden feeling of heat in your face, neck and upper body, often with flushing and sweating, and most last from 30 seconds to 10 minutes [@nia-what-is-menopause]. Dressing in layers and carrying a small fan can make them easier to ride out [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do]. Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment, and The Menopause Society also recommends several non-hormone prescriptions [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

Hot flashes and night sweats are the most common menopause symptoms, affecting up to 80% of women. They last 7 to 9 years on average, and more than 10 years for about one in three women [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Is this normal, and could it be perimenopause?

Yes. UK guidance says that for otherwise healthy women aged 45 or over, recent hot flashes together with changes in your periods are usually enough to recognize perimenopause, without blood tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Our [symptoms list](/symptoms/) covers the other common changes.

Here is what happens. As estrogen falls, a group of brain cells next to your brain's temperature-control center becomes overactive (doctors call them KNDy neurons), and this upsets temperature control [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Even a small rise in your core temperature can then set off a hot flash [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Your body tries to cool down fast: your face and neck may flush, red blotches can appear on your chest, back and arms, and heavy sweating and then shivering can follow [@nia-what-is-menopause].

Hot flashes can come several times an hour, a few times a day, or only once or twice a week [@nia-what-is-menopause]. In a large US study, women whose frequent hot flashes started while they still had regular periods, or early in perimenopause, tended to have them for the longest time [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015].

## What else could cause hot flashes?

Menopause is a common cause, but flushing and sweating have other causes too. A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- **A fever or infection**: a high temperature can cause flushing and sweating [@medlineplus-flushing; @medlineplus-sweating].
- **Medicines**: tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, opioids, tricyclic antidepressants and steroids can cause hot flashes [@medlineplus-cancer-hot-flashes-night-sweats]. Some diabetes and cholesterol medicines can cause flushing [@medlineplus-flushing].
- **An overactive thyroid**: sweating along with a fast, pounding heartbeat or weight loss can be a sign of it [@medlineplus-sweating].
- **Rosacea**: this common skin condition causes facial flushing [@medlineplus-flushing].
- **Carcinoid syndrome**: a rare cause of flushing, which is one reason flushing that keeps happening, especially with diarrhea, should be checked [@medlineplus-flushing].
- **Alcohol, hot drinks and spicy food**: these can also bring on flushing [@medlineplus-flushing].

## What actually helps: evidence-graded treatments

Each option below shows its evidence grade. Non-hormone prescriptions usually start to work within 2 weeks [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy], while lower doses of hormone therapy may take 6 to 8 weeks to give full relief [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

- **Hormone therapy (HRT)**: :grade{id="mht-vms"}. The most effective treatment. Compared with placebo, estrogen (alone or with a progestogen) cut how often hot flashes happened each week by about 75% and made them less severe [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The Menopause Society says it should be considered for women under 60 or within 10 years of their last period who have no reason to avoid it [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. If you still have a uterus, a progestogen is added to protect its lining [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [is hormone therapy right for me?](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/)
- **NK3 receptor blockers (fezolinetant, elinzanetant)**: :grade{id="nk3-vms"}. Non-hormone pills that act directly on the brain's temperature control [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. [Fezolinetant (Veozah)](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/) has a boxed warning for liver injury, so liver blood tests are needed before and during treatment [@veozah-prescribing-information]. [Elinzanetant (Lynkuet)](/treatments/nonhormonal/elinzanetant/) was approved in the US in October 2025 [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]; its label warns about daytime sleepiness, requires liver tests, and says it must not be used in pregnancy [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **Low-dose SSRIs and SNRIs**: :grade{id="ssri-snri-vms"}. Paroxetine, escitalopram, citalopram, venlafaxine and desvenlafaxine reduced hot flashes in large trials, and a low-dose form of paroxetine is FDA-approved for them [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. If you take tamoxifen, paroxetine and fluoxetine can interfere with how your body activates it; the Menopause Society names venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, escitalopram and citalopram as safer choices [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. See [SSRIs and SNRIs](/treatments/nonhormonal/ssris-snris/).
- **Gabapentin**: :grade{id="gabapentin-vms"}. Reduces how often and how strongly hot flashes come. Dizziness and drowsiness are common at first and usually ease within a few weeks [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. See [gabapentin](/treatments/nonhormonal/gabapentin/).
- **Oxybutynin**: :grade{id="oxybutynin-vms"}. A bladder medicine that improved moderate to severe hot flashes in trials. Dry mouth and trouble passing urine are common, and long-term use of this type of medicine may be linked with a decline in thinking and memory, especially in older people [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **CBT and clinical hypnosis**: :grade{id="cbt-hypnosis-vms"}. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) makes hot flashes less bothersome and less disruptive; clinical hypnosis reduces how often they happen [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. See [CBT and hypnosis](/treatments/nonhormonal/cbt-and-hypnosis/).
- **Layers, fans and cooling**: :grade{id="cooling-comfort"}. These can make a hot flash easier to get through, but there is no strong evidence that they reduce how many you have [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Supplements and herbal remedies**: :grade{id="otc-supplements-vms"}. The Menopause Society does not recommend them for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Black cohosh, DHEA and soy isoflavones are not proven to work, and some carry risks such as liver damage [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do]. Our [does it work?](/does-it-work/) reviews look at each one.
- **Paced breathing, relaxation and acupuncture**: :grade{id="paced-respiration-acupuncture-vms"}. Paced breathing and relaxation techniques did not ease hot flashes in trials, and acupuncture is not recommended for them [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

Frequent hot flashes are more than a nuisance: they appear to be linked with heart, bone and thinking (cognitive) risks [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @khalifey-hrt-cardiovascular-2026]. Our [heart health after menopause](/postmenopause/heart-health/) guide explains what that means.

## What you can do today

These steps are for comfort. There is no strong evidence that they reduce how many hot flashes you have [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

:::try
1. Dress in layers you can take off as soon as a hot flash starts [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do].
2. Carry a small portable fan [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do].
3. At night, lower the bedroom temperature, use a fan and layer your bedding so you can adjust it [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do].
4. Notice what seems to set off your hot flashes; alcohol, spicy food and caffeine can make symptoms worse [@nia-hot-flashes-what-can-i-do].
:::

To see your pattern, log your hot flashes for a couple of weeks in the [symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) or the [perimenopause symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/), and take it to your appointment.

## When to see a doctor and what to ask

If hot flashes bother you, disturb your sleep or get in the way of work and daily life, talk with a doctor about lifestyle changes or medicines that might help [@nia-what-is-menopause]. Also make an appointment if:

- flushing keeps happening, especially if you also have diarrhea [@medlineplus-flushing]
- you sweat heavily, the sweating lasts a long time, or it can't be explained [@medlineplus-sweating]
- sweating comes with a fever, a fast pounding heartbeat or weight loss [@medlineplus-sweating]

:::urgent
**Call 911** if you have chest pain, pressure or tightness, pain that spreads to your jaw, left arm or back, or a racing heart, sweating, dizziness or shortness of breath that comes with chest discomfort [@medlineplus-chest-pain]. Also call 911 if a racing or pounding heartbeat doesn't go away, or comes with shortness of breath or fainting [@nhs-heart-palpitations].

If you're in the UK: call 999 or go to A&E for these symptoms. If they have stopped, call NHS 111 or ask for an urgent GP appointment [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
:::
