A hot flash (vasomotor symptom) is a sudden feeling of intense heat that spreads rapidly through your chest, neck, and face, often accompanied by flushed skin, sweating, and a racing pulse [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. In our comprehensive [symptoms list](/symptoms/), vasomotor episodes are among the most frequently reported.

Episodes typically last between one and five minutes, and they are caused by estrogen fluctuations narrowing the temperature-tolerance zone in your brain. While lifestyle changes and supplements like [magnesium for relaxation](/does-it-work/magnesium/) are widely discussed, clinical evidence shows prescription interventions offer the greatest reduction in daily surges.

## Is this normal, and could it be perimenopause?

Yes. Hot flashes affect up to 80% of women during the menopause transition [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. 

In a healthy body, your brain allows a normal range of temperature variation without reacting. When estrogen levels fluctuate and decline during perimenopause, the thermoregulatory center in the hypothalamus becomes hypersensitive. A slight change in ambient heat or internal temperature triggers an exaggerated cooling response: blood vessels rapidly dilate, blood rushes to the skin surface, and sweat glands activate to dissipate heat.

## What else could cause hot flashes?

While hormonal transition is the most frequent explanation for women in their 40s and 50s, other conditions can trigger similar flushing:

- **Thyroid disorders**: Hyperthyroidism causes persistent heat sensitivity, sweating, and heart palpitations.
- **Medication reactions**: Antidepressants, raloxifene, tamoxifen, and calcium channel blockers can provoke vasodilation.
- **Carcinoid or pheochromocytoma tumors**: Rare neuroendocrine tumors release vasoactive substances that produce flushing accompanied by severe blood pressure swings.
- **Dietary triggers**: Caffeine, spicy foods containing capsaicin, and alcohol trigger rapid peripheral vasodilation.

:::urgent
Seek prompt medical care if flushing is accompanied by chest tightness, shortness of breath, high fever, severe dizziness, or fainting. These symptoms require immediate medical evaluation to exclude cardiovascular emergencies or acute allergic reactions.
:::

## What actually helps: evidence-graded treatments

Clinical research grades hot flash therapies by how effectively they reduce daily episode counts and distress:

| Treatment Option | Evidence Grade | Clinical Trial Summary |
|---|---|---|
| **Systemic Hormone Therapy (MHT)** | :grade{id="mht-vms"} | The most effective treatment; reduces how often hot flashes happen by about 75% compared with placebo in randomized trials [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. |
| **NK3 Receptor Antagonists** | :grade{id="nk3-vms"} | Daily non-hormonal prescription tablet targeting hypothalamic thermoregulation directly [@veozah-prescribing-information]. |
| **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)** | :grade{id="cbt-hypnosis-vms"} | Significantly reduces hot flash distress and perceived burden in clinical trials [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. |
| **SSRI / SNRI Antidepressants** | :grade{id="ssri-snri-vms"} | Low-dose prescription options (e.g. venlafaxine, paroxetine), recommended by The Menopause Society for women who can't or don't want to take hormones [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. |
| **Moisture-wicking layered clothing** | :grade{id="cooling-comfort"} | Natural fibers and breathable performance fabrics allow rapid temperature adjustment during unpredictable daytime surges. |

When choosing clothing for temperature comfort, look for breathable weaves and quick-drying fabrics. Our [guide to sleepwear for night sweats](/care/sleepwear-for-night-sweats/) compares the main fabric types.

Frequent hot flashes are also linked with higher cardiovascular risk, not just discomfort [@khalifey-hrt-cardiovascular-2026] — see our [heart health after menopause](/postmenopause/heart-health/) guide for what that means and what doesn't change about how HRT is used.

## What you can do today

1. **Identify and reduce personal triggers**: Track whether coffee, hot tea, spicy seasonings, or alcohol precede your flushing episodes.
2. **Dress in removable layers**: Wear breathable base layers so you can adjust your clothing instantly when a wave of heat begins.
3. **Carry portable cooling**: A small handheld fan and a bottle of cold water can provide immediate physical relief when flushing starts.
4. **Track daily episodes**: Use the [Perimenopause symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/) and [Symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) to document frequency and severity before your next clinic visit.

## When to see a doctor and what to ask

If hot flashes interfere with work meetings, daytime activities, or nighttime rest, talk with a healthcare professional.
