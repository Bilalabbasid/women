Bladder leaks become more common around menopause as falling estrogen thins the lining of the urethra (the tube urine leaves through) and the pelvic floor muscles weaken [@nams-2020-gsm]. They are treatable, not just part of aging. Guidelines recommend supervised pelvic floor muscle training as the first treatment for stress and mixed leaks [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence; @cochrane-pfmt-incontinence].

## Why do bladder leaks increase during menopause?

The bladder and urethra are sensitive to hormones, and the tissues there change when estrogen falls [@nams-2020-gsm]. When estrogen levels drop:

1. **The urethral seal weakens**: The lining inside the urethra thins, so it closes less tightly when you cough, lift, or run [@nams-2020-gsm].
2. **Pelvic support decreases**: The muscles and tissues that hold up the bladder and urethra lose strength with age and lower estrogen.
3. **The bladder muscle can become overactive**: Sudden squeezes of the bladder muscle can cause strong, sudden urges to pee even when the bladder isn't full.

## Understanding your leak type: stress vs urge

Treatment depends on which type of leaking you have.

| Feature | Stress incontinence | Urge incontinence | Mixed incontinence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **When leaks happen** | When you cough, sneeze, laugh, run, jump, or lift something heavy | Right after a sudden, strong urge to pee that's hard to hold | A mix of both |
| **Underlying cause** | The urethra doesn't close tightly enough and the pelvic floor is weaker | The bladder muscle squeezes before it should (overactive bladder) | Both pelvic floor weakness and an overactive bladder |
| **First treatment** | Supervised pelvic floor muscle training for at least 3 months [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence] | Bladder training for at least 6 weeks [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence] | Both, guided by which symptoms bother you most [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence] |

## What can help: evidence-based steps

### 1. Pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT)

A Cochrane systematic review found that women who did pelvic floor muscle training were 8 times more likely to report that their leaking had stopped completely than women who had no treatment [@cochrane-pfmt-incontinence].

NICE recommends a supervised program lasting at least 3 months, with at least 8 squeezes done 3 times a day, and a check by a clinician (an internal exam) first to confirm you're squeezing the right muscles [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence]. The right movement is lifting up and in, not pushing down. If training helps, NICE advises keeping it up [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence].

A pelvic floor physical therapist can check your technique and give you feedback. For step-by-step instructions, read our guide on [how to do pelvic floor exercises correctly](/care/pelvic-floor-exercises/).

### 2. Bladder training for urge leaks

If sudden urgency is your main problem, NICE recommends these steps [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence]:

- **Bladder training**: For at least 6 weeks, gradually stretch the time between bathroom trips, using slow breathing and a pelvic floor squeeze to ride out an urge.
- **Cutting down on caffeine**: A trial of less caffeine is recommended for overactive bladder.
- **Adjusting how much you drink**: If you drink a lot more or a lot less than usual, changing your fluid intake may help.

### 3. Local vaginal estrogen

NICE recommends offering vaginal estrogen to women who have overactive bladder symptoms along with vaginal or urinary symptoms of menopause, but not hormone therapy taken by mouth or patch to treat leaks [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence]. Low-dose vaginal estrogen is an effective treatment for the genitourinary syndrome of menopause [@nams-2020-gsm]. Ask your doctor whether it suits you.

## When to see a doctor

:::urgent
See a doctor soon if bladder leaks come with blood in your urine, ongoing pain in your bladder or urethra, or trouble passing urine [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence]. Also mention any symptoms of vaginal prolapse (when the bladder, uterus, or bowel drops into the vagina), which NICE says should be assessed alongside urinary symptoms [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence].
:::

## Questions people ask

### How long does pelvic floor training take to stop bladder leaks?
NICE recommends trying supervised training for at least 3 months before judging whether it works, doing at least 8 squeezes 3 times a day, and carrying on if it helps [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence].

### Should I see a pelvic health physical therapist?
Yes, if you can. NICE recommends that pelvic floor training be supervised, and that your technique is checked before you start [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence]. This is especially useful if you aren't sure you're squeezing the right muscles or leaks haven't improved.
