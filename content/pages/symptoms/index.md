The main symptoms of perimenopause and menopause are changes to your periods, hot flashes and night sweats, vaginal dryness, mood changes, joint and muscle aches, and low sex drive [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. They range from mild to severe and can last a short or long time [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Keeping a simple symptom log is a good first step.

This guide groups symptoms by type, sums up what the research shows, and points to next steps.

## Vasomotor symptoms (heat and perspiration)

Hot flashes and night sweats happen when the brain's temperature control becomes more sensitive as hormone levels change:

- **[Hot flashes (vasomotor symptoms)](/symptoms/hot-flashes/)**: sudden waves of heat, flushed skin, and a fast heartbeat.
- **[Night sweats and waking at night](/symptoms/night-sweats/)**: waking up soaked in sweat, often followed by chills.
- **[Heart palpitations in perimenopause](/symptoms/heart-palpitations/)**: a pounding or fluttering heartbeat, sometimes around hot flashes, and when palpitations are an emergency.
- **[Body odor changes](/symptoms/body-odor/)**: how increased sweating from hot flashes and night sweats likely connects to changes in body odor, and what the direct evidence gap actually looks like.

## Sleep and energy changes

Sleep problems in perimenopause can come from night-time hot flashes, insomnia, or both:

- **[Menopause insomnia and fragmented sleep](/symptoms/insomnia/)**: difficulty falling asleep or returning to sleep after midnight. Many readers also explore whether [magnesium improves sleep](/does-it-work/magnesium/).
- **[Waking at 3 a.m.](/symptoms/waking-at-3am/)**: a consistent middle-of-the-night awakening, sometimes without any noticeable sweating.
- **[Fatigue](/symptoms/fatigue/)**: waking unrefreshed despite remaining in bed for seven or eight hours, often linked to disrupted sleep rather than tiredness alone.

## Cognitive and mood shifts

Changing hormone levels can affect mood, focus, and memory:

- **[Menopause brain fog](/symptoms/brain-fog/)** and **[memory problems](/symptoms/memory-problems/)**: momentary lapses in word recall, concentration challenges, and memory slips — and what the research says about whether this signals anything more serious.
- **[Irritability and rage](/symptoms/irritability/)**: rapid mood fluctuations that feel uncharacteristic or disproportionate.
- **[Menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/)** and **[panic attacks](/symptoms/panic-attacks/)**: unexplained surges of apprehension, nervous tension, or panic-like sensations, and how to tell them apart from a cardiac or vasomotor event.
- **[Low mood and depression](/symptoms/depression/)**: a heavier, more persistent low mood that NICE guidance treats as distinct from an ordinary diagnosis of depression.
- **[Headaches](/symptoms/headaches/)** and **[migraines](/symptoms/migraines/)**: changing headache patterns, and why migraine with aura changes hormone therapy safety specifically.
- **[Dizziness](/symptoms/dizziness/)**: lightheaded or off-balance sensations that track with hot flash severity, and the inner-ear causes that can feel similar.
- **[Tingling and formication](/symptoms/tingling/)**: prickling or crawling-skin sensations, and when they signal something other than menopause.
- **[Tinnitus](/symptoms/tinnitus/)**: ringing or buzzing in the ears, and why the research on a menopause link is genuinely mixed.

## Musculoskeletal, metabolic, and tissue comfort

Estrogen affects many tissues, including joints, skin, the vagina, and the bladder:

- **[Joint and muscle pain](/symptoms/joint-pain/)**: morning stiffness and aching in hands, knees, shoulders, and hips.
- **[Frozen shoulder](/symptoms/frozen-shoulder/)**: stiffness that restricts shoulder movement, and why the hormone link is less settled than clinical folklore suggests.
- **[Weight gain and belly fat in menopause](/symptoms/weight-gain/)**: why body composition shifts toward abdominal fat and how resistance training protects muscle and bone.
- **[Bloating](/symptoms/bloating/)**: digestive changes and a feeling of fullness, and what the evidence does (and doesn't yet) explain.
- **[Digestive changes](/symptoms/digestive-changes/)**: constipation, altered bowel habits, and IBS flares, and when they need a doctor's check.
- **[Breast tenderness](/symptoms/breast-tenderness/)**: cyclical, non-cyclical, and chest-wall pain patterns, and which breast changes need a check.
- **[Hair thinning](/symptoms/hair-thinning/)**, **[facial hair](/symptoms/facial-hair/)**, **[acne](/symptoms/acne/)**, and **[dry, itchy skin](/symptoms/dry-itchy-skin/)**: what's actually driven by estrogen loss, and what evidence-based options exist.
- **[Dry eyes](/symptoms/dry-eyes/)** and **[burning mouth](/symptoms/burning-mouth/)**: two mucosal-dryness symptoms with real, studied hormone links.
- **[Vaginal dryness (GSM)](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/)**: thinner tissue, less natural moisture, and burning or irritation.
- **[Painful sex (dyspareunia)](/symptoms/painful-sex/)**: friction and discomfort during sex caused by thinner, drier vaginal tissue.
- **[Low libido](/symptoms/low-libido/)**: a drop in sex drive that NICE guidance recognizes as a core menopause symptom in its own right.
- **[Bladder urgency](/symptoms/bladder-urgency/)** and **[bladder leaks and incontinence](/symptoms/incontinence/)**: leaks when you cough or sneeze, and sudden urges, linked to a weaker urethra and pelvic floor.
- **[Recurrent UTIs after menopause](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/)**: why urinary tract infections keep coming back after menopause, and how local treatment helps prevent them.

## When your situation isn't the standard one

Most of this guide assumes a natural transition in your 40s or 50s. If menopause happened suddenly after surgery, before age 40, or during cancer treatment, standard timing and treatment trade-offs can change — see our [specialist situation guides](/groups/) for [surgical menopause](/groups/surgical-menopause/), [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/), and [menopause after breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/).

## Tracking your personal pattern

NICE guidance says that for people aged 45 and over, perimenopause and menopause are usually recognized from symptoms and period changes, without blood tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. That makes a record of what you notice over a few weeks especially useful to your clinician.

Start with the [Perimenopause symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/) or record daily entries with the [Symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/).
