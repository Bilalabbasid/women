Menopause insomnia is common: about 1 in 4 women around menopause have sleep problems bad enough to affect their days [@baker-sleep-menopausal-transition-review-2018]. Night sweats are a frequent cause. A short talking therapy called CBT for insomnia has strong trial results, and hormone therapy helps most when night sweats are what wake you [@mccurry-msflash-cbti-2016; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Can menopause cause insomnia?

Yes. Trouble getting to sleep or staying asleep is a recognized symptom of perimenopause and menopause [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. Long-term studies link sleep problems to menopause stage and hormone changes, beyond what getting older explains [@baker-sleep-menopausal-transition-review-2018]. Women with hot flashes are more likely to report broken sleep during the transition [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Why do hot flashes wake you up?

A hot flash at night often comes with an awakening, even if you don't remember feeling hot. In a sleep-lab study of 34 women in [perimenopause](/perimenopause/), 69.4% of hot flashes were linked to waking up, and they accounted for about a quarter of the time spent awake at night [@de-zambotti-hot-flashes-sleep-2014]. In the large SWAN study, women with moderate to severe hot flashes were almost three times as likely to report waking often [@baker-sleep-menopausal-transition-review-2018].

The link can run both ways: research suggests waking up may itself set off a hot flash [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause]. If you wake at the same time every night, see [waking at 3 a.m.](/symptoms/waking-at-3am/), and for the sweats themselves, [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/).

## Can menopause cause anxiety and insomnia together?

Yes, and each can feed the other. Stress, going over worries at night and anxiety commonly overlap with menopause-related insomnia, and some sleep complaints are linked to depression [@baker-sleep-menopausal-transition-review-2018]. Lack of sleep can in turn make you irritable or low [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause]. Ongoing worry with trouble sleeping, restlessness and trouble concentrating can also be signs of an anxiety disorder, which is treatable [@nimh-generalized-anxiety-disorder]. See [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/).

## Other causes a doctor can check

A doctor can check for other causes of poor sleep, such as:

- **Sleep apnea**: breathing that stops and starts during sleep. Compared with women before menopause, women after menopause had 2.6 times the odds of mild or worse sleep apnea in one US study [@young-menopause-sleep-disordered-breathing-2003]. Signs include loud snoring, gasping and daytime sleepiness, and insomnia is a more common symptom in women [@nhlbi-sleep-apnea-symptoms].
- **Movement and breathing problems in sleep**: these become more common after menopause [@baker-sleep-menopausal-transition-review-2018].
- **Depression**: low mood can contribute to poor sleep [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause]. See [depression](/symptoms/depression/).

## Things to try tonight

The National Institute on Aging suggests these steps for menopause sleep problems.

:::try
1. Go to bed and get up at the same times each day, and skip late-afternoon or evening naps [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
2. Keep your bedroom quiet, at a comfortable temperature, and free of the TV, computer and phone [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
3. Avoid caffeine late in the day and large meals close to bedtime [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
4. Go easy on alcohol: even small amounts make it harder to stay asleep [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
5. Exercise regularly, but not close to bedtime [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause].
:::

### How well does CBT for insomnia work?

CBT for insomnia works well for menopause sleep problems, even when hot flashes continue. In a trial of 106 women with insomnia and daily hot flashes, six phone sessions over 8 weeks cut insomnia scores by 9.9 points, against 4.7 for women given menopause education [@mccurry-msflash-cbti-2016]. At 6 months, 84% of the CBT group were in the no-insomnia range versus 43% of the comparison group [@mccurry-msflash-cbti-2016].

The sessions covered setting a sleep window, using the bed only for sleep, sleep habits and changing unhelpful thoughts about sleep [@mccurry-msflash-cbti-2016]. Hot flashes didn't become less frequent, but they bothered women less [@mccurry-msflash-cbti-2016]. See [CBT and hypnosis](/treatments/nonhormonal/cbt-and-hypnosis/).

## Do melatonin or sleeping pills help?

They may help for a short time, but they aren't a fix. The National Institute on Aging says over-the-counter sleep aids such as melatonin and prescription sleep medicines can help briefly but don't solve the underlying problem, and shouldn't be used long term [@nia-sleep-problems-menopause]. For magnesium, a pooled analysis found people fell asleep about 17 minutes sooner, but the studies were low quality [@mah-pitre-magnesium-insomnia-2021].

## How long does menopause insomnia last?

Studies haven't pinned down a typical length for menopause insomnia itself. Because night sweats drive much of it, their timeline matters: frequent hot flashes last 7.4 years on average and more than 10 years for many women [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Treatment doesn't have to wait for them to stop. See [how long menopause symptoms last](/menopause/how-long-symptoms-last/).

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if poor sleep is wearing you down or affecting your days. Before the visit, keep a sleep diary for a couple of weeks: when you go to bed, when you wake in the night, and any caffeine, alcohol or exercise [@nia-sleep-older-adults]. Our [private symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/) can help. Ask about a sleep study if a partner notices snoring, gasping or pauses in your breathing, since untreated sleep apnea can lead to serious problems, including heart attack [@nhlbi-sleep-apnea-symptoms; @nia-sleep-older-adults].

:::urgent
If sleeplessness comes with thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). If you're in danger right now, call 911 [@nimh-generalized-anxiety-disorder].
:::
