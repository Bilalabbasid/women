Sleep often gets worse in perimenopause and menopause, and night-time hot flashes and night sweats are a common reason you wake up [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), a talking therapy, is recommended by The Menopause Society for hot flashes that disrupt sleep [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. When night sweats are the cause, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Trouble falling asleep or waking again and again at 2 or 3 a.m. can happen across [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/), often because of [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) and [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/). Other things can also break up sleep, such as sleep apnea, thyroid changes, restless legs, and [anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/), and a clinician can check for these.

| **[Non-hormonal Rx](/treatments/nonhormonal/)** (gabapentin, SSRIs/SNRIs, NK3 antagonists) | :grade{id="nonhormonal-rx-sleep-vms"} | Level I non-hormonal options when night sweats wake you [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if poor sleep is wearing you down or affecting your days. They can check whether night sweats are the main cause, rule out other causes such as sleep apnea, thyroid changes, restless legs, or anxiety, and talk through CBT and [hormone or non-hormonal treatment](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) for night sweats [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
