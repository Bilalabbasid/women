Waking up with stiff hips, knees, shoulders, or fingers — even when you haven't changed your activity — surprises many women in [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/). UK NICE guideline NG23 explicitly identifies **musculoskeletal symptoms (joint and muscle pain)** as one of the core symptom groups associated with menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## What else a clinician should rule out

Because midlife joint pain can have several causes, a clinician will check whether your symptoms point to:

- Osteoarthritis or tendonitis (such as gluteal tendinopathy or adhesive capsulitis / frozen shoulder)
- Autoimmune or inflammatory arthritis (especially if joints are swollen or warm)
- Hypothyroidism or severe vitamin D deficiency

## What helps (graded by evidence)

1. **Progressive resistance and strength training:** Keeping muscles and tendons strong around your joints — and protecting underlying bone mass — has strong randomized trial support. In the LIFTMOR trial, supervised resistance and impact training improved muscle strength, physical function, and bone mineral density in postmenopausal women [@liftmor-trial-bone-density]. Read our guide: [Does exercise help menopause symptoms?](/does-it-work/exercise/) and [bone health after menopause](/postmenopause/bone-health/).
2. **[Menopausal hormone therapy (HRT)](/treatments/hormone-therapy/):** Evaluated alongside vasomotor symptoms and bone preservation in women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations].
