Yes, aching joints and muscles can be part of perimenopause and menopause: UK guidelines and the NHS list joint and muscle pain as a symptom [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-menopause-symptoms]. Keep moving, because strength training has good trial evidence for building strength and bone [@liftmor-trial-bone-density]. New or lasting pain is worth checking, as arthritis and thyroid problems can feel similar.

The UK NICE menopause guideline (NG23) groups joint and muscle pain under "musculoskeletal symptoms," alongside hot flashes and mood changes [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Learn more about [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/).

## How common is joint pain after menopause?

Very common. In a large US trial of postmenopausal women who had had a hysterectomy (surgery to remove the uterus), about **77%** reported some joint pain and about **40%** reported joint swelling when they joined [@chlebowski-whi-estrogen-joint-symptoms-2013]. That doesn't mean menopause caused all of it. Arthritis and other conditions also become more common in midlife, which is why new or lasting pain is worth checking.

## What else can cause joint pain

Joint pain has many possible causes, and most are common and treatable. A doctor can check for other causes, such as [@medlineplus-joint-pain]:

- **Osteoarthritis**: wear on the joints. Pain that gets worse with walking, and stiffness after activity, usually over age 45, points this way [@nhs-joint-pain].
- **Rheumatoid arthritis or lupus**: conditions where the immune system attacks the joints. Pain and stiffness on both sides of the body that is worse after rest can be a sign of rheumatoid arthritis [@medlineplus-joint-pain; @nhs-joint-pain].
- **Tendinitis or bursitis**: irritation around a joint, often from overuse or strain [@medlineplus-joint-pain].
- **Gout**: sudden, severe pain in one joint, often the big toe [@medlineplus-joint-pain; @nhs-joint-pain].
- **Underactive thyroid**: joint or muscle pain can be an early sign, along with tiredness, low mood, weight gain and feeling cold [@medlineplus-hypothyroidism].

Shoulder stiffness that slowly limits how far you can move your arm may be [frozen shoulder](/symptoms/frozen-shoulder/).

## What helps

:::try
1. Rest the sore joint if you can, but don't stop moving it completely.
2. Put an ice pack or a bag of frozen peas, wrapped in a towel, on the painful area for up to 20 minutes, every 2 to 3 hours.
3. Ask a pharmacist or doctor which pain reliever is right for you [@nhs-joint-pain].
:::

- **Strength and impact training**: In the LIFTMOR trial, postmenopausal women with low bone density who did 8 months of supervised, twice-weekly high-intensity strength and impact training improved their bone density, back and leg strength, and everyday physical function more than women doing a home-based, low-intensity program [@liftmor-trial-bone-density]. The trial measured bone and function, not joint pain itself. For bone density and strength: :grade{id="strength-training-bone"}. Read more: [Does exercise help menopause symptoms?](/does-it-work/exercise/) and [bone health after menopause](/postmenopause/bone-health/).
- **[Hormone therapy (HRT)](/treatments/hormone-therapy/)**: In the Women's Health Initiative trial, women who took estrogen alone reported joint pain a little less often after a year than women on placebo (76.3% vs 79.2%), and the difference lasted through year 3. Joint swelling was slightly more common with estrogen (42.1% vs 39.7%). The authors called the benefit "modest but sustained" [@chlebowski-whi-estrogen-joint-symptoms-2013]. This was a later look at the data, in women without a uterus taking one type of oral estrogen. For women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause who have no medical reason to avoid it, The Menopause Society says the balance of benefits and risks of hormone therapy is favorable; it treats hot flashes and prevents bone loss and fracture [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## When to see a doctor

Make an appointment if:

- your joints stay stiff for more than 30 minutes after you wake up [@nhs-joint-pain]
- pain or swelling is getting worse or keeps coming back, or hasn't improved after 2 weeks of home care [@nhs-joint-pain]
- joint pain lasts more than several days, or you have severe joint pain and swelling you can't explain [@medlineplus-joint-pain]
- you have a fever without flu symptoms, or you've lost 10 pounds or more without trying [@medlineplus-joint-pain]

:::urgent
**Get urgent medical help the same day** if a painful joint is swollen and the skin over it feels hot, or if you have joint pain and feel generally unwell, have a high temperature, or feel hot, cold or shivery. These can be signs of a joint infection [@nhs-joint-pain].

**Call 911 or go to the emergency room** if you have very bad joint pain after a fall or injury, can't walk or put weight on a joint, a joint looks out of place, or you have tingling or no feeling around a joint after an injury [@nhs-joint-pain].

If you're in the UK: call NHS 111 or ask for an urgent GP appointment for a hot, swollen joint or joint pain with a fever; go to A&E or call 999 for the injury signs above.
:::
