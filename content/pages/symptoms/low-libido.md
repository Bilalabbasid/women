A drop in sex drive is one of the most common, and most under-discussed, symptoms of [menopause](/menopause/). NICE guideline NG23 lists sexual difficulties, including low sexual desire, among the core symptoms associated with menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations] — this is a recognized clinical issue, not something you're expected to just live with.

## What's actually driving it

Low libido in menopause is rarely one thing. Common, overlapping contributors include:

- **[Vaginal dryness and painful sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/):** when sex hurts, desire naturally drops — see our [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) page. Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for the genitourinary changes behind this [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Sleep loss, fatigue, and low mood:** all reduce desire independent of hormones.
- **Relationship factors and stress**, which matter regardless of hormonal status.
- **Declining testosterone**, which, alongside estrogen, contributes to sexual desire in some women.

## What can help (graded by evidence)

| Option | Evidence grade | What guidelines say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Testosterone supplementation**, if HRT alone isn't enough | :grade{id="testosterone-libido"} | NICE NG23 recommends considering it for low sexual desire associated with menopause; currently an off-label use in the UK, guided by a specialist [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| **Treating vaginal dryness and painful sex directly** | :grade{id="treat-gsm-for-libido"} | Addressing the physical cause of painful sex, via [moisturizers, lubricants, or vaginal estrogen](/care/moisturizers-and-lubricants/), often restores some desire on its own [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy] |
| **"Libido" supplements marketed for menopause** | :grade{id="libido-supplements"} | No credible clinical trial evidence; check any specific product in our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) before buying it |

See our [hormone therapy overview](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) for more on how testosterone and HRT are used together, and talk to a clinician before starting it — monitoring and dosing matter.

## Prepare for the conversation

A lot of women find this symptom harder to bring up than hot flashes. Our [treatment conversation builder](/tools/treatment-questions/) can help you organize what you want to say.

:::questions
- Is my low libido more likely linked to vaginal dryness and painful sex, hormone levels, sleep and mood, or some combination?
- Would testosterone be appropriate for me if HRT alone doesn't fully address it, and what monitoring would that involve?
