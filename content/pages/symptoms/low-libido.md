A drop in sex drive is a common, recognized symptom of [menopause](/menopause/), and it usually has several causes at once, such as painful sex, poor sleep and low mood [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @trento-postmenopausal-sexual-function-2021]. Painful sex is very treatable, and hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for it [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. For postmenopausal women with distressing low desire, testosterone is the treatment with trial evidence [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

## Is low libido a normal part of menopause?

It's common and recognized. UK guideline NG23 lists sexual difficulties, including low sexual desire, among the core symptoms of menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. In one study of 380 women aged 40 to 65, almost two-thirds (64%) were at risk of sexual problems, with lower scores for desire, comfort, orgasm and satisfaction [@trento-postmenopausal-sexual-function-2021].

Occasional problems with sex are common. US guidance suggests seeing a provider if problems last more than a few months or cause distress for you or your partner [@medlineplus-sexual-problems-women].

## Why does menopause lower sex drive?

Low libido in menopause is rarely one thing. In the study above, the factors most strongly linked with sexual problems were other sexual complaints, vaginal dryness, sleep problems and depressed mood [@trento-postmenopausal-sexual-function-2021]. Common contributors include:

- **Vaginal dryness and [painful sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/)**: women with vaginal dryness had more than three times the odds of sexual problems [@trento-postmenopausal-sexual-function-2021]. Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for the genitourinary changes behind this [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Poor sleep and low mood**: sleep problems and depressed mood each roughly doubled the odds [@trento-postmenopausal-sexual-function-2021]. Anxiety and work stress can also lower desire [@medlineplus-sexual-problems-women].
- **Medicines and health conditions**: some drugs affect desire, as can conditions such as diabetes and heart disease [@medlineplus-sexual-problems-women].
- **Relationship concerns or past trauma**: these can matter as much as physical causes [@medlineplus-sexual-problems-women].

What about hormones? Testosterone levels are often blamed, but experts say the link between a woman's own testosterone levels and her sexual function is still uncertain, partly because blood tests for it aren't always reliable [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

## Does libido come back after menopause?

The research doesn't give a reliable timeline for desire returning on its own. What it does show is that vaginal dryness and painful sex, unlike hot flashes, tend to get worse over time without treatment and rarely clear up by themselves [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. Treating those causes, along with counseling or testosterone for some women, can improve desire [@silva-sexual-counseling-metaanalysis-2022; @davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

### Does HRT increase libido?

HRT works mainly on one cause: it's the most effective treatment for vaginal dryness and the other genitourinary changes that can make sex painful [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. For dryness, moisturizers and lubricants are the first-line options, and low-dose vaginal estrogen is highly effective for moderate to severe symptoms that persist [@nams-2020-gsm]. UK guidance suggests considering testosterone if HRT alone doesn't improve low desire [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) and [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/).

### Does testosterone work for low libido?

An international consensus backed by The Menopause Society and other societies found testosterone works for one specific use: postmenopausal women diagnosed with low desire that causes distress (hypoactive sexual desire disorder), after a full assessment of other causes [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. At doses close to premenopausal levels, it added an average of one satisfying sexual event a month over placebo, and improved desire, arousal, orgasm and pleasure [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

The same statement sets out how it should be used [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]:

- **Form**: not tablets, which harm cholesterol levels; forms used on the skin showed no such harm in short-term studies. Pellets, injections and compounded products aren't recommended.
- **Monitoring**: a blood test before starting, again 3 to 6 weeks in, then every 6 months to check the dose isn't too high.
- **Time frame**: if there's no benefit by 6 months, stop.
- **Side effects**: mild acne or extra body or facial hair in some women; not hair loss or a deeper voice.

In most countries, including the UK, it's prescribed off-label, often as a men's product used at a woman's dose, because no product for women is approved [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. Long-term safety data, including for breast cancer and heart disease, are still lacking [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. More on our [testosterone for women](/treatments/hormone-therapy/testosterone/) page.

### Are there medicines for low sex drive in women?

Two are FDA-approved, with narrow labels:

- **Flibanserin (Addyi)**: a bedtime tablet labeled for women under 65 with acquired (newly developed), distressing low desire that isn't caused by another medical or mental health condition, relationship problems or a medicine [@dailymed-addyi-label-2025]. It has a boxed warning: alcohol taken close to the dose raises the risk of severe low blood pressure and fainting. Common side effects include dizziness, sleepiness, nausea and tiredness [@dailymed-addyi-label-2025].
- **Bremelanotide (Vyleesi)**: an injection labeled only for premenopausal women; its label says it isn't for postmenopausal women [@dailymed-vyleesi-label-2026].

### Can counseling or sex therapy help?

Yes. A review of 8 studies in 619 women in early menopause found that sexual counseling or education programs, usually 4 to 10 sessions and sometimes including CBT or mindfulness, improved sexual function scores, including desire, compared with usual care [@silva-sexual-counseling-metaanalysis-2022]. See [sexual health after menopause](/postmenopause/sexual-health/).

## When to see a doctor about low libido

See a provider if low desire lasts more than a few months or is causing distress for you or your partner [@medlineplus-sexual-problems-women]. Mention any medicines you take, since some affect desire [@medlineplus-sexual-problems-women], and any pain during sex, which has its own effective treatments [@nams-2020-gsm]. A lot of women find this harder to bring up than hot flashes; our [treatment conversation builder](/tools/treatment-questions/) can help you put it into words.
