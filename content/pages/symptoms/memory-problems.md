Forgetting names mid-sentence, losing your train of thought, or struggling to find the right word is a widely reported complaint during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) — often described as "[brain fog](/symptoms/brain-fog/)." It's also one of the most anxiety-provoking symptoms, because many women quietly worry it's an early sign of dementia.

A 2024 clinical review in *Menopause*, the journal of The Menopause Society, offers real reassurance: longitudinal research finds a small but reliable decline in objective memory performance during the perimenopause transition that isn't explained by aging alone — but when that decline happens, performance stays within the normal range for all but a very small number of women [@maki-jaff-brain-fog-2024]. In other words, the lapse you're noticing is real, but it is not, on its own, a sign of dementia.

## Why it happens — and why it's bigger than "just memory"

The same review notes that brain fog extends beyond memory complaints to a wider range of cognitive effects, and that estrogen, hormone therapy, and everyday factors like high blood pressure or a sedentary lifestyle can all be part of the conversation with a clinician [@maki-jaff-brain-fog-2024]. A 2026 clinical review adds that memory and concentration difficulties often occur alongside other perimenopause symptoms — disrupted sleep, fatigue, and mood changes — as part of an overlapping cluster linked to fluctuating hormone levels, rather than as an isolated problem [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026].

That overlap matters practically: a bad run of [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) or [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) can make brain fog noticeably worse the next day.

## What the evidence does — and doesn't — support

- **Hormone therapy for dementia prevention:** **Not supported.** NICE NG23 does not recommend HRT for this purpose [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. This is a distinct question from whether HRT eases day-to-day menopause symptoms.
- **Treating the sleep and mood symptoms that worsen brain fog:** **Moderate.** Addressing [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/), insomnia, and mood symptoms directly can reduce the compounding effect on concentration and memory [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026].
- **"Brain" or nootropic supplements:** **Not supported.** No menopause-specific clinical trial evidence backs these products; check any specific product in our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) before buying it.

## When to ask for more than reassurance

Most perimenopausal memory changes are mild and don't affect daily functioning. Raise it with a clinician sooner if memory problems are severe, progressive, affect your ability to work or manage daily tasks, or come with other new neurological symptoms — these deserve their own assessment rather than being assumed to be hormonal.
