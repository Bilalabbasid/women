Migraines often get worse in perimenopause, when estrogen and progesterone rise and fall unevenly, and many women find they ease after menopause [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026; @ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015]. If you're considering HRT, estrogen through the skin at a low dose appears safer for migraine than pills [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].

## Do migraines get worse in perimenopause?

Often, yes. A 2026 review of migraine across the menopause transition found that unstable estradiol and progesterone levels in [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) can make migraine more frequent and less predictable [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]. Population studies also suggest migraine may become more common during perimenopause [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015].

Other midlife changes, such as hot flashes, poor sleep and mood problems, can make migraine harder to manage at this stage [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]. If you're not sure whether you have a [headache](/symptoms/headaches/) or a migraine, start there, as the difference changes what's safe to try.

## Do migraines stop after menopause?

For many women they improve, but not always. Large population studies show migraine tends to get better after menopause, though studies of people treated at headache clinics found no improvement or even worsening [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015].

- **Migraine without aura**: often improves after menopause [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].
- **Migraine with aura**: tends to continue, and on its own raises the risk of ischemic stroke and other blood vessel problems [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].
- **After surgical menopause**: improvement is less likely than after natural menopause [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015].

## Does HRT make migraines better or worse?

It can go either way, and the type of hormone therapy matters most. A 2026 review found hormone therapy has variable effects on migraine: estrogen pills, especially at higher doses, may make it worse, while low-dose estrogen through the skin appears safer and better tolerated [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].

In observational studies pulled together in a 2015 review, HRT after menopause was linked with migraine getting worse, and trials of specific regimens gave mixed results [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015]. Taking progestogen every day, rather than in cycles, may mean fewer attacks triggered by hormone drops [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].

## Is HRT safe if I get migraines with aura?

It can be, but the route matters more than usual, and it needs a personal risk check with a clinician. This is one of the few places where how you take hormone therapy, not just whether you take it, changes the safety picture:

| Factor | What the evidence shows | Bottom line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Estrogen pills** | May worsen migraine and raise vascular risk, especially at higher doses and in women who get aura [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026] | **Caution** |
| **Low-dose estrogen through the skin** (patch or gel) | Appears safer and better tolerated for migraine, and is the route The North American Menopause Society recommends, per this review [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026] | **Preferred when HRT is used** |
| **Daily vs. cyclic progestogen** | Daily (continuous) regimens may reduce withdrawal-related attacks compared with cyclic ones [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026] | :grade{id="continuous-progestogen-migraine"} |
| **Starting stroke risk, women under 60** | Very low; unlikely to rise with combined HRT that uses estrogen through the skin, but rises with combined HRT using estrogen pills, more so with higher doses and longer use [@nice-ng23-recommendations] | **Context, not a verdict for any individual** |

Your personal and family history of stroke, blood clots and heart disease all count alongside your migraine type. See [patch vs. pill](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/), our [HRT overview](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) and the [FDA boxed warning explainer](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/) for the wider safety picture.

:::urgent
Call 911 for a sudden, severe headache with no known cause, or a headache with sudden numbness or weakness on one side, confusion, trouble speaking, trouble seeing, or loss of balance, as these can be signs of a stroke [@cdc-stroke-signs-2026]. Also get emergency care for an explosive headache that is the worst you've ever had, even if you often get migraines [@medlineplus-headache].
:::

## What treatments help menopause migraines?

Standard migraine treatments still apply, chosen with your heart, bone and metabolic health in mind. A 2026 review found these remain central during the menopause transition [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]:

- **Established migraine medicines**: triptans, anti-inflammatory painkillers (NSAIDs), beta-blockers, topiramate and some antidepressants [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].
- **Newer options**: CGRP antibodies, gepants and ditans, which don't narrow blood vessels and may suit women who can't take some older medicines for heart reasons [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026].
- **If you also have hot flashes**: non-hormonal options, especially SNRI antidepressants, may be considered [@korn-bernstein-migraine-menopause-2026]; see [SSRIs and SNRIs](/treatments/nonhormonal/ssris-snris/).

These are prescription decisions to make with a clinician. No migraine prevention plan is recommended specifically for menopausal women yet [@ripa-migraine-menopausal-women-review-2015].
