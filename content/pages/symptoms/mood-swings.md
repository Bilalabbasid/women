Yes, perimenopause can cause mood swings. The NHS says mood swings, low mood and anxiety are common around perimenopause and menopause [@nhs-menopause-things-you-can-do]. In a large US study, women in early perimenopause were more likely than women with regular periods to report frequent mood changes, irritability and nervousness [@bromberger-swan-mood-symptoms-2003]. Rest, exercise, better sleep and talking therapy help many people, and some do well on hormone therapy.

**Mood swings in menopause at a glance**

- **How common**: In the SWAN study, 14.9% to 18.4% of women in early perimenopause had persistent mood symptoms, compared with 8% to 12% of women still having regular periods [@bromberger-swan-mood-symptoms-2003].
- **When they peak**: Feeling tense, low and irritable was most common in early perimenopause (28.9%) and less common before (20.9%) and after menopause (22%) [@bromberger-swan-psychologic-distress-2001].
- **What makes them worse**: Hot flashes and night sweats are linked with low mood, partly through broken sleep [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026].
- **What helps**: Rest, regular exercise and relaxing activities; CBT for low mood, anxiety and sleep [@nhs-menopause-things-you-can-do].
- **Medicines**: HRT can be considered for low mood that comes with other menopause symptoms; antidepressants may be offered if HRT doesn't suit you [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-menopause-treatment-2026].
- **When it's more than mood swings**: A low or empty mood most of the day, nearly every day, for 2 weeks or more can be depression and needs a doctor [@nimh-depression-booklet].

## Are mood swings a sign of perimenopause?

They can be. Mood changes are one of the symptoms listed by the NHS and by the UK NICE menopause guideline, alongside period changes, hot flashes and sleep problems [@nhs-menopause-things-you-can-do; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

The clearest evidence comes from the Study of Women's Health Across the Nation (SWAN), which followed a large, mixed group of US women through midlife. Among 3,302 women aged 42 to 52, those in early perimenopause, whose periods had become less regular, had higher odds of **irritability, nervousness and frequent mood changes** than women whose cycles hadn't changed. That held after the researchers allowed for other factors. Interestingly, early perimenopause was not linked with simply feeling "blue" [@bromberger-swan-mood-symptoms-2003]. So the typical pattern is a shorter fuse and moods that shift quickly, rather than a steady low.

Mood swings on their own don't confirm perimenopause. They are more likely to be part of it if they arrive in your 40s along with changes to your periods, [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/), [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) or [poor sleep](/symptoms/insomnia/). Our [perimenopause early signs](/perimenopause/early-signs/) guide covers the rest of the picture.

## Why does perimenopause cause mood swings?

Researchers think several things add up:

- **Changing hormone levels**: In SWAN's survey of 16,065 women, feeling tense, low and irritable was most common in early perimenopause, when periods become irregular, and less common after menopause. The higher risk in early perimenopause remained after allowing for hot flashes and sleep problems, which points to the transition itself playing a part [@bromberger-swan-psychologic-distress-2001].
- **Hot flashes, night sweats and lost sleep**: A 2026 review of the research found that women with heavier hot flashes and night sweats were more likely to have depressive symptoms, and several studies suggested broken sleep explains part of the link. The studies can't prove cause and effect [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026]. See [waking at 3am](/symptoms/waking-at-3am/) for help with broken nights.
- **Midlife life stresses**: Guidelines on perimenopausal depression advise doctors to consider stresses common at this age, alongside menopause stage and symptoms [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018].

## Who is more likely to get them?

- **People who have had depression before**: Most women who have a major depressive episode during perimenopause have had one earlier in life [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018].
- **People with ongoing low mood before menopause**: In SWAN, women with moderate or high depressive symptoms before their final period were more likely to have them afterward. High anxiety, sleep problems, frequent hot flashes and low social support before the final period were also linked with more depressive symptoms after it [@kravitz-swan-postmenopause-depression-2022].
- **Social circumstances**: In SWAN, the link between early perimenopause and low, unsettled mood was strongest in women who had not finished high school [@bromberger-swan-mood-symptoms-2003].

## Mood swings, irritability, anxiety or depression?

These often overlap, and each has its own guide:

- **[Irritability and rage](/symptoms/irritability/)**: a short fuse or sudden anger that feels out of character.
- **[Anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/)** and **[panic attacks](/symptoms/panic-attacks/)**: worry, tension, or sudden waves of fear with a racing heart.
- **[Low mood and depression](/symptoms/depression/)**: depression is a low, anxious or "empty" mood or loss of interest that lasts most of the day, nearly every day, for at least 2 weeks [@nimh-depression-booklet]. Perimenopause is a time of higher risk for depression [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018].

Mood swings that come and go are common. A low mood that settles in and stays is different and deserves a proper check.

## What helps mood swings in menopause

| Option | Evidence grade | What it involves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)** | :grade{id="cbt-mood"} | A short course of talking therapy. NICE recommends menopause-specific CBT for mood symptoms, and the NHS says CBT helps low mood, anxiety and sleep [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-menopause-things-you-can-do] |
| **Treating night sweats and poor sleep** | :grade{id="treat-vms-for-mood"} | Mood symptoms track with hot flash burden, partly through sleep; see [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) and [non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/) [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026] |
| **Hormone therapy (HRT)** | :grade{id="mht-depressive-symptoms"} | Can be considered for low mood that starts alongside other menopause symptoms; it is not a treatment for diagnosed depression [@nice-ng23-recommendations] |
| **Antidepressants** | :grade{id="depression-care"} | Standard care for diagnosed depression; the NHS says they may be offered for menopause-related low mood or anxiety if HRT isn't right for you [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-menopause-treatment-2026] |
| **"Calming" herbal supplements** | :grade{id="otc-mood-supplements"} | Not recommended by The Menopause Society [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |

3. Track your mood for 2 to 4 weeks next to your periods, hot flashes and sleep in the [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/), so you and your doctor can see the pattern.

## When to see a doctor

Book an appointment if:

- mood swings are affecting your relationships, work or daily life
- your mood is low, anxious or "empty" most of the day, nearly every day, for 2 weeks or more [@nimh-depression-booklet]
- you've had depression before and notice it coming back [@maki-perimenopausal-depression-guidelines-2018]
- you'd like to talk about HRT, CBT or other treatment [@nice-ng23-recommendations]

You can bring your mood log and our [questions to ask your doctor](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/). If you feel dismissed, see [what to do when your doctor dismisses your symptoms](/care/when-your-doctor-dismisses-you/).
