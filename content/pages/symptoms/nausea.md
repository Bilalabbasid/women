Nausea isn't an established symptom of perimenopause or menopause, and research on it is thin: guidelines don't count digestive symptoms among the key menopause symptoms, and few studies have looked at nausea [@shaw-gi-symptoms-menopause-scoping-review-2025]. What is well documented is that HRT and some non-hormonal hot flash medicines can cause nausea as a side effect [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023; @brisdelle-prescribing-information]. Because nausea has many possible causes, including pregnancy, which is still possible in perimenopause, nausea that keeps coming back is worth checking with a doctor [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea].

**Menopause nausea at a glance**

- **Is it a menopause symptom?**: Not an established one. Of 122 studies on digestive symptoms around menopause, 19 looked at nausea and only 4 at vomiting [@shaw-gi-symptoms-menopause-scoping-review-2025].
- **How common**: In one US online survey of 1,051 women aged 40 to 60 who weren't taking hormones, 18.9% reported nausea or vomiting [@im-gi-symptoms-midlife-women-2021].
- **HRT**: The NHS lists feeling sick as a side effect of both estrogen and progestogen; it often goes away after a few weeks [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023].
- **Pregnancy**: Feeling or being sick is a common early sign of pregnancy, usually from around 4 to 6 weeks [@nhs-signs-of-pregnancy].
- **What helps**: Fresh air, sips of a cold drink, ginger or peppermint tea, and smaller, more frequent meals [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea].
- **Emergency signs**: Sudden nausea with chest pain, pain spreading to your arms, back, neck or jaw, or shortness of breath could be a heart attack [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea].

## Is nausea a sign of perimenopause or menopause?

Probably not on its own, though the honest answer is that nobody knows for sure. A 2025 review mapped all the research it could find on digestive symptoms in natural perimenopause and postmenopause. It found 122 studies, but most looked at constipation (58 studies); only 19 included nausea and 4 included vomiting [@shaw-gi-symptoms-menopause-scoping-review-2025]. The authors point out that digestive symptoms are not recognized as key menopause symptoms in international or UK guidelines, partly because the standard menopause symptom questionnaires leave them out [@shaw-gi-symptoms-menopause-scoping-review-2025]. The NHS list of menopause symptoms doesn't include nausea either [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].

The best direct figure comes from one survey. Among 1,051 US women aged 40 to 60 who hadn't taken hormones in the previous 3 months, 18.9% reported nausea or vomiting [@im-gi-symptoms-midlife-women-2021]. Women whose periods were still regular reported fewer digestive symptoms overall than women in or after perimenopause. But the study didn't report nausea by menopause stage, relied on self-reports, and took a single snapshot in time, so it can't show that menopause causes nausea [@im-gi-symptoms-midlife-women-2021].

## Why might you feel sick around menopause?

There's no proven hormonal mechanism for nausea. A few indirect links are documented:

- **Hot flashes and anxiety**: Nausea isn't really a hot flash symptom, but a hot flash can make you anxious, and anxiety can make you feel sick. Feeling sick with a hot flash is possible but very uncommon [@cleveland-clinic-hot-flashes-nausea-2023]. The NHS lists anxiety as a cause of nausea [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea]. See [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/).
- **Migraines**: The NHS says headaches and migraines can be worse than usual around menopause [@nhs-menopause-symptoms], and migraines often come with nausea [@medlineplus-headache]. See [menopause migraines](/symptoms/migraines/).
- **Digestive changes**: A 2009 review found digestive symptoms such as bloating, belly pain and changes in bowel habits are reported more in perimenopause. It suggested changing estrogen and progesterone as one possible reason, but said this still needs testing, and it didn't focus on nausea [@heitkemper-chang-ibs-hormones-2009]. More in [digestive changes in menopause](/symptoms/digestive-changes/).

## Can HRT cause nausea?

Yes. The NHS lists feeling sick (nausea) as a possible side effect of estrogen and of progestogen, the hormone added to protect the womb lining [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. MedlinePlus lists upset stomach and vomiting among estrogen side effects to tell your doctor about if they are severe or don't go away [@medlineplus-estrogen-druginfo].

These side effects usually improve with time and often go away after a few weeks. The NHS suggests carrying on for at least 3 months if possible, and seeing a GP if side effects are severe or last longer than 3 months [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. A GP may suggest a different dose, a different type of HRT, or a different way of taking it, such as patches instead of tablets [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. Our guide to [HRT side effects](/treatments/hormone-therapy/side-effects/) covers the rest.

## Can non-hormonal menopause treatments cause nausea?

Some can. Nausea is a listed side effect of several prescription medicines for hot flashes:

- **Low-dose paroxetine (Brisdelle)**: Nausea or vomiting affected 4.3% of people in trials, against 2.3% on placebo [@brisdelle-prescribing-information].
- **Venlafaxine**: An evidence summary found SNRIs caused more side effects, with venlafaxine linked to nausea, dry mouth and constipation [@aafp-ssri-snri-vms-2022]. See [SSRIs and SNRIs for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/ssris-snris/).
- **Elinzanetant (Lynkuet)**: Nausea and reflux are among its common side effects. Its label also lists nausea and vomiting among possible signs of liver injury [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. See [elinzanetant](/treatments/nonhormonal/elinzanetant/).
- **Fezolinetant (Veozah)**: The FDA says to stop taking it and contact your prescriber if you get signs of liver problems, which include nausea, vomiting, unusual tiredness, itching, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, pale stools, or pain in the upper right belly [@fda-veozah-liver-injury-2024]. See [fezolinetant](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/).

If nausea starts after a new medicine, tell your prescriber: stopping a medicine that is causing nausea is one way it gets treated [@medlineplus-nausea-vomiting-adults].

## Why do I feel sick after eating?

Nausea after meals hasn't been studied as a perimenopause symptom. The NHS says feeling sick with heartburn or bloating after eating can be a sign of acid reflux [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea]. Other digestive causes of nausea that MedlinePlus lists include slowed stomach emptying and stomach ulcers [@medlineplus-nausea-vomiting-adults].

The NHS suggests not eating too quickly, avoiding hot, fried or greasy food, not having a large drink with meals, not lying down soon after eating, and avoiding clothes that are tight around your waist [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea]. If you're also bloated, see [perimenopause bloating](/symptoms/bloating/).

## Menopause nausea and vomiting

Vomiting is the least studied digestive symptom around menopause, with only 4 studies [@shaw-gi-symptoms-menopause-scoping-review-2025], so there's no evidence that menopause itself makes people vomit. Vomiting from a stomach bug usually stops within 1 or 2 days [@nhs-diarrhoea-and-vomiting]. The main risk is losing too much fluid: dark, smelly pee or peeing less than usual can be a sign of dehydration [@nhs-diarrhoea-and-vomiting]. Frequent sips of clear liquids can help [@medlineplus-nausea-vomiting-adults].

## Could it be pregnancy?

It could, if you still have periods, even irregular ones. Menopause is reached after 12 months without a period, and from then on you can no longer get pregnant [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. Until then, pregnancy can't be ruled out. Feeling or being sick is a common early sign of pregnancy and can happen at any time of day, usually starting at around 4 to 6 weeks [@nhs-signs-of-pregnancy]. If there's any chance, a pregnancy test is a simple first step [@nhs-signs-of-pregnancy]. More in [can you get pregnant during perimenopause?](/perimenopause/pregnancy/)

## What else can cause nausea?

Most nausea has an everyday cause. The NHS and MedlinePlus list [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea; @medlineplus-nausea-vomiting-adults]:

- **Infections and food poisoning**: Often with diarrhea, vomiting or a high temperature.
- **Acid reflux**: Often with heartburn or bloating after eating.
- **Migraine**: With a headache and sensitivity to light or sound.
- **Inner ear problems**: Labyrinthitis or vertigo, with [dizziness](/symptoms/dizziness/).
- **Medicines**: Including diabetes and weight-loss medicines.
- **Alcohol, motion sickness, anxiety or recent surgery**.

Less often, nausea and vomiting come from something serious, such as appendicitis, a blocked bowel, an ulcer or a tumor [@medlineplus-nausea-vomiting-adults]. That's why nausea that doesn't settle needs a doctor's check.

## How can I relieve nausea?

Simple steps help many people. The NHS suggests getting plenty of fresh air, distracting yourself, taking regular sips of a cold drink, drinking ginger or peppermint tea, eating foods that contain ginger, and eating smaller, more frequent meals [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea]. Avoid cooking or eating strong-smelling food [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea]. If nausea keeps coming back, a GP can look for the cause and may prescribe anti-sickness medicine if needed [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea].

:::try
1. Swap one big meal for smaller, more frequent ones, and stay upright after eating [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea].
2. Keep a cold drink nearby and sip it regularly, or try ginger or peppermint tea [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea].
3. Log when nausea happens next to your periods, meals, hot flashes and any new medicines in the [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/), so you and your doctor can spot a pattern.
:::

## When to see a doctor about nausea

See a GP or your doctor if you feel sick and don't feel better within a few days, or if nausea keeps coming back [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea]. Contact a doctor promptly if you have vomited for more than 24 hours, can't keep fluids down for 12 hours or more, haven't peed for 8 hours or more, or have vomited 3 or more times in a day [@medlineplus-nausea-vomiting-adults]. Also mention nausea that started with a new medicine, or HRT side effects that last more than 3 months [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023].

:::urgent
**Call 911** if you suddenly feel sick and have chest pain that feels tight or heavy, pain that spreads to your arms, back, neck or jaw, or shortness of breath: this could be a heart attack [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea]. **Get emergency care** if you vomit blood or something that looks like coffee grounds, or might have swallowed something poisonous [@medlineplus-nausea-vomiting-adults; @nhs-diarrhoea-and-vomiting].

**Get emergency help** if you're vomiting and have a sudden, severe headache or belly pain, a stiff neck with pain looking at bright lights, green vomit, or confusion [@nhs-diarrhoea-and-vomiting].

**Get same-day advice** if you keep being sick and can't keep fluids down, or have been vomiting for more than 2 days [@nhs-diarrhoea-and-vomiting]. If you take fezolinetant or elinzanetant, stop and contact your prescriber about nausea or vomiting with tiredness, itching, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or upper right belly pain [@fda-veozah-liver-injury-2024; @lynkuet-prescribing-information].

**If you're in the UK:** call **999** or go to A&E for the emergency signs, and call **NHS 111** for the same-day signs [@nhs-feeling-sick-nausea; @nhs-diarrhoea-and-vomiting].
:::
