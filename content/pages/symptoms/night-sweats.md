Waking up at 3 a.m. drenched in sweat with a racing heart is one of the most disruptive symptoms of perimenopause. As outlined in our [menopause symptoms overview](/symptoms/), nocturnal hot flashes (night sweats) occur when fluctuating estrogen destabilizes the temperature-control center in your brain, causing your body to trigger sudden heat-release mechanisms while you sleep [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Tracking your pattern over two weeks helps differentiate typical hormonal night sweats from other medical issues and gives your clinician the clear data needed for targeted care. Many women also research over-the-counter sleep aids, so we have evaluated whether [magnesium helps menopause sleep](/does-it-work/magnesium/).

## Is this normal, and could it be perimenopause?

Yes. Night sweats are common in perimenopause and can start years before your periods stop [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. 

During the menopause transition, estrogen levels do not decline smoothly; they oscillate unpredictably. In the hypothalamus, specialized thermoregulatory neurons misinterpret normal core temperature fluctuations as overheating. Your brain sends signals to dilate surface blood vessels and activate sweat glands. You wake suddenly, feeling intense heat, followed quickly by chills and shivering as the sweat cools your skin.

## What else could cause night sweats?

While perimenopause is the most frequent cause in women in their 40s and 50s, night sweats can also stem from non-hormonal medical conditions:

- **Thyroid dysfunction**: An overactive thyroid gland (hyperthyroidism) accelerates metabolism and causes excessive sweating, heat intolerance, and heart palpitations.
- **Obstructive sleep apnea**: Repeated nighttime breathing pauses trigger sympathetic nervous system surges that produce nighttime sweating.
- **Medication side effects**: Antidepressants (such as SSRIs/SNRIs), steroids, and certain blood pressure medications frequently list night sweats as an adverse reaction.
- **Blood glucose dips**: Nocturnal hypoglycemia, particularly in individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, causes sweating and sudden awakenings.
- **Alcohol intake**: Evening alcohol consumption dilates peripheral blood vessels and alters REM sleep cycles, amplifying nocturnal sweating.

:::urgent
Seek prompt medical evaluation if your night sweats are accompanied by drenching daytime fevers, unexplained weight loss, swollen lymph nodes in your neck or armpits, persistent cough, or localized bone pain. These are red flags that require evaluation by a physician to rule out infection, autoimmune disease, or hematologic conditions.
:::

## What actually helps: evidence-graded options

Clinical trials evaluate treatments for vasomotor episodes based on how consistently they reduce symptom frequency and severity:

| Option | Evidence Grade | Clinical Trial Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Systemic Hormone Therapy (MHT)** | **Strong** | Reduces night sweats and daytime hot flashes by 75% to 90% in randomized trials [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Transdermal patches or gels deliver continuous hormone levels overnight. |
| **NK3 Receptor Antagonists** | **Strong** | Non-hormonal prescription tablets that target KNDy neurons in the hypothalamus, significantly reducing vasomotor episodes [@veozah-prescribing-information, @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. |
| **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)** | **Moderate** | Helps restructure sleep patterns and reduces the distress and arousal that follow nighttime awakenings [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. |
| **Breathable cooling bedding** | **Comfort only** | Bamboo, eucalyptus, and linen sheets wick moisture and prevent trapped body heat. They do not prevent internal hormonal surges, but they lessen waking discomfort. |

## What you can do today

1. **Lower room temperature**: Keep the bedroom between 65°F and 68°F (18°C to 20°C) with continuous low-speed airflow from a bedside fan.
2. **Dress in layers**: Choose loose, moisture-wicking sleepwear rather than heavy cotton or fleece.
3. **Move evening meals and alcohol**: Stop eating two to three hours before sleep and avoid wine or cocktails in the evening, as alcohol reliably triggers nocturnal vasodilation.
4. **Log your nights**: Record daily night sweat frequency and cycle timing with the [Two-week symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/).

## When to see a doctor and what to ask

If night sweats disrupt your sleep multiple times a week or leave you exhausted during the day, schedule an appointment with your healthcare professional.
