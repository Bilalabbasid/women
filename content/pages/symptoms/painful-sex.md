As explored in our [menopause symptoms directory](/symptoms/), pain during intercourse (dyspareunia) is one of the most common and distressing symptoms of perimenopause and menopause, affecting up to 50% of women [@nams-2020-gsm]. Sex hurts primarily because declining estrogen causes the vaginal walls to thin, lose elasticity, and produce less natural lubrication [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. While common, pain is not an inevitable reality you have to accept: dedicated lubricants, regular moisturisers, and prescription local therapies can make intimacy comfortable again [@nams-2020-gsm].

## Why does sex hurt during menopause?

The physical discomfort women experience during sex usually stems from three interconnected factors:

1. **Loss of vaginal elasticity and rugae**: Estrogen maintains the folds (rugae) that allow vaginal tissues to stretch comfortably during arousal and penetration [@nams-2020-gsm]. Without estrogen, the tissue becomes narrower and less compliant.
2. **Reduced lubrication**: Natural moisture production slows down significantly, turning normal friction into painful scraping or burning sensations [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
3. **Involuntary muscle guarding**: When intimacy has caused pain in the past, the pelvic floor muscles often reflexively clench in anticipation of discomfort, further narrowing the vaginal opening and increasing pain.

To check whether you are experiencing related symptoms such as bladder urgency or night sweats, complete our [symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/).

## Practical steps to make sex comfortable again

Clinical consensus guidelines recommend a stepwise approach to managing intercourse pain [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]:

### Step 1: Use a high-glide personal lubricant

Using a generous amount of dedicated lubricant is essential [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. Thinning vaginal tissues absorb water-based fluids rapidly, which can cause them to become sticky midway through intercourse. High-grade silicone lubricants do not absorb into skin, providing lasting glide and reducing friction without irritation.

### Step 2: Restore day-to-day tissue hydration

Lubricant helps during intercourse, but if your vaginal tissues are dry and tight day to day, friction easily causes micro-tears [@nams-2020-gsm]. Applying a polycarbophil-based vaginal moisturiser two to three times a week keeps mucosal tissues softer and more pliable between intimate moments.

### Step 3: Explore prescription medical options

When non-hormonal lubricants and moisturisers do not fully relieve pain, consult a healthcare provider about prescription medical therapies:
- **Low-dose vaginal estrogen**: Delivered via small tablets, creams, or flexible rings directly into the vagina. It restores epithelial thickness and natural moisture with minimal systemic hormone absorption [@nams-2020-gsm].
- **Vaginal DHEA (prasterone)**: An FDA-approved daily vaginal insert that local cells convert into estrogen and androgens to improve tissue integrity.
- **Oral ospemifene**: A non-hormonal selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) pill that improves vaginal tissue thickness.

### Step 4: Pelvic floor physical therapy

If pain feels like hitting a tight "wall" at the vaginal opening or involves deep pelvic aching, tight (hypertonic) pelvic floor muscles may be clenching involuntarily. A specialized pelvic health physical therapist can teach relaxation techniques and manual trigger release to alleviate muscle tension.

:::urgent
Consult a doctor promptly if you notice bleeding after intercourse (postcoital bleeding), severe sharp pelvic pain, or sores/lesions on the vulva [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. Postmenopausal bleeding is never normal and requires formal clinical examination to rule out cervical or uterine conditions.
:::

## Questions people ask

### Will painful sex go away on its own?
Unlike vasomotor symptoms like hot flashes, which often diminish over several years, the genitourinary changes causing painful sex generally progress without active management [@nams-2020-gsm]. Early treatment with moisturisers, lubricants, or local estrogen prevents worsening tissue fragility.

### Can I resume comfortable intercourse if it has been painful for years?
Yes. With consistent treatment using local estrogen or non-hormonal moisturisers to restore tissue compliance, often combined with gradual re-dilation and pelvic muscle relaxation, women can regain pain-free intimacy even after prolonged discomfort [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
