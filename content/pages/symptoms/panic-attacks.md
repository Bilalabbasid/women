A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear with physical symptoms — a racing heart, chest tightness, shortness of breath, trembling, or a feeling of losing control — that peaks within minutes [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Some women have their first panic attack during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/), even with no previous history of anxiety.

NICE guideline NG23 lists effects on mood among the core symptoms associated with menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. A 2026 systematic review of observational studies in roughly 9,900 peri- and postmenopausal women found that heavier [hot flash and night sweat](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) burden was linked with anxiety symptoms, though this evidence was weaker and less certain than the link with depressive symptoms, and no study reported an adjusted effect estimate for anxiety specifically [@neda-stepan-vms-mood-systematic-review-2026].

## Panic attack, hot flash, or heart problem?

These three can feel similar, and they can also happen together:

- **A hot flash** usually starts with heat and flushing, with a racing heart following as a side effect.
- **A panic attack** usually starts with fear, dread, or a sense something is badly wrong, with physical symptoms (racing heart, breathlessness, chest tightness) following.
- **[Heart palpitations](/symptoms/heart-palpitations/)** on their own, without fear or dread, can be either a benign vasomotor symptom or a sign of a heart rhythm problem, thyroid condition, or anemia that needs checking.

Because the physical symptoms overlap so closely, a new pattern of racing heart, chest tightness, or breathlessness should always be assessed by a clinician — not assumed to be anxiety or "just hormones."

:::urgent
Call 911 (US) or 999 (UK), or go to the emergency room, if a racing or fluttering heart does not settle, or comes with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. If you are having thoughts of harming yourself or feel unable to stay safe, call or text **988** in the US [@lifeline-988] or call **116 123** (Samaritans) in the UK [@samaritans-contact], free and confidential, 24/7.
:::

## What can help

- **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT):** Level I evidence from The Menopause Society (2023) supports CBT for menopause-associated symptoms, including mood and vasomotor distress [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Treating the vasomotor symptoms driving it:** When hot flashes and disrupted sleep are frequent, both [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) and [non-hormonal prescription options](/treatments/nonhormonal/) can reduce the physical triggers that panic attacks latch onto.
- **Ruling out other causes:** Thyroid disease, low iron, caffeine, and some medications can all mimic or worsen panic symptoms and should be checked rather than assumed.
- **Skipping unproven calming supplements:** Over-the-counter herbal blends and cannabinoids are not recommended by guidelines for menopause-associated symptoms (Levels I–II) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] — see our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/).
