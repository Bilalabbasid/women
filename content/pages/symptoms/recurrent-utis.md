UTIs that keep coming back are common after [menopause](/menopause/), because lower estrogen changes the tissues of the vagina, urethra and bladder, a condition called genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) [@medlineplus-uti-adults; @da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. Low-dose vaginal estrogen is the main prevention option guidelines suggest after menopause [@perrotta-cochrane-estrogens-recurrent-uti-2008; @nice-ng112-recurrent-uti]. Get care quickly for fever, back or side pain, or vomiting [@medlineplus-uti-adults].

## Why do I keep getting UTIs after menopause?

Menopause raises the risk of UTIs [@medlineplus-uti-adults]. As estrogen falls, the vaginal and urinary tissues become thinner and drier, and the vagina becomes less acidic (higher pH) [@nams-2020-gsm; @da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. Recurrent UTIs are one of the recognized symptoms of GSM, along with urgency, frequency and burning when you pee [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. Genitourinary changes are listed among the core symptoms of menopause in UK guidance [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

Other changes that come with lower estrogen add to the risk. A Cochrane review names a sagging bladder (prolapse), urine left in the bladder after peeing, and bladder leaks as the main factors linked to recurrent UTIs after menopause [@perrotta-cochrane-estrogens-recurrent-uti-2008]. Women are also more likely to get a UTI after sex [@medlineplus-uti-adults]. See [incontinence in menopause](/symptoms/incontinence/) if leaks are part of the picture.

## What counts as recurrent UTIs?

Doctors call UTIs recurrent when you have 2 or more in 6 months, or 3 or more in a year [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti; @perrotta-cochrane-estrogens-recurrent-uti-2008]. Contact a provider if your symptoms come back soon after a course of antibiotics [@medlineplus-uti-adults].

## Is it a UTI or bladder symptoms of menopause?

It can be hard to tell from symptoms alone. A bladder infection usually brings burning when you pee, a strong and frequent urge to go, pressure in the lower belly or back, and cloudy or strong-smelling urine [@medlineplus-uti-adults]. GSM can cause burning, urgency and frequency too [@da-silva-gsm-management-2021]. A urine test and urine culture show whether bacteria are present and which antibiotic will work [@medlineplus-uti-adults].

If tests keep coming back clear, see [bladder urgency in menopause](/symptoms/bladder-urgency/). In older people, confusion or other mental changes may be the only sign of a UTI [@medlineplus-uti-adults].

## Does vaginal estrogen prevent UTIs?

It can. In a Cochrane review, low-dose [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) reduced the number of women with recurrent UTIs who went on to get one in two small placebo-controlled trials; estrogen tablets taken by mouth did not reduce UTIs [@perrotta-cochrane-estrogens-recurrent-uti-2008]. Our grade for vaginal estrogen for preventing recurrent UTIs: :grade{id="vaginal-estrogen-recurrent-uti"}.

How much it helped varied with the product and how long it was used [@perrotta-cochrane-estrogens-recurrent-uti-2008]. Vaginal estrogen also treats the dryness and thinning behind GSM, restoring the vaginal lining [@nams-2020-gsm; @cochrane-vaginal-moisturizers]. UK guidance suggests considering it for recurrent UTIs when everyday measures alone haven't worked, in whichever form you prefer: cream, gel, tablet, pessary or ring [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti].

### Is vaginal estrogen safe?

Only a minimal amount is absorbed into the bloodstream, and serious side effects are very rare, according to UK guidance, which suggests reviewing treatment within 12 months [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti]. Side effects reported in trials included breast tenderness, spotting, discharge, and vaginal irritation, burning or itching [@perrotta-cochrane-estrogens-recurrent-uti-2008]. The Menopause Society calls low-dose vaginal estrogen generally safe, and says safety in women with breast cancer isn't yet confirmed, so those decisions involve your oncologist [@nams-2020-gsm].

## Does cranberry help prevent UTIs?

Possibly, though the evidence for women after menopause is weaker. A 2023 Cochrane review found cranberry products probably lowered the risk of confirmed UTIs in women with recurrent UTIs by about a quarter (RR 0.74), with no clear difference between juice and tablets [@williams-cochrane-cranberries-uti-2023]. The same review found little or no benefit in older people living in care homes [@williams-cochrane-cranberries-uti-2023].

UK guidance says the evidence for cranberry is uncertain and that there's no evidence of benefit for older women [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti]. Stomach side effects were about the same as with placebo [@williams-cochrane-cranberries-uti-2023]. If you try cranberry juice or D-mannose, count the sugar they contain [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti].

## What about D-mannose and other options?

These are the other prevention options, usually discussed after everyday measures and vaginal estrogen:

- **D-mannose**: a sugar sold as powder or tablets, not a medicine. UK guidance says some people may wish to try it, but the evidence comes from a single study [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti].
- **Methenamine hippurate**: a medicine UK guidance suggests considering as an alternative to daily preventive antibiotics, reviewed within 6 months [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti].
- **Preventive antibiotics**: a clinician may suggest a single dose after sex, a short course to keep at home and start when symptoms begin, or a low daily dose [@medlineplus-uti-adults]. UK guidance suggests them only when everyday measures and vaginal estrogen haven't worked or aren't suitable [@nice-ng112-recurrent-uti].
- **Moisturizers and lubricants**: these treat the vaginal dryness and painful sex of GSM, and lubricants reduce friction during sex [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm; @nams-2020-gsm]. See [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/).

## When to get help now

:::urgent
Contact a clinician right away if bladder symptoms come with **fever, chills, back or side pain, or vomiting**. These can be signs that the infection has spread to the kidneys [@medlineplus-uti-adults]. In older people, **new confusion** may be the only sign of a UTI [@medlineplus-uti-adults].
:::

With treatment, bladder infection symptoms usually ease within 24 to 48 hours; a kidney infection can take a week or longer [@medlineplus-uti-adults].
