Getting one urinary tract infection (UTI) after another during [menopause](/menopause/) or [postmenopause](/postmenopause/) — often requiring repeated rounds of antibiotics — is a classic part of the **genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)** [@nams-2020-gsm; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Why low estrogen makes UTIs recur

In premenopausal years, estrogen supplies glycogen to the vaginal lining, feeding protective *Lactobacillus* bacteria that keep vaginal pH acidic. As estrogen falls, *Lactobacilli* decline, pH rises, and the tissues of the urethra, bladder trigone, and vaginal entrance become thinner and more fragile [@nams-2020-gsm; @cochrane-vaginal-moisturizers]. Also note that GSM itself can cause burning and urinary urgency even when a urine culture is negative, so confirming whether an infection is present is important [@nams-2020-gsm].

## Evidence-graded prevention and care

1. **Low-dose local [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/):** Graded **Strong evidence** in GSM guidelines for restoring vaginal mucosal maturation, lowering pH, and supporting healthy urogenital tissue [@nams-2020-gsm; @cochrane-vaginal-moisturizers].
2. **First-line non-hormonal tissue care:** Regular non-hormonal [vaginal moisturisers and lubricants](/care/moisturizers-and-lubricants/) reduce mechanical irritation and friction during sex that can trigger post-coital urinary symptoms [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. See also [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) and [incontinence in menopause](/symptoms/incontinence/).
3. **Targeted medical workup:** Always complete a urine culture for suspected infections, and use our [treatment conversation builder](/tools/treatment-questions/) and [Does It Work? directory](/does-it-work/) to prepare for your appointment.

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Seek urgent medical care** if urinary burning or frequency comes with **fever, chills, back or flank pain (below the ribs), nausea/vomiting, confusion, or visible blood in your urine**, as these can be signs of a kidney infection (pyelonephritis).
:::

## Questions to take to your clinician

:::questions
- Are my recurrent urinary symptoms confirmed bacterial UTIs on culture, or is genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) causing ongoing urethral irritation?
- Would low-dose local vaginal estrogen (cream, tablet, or ring) help prevent future UTIs for me?
:::
