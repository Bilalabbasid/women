Spotting is common during perimenopause, but it shouldn't be written off as "just hormones". In a large US study, two in three women had several runs of spotting during the menopause transition [@paramsothy-swan-bleeding-patterns-2014]. Even so, ACOG says bleeding or spotting between periods or after sex is not normal [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq], and the NHS says to always get it checked. It is not usually serious [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. After 12 months without a period, any spotting needs a doctor [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].

**Perimenopause spotting at a glance**

- **How common**: In SWAN, 66.8% of women reported at least three episodes of 6 or more days of spotting during the menopause transition [@paramsothy-swan-bleeding-patterns-2014].
- **Is it normal?**: Lighter, heavier, shorter, longer and skipped periods are all typical, but spotting between periods or after sex is not [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq].
- **Common causes**: Hormonal contraception, polyps or fibroids, and infections such as chlamydia; after sex, also cervical changes and vaginal dryness [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024].
- **Usually not serious**: But bleeding between periods or after sex can sometimes be a sign of cancer, which is why it gets checked [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024].
- **On HRT**: Spotting is a common side effect and usually settles within 6 months [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023].
- **After menopause**: Any bleeding, even a single spot, should be checked by a doctor [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].

:::urgent
**Get urgent help** if you've recently missed a period and have unusual vaginal bleeding with pain anywhere in your tummy, including low pelvic pain: it could be an ectopic pregnancy and needs checking as soon as possible [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. In the UK, ask for an urgent GP appointment or call 111. Also contact a clinician if you soak through one or more pads or tampons every hour for several hours in a row [@cdc-heavy-menstrual-bleeding], or if bleeding makes you feel weak or lightheaded, or comes with fever or lower tummy pain [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding].
:::

## Is spotting normal in perimenopause?

It's common, but it isn't something to assume is harmless. Periods change a lot in perimenopause: they can get shorter or longer, lighter or heavier, come closer together or further apart, or be skipped [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. Cleveland Clinic notes that cycles can become unpredictable, with spotting or missed periods [@cleveland-clinic-perimenopause].

The best data on how often spotting happens come from the Study of Women's Health Across the Nation (SWAN). Among 1,320 women who kept daily bleeding calendars through midlife, 66.8% had at least three episodes of spotting that lasted 6 days or more [@paramsothy-swan-bleeding-patterns-2014]. An older Massachusetts study found short bleeding or spotting episodes were more common early in perimenopause [@harlow-menstruation-menopause-transition-2011].

Here is where the evidence runs thin. The SWAN summary doesn't say whether those spotting days fell between periods or just before or after a period [@paramsothy-swan-bleeding-patterns-2014], and we found no study that says how often spotting in perimenopause turns out to be from hormone changes rather than something else. That is why guidance is cautious. ACOG lists bleeding or spotting between periods or after sex as not normal [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq], and Mayo Clinic, which calls irregular periods in perimenopause usually nothing to worry about, still advises seeing a professional for bleeding between periods [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].

So spotting alone doesn't tell you that perimenopause has started. UK guidance identifies perimenopause from age 45 when hot flashes or night sweats begin alongside a change in your cycle [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. For the wider picture of changing cycles, see [irregular periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/).

## Why does perimenopause cause spotting?

In perimenopause the ovaries release an egg in some months and not in others [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. Irregular or absent ovulation is a recognized cause of abnormal bleeding, and it becomes more common as menopause approaches [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]. In SWAN, cycles that were unusually short or long were more likely to be ones where no egg was released [@harlow-menstruation-menopause-transition-2011].

The SWAN calendar study also found that long runs of spotting were linked with the stage of the transition, with uterine fibroids, and with hormone use [@paramsothy-swan-bleeding-patterns-2014]. In other words, hormone changes play a part, but so do other things a doctor can check for.

## What else can cause spotting between periods?

The NHS lists these as common causes of bleeding between periods [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]:

- **Hormonal contraception**, such as the pill
- **Polyps or fibroids**: small growths in and around the womb
- **Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)**, such as chlamydia

Other causes a doctor may look for include adenomyosis (lining tissue growing into the muscle of the womb), an IUD, thyroid problems, polycystic ovary syndrome, a bleeding disorder, and medicines such as blood thinners [@medlineplus-vaginal-uterine-bleeding]. ACOG adds a thinned womb lining (endometrial atrophy) and a thickened one (endometrial hyperplasia) [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq].

Less often, the cause is cancer, such as cancer of the womb lining, which is why the NHS says to always get unusual bleeding checked [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq; @nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024].

## Spotting after sex

Bleeding after sex has its own short list of causes: changes on the surface of the cervix (cervical ectropion), small growths on the cervix (polyps), vaginal dryness, and STIs such as chlamydia [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. Lower estrogen makes vaginal tissue thinner and drier [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Moisturizers and lubricants are the first things to try for dryness, with low-dose vaginal estrogen for symptoms that persist [@nams-2020-gsm]. See [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) and [painful sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/). Bleeding after sex still needs a check, even if dryness seems the likely reason [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024].

## Spotting instead of a period

A light bleed where you expected a period can be part of perimenopause, because flow can get lighter as well as heavier [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq; @nhs-menopause-symptoms]. Short bleeding or spotting episodes are more common early in the transition [@harlow-menstruation-menopause-transition-2011].

Rule out pregnancy first. Getting pregnant is harder in perimenopause but still possible while you're having periods [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026], and implantation bleeding can happen at the start of a pregnancy [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. A pregnancy test is one of the first checks a doctor or nurse is likely to do [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. If you also have tummy or pelvic pain after a missed period, get urgent help (see the box above). Hormonal contraception is also a common cause of bleeding between periods [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. More on [pregnancy in perimenopause](/perimenopause/pregnancy/).

## Spotting when wiping: where is the blood coming from?

Blood on the toilet paper can come from the vagina, the urinary tract or the bottom, and the NHS notes that bleeding from the vagina or bottom can be mistaken for blood in the urine [@nhs-blood-in-urine-2026]. Clues to look for:

- **From the bottom**: bright red blood after a poo, often with itching or lumps around the anus, is a common sign of piles [@nhs-piles-2026]. The NHS advises an urgent GP appointment for painless bleeding from the bottom, and A&E or 999 if bleeding won't stop or there are large clots [@nhs-piles-2026].
- **In your pee**: blood in urine may look bright pink, red or dark brown. It needs an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111, even if there's only a little or you're not sure it's blood [@nhs-blood-in-urine-2026]. A burning pain when peeing and needing to go often point to a urine infection [@nhs-blood-in-urine-2026]; see [recurrent UTIs](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/).
- **From the vagina**: spotting that shows up only when you wipe counts as bleeding between periods and should be checked [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024].

## Spotting on HRT or vaginal estrogen

Unexpected bleeding or spotting is a known side effect of both the estrogen and the progestogen in [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/), and it usually settles within 6 months [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. With sequential (cyclical) combined HRT, a bleed at the end of each progestogen course is expected; this planned bleed is different from unexpected spotting [@nhs-types-of-hrt]. Vaginal estrogen can also cause unexpected bleeding in the first 3 to 6 months [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-side-effects-2023].

Speak to a doctor about irregular bleeding that goes on for more than 6 months, bleeding that gets heavier, or bleeding that starts after your periods had stopped for a while [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. A doctor may suggest changing the dose or type of HRT [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. See [HRT side effects](/treatments/hormone-therapy/side-effects/).

## Spotting after menopause

Once you've gone 12 months without a period, any vaginal bleeding, even a single spot, needs checking by a doctor [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. It is usually not serious, but it can be a sign of cancer, so book an appointment rather than waiting to see if it happens again [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. Our guide to [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/) covers the causes and what the checks involve, and the [12-month counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) tracks how long it's been since your last period.

## What helps spotting?

The right treatment depends on the cause, so finding the cause comes first [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. Spotting from HRT usually settles within 6 months [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023], and spotting after sex from vaginal dryness may ease with moisturizers, lubricants or vaginal estrogen [@nams-2020-gsm]. Polyps, fibroids and infections are looked for with the checks described below [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. If heavier flow is part of the picture, see [heavy periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/heavy-periods/) for treatments.

:::try
1. Write down the date, how many days it lasts, how much there is, and whether it follows sex. Tracking your bleeding over several cycles helps your doctor [@acog-heavy-menstrual-bleeding-faq]; the [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/) keeps it in one place.
2. If there's any chance you could be pregnant, take a home pregnancy test, since pregnancy is still possible while you're having periods [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
3. If you take HRT or hormonal contraception, note when you started or last changed it, because that affects how bleeding is assessed [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023].
:::

## When to see a doctor about spotting

See a GP, gynecologist or sexual health clinic if you have any bleeding between periods or after sex [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024; @acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. The NHS treats this as non-urgent, unless you have the warning signs in the box above [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]. US guidance also lists bleeding that lasts longer than 7 days and periods that usually come less than 21 days apart [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026], and the [irregular periods](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) guide covers those patterns.

A doctor or nurse will ask about your symptoms and medical history. The NHS lists these possible checks [@nhs-bleeding-between-periods-after-sex-2024]:

- a pregnancy test and a blood test
- an STI test, from a urine sample or a vaginal swab
- an examination of the vagina and cervix
- if needed, an ultrasound scan, a colposcopy (a closer look at the cervix) or a hysteroscopy (a thin camera inside the womb)

ACOG adds that an endometrial biopsy, a small sample of the womb lining, may be taken [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq].
