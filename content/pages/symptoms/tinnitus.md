Ringing, buzzing, hissing, or humming in the ears — tinnitus — is a symptom some women notice for the first time around menopause. We want to be upfront here: this is one of the symptoms on this site where the evidence is genuinely **limited and mixed**, not because the topic hasn't been studied, but because the studies that exist don't point in a consistent direction.

## What the evidence actually says

A 2020 review focused specifically on hearing and balance in peri- and postmenopausal women found that **worldwide publications on hearing loss and dizziness in this population are inconsistent**, and that reports describing how sex hormones influence the ear and its aging are **similarly controversial** [@orendorz-fraczkowska-hearing-balance-menopause-2020]. Where the review does find a clearer signal is indirect: hearing and balance impairment appears significantly related to **osteoporosis**, which is itself a well-established consequence of estrogen decline after menopause — rather than tinnitus or hearing changes being a direct, independent effect of hormone loss on the ear [@orendorz-fraczkowska-hearing-balance-menopause-2020].

The review explicitly concludes that **further detailed study is necessary** to clarify how hormone replacement therapy, specific hormone types, and bone health together affect hearing and balance in postmenopausal women [@orendorz-fraczkowska-hearing-balance-menopause-2020]. In plain terms: there isn't yet a settled answer, and we'd rather tell you that clearly than overstate a hormonal explanation the research doesn't firmly support.

## Other common causes worth ruling out

Tinnitus at midlife has several well-established causes that have nothing to do with menopause specifically:

- **Cumulative noise exposure** over a lifetime, which is one of the most common tinnitus causes
- **Age-related hearing loss (presbycusis)**
- **Certain medications**, including some pain relievers, antibiotics, and diuretics (ask your pharmacist if a new medication coincided with onset)
- **Earwax buildup**, a simple and treatable cause
- **Jaw joint (TMJ) problems**
- **High blood pressure**, which can sometimes cause a pulsing type of tinnitus

## When to get it checked

See a doctor or audiologist for tinnitus that is **new, one-sided, pulsing in time with your heartbeat, or accompanied by hearing loss or dizziness** — these patterns deserve a proper hearing and balance evaluation rather than being assumed to be hormonal. If you're also experiencing [dizziness](/symptoms/dizziness/) or [headaches](/symptoms/headaches/) alongside the ringing, mention all three together, since that combination is worth evaluating as a pattern rather than separately.
