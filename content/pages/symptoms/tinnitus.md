Tinnitus, hearing ringing, buzzing or hissing that has no outside source, is common at midlife, but research hasn't shown that menopause causes it [@nidcd-tinnitus; @orendorz-fraczkowska-hearing-balance-menopause-2020]. Studies of hormone therapy and tinnitus point in opposite directions [@chen-hrt-tinnitus-taiwan-2018; @lee-hrt-tinnitus-korea-2017]. A hearing check is a sensible first step, and sound therapy and talking therapy can make it easier to live with [@nidcd-tinnitus].

## Can menopause cause tinnitus?

It may play a part for some women, but the research hasn't settled it. A 2020 review of hearing and balance in women around and after menopause found the studies inconsistent, and reports on how sex hormones affect the ear just as conflicting [@orendorz-fraczkowska-hearing-balance-menopause-2020].

The clearest link the review found was indirect: hearing and balance problems were tied to thinning bones (osteoporosis), which becomes more common as estrogen falls [@orendorz-fraczkowska-hearing-balance-menopause-2020]. It called for more detailed studies before drawing conclusions about hormones [@orendorz-fraczkowska-hearing-balance-menopause-2020].

Tinnitus itself is common. Surveys estimate 10 to 25% of adults have it, and most people with tinnitus have some hearing loss [@nidcd-tinnitus]. In a Korean national survey, 22.2% of women past menopause reported tinnitus [@lee-hrt-tinnitus-korea-2017].

## Does HRT help or make tinnitus worse?

Nobody knows yet, because the two largest studies found opposite results:

- **Taiwan health records**: among 55,680 women aged 45 to 79, fewer hormone therapy users developed tinnitus (0.43% vs 0.59%), about half the risk once other factors were accounted for [@chen-hrt-tinnitus-taiwan-2018].
- **Korean national survey**: among 2,736 women past menopause, longer hormone therapy use was linked to more tinnitus [@lee-hrt-tinnitus-korea-2017].

Both studies looked at patterns in large groups, so they show links, not cause and effect, and neither tested HRT as a tinnitus treatment [@chen-hrt-tinnitus-taiwan-2018; @lee-hrt-tinnitus-korea-2017]. There is no FDA-approved medicine for tinnitus [@nidcd-tinnitus]. If you're weighing hormone therapy for other symptoms, see [is HRT right for me?](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/)

## What else causes ringing in the ears at midlife?

A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- **Hearing loss**: from aging or loud noise, and strongly linked to tinnitus [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Noise exposure**: work, concerts or sporting events [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Medicines**: especially at high doses, including ibuprofen, naproxen and aspirin, some antibiotics, some cancer medicines and antidepressants [@nidcd-tinnitus]. Some antidepressants (SSRIs and SNRIs) are also used for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Earwax or an ear infection**: both can block the ear canal and are treatable [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Jaw joint problems**: clenching or grinding can cause or worsen it [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Blood vessel problems**: including high blood pressure [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Other conditions**: diabetes, migraine, thyroid problems, anemia, and Ménière's disease, an inner ear problem that also affects balance and hearing [@nidcd-tinnitus]. If you also feel dizzy, see [menopause dizziness](/symptoms/dizziness/).

Some people develop tinnitus with no known cause [@nidcd-tinnitus].

## Does tinnitus go away?

It can. Tinnitus may improve or go away over time, but sometimes it gets worse, and it counts as long-term (chronic) once it lasts 3 months or more [@nidcd-tinnitus]. When there's a fixable cause, such as earwax or a jaw problem, treating it can stop the noise or greatly reduce it [@nidcd-tinnitus].

No study has followed tinnitus that starts around menopause to see whether it fades afterward, so we can't say how long it lasts.

## What helps tinnitus?

There is no medicine made for tinnitus, but several approaches can make it less noticeable and less upsetting [@nidcd-tinnitus].

:::try
1. Avoid total silence: play soft music, nature sounds or a fan, especially at bedtime [@nhs-tinnitus; @nidcd-tinnitus].
2. Protect your ears from loud noise and avoid loud background noise [@nidcd-tinnitus; @nhs-tinnitus].
3. Try relaxing: deep breathing or yoga may help [@nhs-tinnitus].
4. Keep a bedtime routine and cut down on caffeine [@nhs-tinnitus].
5. Ask your pharmacist or doctor whether any medicine you take is linked to tinnitus [@nidcd-tinnitus].
:::

- **Hearing aids**: a main option if you have hearing loss, because they make outside sounds louder and the tinnitus less noticeable [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Sound therapy**: tabletop or phone sound generators, or small devices worn in the ear [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)**: helps change how you react to the noise; studies show it can improve well-being [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Tinnitus retraining therapy**: counseling plus low-level sound to help your brain tune the noise out [@nidcd-tinnitus].
- **Supplements**: none advertised for tinnitus has been proven to work [@nidcd-tinnitus].

If tinnitus is keeping you awake, see [insomnia in menopause](/symptoms/insomnia/).

## When to see a doctor about tinnitus

See your primary care doctor if tinnitus is regular or constant, getting worse, or affecting your sleep, focus or mood [@nhs-tinnitus]. They'll check for earwax or infection and review your medicines, and may refer you to an ear, nose and throat doctor (ENT) or an audiologist for a hearing test [@nidcd-tinnitus].

Get a prompt appointment if the noise beats in time with your pulse [@nhs-tinnitus]. An ENT may order a scan for this type, which often has a cause that can be found and treated [@nidcd-tinnitus].

:::urgent
Sudden hearing loss, all at once or over a few days and often in one ear, is a medical emergency: see a doctor right away, because treatment works best when it starts early [@nidcd-sudden-deafness]. Call 911 if ringing comes with sudden weakness or numbness in your face, trouble speaking, confusion, or sudden dizziness or loss of balance, as these can be stroke signs [@cdc-stroke-signs-2026]. Get urgent care for tinnitus after a head injury [@nhs-tinnitus].
:::

If you're in the UK: see a GP if your tinnitus is regular, getting worse or bothering you, and ask for an urgent GP appointment if it beats in time with your pulse. The GP can check your ears and hearing and refer you to a specialist; NHS treatments include CBT, tinnitus counselling and, for severe tinnitus, tinnitus retraining therapy. Go to A&E or call 999 for tinnitus after a head injury, or with sudden hearing loss, face weakness or a spinning feeling (vertigo) [@nhs-tinnitus].
