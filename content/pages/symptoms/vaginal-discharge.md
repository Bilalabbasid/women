Yes, some vaginal discharge is normal in perimenopause and after menopause. The NHS says discharge keeps the vagina clean and moist, you can get it at any age, and it is usually fine if it is clear or white with no strong smell [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024]. The amount changes with hormone levels, and menopause can change it too [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025; @cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026]. A new color, smell or texture, itching, or any blood after menopause needs checking [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024; @nhs-menopause-symptoms].

**Discharge in perimenopause at a glance**

- **What's normal**: Clear or white, no strong or unpleasant smell, and either thick and sticky or slippery and wet [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024].
- **Why it changes**: The amount varies through the cycle as hormone levels change, and ovulation makes it wetter [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025; @nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024].
- **After menopause**: Low estrogen thins the vaginal lining. This can cause dryness and a slight discharge with itching or burning [@medlineplus-vaginal-dryness-2025; @acog-vaginitis-faq-2025].
- **Signs of infection**: A fishy smell, cottage-cheese texture, green, yellow or frothy discharge, or discharge with pelvic pain or bleeding [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024].
- **Blood**: After 12 months without a period, pink or brown discharge counts as postmenopausal bleeding and needs a doctor's check [@nhs-menopause-symptoms].
- **Hormone treatment**: The NHS lists HRT among the things that can lead to thrush [@nhs-thrush-2023].

:::urgent
**Get checked by a doctor if you have any bleeding after menopause.** That includes spotting or pink or brown discharge, even if it happened once or you're not sure it's blood [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. It is not usually serious, but it can be a sign of cancer [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. Read more about [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).

**Contact a doctor right away** if you have discharge with a fever or pain in your pelvis or belly, or you may have been exposed to a sexually transmitted infection (STI) [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025].
:::

## Is discharge normal in perimenopause and menopause?

Yes, in most cases. Glands in the cervix and vaginal walls normally make clear mucus, which can turn white or yellow when it meets the air [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025]. Normal discharge can be watery, sticky, gooey, thick or pasty, and may have a mild smell [@cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026].

Everyone's normal is a bit different. What matters is a change from what is usual for you: in amount, color, smell or texture [@cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026].

## Why does discharge change in perimenopause?

Several things can change it. Be aware that the research here is thin. We found no study that tracked discharge through perimenopause, and the NHS list of menopause symptoms mentions vaginal dryness but not extra discharge [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]. So there's no reliable picture of how a typical perimenopause affects discharge. What is known:

- **Your cycle still has an effect.** In perimenopause, the ovaries release an egg in some months and not in others, which is also why [periods become irregular](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq]. Discharge is often clearer, wetter and easier to notice around ovulation [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024].
- **Hormone levels shift.** The amount of discharge rises and falls with hormone levels, and hormonal changes including menopause can change it [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025; @cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026].
- **Treatments and contraception.** Discharge is usually heavier if you're using birth control [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024]. The NHS lists HRT among the things that can lead to thrush [@nhs-thrush-2023], and having an IUD makes bacterial vaginosis (BV) more likely [@nhs-bacterial-vaginosis-2026].

### Why might discharge increase?

More discharge than usual is one of the reasons the NHS gives for getting advice [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024]. Increased discharge with a strong fishy smell is the main symptom of BV, and vaginitis (inflammation of the vagina) can cause a large amount of discharge [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025]. If you take HRT, vaginal discharge is listed as a common side effect (up to 1 in 10 people) in the leaflet for one estradiol gel [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026]. It was also reported in a 3-year trial of progesterone capsules taken with estrogen [@prometrium-prescribing-information].

### Why is my discharge watery?

Watery discharge is often normal, especially around ovulation, when discharge can become extra slippery and wet [@cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026]. Thin, watery, greyish-white discharge with a fishy smell, often stronger after sex, points to BV [@nhs-bacterial-vaginosis-2026]. A change in discharge is also listed among the symptoms of womb and cervical cancer. The NHS says these symptoms are common and can be caused by many different conditions, but they should be checked by a doctor [@nhs-womb-cancer-symptoms-2024; @nhs-cervical-cancer-symptoms-2024].

## What do different discharge colors mean?

Color on its own can't tell you the cause. The NHS advises against diagnosing yourself and says to get advice if you're worried [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024].

| Color or texture | What it may mean |
| --- | --- |
| **Clear, milky white or off-white** | Usually normal [@cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026] |
| **Thick and white, like cottage cheese, with itching** | Thrush (a yeast infection) [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024; @nhs-thrush-2023] |
| **Greyish-white or grey, thin, fishy smell** | Bacterial vaginosis [@nhs-bacterial-vaginosis-2026; @acog-vaginitis-faq-2025] |
| **Green, yellow or frothy** | Trichomoniasis, an STI [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024] |
| **Cloudy, yellow or green** | Sometimes gonorrhea or chlamydia [@cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026] |
| **Brown, pink or red** | If it isn't your period, it may mean a problem [@cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026]. After menopause, always get it checked [@nhs-menopause-symptoms] |

## What is normal discharge after menopause?

After menopause, the vagina tends to become drier. Estrogen keeps vaginal tissue lubricated, and the lining normally makes a clear, lubricating fluid. When estrogen falls after menopause, the tissue slowly shrinks and thins, causing dryness and inflammation [@medlineplus-vaginal-dryness-2025]. This is known as vaginal atrophy or atrophic vaginitis. When it happens around menopause, it is called the genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025]. Our [vaginal dryness guide](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) explains it in more detail.

Atrophy is not an infection, but it can cause discharge and irritation, along with dryness, itching, burning and [pain during sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/) [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025]. MedlinePlus describes the discharge as slight [@medlineplus-vaginal-dryness-2025].

Atrophy can also make infections more likely. Vaginal dryness raises the chance of yeast and bacterial infections, and of [urinary tract infections](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/) [@medlineplus-vaginal-dryness-2025]. After menopause, the vagina becomes less acidic and has fewer lactobacilli, its usual bacteria [@van-gerwen-bv-postmenopausal-2023].

Diagnosis can be tricky. One expert review found that reported rates of BV after menopause range from 2% to 57%. The authors think these are overestimates, because the standard tests were validated only in premenopausal women. In their opinion, someone after menopause with typical symptoms and a raised vaginal pH should be treated for low estrogen first, before BV is considered [@van-gerwen-bv-postmenopausal-2023]. This is expert opinion rather than trial evidence.

## What else can cause discharge changes?

When discharge isn't normal, thrush and BV are the most common causes [@cleveland-clinic-vaginal-discharge-2026]. The main causes are:

- **Bacterial vaginosis**: an imbalance of vaginal bacteria. It isn't classed as an STI, but sex can trigger it. Half of women with BV have no symptoms, and it often comes back within a few months of treatment [@nhs-bacterial-vaginosis-2026].
- **Thrush**: causes itching and soreness, and stinging during sex or when peeing. It is more likely with antibiotics, poorly controlled diabetes, a weakened immune system or HRT [@nhs-thrush-2023].
- **STIs**: trichomoniasis, chlamydia, gonorrhea and genital herpes can all change discharge [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024]. Condoms lower the risk [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025].
- **Irritation**: chemicals in detergents, fabric softeners, feminine sprays, douches and some contraceptive products can irritate the vagina or the skin around it [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025].
- **A forgotten tampon or other object**: this can cause a foul smell [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025].
- **Less common causes**: skin conditions such as lichen planus, and cancer of the vulva, cervix, vagina, uterus or fallopian tubes [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025].

## What helps?

Treatment depends on the cause, so getting the right diagnosis comes first [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025]. There is no graded treatment table here, because the treatment is for the cause rather than for discharge itself:

- **BV**: usually antibiotic tablets, gels or creams [@nhs-bacterial-vaginosis-2026].
- **Thrush**: antifungal medicine, which should clear it within 7 to 14 days [@nhs-thrush-2023]. Over-the-counter treatment is safe and often works. But if the problem is something else, it won't help and may delay the right diagnosis. ACOG advises seeing a doctor the first time you have vaginal symptoms [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025].
- **Trichomoniasis**: metronidazole tablets, with sex partners treated too [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025].
- **Vaginal atrophy (GSM)**: estrogen applied inside the vagina [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025]. Over-the-counter lubricants and vaginal moisturizers can help with dryness [@medlineplus-vaginal-dryness-2025]. See [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) and [moisturizers and lubricants](/care/moisturizers-and-lubricants/). Vaginal estrogen can itself cause discharge or a vaginal infection as a side effect [@perrotta-cochrane-estrogens-recurrent-uti-2008; @nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-side-effects-2023].

:::try
1. Wash the skin around your vagina gently with warm water and a mild, unperfumed soap. Don't douche, and avoid perfumed gels, deodorants and scented wipes [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024].
2. Wear cotton underwear and loose-fitting clothes so more air reaches the area [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025].
3. Note changes in color, smell, amount and itching next to your periods in the [symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/), so you can describe the pattern to your doctor.
:::

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor, gynecologist or sexual health clinic if:

- your discharge changes color, smell or texture, or you have more than usual [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024]
- you feel itchy or sore, have pain when peeing or pelvic pain, or bleed between periods or after sex [@nhs-vaginal-discharge-2024]
- you have thrush-like symptoms for the first time, you're over 60, it comes back more than 4 times in 12 months, or treatment hasn't worked [@nhs-thrush-2023]
- symptoms get worse or last longer than 1 week despite home care [@medlineplus-vaginal-itching-discharge-2025]
- you notice any blood or pink or brown discharge after menopause [@nhs-menopause-symptoms]

To make the tests accurate, don't use vaginal medicines for at least 3 days before your appointment. Also avoid douching, sex and spermicides beforehand [@acog-vaginitis-faq-2025].
