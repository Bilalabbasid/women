As cataloged in our [menopause symptoms directory](/symptoms/), vaginal dryness after menopause happens because declining estrogen levels cause vaginal tissues to become thinner, less elastic, and produce less natural lubrication [@nams-2020-gsm]. Unlike hot flashes, which usually improve over time, vaginal dryness typically persists without active management, but non-hormonal moisturisers, personal lubricants, and low-dose prescription vaginal estrogen provide reliable relief [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].

## Why does vaginal dryness happen?

During your reproductive years, circulating estrogen maintains a thick, moist vaginal lining rich in glycogen, which supports beneficial lactobacilli bacteria and keeps the vaginal pH acidic (typically 3.8 to 4.5) [@nams-2020-gsm]. 

When estrogen levels drop during perimenopause and postmenopause:
1. **The tissue thins**: The mucosal lining becomes significantly thinner, more fragile, and prone to micro-tears or irritation [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
2. **Moisture declines**: Natural fluid secretion decreases, leading to sensations of tightness, itching, burning, and friction [@nams-2020-gsm].
3. **The pH rises**: Less glycogen means fewer lactobacilli, causing the pH to rise above 5.0, which increases susceptibility to irritation and urinary tract infections [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].

Clinicians refer to this collection of changes as the **genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)** [@nams-2020-gsm].

## What can help: options compared

Medical societies recommend starting with non-hormonal comfort measures, adding prescription options if symptoms remain persistent or interfere with daily life [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].

| Approach | How it works | Frequency of use | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vaginal moisturiser** | Binds water to vaginal tissue to restore continuous moisture | 2 to 3 times weekly | Daily dryness, burning, and general discomfort |
| **Personal lubricant** | Reduces friction on skin surfaces temporarily | During sexual activity | Discomfort or friction specifically during intercourse |
| **Prescription vaginal estrogen** | Rebuilds local tissue thickness, elasticity, and acidic pH | Regular prescribed schedule | Moderate-to-severe dryness not relieved by non-hormonal options |

### 1. Vaginal moisturisers for ongoing hydration

Vaginal moisturisers are designed for continuous, everyday comfort [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. They contain water-binding polymers or hyaluronic acid that adhere to the mucosal lining, keeping tissues hydrated for several days per application [@cochrane-vaginal-moisturizers].

If daily dryness causes discomfort while sitting, walking, or wearing tight clothing, regular application of a non-hormonal moisturiser provides a sensible first step.

### 2. Personal lubricants for sexual comfort

While moisturisers work continuously over several days, lubricants are formulated for immediate friction relief during intimacy [@nams-2020-gsm]. Thinning vaginal tissues require significant lubrication to prevent painful friction and micro-tears during sex [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].

High-grade silicone lubricants provide long-lasting surface glide because silicone does not evaporate or absorb into tissue during use.

### 3. Prescription local estrogen

When over-the-counter moisturisers and lubricants do not provide enough relief, medical guidelines recommend low-dose prescription vaginal estrogen (available as creams, tablets, or flexible rings) [@nams-2020-gsm]. Local estrogen delivers minimal systemic absorption while directly restoring cellular layers, natural lubrication, and healthy acidic pH [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. Discuss with your clinician whether local therapy is appropriate for your medical history.

:::urgent
If you experience any postmenopausal bleeding (even slight spotting), unusual foul-smelling discharge, severe pelvic pain, or pain when urinating, consult a doctor promptly [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. Postmenopausal bleeding is never a normal symptom of menopause and requires medical evaluation.
:::

## Questions people ask

### How long does a vaginal moisturiser take to work?
Most women notice improved comfort within one to two weeks of consistent use (applied two to three times per week) [@cochrane-vaginal-moisturizers]. Because moisturisers hydrate existing cells rather than stimulating new cell growth, ongoing use is required to maintain tissue hydration.

### Can I use a moisturiser and lubricant together?
Yes. Many women use a vaginal moisturiser two to three times a week for baseline comfort, and apply a silicone or water-based lubricant immediately before intercourse to reduce friction [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
