Waking at almost exactly the same time every night — often 2 or 3 a.m. — and then struggling to fall back asleep is one of the most specific complaints of [perimenopause](/perimenopause/). It's closely related to, but not quite the same as, general [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/): many women describe falling asleep easily, then waking abruptly hours later.

A brief vasomotor surge can trigger this kind of awakening even without noticeable sweating, which is why the pattern can feel confusing — you wake up, but you don't feel like you had a [night sweat](/symptoms/night-sweats/). A 2026 clinical review notes that sleep disturbance in perimenopause frequently occurs alongside fatigue and cognitive concerns as part of an overlapping symptom cluster linked to fluctuating hormone levels, rather than as an isolated sleep problem [@stute-nonhormonal-transition-2026].

## What else can cause it

Before assuming every early-morning waking is hormonal, it's worth ruling out:

- **Sleep apnea**, which becomes more common around menopause and often goes undiagnosed
- **Anxiety or rumination**, which can both cause and be worsened by middle-of-the-night waking — see [menopause anxiety](/symptoms/anxiety/)
- **Caffeine or alcohol**, especially in the afternoon or evening
- **Bladder urgency** waking you directly, rather than a vasomotor surge

## What can help (graded by evidence)

| Option | Evidence grade | What guidelines say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)**, including menopause-specific CBT | **Strong** | NICE recommends it specifically for night-time awakening linked to vasomotor symptoms; The Menopause Society rates it Level I [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |
| **[Hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/)** | **Strong** | The most effective treatment when nocturnal vasomotor surges are the trigger [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy] |
| **[Non-hormonal prescription options](/treatments/nonhormonal/)** (gabapentin, SSRIs/SNRIs) | **Strong** | Level I evidence for sleep disruption linked to vasomotor symptoms [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |
| **Breathable [sleepwear](/care/sleepwear-for-night-sweats/)** | **Limited** | A practical comfort measure, not a treatment that stops the underlying vasomotor surge |
| **Over-the-counter [magnesium](/does-it-work/magnesium/) and herbal sleep supplements** | **Not supported** | Not recommended by The Menopause Society (2023) for vasomotor-linked sleep disruption (Levels I–II) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |

## Track your wake times

A two-week sleep log in our [private symptom tracker and doctor report](/tools/symptom-tracker/) — noting the exact wake time, whether you felt hot, and the day before's caffeine and stress — gives your clinician something concrete to work from.
