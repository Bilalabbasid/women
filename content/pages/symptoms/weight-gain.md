Menopause doesn't seem to speed up weight gain on the scale, but it does change your body's makeup: fat builds faster, muscle starts to shrink, and more fat settles around the belly [@greendale-swan-body-composition-2019; @davis-ims-weight-gain-menopause-2012]. Strength and impact training has good evidence for building strength and bone [@liftmor-trial-bone-density]. A doctor can also check for other causes, such as an underactive thyroid [@nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms].

## What changes in your body

If your waistline is changing during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) or [menopause](/menopause/) even though your habits haven't, this is a common shift in the body, not a personal failing.

- **Fat gain speeds up and muscle declines**: In SWAN, a large US study that tracked women for years, the rate of fat gain doubled as women entered the menopause transition and muscle began to shrink. Both changes continued until about 2 years after the final period, then leveled off. Total weight kept climbing at the same steady rate, with no sudden jump [@greendale-swan-body-composition-2019].
- **More fat deep in the belly**: In a 4-year study of 156 women, everyone gained some fat under the skin of the belly with age, but only women who went through menopause had a clear rise in the deeper fat around the organs (visceral fat) [@lovejoy-visceral-fat-menopause-2008]. A 2012 International Menopause Society review also linked the hormone changes of menopause to more total and abdominal fat [@davis-ims-weight-gain-menopause-2012].
- **Burning a little less energy**: In the same 4-year study, energy used during sleep fell more in women who reached menopause than in those who didn't (7.9% vs 5.3%), and their fat burning dropped by 32%. Physical activity also fell about 2 years before menopause and stayed low [@lovejoy-visceral-fat-menopause-2008].

## Why it matters for your health

The International Menopause Society review found that extra weight at midlife is linked to a higher risk of heart disease and metabolic disease (such as type 2 diabetes), and can affect quality of life and sex life [@davis-ims-weight-gain-menopause-2012]. That's a reason to look at strength, activity, and heart health, not just the scale.

## What helps (and what doesn't)

- **Strength and impact training**: In the LIFTMOR trial, postmenopausal women with low bone density did 30-minute supervised high-intensity resistance and impact sessions twice a week for 8 months. Their spine and hip bone density, muscle strength, and physical function improved compared with low-intensity home exercise [@liftmor-trial-bone-density] :grade{id="strength-training-bone"}. The trial measured bone and strength, not weight. Exercise guidance for women in midlife also stresses strength training that gets gradually harder, plus enough protein, to protect muscle and bone [@sims-next-level].
- **Staying active as activity drops**: Because activity and energy use tend to fall around menopause, the authors of the 4-year study say these changes can lead to weight gain if lifestyle changes aren't made [@lovejoy-visceral-fat-menopause-2008].
- **Weight and hot flashes**: The Menopause Society lists weight loss among the options it recommends for hot flashes, based on limited evidence and expert opinion (Levels II–III) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Hormone therapy**: The 2012 review found studies mostly show less overall body fat with estrogen therapy, but said more research is needed to know which women benefit before making clinical recommendations [@davis-ims-weight-gain-menopause-2012]. See [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) for its main uses, benefits, and risks [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **"Metabolism booster" and "fat burner" pills**: The Menopause Society does not recommend over-the-counter supplements or herbal remedies for menopause symptoms [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] :grade{id="hormone-balance-supplements"}. See [Do "hormone balance" supplements work?](/does-it-work/hormone-balance-supplements/).

## Things you can start this week

:::try
1. Add more movement to your days, since activity tends to drop in the years around menopause [@lovejoy-visceral-fat-menopause-2008].
2. Build strength work into your week. The best-studied program was supervised, so ask about a supervised class if you have low bone density [@liftmor-trial-bone-density].
3. Include some protein across your meals to help protect muscle [@sims-next-level].
4. Track sleep, energy, and workouts alongside your weight, so you notice changes in strength, not just the scale.
:::

## When to see a doctor

A doctor can check for other causes and look at your overall health. Book an appointment if:

- Weight gain comes with feeling very tired, feeling cold more than usual, low mood, constipation, or dry skin or hair. These can be signs of an underactive thyroid, which a blood test can check [@nhs-underactive-thyroid-symptoms].
- You'd like to check your heart health and risk of related conditions as your body changes in midlife [@davis-ims-weight-gain-menopause-2012].
