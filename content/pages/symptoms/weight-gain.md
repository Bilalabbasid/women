Noticing weight redistributing around your middle during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/) — even when your eating and activity habits have not changed — is a biological shift in **body composition**, not a personal failing [@sims-next-level].

As ovarian estrogen declines, fat storage shifts toward the abdomen while age-related loss of lean skeletal muscle (**sarcopenia**) reduces resting metabolic rate [@sims-next-level]. Chronic sleep deprivation from [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) and [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) compounds the problem by increasing daytime fatigue and appetite.

## What actually helps (and what doesn't)

1. **Prioritize muscle and bone strength over crash dieting:** Severe calorie restriction without resistance training strips away lean muscle and bone mineral density. Supervised high-intensity resistance and impact training (HiRIT) is proven in randomized trials to build muscle strength, functional performance, and spine/hip bone density in postmenopausal women [@liftmor-trial-bone-density], and exercise physiology frameworks emphasize progressive resistance training and protein intake [@sims-next-level]. Read [Does exercise help menopause symptoms?](/does-it-work/exercise/) and our review of [*Next Level* by Dr Stacy Sims](/care/menopause-books/).
2. **Protect sleep:** Treating nocturnal hot flashes and [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) makes consistent movement and recovery far easier [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
3. **Skip "menopause weight loss" pills:** MenoVerdict bans diet pills and "metabolism booster" supplements; over-the-counter menopause supplements are not recommended by clinical guidelines [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] — see [Do "hormone balance" supplements work?](/does-it-work/hormone-balance-supplements/).
