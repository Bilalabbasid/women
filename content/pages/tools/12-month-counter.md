Both the international **STRAW+10 staging criteria** [@straw-10-2012] and **UK NICE guideline NG23** [@nice-ng23-recommendations] define natural menopause retrospectively: you reach it after **12 consecutive months without a menstrual period** (when not using hormonal contraception). Use the private counter below to track the days since your last period — and to see when bleeding needs a medical check.

## Why the 12-month mark matters — and when the clock resets

During the late menopausal transition, intervals of **60 days or more** without a period are normal and last an average of **1 to 3 years** before the final menstrual period [@straw-10-2012]. If a menstrual period returns before 12 full months have passed, the 12-month countdown starts again from the first day of that new period.

Once you have gone **12 consecutive months** without a period, any subsequent vaginal bleeding or spotting is considered **postmenopausal bleeding** and should always be checked by a doctor, even if it is light or happens only once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. It is usually not caused by something serious, but a clinician must rule out endometrial hyperplasia or cancer [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Heavy periods affecting your life, severe pain, or bleeding between periods or after sex** should also be checked by a clinician [@nhs-heavy-periods].
- **Palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting:** call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text **988** (US) [@lifeline-988] or call **116 123** (Samaritans UK) [@samaritans-contact], free and confidential 24/7.
:::
