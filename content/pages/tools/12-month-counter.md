You've reached menopause once you've gone **12 months in a row without a period**, as long as you aren't using hormonal contraception [@straw-10-2012; @nice-ng23-recommendations; @medlineplus-menopause-2026]. Enter the first day of your last period below to see how many days are left to that mark. If a period comes back, log it and the count starts again.

::tool{id="12-month-counter"}

## How do I know when I've reached menopause?

You know you've reached menopause when you can look back on **12 full months with no period**, and nothing else, such as hormonal contraception, explains the gap [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. It's only clear in hindsight: on the day of your last period, nobody can tell it was the last one [@straw-10-2012]. That's why counting from your last period is the practical way to know.

Do you need a blood test? Usually not, if you're 45 or over:

- **Age 45 or over and otherwise healthy:** UK NICE guidance says a clinician can identify menopause from 12 months without a period, with no lab tests, and advises against hormone tests such as estradiol or AMH at this age [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Age 40 to 45 with symptoms, or under 40:** NICE says a clinician may consider an FSH blood test to help confirm menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## How the 12-month count is calculated

The counter uses calendar dates only:

- **Days since your last period:** today's date minus the first day of your most recent period.
- **Your 12-month date:** the same day and month, one year later.
- **Days remaining:** the days from today to that date. Once it passes, the counter says you've reached 12 months.

**Example.** Your last period started on March 3, 2026, so your 12-month date is March 3, 2027. Then a light period starts on January 10, 2027. That resets the count: the new 12-month date is January 10, 2028. If you log both dates, the counter always works from the latest one, and your past periods stay listed so you can see the gaps between them.

## Why the clock resets, and long gaps before the final period

During the late menopausal transition, intervals of **60 days or more** without a period are common, and this stage lasts an average of **1 to 3 years** before the final menstrual period [@straw-10-2012]. If a period returns before 12 full months have passed, the 12-month countdown starts again from the first day of that new period. Our guide to [irregular periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) explains the patterns in more detail.

## What happens after 12 months without a period?

After 12 months in a row without a period, you're **postmenopausal**, and that stage lasts for the rest of your life [@cleveland-clinic-postmenopause-2024]. Some symptoms can carry on for a while, though. In the large US SWAN study, frequent hot flashes and night sweats lasted a median of **4.5 years** after the final period [@swan-vms-duration-avis-2015].

From this point on, any vaginal bleeding or spotting counts as **postmenopausal bleeding** and should always be checked by a doctor, even if it is light or happens only once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. It usually isn't caused by anything serious, but it can be a sign of cancer, so it needs checking [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. Read more in our guides to [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/) and [life after menopause](/postmenopause/life-after-menopause/).

## When the 12-month rule doesn't apply

- **Hormonal contraception, a hormonal IUD or HRT that changes bleeding:** a year without periods can't confirm menopause on its own [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Ask your clinician how they'd assess your stage.
- **A hysterectomy:** there are no periods to count, so NICE says menopause is identified from the type and pattern of symptoms, such as hot flashes [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Under 40:** missed periods at this age need a medical assessment, including for [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).

See [how menopause is diagnosed](/menopause/diagnosis/) for what clinicians check in each of these situations.

## Can it predict when you'll reach menopause?

No, and be wary of calculators that claim to. Among US women, the average age at natural menopause was **49.9 years** in 2015 to 2018, and about 1 in 9 (11.8%) reached it between 40 and 45 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. Averages like these describe groups, not you. This counter only measures time since your last period. For more on timing, see [the average age of menopause](/menopause/age/).

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Heavy periods affecting your life, severe pain, or bleeding between periods or after sex** should also be checked by a clinician [@nhs-heavy-periods].
- **Palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting:** call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text **988** (US) [@lifeline-988] or call **116 123** (Samaritans UK) [@samaritans-contact], free and confidential 24/7.
:::
