Both the international **STRAW+10 staging criteria** [@straw-10-2012] and **UK NICE guideline NG23** [@nice-ng23-recommendations] identify natural menopause looking back: you reach it after **12 consecutive months without a menstrual period**, when you aren't using hormonal contraception. Enter the first day of your last period and the counter shows the days since, the days left to the 12-month mark and the date it falls on. If a new period starts, log it and the count restarts from there.

::tool{id="12-month-counter"}

## How the 12-month count is calculated

The counter uses calendar dates only:

- **Days since your last period:** today's date minus the first day of your most recent period.
- **Your 12-month date:** the same day and month, one year later.
- **Days remaining:** the days from today to that date. Once it passes, the counter says you've reached 12 months.

**Example.** Your last period started on 3 March 2026, so your 12-month date is 3 March 2027. Then a light period starts on 10 January 2027. That resets the count: the new 12-month date is 10 January 2028. If you log both dates, the counter always works from the latest one, and your past periods stay listed so you can see the gaps between them.

## Why the clock resets, and long gaps before the final period

During the late menopausal transition, intervals of **60 days or more** without a period are common, and this stage lasts an average of **1 to 3 years** before the final menstrual period [@straw-10-2012]. If a period returns before 12 full months have passed, the 12-month countdown starts again from the first day of that new period. Our guide to [irregular periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) explains the patterns in more detail.

Once you have gone **12 consecutive months** without a period, any later vaginal bleeding or spotting counts as **postmenopausal bleeding** and should always be checked by a doctor, even if it is light or happens only once [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. It usually isn't caused by anything serious, but a clinician needs to rule out conditions of the womb lining. Read more in our guide to [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).

## When the 12-month rule doesn't apply

- **Hormonal contraception, a hormonal IUD or HRT that changes bleeding:** a year without periods can't confirm menopause on its own [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Ask your clinician how they'd assess your stage.
- **A hysterectomy:** there are no periods to count, so clinicians go by age and symptoms instead.
- **Under 40:** missed periods at this age need a medical assessment, including for [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).

See [how menopause is diagnosed](/menopause/diagnosis/) for what clinicians check in each of these situations.

## Can it predict when you'll reach menopause?

No, and be wary of calculators that claim to. Among US women, the average age at natural menopause was **49.9 years** in 2015 to 2018, and about 1 in 9 (11.8%) reached it between 40 and 45 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. Averages like these describe groups, not you. This counter only measures time since your last period. For more on timing, see [the average age of menopause](/menopause/age/).

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Heavy periods affecting your life, severe pain, or bleeding between periods or after sex** should also be checked by a clinician [@nhs-heavy-periods].
- **Palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting:** call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text **988** (US) [@lifeline-988] or call **116 123** (Samaritans UK) [@samaritans-contact], free and confidential 24/7.
:::
