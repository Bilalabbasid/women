A menopause age calculator can't tell you when you'll go through menopause, because no source can predict one person's age. What it can do is show where your age sits against the **45 to 55** range in which most people reach menopause, and date your 12-month mark if your periods have stopped [@medlineplus-menopause-2026; @nice-ng23-recommendations].

::tool{id="menopause-age-calculator"}

## How the menopause age calculator works

The calculator compares your age with published figures and adds notes from the same sources. It does not add or subtract years for any answer.

- **Your age against the typical range:** most people reach menopause between 45 and 55 [@medlineplus-menopause-2026; @nhs-menopause-overview]. The calculator shows how many years you are from 45, or past 55.
- **Your 12-month date:** menopause is identified once you've gone 12 months in a row without a period, if you're 45 or over and not using hormonal contraception [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @straw-10-2012]. If you enter the first day of your last period, it gives the date one year on.
- **Notes for your answers:** short, sourced notes on early or premature menopause, family history, smoking, cancer treatment and surgery.

**Example.** You're 47, your last period started on February 10, 2026, and you've never smoked. The calculator shows you're inside the 45 to 55 range and that your 12-month date is February 10, 2027. If a period comes back before then, the count starts again from that new period [@straw-10-2012].

Nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere. Your answers disappear when you close the page.

## Can you predict when you'll go through menopause?

No. Averages describe groups of people, and none of the sources we use gives a way to turn your age, your mother's age or your habits into a date for you [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. Be wary of calculators that give a single predicted age.

What about blood tests? For people 45 and over, UK NICE guidance says not to use hormone tests such as AMH or estradiol to identify perimenopause or menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. It says an FSH test may be considered at 40 to 45 with symptoms, or under 40 when menopause is suspected [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [blood tests for perimenopause](/perimenopause/blood-tests/) for what they can and can't show.

## What is the average age of menopause?

In the US, the average is about 51 or 52, and most people reach menopause between 45 and 55 [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. The sources measure it in slightly different ways:

| Source | Average age | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| National Institute on Aging | 52 [@nia-what-is-menopause] | Average age of menopause in the US [@nia-what-is-menopause] |
| American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) | 51 [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] | Average age of the last period in the US [@acog-perimenopausal-bleeding-faq] |
| US national survey, 2015-2018 | 49.9 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021] | Natural menopause, as women reported it [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021] |

The survey's average rose from 48.4 years in 1959-1962 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021]. For more on these numbers, see [the average age of menopause](/menopause/age/).

## What age is too early for menopause?

Menopause between 40 and 44 is called **early menopause**, and before 40 it's called **premature menopause**, also known as primary ovarian insufficiency (POI) [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Early menopause isn't rare: in a US national survey, 11.8% of women with natural menopause reported it between 40 and 45 [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021].

If your periods stop or become irregular before 45, tell your doctor [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Under 40, NICE says POI is diagnosed from symptoms, such as no or infrequent periods, plus raised FSH on 2 blood tests taken 4 to 6 weeks apart [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Our guides to [early menopause](/groups/early-menopause/) and [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/) explain what happens next.

## What can make menopause come earlier?

Sometimes there's no clear reason [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026]. Things linked to earlier menopause include:

- **Family history**: early or premature menopause is more likely if it runs in your family [@nhs-early-menopause; @mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026].
- **Smoking**: menopause comes 1 to 2 years earlier in people who smoke [@mayo-perimenopause-symptoms-causes-2026], and a US survey linked current and former smoking with earlier natural menopause [@appiah-menopause-age-trends-2021].
- **Cancer treatment**: chemotherapy and radiotherapy can cause early menopause [@nhs-early-menopause]. See [menopause after chemotherapy](/groups/chemotherapy-induced-menopause/).
- **Surgery**: removing both ovaries brings on menopause [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. Removing only the uterus stops periods but doesn't cause menopause unless the ovaries are removed too [@medlineplus-menopause-2026]. See [surgical menopause](/groups/surgical-menopause/).

These links describe groups. They raise or lower the chances, but they don't set your own age.

## How do you know when you've reached menopause?

You've reached menopause when you can look back on 12 months in a row with no period, if you're 45 or over and not using hormonal contraception [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. It's only clear in hindsight, so counting from your last period is the practical way to know [@straw-10-2012]. The [12-month counter](/tools/12-month-counter/) keeps that count for you.

If hormonal contraception, a hormonal IUD or a hysterectomy affects your bleeding, periods can't show when menopause happens. After a hysterectomy, NICE says menopause is identified from your symptoms, such as hot flashes (hot flushes in the UK) [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [how menopause is diagnosed](/menopause/diagnosis/).

## When to see a doctor

- **Your periods stop or become irregular before 45**: tell your doctor [@owh-early-premature-menopause-2026].
- **You'd like to talk about symptoms**: the NHS says to see a GP about symptoms you think could be menopause [@nhs-early-menopause].

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after 12 months without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. Read more about [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
:::
