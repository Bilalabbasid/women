Answer five short questions about your age, your periods and hot flashes, and the guide shows which stage of the **STRAW+10 staging criteria** your pattern resembles [@straw-10-2012], plus how **UK NICE guideline NG23** uses the same information [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Unlike most perimenopause quizzes, it doesn't give you a score or ask for your email, and it can't diagnose you: cycle changes can have other causes that a clinician needs to check.

::tool{id="stage-guide"}

## How the stage guide reads your answers

There's no scoring. Each answer selects the part of the published criteria that applies to it:

| Your periods over the past year | What STRAW+10 calls that pattern |
|---|---|
| Regular, varying by fewer than 7 days | Late reproductive stage, before the menopausal transition |
| Cycle length differing by 7 days or more, again and again | Early menopausal transition (Stage −2), when perimenopause begins |
| A gap of 60 days or more without a period | Late menopausal transition (Stage −1), on average 1 to 3 years before the final period |
| No period at all for 12 months or more | Perimenopause has ended; natural menopause is identified looking back |

These definitions all come from STRAW+10 [@straw-10-2012]. The age question matters because NICE handles suspected menopause under 40 (as possible premature ovarian insufficiency) differently from people in their 40s [@nice-ng23], and the hot flash question matters because NICE uses new hot flashes or sweats alongside period changes to identify perimenopause at 45 and over.

### A worked example

Say you're 46, you don't use hormonal contraception, your last two cycles were 24 days and 35 days long, and you've started waking with night sweats. A difference of 7 days or more between consecutive cycles matches STRAW+10's early menopausal transition [@straw-10-2012]. And because you're 45 or over with new night sweats and changing periods, NICE advises that a clinician can usually identify perimenopause from that history without blood tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The guide shows both points so you can raise them at your appointment.

### When the guide can't place you

- **Hormonal contraception, a hormonal IUD or a hysterectomy:** bleeding no longer reflects your natural cycle, so the period-based stages don't apply.
- **Under 40:** the criteria weren't designed to identify menopause at this age, and NICE advises a specific assessment for [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).

## How the STRAW+10 staging criteria work

The Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop + 10 (STRAW+10) is the international consensus framework used in research and clinics to describe reproductive aging [@straw-10-2012]:

- **Early menopausal transition (Stage −2):** Cycle length varies by **7 days or more** in consecutive cycles, and that difference recurs within 10 cycles [@straw-10-2012].
- **Late menopausal transition (Stage −1):** An interval of **60 days or more** without a period. On average, this stage lasts **1 to 3 years** before the final menstrual period [@straw-10-2012].
- **Perimenopause:** Begins at the start of the early transition and ends **12 months after the final menstrual period** [@straw-10-2012].

For more on each stage, read our guide to [the stages of perimenopause](/perimenopause/stages/), how [perimenopause differs from menopause](/perimenopause/vs-menopause/), and what [irregular periods in perimenopause](/perimenopause/irregular-periods/) can look like.

## Why blood tests are rarely used at 45 and over

In the UK, NICE guideline NG23 advises that for otherwise healthy people aged 45 or over, perimenopause can be identified from recently started vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes and sweats) together with a change in menstrual cycles, **without laboratory tests** [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. At 45 and over, NICE specifically advises against using anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), inhibin A or B, oestradiol, antral follicle count or ovarian volume to identify perimenopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [questions to ask your doctor about perimenopause](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/) for when blood tests *are* considered, such as under 45.

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Heart palpitations that do not go away, or come with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting:** call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) or go to the emergency room [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text **988** in the US [@lifeline-988] or call **116 123** (Samaritans) in the UK [@samaritans-contact], free and confidential 24/7.
:::
