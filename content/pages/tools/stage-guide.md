Online quizzes often promise a score that tells you whether you are in perimenopause. No quiz can actually diagnose you, because cycle changes and symptoms can have other causes that a clinician needs to check. What an educational tool *can* do is show you the exact criteria researchers and doctors use — the **STRAW+10 staging system** [@straw-10-2012] and **NICE NG23 clinical guidance** [@nice-ng23-recommendations] — and where your pattern fits.

::tool{id="stage-guide"}

## How the STRAW+10 staging criteria work

The Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop + 10 (STRAW+10) is the international consensus framework used in research and clinics to describe reproductive aging [@straw-10-2012]:

- **Early menopausal transition (Stage −2):** Cycle length varies by **7 days or more** in consecutive cycles, and that difference recurs within 10 cycles [@straw-10-2012].
- **Late menopausal transition (Stage −1):** An interval of **60 days or more** without a period. On average, this stage lasts **1 to 3 years** before the final menstrual period [@straw-10-2012].
- **Perimenopause:** Begins at the start of the early transition and ends **12 months after the final menstrual period** [@straw-10-2012].

For a deeper read on how clinicians evaluate symptoms alongside cycle shifts, see our [perimenopause overview](/perimenopause/) and our guide to the [early signs of perimenopause](/perimenopause/early-signs/).

## Why blood tests are rarely used at 45 and over

In the UK, NICE guideline NG23 advises that for otherwise healthy people aged 45 or over, perimenopause can be identified from recently started vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes and sweats) together with a change in menstrual cycles, **without laboratory tests** [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. At 45 and over, NICE specifically advises against ordering anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), inhibin A or B, oestradiol, antral follicle count, or ovarian volume tests to diagnose perimenopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Look at [questions to ask your doctor about perimenopause](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/) for when blood tests *are* considered (such as under age 45).

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Heart palpitations that do not go away, or come with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting:** call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) or go to the emergency room [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text **988** in the US [@lifeline-988] or call **116 123** (Samaritans) in the UK [@samaritans-contact], free and confidential 24/7.
:::
