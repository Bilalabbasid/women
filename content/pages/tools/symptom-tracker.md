Log how you feel in under a minute, daily or once a week, and print a one-page summary for your appointment. Symptoms around menopause can range from mild to severe and come and go over months or years [@nice-ng23-recommendations], so a record over several weeks shows a clinician more than memory can. Everything stays on your device.

[Open the tracker](/app/today/)

## What you can record

- How you feel overall, on your own 1 to 5 scale
- Symptoms from a list, plus your own, each rated 1 (mild) to 5 (severe)
- Sleep, and whether you woke in the night
- Hot flashes, as a count for the day or a total for the week
- Bleeding, from spotting to heavy
- Treatments you've been prescribed, as you want to record them, and when you take or change them
- Notes in your own words

Not sure where to start? Use the [symptoms checklist](/tools/symptoms-checklist/), then add the symptoms you ticked to your tracker.

## Daily or weekly

Choose **This day** to log a single day, or **The past 7 days** for a weekly summary. Weekly entries are quicker but less precise: you enter your average sleep per night and your total hot flashes for the week. The report shows how many of each kind it includes.

## Your doctor report

The report covers the last 14, 30, 60 or 90 days. It shows your symptoms (how often you logged each one and your average rating), hot flashes per day, sleep, any bleeding you logged, your treatments as you recorded them, and your own questions. Print it or save it as a PDF from your browser.

## What the tracker doesn't do

- It doesn't diagnose, or tell you which stage you're in.
- It doesn't produce a score on a validated scale or compare you with other people.
- It doesn't recommend treatments or doses.
- Patterns it shows are things that happened at the same time, not proof that one caused the other.

## When to get help now

:::urgent
If you log something that may need prompt care, the tracker shows when to get checked. Don't wait for the tracker, though:

- **Any bleeding after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Palpitations that don't go away, or that come with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting:** call 911 or go to the emergency room [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text 988, free and confidential, 24/7 [@lifeline-988].

More options are on our [urgent help page](/urgent-help/).
:::

## Your privacy

Entries are stored in your browser's own storage on this device (IndexedDB). There's no account and no upload. Clearing your browser data deletes them, so use **Export** on the Privacy screen to keep a copy. **Delete all my data** removes everything at once.
