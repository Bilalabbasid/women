Tick the symptoms you have noticed recently, then print your list or add it to your private tracker to take to an appointment. This checklist can't tell you whether you are in perimenopause. Symptoms around menopause vary from mild to severe and can last a short or long time [@nice-ng23-recommendations], and many have other causes, so a clinician should look at the whole picture.

::tool{id="symptoms-checklist"}

## What this checklist can and can't tell you

It lists symptoms people often ask about around perimenopause and menopause, grouped so they are easy to scan. Some are well recognised. UK guidance lists changes in periods, hot flushes and sweats, vaginal dryness and other genitourinary symptoms, effects on mood, joint and muscle pain, and sexual difficulties [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Others on the list are things people commonly report and want to ask about.

What it can't do:

- It doesn't score you or count your ticks against a threshold.
- It doesn't say which stage you are in.
- It can't rule out other causes. Many of these symptoms have other explanations that a clinician can check.

## How clinicians usually identify perimenopause

In the UK, NICE advises that for otherwise healthy people aged 45 or over, perimenopause can be identified without laboratory tests when hot flashes or sweats have recently started along with any change in periods [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. At that age, NICE also advises against using several hormone and ovary tests (including AMH, inhibin, oestradiol and antral follicle count) to identify perimenopause or menopause.

For people aged 40 to 45, and for people under 40 when menopause is suspected, NICE says a blood test for follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) may be considered. If you are under 40, ask about [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/). This is UK guidance; your clinician will follow the guidance that applies where you live.

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any bleeding after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Palpitations that don't go away, or that come with chest pain, shortness of breath, or feeling faint or fainting:** call 911 or go to the emergency room [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text 988 for free, confidential support, 24/7 [@lifeline-988].

More options are on our [urgent help page](/urgent-help/).
:::

## Questions to take to your appointment

:::questions
- Could these symptoms be related to perimenopause or menopause, and what else should we check?
- Which of these symptoms are worth treating, and what are the options for me?
- Is anything on this list something that should be checked soon?
- Would tracking my symptoms for a few weeks help, and what should I record?
:::
