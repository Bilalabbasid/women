This free checklist lets you tick the perimenopause and menopause symptoms you've noticed, then print it, download a PDF, or save a dated copy on your device. There's no sign-up, and nothing you tick leaves your browser. It can't tell you whether you're in perimenopause, because many symptoms have other causes, so take it to a clinician.

::tool{id="symptoms-checklist"}

## How to use the checklist

1. **Tick what you've noticed recently**, even if it seems minor or unrelated. Symptoms with their own guide have a *Read more* link.
2. **Check the summary under "Your list".** If you tick something that needs prompt care, such as bleeding after a year without periods, the advice to get checked appears there first.
3. **Print it, download it, or save a dated copy.** Use *Print my list* or *Download PDF* for your appointment. Saved copies stay in this browser on this device, so you can fill the checklist in again in a month and see what has changed.
4. **Want more detail over time?** Use *Add these to my tracker* to copy the symptoms you ticked into the [private symptom diary](/tools/symptom-tracker/), where you can rate them daily or weekly and print a doctor report.

## What's on the list, and why

The checklist groups symptoms so they're quick to scan. Some are well recognized: UK guidance lists changes in periods, hot flashes and sweats, vaginal dryness and other vaginal and urinary symptoms, effects on mood, joint and muscle pain, and sexual difficulties, ranging from minor to severe and lasting a short or long time [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Others, such as brain fog or palpitations, are on the list so you remember to mention them.

You may have seen lists of "34 symptoms of perimenopause". That number comes from popular lists: NICE describes groups of symptoms rather than a fixed count [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Nobody has every symptom, and no number of ticks means you are, or aren't, in perimenopause.

### What it can't do

- **No score**: it doesn't count your ticks against a threshold, and it isn't a validated rating scale.
- **No stage**: it doesn't say which stage you are in. For that question, see how the research criteria describe your period pattern in the [perimenopause stage guide](/tools/stage-guide/).
- **No ruling out**: many of these symptoms have other explanations that a clinician can check.

## A worked example

Say you're 47, your periods have become irregular over the past year, and you've started waking hot and sweaty at night. You tick *Periods coming closer together, further apart, or skipped*, *Night sweats* and *Waking in the early hours*, and save the list. NICE advises that in otherwise healthy people aged 45 or over, recently started hot flashes or sweats together with a change in periods is enough for a clinician to identify perimenopause, without blood tests [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Your list, plus how long each symptom has been going on, gives that conversation a head start.

## How clinicians usually identify perimenopause

In the UK, NICE advises that for otherwise healthy people aged 45 or over, perimenopause can be identified without laboratory tests when hot flashes or sweats have recently started along with any change in periods [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. At that age, NICE also advises against using several hormone blood tests and ovary scans (such as AMH and estradiol levels) to identify perimenopause or menopause [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Aged 40 to 45**: NICE says a follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) blood test may be considered to confirm menopause when there are symptoms, including a change in periods [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Under 40**: an FSH test may be considered when menopause is suspected [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Ask about [premature ovarian insufficiency](/groups/premature-ovarian-insufficiency/).
- **Taking hormonal treatments**: NICE notes menopause can be hard to identify, and advises against an FSH test to identify menopause in people using combined hormonal contraception or a high-dose progestogen (a progesterone-like hormone) [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

Our guide to [blood tests for perimenopause](/perimenopause/blood-tests/) explains when they help. This is UK guidance; your clinician will follow the guidance that applies where you live.

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any bleeding after 12 months or more without a period** should be checked by a doctor, even a small amount or a single episode [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Palpitations that don't go away, or that come with chest pain, shortness of breath, or feeling faint or fainting:** call 911 or go to the emergency room [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text 988 for free, confidential support, 24/7 [@lifeline-988].

If you're in the UK: call 999 or go to A&E for those palpitation symptoms, or, if they've stopped, ask for an urgent GP appointment or call NHS 111 [@nhs-heart-palpitations]. Samaritans are free on 116 123, any time, day or night [@samaritans-contact].

More options are on our [urgent help page](/urgent-help/).
:::
