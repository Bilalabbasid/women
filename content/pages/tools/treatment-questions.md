Appointments are short. Choose the topics that matter most to you and the builder turns them into a printable list of questions, covering how the benefit-risk balance of hormone therapy depends on your age and time since menopause [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy], what the 2025–2026 FDA boxed-warning changes mean [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025], and what monitoring non-hormonal options such as fezolinetant need [@veozah-prescribing-information]. It never recommends a medicine or a dose.

## How to use the question builder

1. **Tick the topics you want to cover**, such as starting HRT, the FDA label change, non-hormonal options, vaginal dryness or bladder symptoms, and cost and follow-up.
2. **Tick any symptom that should be raised first.** Bleeding after a year without periods, palpitations with chest pain or thoughts of self-harm bring up advice to get help now, before anything else.
3. **Add your own notes**, such as current medicines or family history.
4. **Print the sheet, or save it with your appointment date.** Saved sheets stay in this browser on this device, so you can prepare for a follow-up and compare what you asked last time.

## What the questions cover, and why

- **Starting hormone therapy:** The Menopause Society says the benefit-risk balance is most favorable for people under 60 or within 10 years of menopause who have no contraindications [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy], so the questions ask how that applies to you, and about routes (patch, gel, spray or tablet) and protecting the womb lining if you still have one. Our guides to [whether HRT is right for you](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/) and [patches versus pills](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) cover the background.
- **The FDA label change:** the FDA asked for cardiovascular, breast cancer and dementia statements to come out of the boxed warning in November 2025 [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025] and approved the first six updated labels in February 2026 [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. Read [what the boxed-warning change means](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/).
- **Non-hormonal options:** questions about the Level I options in The Menopause Society's 2023 statement [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy], and about the liver tests fezolinetant needs before and during treatment [@veozah-prescribing-information]. See [non-hormonal treatments for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/).
- **Vaginal and bladder symptoms:** first-line moisturisers and lubricants, low-dose vaginal estrogen [@nams-2020-gsm], and supervised pelvic floor training for leaks [@nice-ng123-urinary-incontinence].
- **Cost and follow-up:** generic coverage, and when to review whether the plan is working.

## How to pair this sheet with your symptom log

1. **Bring two numbers:** the date of your last period (or how much your cycle length has shifted) and how often your most disruptive symptom happens per day or week. You can record both in our [private symptom tracker](/tools/symptom-tracker/).
2. **State your goal in the first minute:** for example, *"Night sweats are waking me three times a night and I want to compare transdermal hormone therapy with non-hormonal options."*
3. **Check the evidence before you go:** look up any option you've read about in the [menopause treatment evidence checker](/tools/does-it-work-checker/), and see our guides on [evidence-graded options for hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [questions to ask about perimenopause](/perimenopause/questions-for-your-doctor/).

## When to get help now

:::urgent
- **Any bleeding after 12 months without a period** should be checked by a doctor [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Palpitations that do not go away, or come with chest pain, breathlessness, or fainting:** call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) [@nhs-heart-palpitations].
- **If you are thinking about harming yourself:** call or text **988** (US) [@lifeline-988] or call **116 123** (Samaritans UK) [@samaritans-contact], free and confidential 24/7.
:::
