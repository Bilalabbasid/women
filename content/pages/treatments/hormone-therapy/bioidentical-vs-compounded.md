"Bioidentical" and "compounded" get used almost interchangeably in marketing, but they describe two different things — and mixing them up matters for safety. ACOG's 2023 Clinical Consensus addresses this directly: many compounding pharmacies use "bioidentical hormone" as a marketing term implying their products are more natural, and therefore safer and more effective, than FDA-approved [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) — but evidence to support those specific marketing claims is lacking [@acog-compounded-bioidentical-2023].

## What each word actually means

- **Bioidentical** describes the hormone's chemical structure — it matches what the human body produces. Several FDA-approved products, including estradiol patches and micronised progesterone, are already bioidentical.
- **Compounded** describes how and where a product is made — by a compounding pharmacy, as a custom preparation outside FDA approval, standardized manufacturing, and routine testing.

Being bioidentical doesn't require being compounded. The confusion is largely a marketing choice, not a medical one.

## What ACOG recommends

ACOG's Clinical Consensus states [@acog-compounded-bioidentical-2023]:

- **Compounded bioidentical menopausal hormone therapy should not be prescribed routinely when FDA-approved formulations exist.**
- Clinicians should counsel patients that **FDA-approved menopausal hormone therapies are recommended over compounded bioidentical products.**
- If a patient still wants a compounded product, clinicians should explain the **lack of FDA approval and the risks specific to compounding** — including that compounded preparations aren't subject to the same testing for purity, potency, and sterility as FDA-approved medications.
- High-quality randomized controlled trials comparing compounded products with FDA-approved MHT are **still needed** to properly understand their benefits and harms [@acog-compounded-bioidentical-2023].

NICE guidance reaches a similar conclusion from a different angle, recommending that patients be told plainly that the **efficacy and safety of unregulated hormone preparations are unknown** [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

If you're considering a compounded "bioidentical" product — including pellets, custom creams, or troche formulations — that's worth raising directly with your clinician alongside the FDA-approved alternatives that may address the same symptoms with established testing and monitoring.
