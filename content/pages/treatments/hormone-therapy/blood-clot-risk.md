Of all the safety questions around [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/), blood clot (venous thromboembolism, or VTE) and stroke risk are where the **route** you take it — not just the decision to take it — makes the biggest difference. NICE guideline NG23 is specific about this [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Blood clots (VTE): patch and gel are not the same as a pill

- **Transdermal HRT (patch or gel): VTE risk is not increased.**
- **Oral HRT (pills): VTE risk is increased**, and is **greater than with transdermal HRT** [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

This is one reason NICE specifically recommends **considering transdermal HRT instead of oral HRT** for people already at higher VTE risk, including those with a BMI over 30 [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See our [patch vs. pill comparison](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) for more on how the two routes differ.

If you have a strong family history of blood clots, or a known hereditary thrombophilia, NICE recommends being **referred to a haematologist for assessment before** considering HRT at all — not after a clot has already happened [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Stroke: route and dose both matter

- **Baseline stroke risk in women under 60 is very low** to begin with [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Combined HRT with transdermal estrogen:** stroke risk is unlikely to increase.
- **Combined HRT with oral estrogen:** stroke risk increases, and rises further with higher estrogen dose, longer duration of use (for example, more than 5 years), and older age at first starting HRT. NICE also notes this risk differs between ethnic groups and may be greater in Black people [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Estrogen-only HRT, oral:** stroke risk increases, rising with dose and with age at starting.
- **Estrogen-only HRT, transdermal:** stroke risk is unlikely to increase [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

See our [heart health after menopause](/postmenopause/heart-health/) guide for the broader cardiovascular picture, including why HRT isn't used to prevent heart disease even though these specific risks are route-dependent.

## What this means in practice

For most of these risks, the pattern is consistent: **transdermal (through-the-skin) estrogen carries a more favorable safety profile for clotting and stroke than oral estrogen**, which is why it's often the preferred starting point for people with any additional risk factors. This doesn't automatically rule oral HRT out for everyone — it's a conversation about your personal risk factors, not a blanket rule.
