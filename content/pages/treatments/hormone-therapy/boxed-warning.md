The FDA is removing heart disease, breast cancer, and probable dementia from the boxed ("black box") warning on hormone therapy labels. It asked for the change on November 10, 2025, and approved the first six new labels on February 12, 2026 [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. Whole-body estrogen-only products keep a boxed warning about uterine cancer [@hhs-mht-fact-sheet-2025].

## What changed on the HRT label?

The request covers every hormone therapy product that contains estrogen or a progestogen, in all forms [@fda-hrt-consumer-update-2026]. Here is where each type of product stands after the change:

| Product type | Boxed warning after the change |
|---|---|
| Whole-body estrogen plus progestogen (combined) | Removed [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @public-citizen-mht-labels-2026] |
| Whole-body estrogen alone | Kept, for endometrial (uterine) cancer only [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @hhs-mht-fact-sheet-2025] |
| Whole-body progestogen alone | Removed [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @public-citizen-mht-labels-2026] |
| Vaginal estrogen | Removed [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @public-citizen-mht-labels-2026] |

"Whole-body" (systemic) means pills, patches, gels, and sprays that reach the whole bloodstream. Vaginal estrogen is used locally for dryness and urinary symptoms.

### What was removed

- **Heart disease, breast cancer, and probable dementia**: This language comes out of the boxed warning for all products [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. The FDA also asked for the probable dementia warning to come off the labels [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Endometrial cancer**: This boxed language comes off every product type except whole-body estrogen-alone products [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **"Lowest effective dose for the shortest amount of time"**: This long-standing advice is removed [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].

### What was added

- **Timing for whole-body products**: Labels add consideration of starting hormone therapy for moderate to severe hot flashes in women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @hhs-mht-fact-sheet-2025]. Our page on [when to start HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/timing/) explains why timing matters.
- **Data for women in their 50s**: Labels for whole-body products add Women's Health Initiative (WHI) results for women aged 50 to 59 [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Shorter labels for vaginal products**: Safety information for [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) is condensed to focus on what matters most for a product used locally [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].

## When was the HRT black box warning removed?

The change is happening in steps, product by product:

- **November 10, 2025**: The FDA asked makers of all hormone therapy products, both whole-body and vaginal, to change their labels [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **By February 2026**: 29 drug companies had sent in proposed label changes [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026].
- **February 12, 2026**: The FDA approved the first six updated labels. They cover four product types: combined, estrogen-alone, progestogen-alone, and vaginal estrogen [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026].

## Has the label on my HRT changed yet?

Maybe not yet. Each company must submit its own label change, and the FDA approves them product by product. By February 2026, 29 companies had submitted changes; the first six products were approved that month [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. Your pharmacist can tell you whether the package insert for your product has been updated.

## What warnings are still on the label?

The FDA did not say hormone therapy is risk-free. These warnings stay:

- **Endometrial cancer (whole-body estrogen alone)**: The boxed warning stays on these products [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @hhs-mht-fact-sheet-2025]. In someone with a uterus, whole-body estrogen taken without a progestogen (a progesterone-like hormone) raises the risk of endometrial cancer, so The Menopause Society recommends adding a progestogen [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Heart disease and breast cancer (whole-body products)**: The FDA did not ask companies to remove these risks from the label's "Warnings and Precautions" section, only from the box at the top [@fda-hrt-consumer-update-2026; @fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Who usually shouldn't use hormone therapy**: The Menopause Society lists conditions that rule out both oral and skin forms. These include unexplained vaginal bleeding, liver disease, a past estrogen-sensitive cancer such as breast cancer, past heart disease, stroke, heart attack or blood clot, or a personal history or inherited high risk of clots [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Why did the FDA remove the black box warning?

The FDA says it has reconsidered the benefits and risks of these drugs, and that the old warning was based on trials in women who were mostly older than those who usually start hormone therapy [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025]. In 2003, the FDA began approving label changes for these products based on the Women's Health Initiative (WHI) trials, and those changes eventually added the boxed warning [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].

The FDA's reasons for changing it now:

- **Older trial participants**: The WHI trials mainly studied heart disease and other long-term conditions, and the average participant was 63 [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025]. That is older than most women who start hormone therapy for symptoms [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Women going without treatment**: The FDA says it is concerned that many women with symptoms who could benefit are not being prescribed hormone therapy [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Expert input**: The FDA held an expert panel on July 17, 2025, and then asked for public comment [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].

This fits The Menopause Society's 2022 position. For women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause who have no reason to avoid it, the benefits of hormone therapy generally outweigh the risks. Starting later is less favorable because the risks of heart disease, stroke, blood clots, and dementia are higher [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Is HRT safe now that the warning is gone?

Not for everyone. Removing the box changed how risks are presented, not the risks themselves: heart disease and breast cancer information stays in the label, and risks are greater for women who start later or further from menopause [@fda-hrt-consumer-update-2026; @menopause-society-fda-comment-2025]. Hormone therapy is still the most effective treatment for hot flashes [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy], and its evidence for them is graded :grade{id="mht-vms"} on our scale. For more on the risks that stay in the label, see [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/) and [HRT and blood clot risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/blood-clot-risk/).

## Not everyone agrees with the change

Expert groups broadly support the change for vaginal estrogen but disagree about whole-body hormone therapy:

- **The Menopause Society**: Agrees with removing the boxed warning from low-dose vaginal estrogen. It says whole-body estrogen still has potential risks for some people that should be reviewed in detail. Those risks are low for younger, healthy women who start closer to menopause, and greater for women who start older or further from menopause [@menopause-society-fda-comment-2025].
- **The International Menopause Society**: Supports removing the boxed warning from vaginal estrogen, but does not support removing all warnings from whole-body hormone therapy. It notes that whole-body therapy can raise heart disease risk, breast cancer, and dementia in some groups, especially women who start later or already have these conditions [@ims-statement-boxed-warning-2025].
- **Public Citizen (a consumer advocacy group)**: Argues the change was based on opinion rather than new safety data, and was made without a formal, independent advisory committee [@public-citizen-mht-labels-2026; @public-citizen-mht-warnings-2025]. It also says officials promoted unapproved uses, such as preventing heart disease, and points out that the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force advises against hormone therapy to prevent chronic conditions [@public-citizen-mht-warnings-2025].

## What this means for your appointment

The label change doesn't decide whether hormone therapy is right for you. That still depends on your age, how long it's been since menopause, your symptoms, and your health history [@menopause-society-fda-comment-2025; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. If a clinician once ruled out [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) because of the boxed warning, the new labels are a fair reason to ask again. Our guide to [starting and stopping HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/starting-and-stopping/) covers what the first months look like.

Demand for patches has risen since the change. In September 2026 the FDA said some people were having trouble getting their usual estradiol patch, that supply had nearly doubled over the past year, and that it was working with all six makers to increase it [@fda-estradiol-patch-availability-2026]. If your patch is hard to find, ask your pharmacist or prescriber about other strengths or forms.

## When to see a doctor

See your doctor about any vaginal bleeding after menopause, even a small amount or a single episode. It's usually not serious, but it should be checked [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding]. If you take hormone therapy, get help urgently for signs of a stroke or blood clot.

:::urgent
**Call 911 right away** for signs of a stroke [@cdc-stroke-signs]:

- Sudden numbness or weakness in the face, arm, or leg, especially on one side of the body
- Sudden confusion, trouble speaking, or trouble understanding speech
- Sudden trouble seeing, or sudden trouble walking, dizziness, or loss of balance
- A sudden severe headache with no known cause

**Seek medical help immediately** for possible signs of a blood clot in the lungs: trouble breathing, a fast or irregular heartbeat, chest pain that gets worse when you breathe in deeply or cough, coughing up blood, or feeling lightheaded or fainting [@cdc-vte-about].

**See a doctor as soon as possible** for swelling, pain, warmth, or redness in a leg or arm, which can be signs of a clot [@cdc-vte-about].

If you're in the UK: call 999 in an emergency, or NHS 111 for urgent advice.
:::
