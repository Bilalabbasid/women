In late 2025 and early 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) initiated landmark labeling modernizations for menopausal hormone therapy (MHT) products [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025, @fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. As covered in our guide to [menopause treatment options](/treatments/), the agency instructed manufacturers to remove broad risk statements regarding cardiovascular disease, breast cancer, and dementia from the prominent boxed warning (often called the &ldquo;black box warning&rdquo;).

This decision marks the most significant regulatory shift in hormone therapy since the initial 2002 Women&apos;s Health Initiative (WHI) reports. If you are tracking daily hot flashes using our [symptom diary and report](/tools/symptom-tracker/), this regulatory change provides essential context for your appointment.

## What changed on the labels?

On February 12, 2026, the FDA approved revised prescribing information for an initial cohort of six systemic and local hormone products [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. Key modifications include:

1. **Removal of cardiovascular and dementia warnings from the box**: Blanket warnings that hormone therapy causes heart disease and probable dementia have been removed from the prominent boxed text for systemic formulations.
2. **Elimination of the &ldquo;lowest dose, shortest time&rdquo; advice**: The historic recommendation that clinicians prescribe only the lowest possible dose for the shortest duration was retired [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025]. Modern clinical consensus recognizes that treatment duration should be individualized based on personal goals and ongoing symptom evaluation.
3. **Differentiation of vaginal estrogen**: Low-dose vaginal estrogen products are increasingly distinguished from systemic hormones, reflecting their minimal systemic absorption and localized safety profile.
4. **Incorporation of the &ldquo;timing hypothesis&rdquo;**: Prescribing information now reflects modern clinical evidence indicating that initiating systemic hormone therapy before age 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset yields a favorable benefit-risk profile for moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025, @menopause-society-fda-comment-2025].

## What remains in the warnings?

The FDA did not declare hormone therapy free of all risk [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. Important safety information remains in the standard &ldquo;Warnings and Precautions&rdquo; sections:

- **Endometrial cancer protection**: For women who have an intact uterus, systemic estrogen taken without a progestogen causes a clear risk of endometrial hyperplasia and uterine cancer. The boxed warning retains this requirement: a progestogen or tissue-selective estrogen complex must be co-prescribed.
- **Venous thromboembolism (blood clots)**: Oral estrogens undergo first-pass liver metabolism, which increases clotting factors. Transdermal patches and gels largely avoid this first-pass effect.
- **Contraindications**: Hormone therapy remains contraindicated for women with unexplained abnormal vaginal bleeding, active liver disease, history of stroke or myocardial infarction, or active estrogen-dependent malignancies.

## Why did it take more than 20 years to update?

The original 2002 black box warning was applied broadly to all hormone products after the primary WHI trial was halted [@ims-statement-boxed-warning-2025]. However, the average age of participants in that trial was 63—over a decade older than the typical woman entering perimenopause. 

Over the subsequent two decades, re-analyses by major endocrine and gynecological societies demonstrated that starting hormones in newly menopausal women (under age 60) does not carry the same cardiovascular risks observed in older cohorts [@menopause-society-fda-comment-2025]. The FDA&apos;s 2025–2026 actions align official product labeling with current clinical practice.
