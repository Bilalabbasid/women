Estrogen therapy replaces the estrogen (oestrogen in the UK) your body makes less of around menopause, and it is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and vaginal dryness [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. If you still have a uterus, you also need a progestogen to protect its lining [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. It comes as pills, patches, gels and sprays [@nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026].

## What does estrogen therapy do?

Falling estrogen during [perimenopause](/perimenopause/) and [menopause](/menopause/) drives many of the classic symptoms, such as [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) (hot flushes in the UK), [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) and [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/) [@nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026]. Replacing it is the core of [hormone therapy (HRT)](/treatments/hormone-therapy/), graded :grade{id="mht-vms"} on our scale. The Menopause Society calls it the most effective treatment for hot flashes and for vaginal and urinary symptoms of menopause, and says it also prevents bone loss and fractures [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Estrogen alone, or estrogen with a progestogen?

This depends on one thing: whether you still have a uterus [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Estrogen-only HRT**: Offered to people who've had a total hysterectomy, since there's no womb lining to protect [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026].
- **Combined HRT**: Estrogen plus a progestogen, offered to people who still have a uterus, because estrogen alone raises the risk of cancer of the womb lining [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See our [progesterone and progestogens](/treatments/hormone-therapy/progesterone/) page for more.

This isn't a preference call; it's a safety step based on your anatomy.

## Forms and dose

Estrogen for the whole body comes as tablets, patches, gel and spray [@nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026]. Low-dose vaginal creams, tablets and rings treat local symptoms only; see our [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) page. NICE recommends using the lowest dose that works [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

The route changes more than convenience: patches and gels don't raise blood clot risk the way pills do. See [HRT patch vs. pill](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) for the details and for how to use patches.

## Oestrogel and Evorel: UK estradiol brands explained

Oestrogel and Evorel are two of the estrogen brands the NHS lists, along with others such as Estradot, FemSeven, Lenzetto, Progynova and Sandrena [@nhs-hrt-medicines-2026]. Both contain estradiol, a form of estrogen [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026; @emc-evorel-pil-2025]. This section explains what the leaflets say; it isn't a recommendation of one brand over another.

### What is Oestrogel?

Oestrogel is an estradiol gel in a pump pack, with 750 micrograms of estradiol in each pump [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026]. Its leaflet says it is used to relieve menopause symptoms such as hot flushes, and to prevent bone thinning when other medicines aren't suitable [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026]. If you still have a womb, it's prescribed with a separate progestogen [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026].

### How do you apply Oestrogel?

Once a day, on clean, dry, unbroken skin, at about the same time each day [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026]. Your prescriber decides how many pumps you use; the leaflet says the usual dose is 2 pumps, and a pump pack then lasts 4 weeks [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026].

- **Where**: Spread it over a large area of the outer arm and shoulder of both arms, or the mid-inner thigh of both legs [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026]. The NHS also mentions the lower body, and says not to use the same area 2 days in a row [@nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026].
- **Where not**: Not on or near the breasts or genitals [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026]. Neither source lists the face or stomach, so ask your prescriber before using other areas.
- **New pump**: Press the pump a few times and throw away the first dose before first use [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026].
- **After applying**: Let it dry for 5 minutes before dressing, wash your hands, and don't wash the area or put other skin products on it for at least 1 hour [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026].
- **Other people**: Avoid close skin contact with a partner for 1 hour, and don't let children touch the area; if they do, wash their skin with soap and water [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026].
- **Missed dose**: If it's more than 12 hours until your next dose, apply it now; if less, skip it. Never double up [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026].

The gel contains alcohol, so keep away from flames and heat until it has dried [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026].

### Oestrogel side effects

Common side effects listed in the leaflet (up to 1 in 10 people) include headache, nausea, stomach pain, breast pain, heavier or irregular bleeding, vaginal discharge and weight change [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026]. Many HRT side effects ease within a few weeks to 3 months [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. See [HRT side effects](/treatments/hormone-therapy/side-effects/).

### What is Evorel?

Evorel is an estradiol skin patch: the hormone is spread through the patch and passes slowly into your body through the skin [@emc-evorel-pil-2025]. It is changed twice a week on the same two days, below the waist, such as on the thigh or bottom [@emc-evorel-pil-2025]. Evorel Conti and Evorel Sequi are different products that also contain a progestogen [@nhs-hrt-medicines-2026], and an Evorel gel also exists [@nhsbsa-hrt-ppc-medicines]. Our [HRT patches guide](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) covers where to stick patches and what to do if one falls off.

### Is Evorel 50 a high dose?

Evorel 50 is the second-lowest of Evorel's 4 strengths (25, 50, 75 and 100), and the leaflet says the highest dose is 100 micrograms of estradiol a day, the Evorel 100 patch [@emc-evorel-pil-2025]. The number is the micrograms of estradiol the patch releases each day [@emc-evorel-pil-2025]. The right dose is individual: NICE recommends the lowest dose that controls your symptoms [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

### Are Oestrogel and Evorel "body identical"?

Yes, in the sense people usually mean: both contain estradiol [@emc-oestrogel-pil-2026; @emc-evorel-pil-2025], which The Menopause Society lists among FDA-approved bioidentical hormones [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. That is different from custom-compounded "bioidentical" hormones; see [bioidentical vs. compounded hormones](/treatments/hormone-therapy/bioidentical-vs-compounded/).

### How much do Oestrogel and Evorel cost in the UK?

In England, each prescription item usually costs £9.90, and an HRT prescription prepayment certificate costs £19.80 for 12 months of unlimited qualifying HRT items, including Oestrogel and Evorel [@nhsbsa-hrt-ppc-medicines; @nhs-prescription-prepayment-certificate-2023]. Prescriptions are free in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland [@commons-library-prescription-charges-england-2026]. To see whether it would save you money, try our [HRT prepayment certificate calculator](/tools/hrt-prepayment-certificate-calculator/). For US prices, see our [HRT cost guide](/care/hrt-cost/).

## Where this fits in the bigger picture

Estrogen's benefits and risks, such as breast cancer, blood clots and stroke, vary by type, route and your personal history [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [types of HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/types/), [who HRT may suit](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/), and our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) to compare it with non-hormonal options.

## When to see a doctor

The NHS says your treatment should be reviewed after the first 3 months, and to talk to a doctor if side effects bother you or don't go away [@nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026]. Bleeding that carries on beyond the first 6 months of HRT should be checked promptly [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

:::urgent
**Call 911** for signs of a stroke, such as sudden numbness or weakness on one side, confusion, trouble speaking or seeing, or a sudden severe headache [@cdc-stroke-signs]. **Seek help immediately** for trouble breathing, chest pain that worsens when you breathe in, or coughing up blood, which can be signs of a clot in the lungs [@cdc-vte-about].

**See a doctor as soon as possible** for swelling, pain, warmth or redness in one leg or arm [@cdc-vte-about], or for yellowing of the skin or eyes [@nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026].

If you're in the UK: call 999 for possible signs of a clot in the lungs, a stroke or a severe allergic reaction, and NHS 111 for other serious side effects [@nhs-oestrogen-medicine-2026].
:::
