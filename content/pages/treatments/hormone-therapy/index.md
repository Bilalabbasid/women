**Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT)** — commonly called **hormone replacement therapy (HRT)** — is graded :grade{id="mht-vms"} on our scale. The Menopause Society calls it the most effective treatment for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/), and is proven to prevent postmenopausal [bone loss](/postmenopause/bone-health/) and fracture [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. For the bigger picture of what matters after menopause, see our [long-term health checklist](/postmenopause/health-checklist/) and [life after menopause](/postmenopause/life-after-menopause/) guide.

## How clinicians weigh benefits and risks

1. **Timing window:** For women under age 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset who have no contraindications, the benefit-risk ratio of systemic HRT is favorable [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @fda-mht-labeling-request-2025]. Starting more than 10 years after menopause or after age 60 is associated with higher absolute risks of coronary heart disease, stroke, venous thromboembolism, and dementia [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
2. **Uterine protection:** Women with an intact uterus who take systemic estrogen also need a progestogen to protect the uterine lining against endometrial hyperplasia and cancer; unopposed systemic estrogen retains an FDA boxed warning for endometrial cancer [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
3. **Individual contraindications:** Systemic HRT is generally avoided in people with a history of estrogen-sensitive [breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/), unexplained [vaginal bleeding](/postmenopause/bleeding/), active liver disease, or prior blood clots or stroke [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
