Hormone therapy (HRT) uses estrogen, plus a progestogen if you still have a uterus, and it is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. It also helps prevent bone loss and fractures [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. It is not risk-free, so your age, the time since your last period, and your health history all matter [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

Its full name is menopausal hormone therapy (MHT). On our scale, its evidence for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) is graded :grade{id="mht-vms"}. A progestogen is progesterone or a similar hormone; it protects the lining of the uterus [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The FDA shortened the boxed warning on hormone therapy labels in 2025 and 2026, but the main risks are still listed. This page sets out what HRT treats, what changed, which risks remain, and when to get help. For the bigger picture after menopause, see our [long-term health checklist](/postmenopause/health-checklist/), [bone health](/postmenopause/bone-health/) and [life after menopause](/postmenopause/life-after-menopause/) guides.

## What HRT is used for

- **Hot flashes and night sweats**: The Menopause Society calls hormone therapy the most effective treatment for these symptoms [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Vaginal and urinary symptoms**: Hormone therapy is also the most effective treatment for vaginal dryness, irritation and related urinary symptoms (genitourinary syndrome of menopause). When these are the only symptoms and over-the-counter products haven't helped, The Menopause Society points to low-dose [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) or other local therapies [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Bone protection**: Hormone therapy has been shown to prevent bone loss and fracture [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## What changed on US labels in 2025 and 2026

On November 10, 2025, the FDA asked makers of menopausal hormone therapy to change their labels [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025]:

- **Boxed warning, all products**: Remove the language on heart disease (cardiovascular disease), breast cancer, and probable dementia, and remove the advice to use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time. Language on cancer of the uterine lining (endometrial cancer) comes out of the box too, except on whole-body estrogen-alone products [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Whole label, all products**: Remove the probable dementia warning [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Whole-body (systemic) products, such as pills, patches, gels and sprays**: Add that starting therapy for moderate to severe hot flashes can be considered before age 60 or within 10 years of menopause, add Women's Health Initiative data for women aged 50 to 59, and keep the heart and blood vessel and breast cancer information in the warnings [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].
- **Low-dose vaginal estrogen**: Condense the safety information to what matters most for a local product [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025].

On February 12, 2026, the FDA approved the first revised labels for six products, covering whole-body combined, estrogen-alone and progestogen-alone products, and vaginal estrogen. By then 29 companies had submitted proposed changes [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026].

Medical groups don't all read this the same way. The Menopause Society agrees with removing the boxed warning from low-dose vaginal estrogen, but says whole-body estrogen still carries potential risks for some people that should be reviewed in detail [@menopause-society-fda-comment-2025]. The International Menopause Society does not support removing all warnings from whole-body therapy, which can raise heart and blood vessel, breast cancer and dementia risk in some groups, especially people who start later [@ims-statement-boxed-warning-2025]. The consumer group Public Citizen opposed the changes, arguing they were based on opinion rather than new safety data [@public-citizen-mht-labels-2026]. Our [boxed warning explainer](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/) covers the debate in more depth.

## Risks that remain

The box is shorter, but the risks are still on the label and in guidelines. Overall, The Menopause Society describes the extra risks from estrogen-alone and combined therapy as rare: fewer than 10 extra cases per 10,000 women each year [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

- **Cancer of the uterine lining, if you have a uterus**: In people with a uterus, estrogen-only HRT raises the risk of endometrial cancer, whether taken as a pill or through the skin [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. That's why whole-body estrogen-alone products keep a boxed warning for it [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025], and why people with a uterus are offered combined HRT (estrogen plus a progestogen) or a combination product that protects the lining without a progestogen (a tissue-selective estrogen complex) [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [progesterone and progestogens in HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/progesterone/).
- **Blood clots (VTE)**: Estrogen-alone and combined therapy are each linked to a rare increase in blood clot risk [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. NICE states that clot risk is increased with oral HRT but not with HRT taken through the skin (transdermal, such as a patch or gel) [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The Menopause Society notes that this route difference comes from studies that followed people over time, because trials comparing the routes head to head haven't been done [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. More in [HRT and blood clot risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/blood-clot-risk/).
- **Stroke**: Estrogen-alone and combined therapy are each linked to a rare increase in stroke risk [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. NICE states the stroke risk rises with oral estrogen and is unlikely to rise with estrogen taken through the skin [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Breast cancer**: NICE states breast cancer risk increases with combined HRT and rises the longer it is used, while estrogen-only HRT causes very little or no increase [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. More in [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/).
- **Starting later**: Starting more than 10 years after menopause or after age 60 is less favorable, because the risks of heart disease, stroke, blood clots and dementia are greater [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The FDA has removed the probable dementia warning from labels [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025], but The Menopause Society's 2022 statement still lists dementia among the higher risks for later starters [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [the HRT timing window](/treatments/hormone-therapy/timing/).

## Who usually can't take whole-body HRT

The Menopause Society lists these as reasons not to use hormone therapy taken as a pill or through the skin (contraindications) [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]:

- **Unexplained vaginal bleeding**: Bleeding needs to be checked first. See [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/).
- **Liver disease**: This applies to both pills and skin products [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Past estrogen-sensitive cancer**: This includes [breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/) [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Heart disease, stroke or clots in the past**: A history of heart disease, stroke, heart attack or blood clot, or a personal or inherited high risk of clots [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

If any of these apply, our guide to [who HRT may suit, and who needs specialist advice](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/) explains the options, and our [non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/) section covers alternatives.

## When to see a doctor

- **Bleeding on HRT**: If you have a uterus, some bleeding is common in the first 6 months of whole-body HRT and within 3 months of changing the dose or product. NICE advises getting medical help promptly for unscheduled bleeding outside those times [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Before starting**: Tell your clinician about any past cancer, blood clot, stroke, heart disease or liver disease, since these change whether HRT is suitable [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

:::urgent
**Possible stroke: call 911 right away** if you notice sudden numbness or weakness in the face, arm or leg (especially on one side), sudden confusion or trouble speaking or understanding, sudden trouble seeing, sudden trouble walking, dizziness or loss of balance, or a sudden severe headache with no known cause [@cdc-stroke-signs-2026].

**Possible clot in the lungs: get medical help immediately (call 911)** if you have trouble breathing, a fast or irregular heartbeat, chest pain that gets worse with a deep breath or cough, coughing up blood, or feel lightheaded or faint [@cdc-blood-clots-about-2025].

**Possible clot in a leg or arm: see a doctor as soon as possible** for swelling, pain or tenderness, warmth, or redness or discoloration in the leg or arm [@cdc-blood-clots-about-2025].

If you're in the UK: call 999 in an emergency, or NHS 111 for urgent advice.
:::
