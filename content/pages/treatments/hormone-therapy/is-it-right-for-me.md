There's no single test that says "yes" or "no" to [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) — it's a decision built from your symptoms, your timing relative to menopause, and your personal and family health history. NICE guideline NG23 gives a clear framework for working through it [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Where the benefit-risk balance tends to favor HRT

For people **under 60, or within 10 years of menopause onset**, without contraindications, both NICE and The Menopause Society (2022) describe a more favorable benefit-risk ratio [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. This is often called the "timing window" — starting later, or after age 60, shifts the balance because absolute risks of coronary heart disease, stroke, and blood clots rise with age regardless of HRT.

## Situations that call for extra care or specialist input

NICE sets out specific guidance, rather than a blanket "no," for several groups [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **Type 2 diabetes:** HRT can still be considered, after weighing comorbidities and seeking specialist advice if needed.
- **Increased risk of blood clots (VTE):** Transdermal (patch/gel) HRT is preferred over oral; people at high VTE risk should be referred to a haematologist for assessment before considering HRT. See [HRT and blood clot risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/blood-clot-risk/).
- **Personal history of coronary heart disease or stroke:** HRT should be discussed and offered, if appropriate, specifically by a healthcare professional with menopause expertise.
- **Personal history of, or high risk of, breast cancer:** Requires information on all available options and referral to a menopause specialist — see our dedicated [breast cancer survivors](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/) guide and [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/).
- **Diagnosed with breast cancer while already on HRT:** NICE recommends **stopping systemic HRT**, following its breast cancer guideline [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

In every one of these situations, NICE's actual recommendation is **referral to someone with menopause expertise**, not an automatic rule-out — the right answer depends on the details of your case [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## General contraindications worth raising directly

Beyond the specific groups above, actively unexplained vaginal bleeding, active liver disease, and a personal history of hormone-sensitive cancers are the kinds of things that need to be discussed and ruled out or addressed before starting HRT — raise these proactively rather than waiting to be asked.
