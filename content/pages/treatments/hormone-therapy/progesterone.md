If you take estrogen and still have a uterus, you also need a progestogen (progesterone or a similar medicine), because estrogen alone raises the risk of cancer of the womb lining [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. Micronized progesterone is the form identical to your body's own, usually taken as a capsule at bedtime [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @prometrium-prescribing-information].

## Do I need progesterone with estrogen?

Yes, if you have a uterus: a progestogen protects the lining of the uterus (endometrium) from cancer, and the Office on Women's Health says you need one whenever you take estrogen [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. NICE recommends combined HRT (estrogen plus a progestogen) for anyone with a uterus, because estrogen-only HRT raises endometrial cancer risk whether it is taken as a pill or through the skin [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

NICE's estimates show the difference over 5 years from age 50 [@nice-ng23-hrt-discussion-aid-2024]:

- **No HRT**: About 4 in 1,000 women with a uterus develop endometrial cancer [@nice-ng23-hrt-discussion-aid-2024].
- **Estrogen-only HRT with a uterus**: About 11 in 1,000, or 7 more [@nice-ng23-hrt-discussion-aid-2024].
- **Sequential combined HRT**: About 8 in 1,000, or 4 more [@nice-ng23-hrt-discussion-aid-2024].
- **Continuous combined HRT**: About 1 in 1,000, or 3 fewer than with no HRT [@nice-ng23-hrt-discussion-aid-2024].

With an adequate progestogen, The Menopause Society says the risk of abnormal growth or cancer of the lining is not higher than in women not taking HRT [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [estrogen in HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/estrogen/) for the other half of the picture.

### Do I need progesterone after a hysterectomy?

Usually not: NICE recommends estrogen-only HRT for people who have had a total hysterectomy, since there is no womb lining to protect [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The Office on Women's Health suggests that anyone without a uterus ask their provider whether they need progesterone [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].

### Do I need progesterone with vaginal estrogen?

Generally not, at the usual low doses: The Menopause Society says a progestogen is generally not needed with low-dose vaginal estrogen for vaginal and urinary symptoms [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. It adds that trial data on the womb lining beyond 1 year of use are lacking [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. More in our [vaginal estrogen guide](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/).

## What is micronized progesterone?

Micronized progesterone is progesterone with the same structure as the hormone your ovaries make, in a capsule taken by mouth [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @prometrium-prescribing-information]. It is available as an FDA-approved product: one brand's label says it is used to protect the womb lining in postmenopausal women with a uterus who take estrogen [@prometrium-prescribing-information].

That makes it "bioidentical" in the plain sense, but it is not the same as compounded "bioidentical" hormones mixed by a pharmacy, which The Menopause Society says lack good safety and effectiveness data [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [bioidentical vs. compounded hormones](/treatments/hormone-therapy/bioidentical-vs-compounded/).

### Progesterone vs. progestin: what's the difference?

"Progestogen" is the umbrella term; progesterone is the natural hormone, and progestins are synthetic versions. The Menopause Society lists medroxyprogesterone acetate, levonorgestrel and norethindrone acetate as synthetic progestins, and micronized progesterone as structurally identical to the body's progesterone [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. All three are commonly used with estrogen to protect the womb lining, and the dose needed depends on the progestogen's strength and the estrogen dose [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## How is progesterone taken in HRT?

There are two schedules and several forms, and the choice affects bleeding and risks [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-types-of-hrt].

- **Sequential (cyclical)**: Estrogen every day, plus a progestogen for the last 10 to 14 days of each cycle, with a bleed expected afterward. It is used while periods continue [@nhs-types-of-hrt].
- **Continuous combined**: Estrogen and a progestogen every day, usually once you have gone 1 year without a period [@nhs-types-of-hrt].
- **Capsule**: The Prometrium label gives 200 mg at bedtime for 12 days of each 28-day cycle to protect the lining [@prometrium-prescribing-information]. The Menopause Society notes oral micronized progesterone must be dosed adequately for protection [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Patch**: Some patches combine estrogen and a progestogen [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Hormonal IUD (intrauterine system)**: NICE lists it as a way to give the progestogen, and the NHS says the Mirena coil may be suitable [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-types-of-hrt]. In the US this use is off-label, with limited trial data [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

The schedules trade off risks: NICE says breast cancer risk is lower with sequential than continuous combined HRT, while womb cancer risk falls with continuous and may rise slightly with sequential [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. More in [types of HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/types/).

## What are the side effects of progesterone?

The NHS lists changes in periods or spotting, headaches, breast tenderness, nausea, diarrhea, feeling tired or dizzy, mood changes, mild rash and acne as possible progestogen side effects [@nhs-hrt-side-effects-2023]. The Menopause Society notes that mood swings with HRT are often linked to the progestogen [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

For oral micronized progesterone, the label warns of short-lived dizziness and drowsiness and advises caution with driving [@prometrium-prescribing-information]. The capsules contain peanut oil and should not be used by people allergic to peanuts [@prometrium-prescribing-information]. Side effects often ease within a few weeks to 3 months; see [HRT side effects](/treatments/hormone-therapy/side-effects/).

## Does progesterone help with sleep or hot flashes?

Possibly a little, but in HRT it is prescribed to protect the womb lining [@prometrium-prescribing-information]. A review cited by The Menopause Society found micronized progesterone helped women fall asleep faster, but did not make sleep longer or more efficient [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

For hot flashes, The Menopause Society says micronized progesterone 300 mg at night reduced them compared with placebo [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. No long-term results exist, and using a progestogen without estrogen for this is off-label [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Is micronized progesterone safer than other progestogens?

It's genuinely uncertain, and the answer may differ by risk. NICE says there isn't enough evidence to tell whether breast cancer risk with combined HRT differs between micronized progesterone and other progestogens [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Breast cancer, stroke and clots**: The Menopause Society says it is unknown whether HRT with oral micronized progesterone raises these risks as much as the progestin tested in the WHI, because trials have not been designed to test it [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Clots**: The Menopause Society says it is potentially less likely to promote clots than other progestogens, but this has not been tested in trials [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Womb lining**: With long-term use, observational studies suggest a possibly higher womb cancer risk with micronized progesterone than with other progestogens, and with cyclical than continuous schedules [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

See [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/) for the fuller picture.
