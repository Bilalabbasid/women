Starting [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) usually comes with some adjustment, and NICE guideline NG23 builds a specific review process around that expectation, rather than leaving you to simply wait and see [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Bleeding changes: expected, within limits

The side effect NICE addresses most specifically is bleeding. For people with a uterus taking combined HRT [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **Vaginal bleeding is a common side effect during the first 6 months** of taking systemic HRT
- It's also common **within 3 months** of changing the dose or type of HRT
- Beyond those windows, **unscheduled bleeding should be checked promptly** by a doctor — see [bleeding after menopause](/postmenopause/bleeding/) if you're already past the 12-month menopause mark

:::urgent
Seek medical help promptly for vaginal bleeding that falls outside these expected early-adjustment windows, rather than waiting for your next scheduled review.
:::

## Other commonly reported effects

Breast tenderness, nausea, bloating, and headaches are commonly reported when starting HRT or adjusting a dose, and tend to ease as your body adjusts. These vary a lot by individual and by formulation and route, which is one reason the standard approach is a structured review rather than guessing — see [patch vs. pill](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) and [types of HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/types/) for how switching route or formulation can sometimes address a specific side effect.

## The review schedule that's built around this

NICE recommends reviewing any menopause treatment [@nice-ng23-recommendations]:

- **At 3 months**, to check how well it's working and how you're tolerating it
- **Annually after that**, unless there's a specific reason to review sooner — such as the treatment not working, side effects, or an adverse event

If side effects persist past your 3-month review, or symptoms simply aren't improving, NICE recommends being **referred to a healthcare professional with menopause expertise** [@nice-ng23-recommendations] rather than just continuing on the same plan.
