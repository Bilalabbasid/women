Testosterone is sometimes considered as an addition to [hormone therapy](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) for women in menopause, but its evidence-based use is narrower than general marketing around it suggests. NICE guideline NG23 recommends considering testosterone specifically for **low sexual desire associated with menopause, when HRT alone is not effective** [@nice-ng23-recommendations] — see our [low libido](/symptoms/low-libido/) page for the fuller picture of that symptom, and our [DHEA evidence verdict](/does-it-work/dhea/) for a related hormone sometimes discussed alongside it.

## Why it's currently "off-label"

NICE is explicit that this is currently an **off-label use of testosterone** in the UK [@nice-ng23-recommendations] — meaning the medication exists and is prescribed, but hasn't gone through the formal approval process specifically for this use in women. That doesn't mean it's experimental or unsafe when properly prescribed; it means the regulatory pathway hasn't caught up with established clinical practice, which is why monitoring by an experienced clinician matters.

## What it doesn't treat

Testosterone for women in this context is specifically about low sexual desire, not a general energy, mood, or muscle-building treatment. Be cautious of marketing that implies broader benefits — that's not what the evidence-based guidance actually supports.

## How it's typically given, and why that matters

Testosterone for women is usually prescribed in doses far lower than those used for men, often using products formulated for men but dosed down, since women-specific formulations are limited in many markets. This makes **monitoring important** — your clinician should check levels periodically to avoid excessive dosing. See our [hormone pellets](/treatments/hormone-therapy/pellets/) page if you're specifically considering a pellet-based delivery method, which carries its own additional concerns around dosing consistency.

## What this means for you

This is a conversation to have after HRT alone hasn't resolved low libido, not a first-line addition — and it needs a clinician who will monitor your levels, not a source that doesn't.
