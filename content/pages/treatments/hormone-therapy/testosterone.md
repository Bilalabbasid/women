Testosterone can improve low sex drive after menopause when HRT alone hasn't helped, and that is the only use with good evidence behind it [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019; @nice-ng23-recommendations]. It isn't FDA-approved for women, so it's prescribed off-label [@smith-batur-androgen-therapy-ccf-2021; @fda-testosterone-menopause-workshop-2026]. It needs a clinician who checks your blood levels [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

## Who is testosterone for?

It's for postmenopausal women with distressing low sexual desire, known as hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD), after a full assessment of other causes [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. Low desire has many possible causes, including relationship difficulties, physical illness, psychological distress and cultural or religious pressures, so those are looked at first [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. Our [low libido](/symptoms/low-libido/) page covers those causes.

UK guidance from NICE recommends considering testosterone for low sexual desire linked to menopause **if HRT alone is not effective** [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. On our scale, testosterone for this use is graded :grade{id="testosterone-libido"}.

## Does testosterone help low libido? What the trials found

Yes, modestly. A 2019 review pooled 36 randomized trials with 8,480 women [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019]. In postmenopausal women, testosterone compared with placebo or another treatment:

- **Added about one satisfying sexual event a month**: The average gain was 0.85 a month [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019].
- **Improved desire, arousal, orgasm and pleasure**: It also reduced distress and worry about sex [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019].
- **Worked with or without estrogen**: Many women in the trials were also taking estrogen [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

A guideline from the International Society for the Study of Women's Sexual Health describes the benefit as moderate [@parish-isswsh-testosterone-guideline-2021]. These results apply to women with diagnosed low desire, not to women without a sexual problem [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

## What testosterone doesn't treat

International experts say there isn't enough evidence to use testosterone for any other symptom or to prevent disease [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. In the 2019 review, it had no clear effect on body composition, muscles and bones, or memory and thinking, although few women were studied for these outcomes [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019]. The FDA lists its role in mood, thinking and muscle and bone health as open questions [@fda-testosterone-menopause-workshop-2026].

So be wary of anyone selling testosterone to women for energy, weight loss or brain fog.

## Is testosterone approved for women?

Not in the US. No testosterone product for women has been approved by the FDA [@smith-batur-androgen-therapy-ccf-2021], and the FDA describes its use in menopausal women as off-label [@fda-testosterone-menopause-workshop-2026]. In the UK, NICE notes that using testosterone for low desire is off-label too [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

Off-label means the medicine is approved for something else, here mostly men's products. Where no approved women's product exists, experts say a men's product can be used at a much smaller dose, as long as blood levels stay in the normal female range [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. In the UK, the NHS says you may be able to get testosterone gel or cream on prescription from a menopause specialist [@nhs-menopause-treatment-2026].

## How is testosterone given to women?

Usually as a gel or cream on the skin [@nhs-menopause-treatment-2026]. Expert guidance prefers skin products because testosterone pills raised "bad" LDL cholesterol and lowered "good" HDL cholesterol, while skin products didn't [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019; @davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. The ISSWSH guideline recommends testosterone through the skin [@parish-isswsh-testosterone-guideline-2021].

Experts advise against:

- **Pellets and injections**: They can push levels above the normal female range [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. See our [hormone pellets](/treatments/hormone-therapy/pellets/) page.
- **Compounded testosterone**: There's a lack of evidence on how well it works or how safe it is [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019; @parish-isswsh-testosterone-guideline-2021].
- **DHEA tablets instead**: Oral DHEA hasn't been shown to improve desire or sexual function after menopause [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. See our [DHEA evidence check](/does-it-work/dhea/).

If you're also starting HRT and low desire is a concern, The Menopause Society notes estrogen through the skin may be preferred over pills, because estrogen pills reduce how much testosterone is available in the body [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Testosterone side effects in women

At doses that keep blood levels in the normal female range, side effects are usually mild [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]:

- **Acne and extra hair growth**: Some women get mild acne or more body or facial hair [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019; @islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019].
- **Weight**: The 2019 review recorded a small overall rise in weight [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019].
- **Not seen at these doses**: Hair loss, voice deepening or enlargement of the clitoris [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

No serious side effects were recorded in the trials [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019]. Those trials left out women at high risk of heart disease, though, so the results may not apply to them [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

## How is testosterone monitored, and how long does it take to work?

Experts advise a blood test for total testosterone before starting and another 3 to 6 weeks in [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. After that, levels and signs of too much testosterone are checked about every 6 months [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. If there's no benefit by 6 months, treatment should stop [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. For comparison, the trials in the 2019 review each lasted at least 12 weeks [@islam-testosterone-women-metaanalysis-2019].

A blood level isn't used to diagnose low desire, only to keep treatment in a safe range [@parish-isswsh-testosterone-guideline-2021]. That's why testosterone needs a prescriber who monitors you, not a supplier who doesn't.

## Is testosterone safe long term? Breast cancer and heart health

Long-term safety isn't known. In trials, testosterone didn't increase breast density on mammograms, and short-term use through the skin didn't appear to affect breast cancer risk [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. But trials are too short to judge long-term breast cancer risk or heart attack risk [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019]. The FDA has named the lack of long-term heart and breast cancer data as a key gap [@fda-testosterone-menopause-workshop-2026].

Women with a past breast cancer were left out of the trials, and experts recommend caution for anyone who has had hormone-sensitive breast cancer [@davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].

## What this means for you

Testosterone is a second step for low desire after HRT and other causes have been looked at, not a first-line or general "menopause hormone" [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @davis-global-consensus-testosterone-women-2019].
