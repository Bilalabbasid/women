You'll see the same window referenced across our [HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) guides: **under age 60, or within 10 years of menopause onset**. This section explains what that window actually means, and what changes if you're outside it.

## What the window actually is

The Menopause Society describes a **more favorable benefit-risk ratio** for systemic HRT in women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset, without contraindications [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Starting **more than 10 years after menopause, or after age 60**, is associated with **higher absolute risks** of coronary heart disease, stroke, blood clots, and dementia [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. NICE guidance adds a specific data point: dementia risk might increase with combined HRT if it's started at **age 65 or over** [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## The "timing hypothesis," explained

This research idea goes a step further on cardiovascular risk specifically: HRT started within that same window (10 years of menopause, or before 60) **may confer cardiovascular benefit**, while starting later **may increase cardiovascular risk** [@khalifey-hrt-cardiovascular-2026]. Importantly, a 2026 review on this exact question notes that outcomes vary by **formulation, route, and individual factors**, and that **significant uncertainties persist** in the research [@khalifey-hrt-cardiovascular-2026] — this isn't a settled, simple rule. See our [heart health after menopause](/postmenopause/heart-health/) guide for the broader cardiovascular picture, including why HRT still isn't used to *prevent* heart disease even within this favorable window.

## It's a guideline, not a wall

None of this means HRT is impossible or forbidden after 60, or more than 10 years post-menopause. It means the benefit-risk calculation shifts, and starting later is a more individualized decision — exactly the kind of situation where NICE recommends involving someone with menopause expertise [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See [who HRT may suit, and who needs specialist advice](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/) for that fuller picture.

This timing framework is also part of the broader context behind the 2025–2026 FDA label changes — see [the FDA boxed warning change, explained](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/) for how regulators and medical societies have debated this, and our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) for non-hormonal options if timing rules HRT out for you.
