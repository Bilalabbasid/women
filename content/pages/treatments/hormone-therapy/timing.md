HRT has the most favorable balance of benefits and risks when you start it before age 60 or within 10 years of menopause [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Starting later isn't ruled out, but the risks of heart disease, stroke, blood clots, and dementia are higher, so it becomes a more careful, individual decision [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## When is the best time to start HRT?

If you have bothersome symptoms, the window to aim for is before age 60 or within 10 years of menopause, as long as you have no reason to avoid HRT [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. In that window, The Menopause Society says the benefits for hot flashes and preventing bone loss generally outweigh the risks [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

US labels now say the same. Since the FDA's 2025 request, labels for whole-body [HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) add consideration of starting it for moderate to severe hot flashes in women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @hhs-mht-fact-sheet-2025]. See [the FDA boxed warning change](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/) for what else changed.

## What is the HRT timing hypothesis?

The timing hypothesis is the idea that HRT's effect on the heart depends on when you start it. Started within 10 years of menopause or before 60, it may help heart health; started later, it may add risk [@khalifey-hrt-cardiovascular-2026]. A 2026 review notes that results also depend on the type of HRT, how it's taken, and the person, and that big questions remain [@khalifey-hrt-cardiovascular-2026].

Some trial data fit the idea. A 2019 analysis of trials, cited by The Menopause Society, found that starting before 60 was linked with lower odds of heart disease and death. Starting at 60 or older showed no effect on those, but a higher risk of stroke and related events [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. For memory and thinking, the timing idea has not been proven in trials [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

HRT is still not approved to prevent heart disease, even inside the window [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Our guide to [heart health after menopause](/postmenopause/heart-health/) covers what does lower that risk.

## Is it too late to start HRT after 60?

Not automatically. The Menopause Society says starting after 60, or more than 10 years after menopause, carries more complex risks that need careful thought, but that some well-informed women in that group do choose to start or restart HRT [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. NICE recommends considering referral to someone with menopause expertise when the best option isn't clear [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

If you're weighing HRT later, these points shape the decision:

- **Higher absolute risks**: Starting after 60 or more than 10 years out is linked with more heart disease, stroke, blood clots, and dementia than starting earlier [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Dose and form**: Using the lowest effective dose, and possibly a skin form instead of a pill, matters more as women get older [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [HRT patch vs pill](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/).
- **Your own health history**: Past blood clots, stroke, heart disease, or breast cancer generally rule out HRT at any age [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [who HRT may suit](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/).

### What about starting HRT at 65 or older?

Starting at 65 or older brings an added concern about dementia. NICE says dementia risk might increase with combined HRT if it's started at 65 or over [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. That's different from continuing HRT you started earlier, covered below.

### What if hot flashes start years after menopause?

New hot flashes many years after menopause need a check-up before starting HRT. They can come from low estrogen, but The Menopause Society notes they may also come from other causes [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. A doctor can check for other causes, such as:

- Sleep apnea (pauses in breathing during sleep)
- An overactive thyroid
- Some medicines, such as antidepressants or diabetes medicines
- Withdrawal from alcohol or opioids
- Certain infections or cancers [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]

## Can you stay on HRT after 60 if you started earlier?

Yes, in many cases. The Menopause Society says HRT doesn't need to be routinely stopped at 60 or 65, and there are no data to support routinely stopping at 65 [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Longer use should include regular reviews of your health and risks [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Our guide to [starting and stopping HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/starting-and-stopping/) covers how long people stay on it and how to stop.

## Does the timing window apply to vaginal estrogen?

The window is about whole-body HRT (pills, patches, gels, and sprays). Observational studies support the long-term safety of low-dose [vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/), a very effective treatment for vaginal dryness and related urinary symptoms [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. At those low doses, a progestogen generally isn't needed [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## What if menopause came early?

Early menopause is a different situation. For women whose menopause came before 40 (including premature ovarian insufficiency), The Menopause Society recommends hormone therapy at least until the average age of menopause, about 52, unless there's a reason to avoid it [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The WHI trial results in older women don't apply to women with early or premature menopause [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
