"HRT" isn't one single product — it covers several different combinations of hormones, delivered in different ways. Here's how the main types break down, based on NICE guideline NG23 [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Combined vs. estrogen-only: which type you're offered

- **Combined HRT (estrogen + progestogen):** Offered to people who still have a **uterus**. The progestogen protects the uterine lining from the effects of estrogen alone, which can otherwise raise the risk of endometrial (uterine lining) cancer [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Estrogen-only HRT:** Offered to people who have had a **total hysterectomy**, since there's no uterine lining left to protect [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

This isn't a preference — it's based on whether you have a uterus, and it matters for safety, not just convenience.

## Within combined HRT: sequential vs. continuous

If you're taking combined HRT, there's a second choice:

- **Sequential combined HRT:** Progestogen is included for only part of each cycle, which usually produces a regular, period-like bleed.
- **Continuous combined HRT:** Progestogen is included every day, aiming to avoid a regular bleed. This is typically used once you're further past menopause.

NICE recommends discussing both options as part of choosing combined HRT [@nice-ng23-recommendations], and it's worth noting that sequential and continuous regimens also carry slightly different breast cancer risk profiles — see our [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/) page.

## The hormones themselves, and how you take them

Beyond the combined/estrogen-only split, there's variation in:

- **Which estrogen and progestogen** are used (different formulations exist, and NICE notes some comparisons, such as micronised progesterone vs. other progestogens, don't yet have enough evidence to show a clear risk difference)
- **Route:** pill, patch, gel, or spray — see our [patch vs. pill comparison](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) for how this affects safety, not just convenience
- **Dose:** NICE recommends using the **lowest effective dose** for anyone taking HRT [@nice-ng23-recommendations]

There's also a separate category — [low-dose vaginal estrogen](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/) — which treats local symptoms only and isn't part of this systemic combined/estrogen-only decision at all.

## Working out which combination fits you

The right combination depends on whether you have a uterus, your personal risk factors, and your preferences around bleeding and routine.
