There are two main types of HRT: combined HRT (estrogen plus a progestogen) for people who still have a uterus, and estrogen-only HRT for people who have had a total hysterectomy [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Either works through the whole body (systemic), while low-dose vaginal estrogen is a separate local treatment for vaginal and urinary symptoms [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## What are the main types of HRT?

HRT is grouped by which hormones it contains and where they act. Whether you have a uterus decides the first choice; this is about safety, not preference [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Combined HRT**: Estrogen plus a progestogen, for people with a uterus. The progestogen protects the uterine lining, because estrogen alone raises the risk of endometrial (uterine lining) cancer [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Estrogen-only HRT**: For people who have had a total hysterectomy, taken every day [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nhs-types-of-hrt].
- **Estrogen with bazedoxifene**: A combination product (a tissue-selective estrogen complex) that protects the uterine lining without a progestogen [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].
- **Low-dose vaginal estrogen**: A local treatment for vaginal dryness, painful sex and urinary symptoms, rather than whole-body HRT [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Its evidence for these symptoms is graded :grade{id="vaginal-estrogen-gsm"} on our scale.

## What is systemic HRT?

Systemic HRT is HRT that is absorbed into the bloodstream and acts throughout the body, which is what treats hot flashes, night sweats and bone loss [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Estrogen can be taken as a pill, through the skin as a patch, gel or spray, or as one type of vaginal ring [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. A progestogen can be a pill, a patch, or a hormonal intrauterine system (IUS) [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

### Systemic vs. local (vaginal) HRT

Local vaginal estrogen is absorbed mainly where it is placed, with only a minimal amount reaching the blood, and this is unlikely to have a significant effect on the rest of the body [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Because of that, it does not help hot flashes, mood swings or sleep problems [@nhs-types-of-hrt]. A progestogen is generally not needed with low-dose vaginal estrogen [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. More in our [vaginal estrogen guide](/treatments/hormone-therapy/vaginal-estrogen/).

## Who gets estrogen-only HRT?

Estrogen-only HRT is offered to people who have had a total hysterectomy, so there is no uterine lining to protect [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. If you have a uterus, estrogen-only HRT raises the risk of endometrial cancer, whether it is taken as a pill or through the skin [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. More in [estrogen in HRT](/treatments/hormone-therapy/estrogen/).

### Is estrogen-only HRT safer than combined HRT?

For breast cancer, NICE says estrogen-only HRT causes very little or no increase in risk, while combined HRT raises it, more so the longer it is used [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. But estrogen-only HRT is only an option without a uterus, and both types carry a rare increase in blood clot and stroke risk [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. See [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/).

## Sequential vs. continuous combined HRT

If you take combined HRT, the next choice is how the progestogen is given. NICE advises discussing both options, because their risks differ [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Sequential (cyclical) combined HRT**: Estrogen every day, plus a progestogen for the last 10 to 14 days of each cycle. A bleed is expected at the end of each progestogen course. It is used if you are still having regular periods [@nhs-types-of-hrt].
- **Continuous combined HRT**: Estrogen and a progestogen every day, with no break. It is usually recommended after menopause, once you have gone 1 year without a period [@nhs-types-of-hrt].

NICE states breast cancer risk with sequential combined HRT is lower than with continuous combined HRT, but higher than with no HRT [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. For the uterine lining it runs the other way: endometrial cancer risk falls with continuous combined HRT and may rise slightly with sequential combined HRT [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## HRT forms: pill, patch, gel, spray and ring

| Form | Systemic or local | What to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pill (tablet)** | Systemic [@nice-ng23-recommendations] | Comes as estrogen-only or combined [@nhs-types-of-hrt]. Blood clot risk is higher with pills than with HRT through the skin [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. |
| **Skin patch** | Systemic [@nice-ng23-recommendations] | Comes as estrogen-only or combined [@nhs-types-of-hrt]. Clot risk is not raised with HRT through the skin [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. |
| **Gel** | Systemic [@nice-ng23-recommendations] | Delivers estrogen through the skin [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. With a uterus, a progestogen is added as a pill, patch or IUS [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. |
| **Spray** | Systemic [@nice-ng23-recommendations] | An estrogen spray used once a day [@nhs-types-of-hrt]. |
| **Systemic vaginal ring** | Systemic [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy] | One vaginal ring is designed to give whole-body levels of estradiol [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. |
| **Low-dose vaginal ring, cream, tablet or insert** | Local [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy] | Treats vaginal and urinary symptoms; one low-dose ring lasts 3 months [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. |

The route can change the risks as well as the routine. Our [HRT patch vs. gel vs. pill comparison](/treatments/hormone-therapy/patch-vs-pill/) covers this in detail.

## Which type of HRT is best?

No single type is best for everyone: The Menopause Society says the different forms, doses and routes all work well for hot flashes, and the choice should be made for each person and reviewed over time [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The deciding factors are whether you have a uterus, your health risks, and your preferences about bleeding and routine [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

- **Route**: NICE advises considering HRT through the skin rather than pills for people at higher risk of blood clots, including those with a body mass index over 30 [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Dose**: NICE recommends the lowest dose that works [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Evidence gaps**: Trials comparing routes head to head for clots, stroke and breast cancer have not been done [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Are bioidentical hormones a different type?

"Bioidentical" describes hormones with the same structure as the body's own, and FDA-approved versions exist, including estradiol and micronized progesterone [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Compounded "bioidentical" products are mixed by a pharmacy, are not FDA approved, and lack safety and effectiveness data [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. More in [bioidentical vs. compounded hormones](/treatments/hormone-therapy/bioidentical-vs-compounded/).
