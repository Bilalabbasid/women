**Low-dose vaginal estrogen** treats vaginal dryness, painful sex and related bladder symptoms, and very little of it reaches the bloodstream [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Guidelines start with a vaginal moisturizer and lubricant, then add vaginal estrogen if symptoms persist [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]. It's graded :grade{id="vaginal-estrogen-gsm"} on our scale.

## What does vaginal estrogen treat?

It treats genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM), the dryness, burning, irritation, painful sex, urgency and repeat urinary tract infections that can follow the drop in estrogen [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. That includes [vaginal dryness](/symptoms/vaginal-dryness/), [painful sex](/symptoms/painful-sex/) and [recurrent UTIs](/symptoms/recurrent-utis/). It restores the vaginal lining, its stretch and its natural acidity better than non-hormonal moisturizers do [@nams-2020-gsm; @cochrane-vaginal-moisturizers].

It won't help hot flashes, mood swings or sleep problems, because so little reaches the rest of the body [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-about-2023].

## Vaginal estrogen vs. moisturizers and lubricants

US guidelines suggest a stepped approach [@nams-2020-gsm; @acog-clinical-consensus-gsm]:

1. **Non-hormonal care first**: A vaginal moisturizer used several times a week keeps tissue hydrated, and a lubricant cuts friction during sex [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].
2. **Low-dose vaginal estrogen next**: For moderate to severe dryness, burning or painful sex that doesn't settle, vaginal estrogen is highly effective and generally safe [@nams-2020-gsm].

You don't have to choose one or the other. UK guidance says vaginal estrogen can be used alone or alongside moisturizers or lubricants, and can be offered to people who already take systemic HRT [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. See our guide to [vaginal moisturizers and lubricants](/care/moisturizers-and-lubricants/).

## Types of vaginal estrogen: cream, tablet or ring?

Low-dose vaginal estrogen comes as creams, small vaginal tablets (inserts), a soft gel insert and a ring [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. All contain estradiol except one cream, which contains conjugated estrogens [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The Menopause Society finds no evidence that one form is safer or works better than another [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy], so the choice comes down to what's easiest for you [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

One ring is different: it's designed to raise estrogen through the whole body, so it isn't a low-dose local product [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Check which ring you've been prescribed.

## How do you use vaginal estrogen, and at what dose?

Most products start with a daily dose for a couple of weeks, then drop to twice a week. Two US labels show the pattern:

- **Estradiol vaginal inserts (Vagifem 10 mcg)**: One insert a day for 2 weeks, then one insert twice a week, for example Tuesday and Friday [@dailymed-vagifem-label].
- **Estradiol vaginal ring (Estring)**: The ring releases about 7.5 mcg of estradiol a day and stays in for 90 days before it's replaced [@dailymed-estring-label].

Creams and gels follow a similar daily-then-twice-weekly pattern, but schedules differ by product [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-how-to-use-2023]. The Menopause Society says products other than the ring work better, with less absorbed, when placed in the lower third of the vagina [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. If you forget a dose, never use two at once to catch up [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-how-to-use-2023]. Follow the schedule your prescriber gives you.

Vaginal estrogen is not a contraceptive, and some estrogen creams and gels can damage condoms and diaphragms [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-common-questions-2023]. Ask which product suits you if you still need birth control.

## How long does vaginal estrogen take to work?

It can take up to 3 months to work fully [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-common-questions-2023]. If you don't feel better by then, the NHS suggests going back to your doctor, since a different product may suit you better [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-common-questions-2023].

## Is vaginal estrogen safe?

For most people, yes. The Menopause Society calls low-dose vaginal estrogen effective and generally safe, with minimal absorption into the blood [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. UK guidance says serious side effects are very rare [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. Long-term follow-up of women in two large US studies, the Women's Health Initiative and the Nurses' Health Study, found no rise in heart disease or cancer among vaginal estrogen users [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

### Do you need progesterone with vaginal estrogen?

Usually not. A progestogen is generally not needed with low-dose vaginal estrogen, although trials of womb-lining safety beyond 1 year are lacking [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Any vaginal bleeding after menopause still needs checking [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

### The 2025–2026 FDA boxed-warning removal

For years, vaginal estrogen carried the same boxed warning about heart disease, breast cancer and dementia as hormone pills, which put many women off using it. In November 2025 the FDA asked makers to remove those warnings, and in February 2026 it approved the first new labels, including a vaginal estrogen product [@fda-mht-labeling-request-2025; @fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026]. The Menopause Society [@menopause-society-fda-comment-2025] and the International Menopause Society [@ims-statement-boxed-warning-2025] both backed the change. Read the full story in [the FDA boxed warning change, explained](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/).

## Vaginal estrogen side effects

Side effects are usually mild. In a 12-month trial of Vagifem 10 mcg, these were more common than with placebo [@dailymed-vagifem-label]:

- **Vaginal yeast infection**: 8%, against 3% on placebo.
- **Vaginal or vulvar itching**: 8%, against 2% on placebo.
- **Back pain**: 7%, against 2% on placebo.
- **Diarrhea**: 5%, against none on placebo.

The NHS adds that headache, stomach or vaginal pain and bleeding can happen early on and usually settle in the first few months [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-about-2023]. It advises seeing a doctor about heavy or unexpected bleeding after 6 months of use [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-side-effects-2023]. There's no good evidence that estrogen causes weight gain [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-common-questions-2023].

## How long can you use vaginal estrogen?

As long as you need it, with regular reviews. The Menopause Society says low-dose vaginal estrogen may be used at any age and for an extended time if needed [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Symptoms often come back when it's stopped, but it can be restarted [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The NHS suggests reviewing it with your doctor at least once a year [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-how-to-use-2023].

## Can you use vaginal estrogen after breast cancer?

Sometimes, after talking with your cancer team. Moisturizers and lubricants come first; if symptoms continue, vaginal estrogen can be considered [@nice-ng23-recommendations; @acs-mht-after-breast-cancer-2025]. UK guidance says:

- **After estrogen receptor-negative cancer**: The small amount absorbed is unlikely to raise the chance of the cancer returning, so it's likely to be safe [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **After estrogen receptor-positive cancer**: It's unknown whether the absorbed amount affects recurrence, though medicines such as tamoxifen would reduce any such effect [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **While taking an aromatase inhibitor**: Options should be worked out with a breast cancer specialist [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. The Menopause Society notes extra concern in this group [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

See our page on [HRT and breast cancer risk](/treatments/hormone-therapy/breast-cancer-risk/) for the wider picture.

## Does vaginal estrogen help with UTIs and bladder symptoms?

Yes. Randomized trials have shown fewer repeat urinary tract infections with vaginal estrogen, but not with estrogen pills [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Vaginal estrogen use has also been linked to less incontinence and fewer overactive bladder symptoms, such as urgency [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Low-dose vaginal estrogen for preventing repeat UTIs after menopause is graded :grade{id="vaginal-estrogen-recurrent-uti"} on our scale.

## What if vaginal estrogen isn't right for you?

Two non-estrogen prescription options are FDA-approved for painful sex after menopause: prasterone (a vaginal DHEA insert) and ospemifene (a daily pill) [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. In a review of 5 trials with 1,611 women, vaginal DHEA improved dryness and pain during sex compared with placebo, with mild and uncommon side effects [@lemos-intravaginal-dhea-meta-analysis-2026]. Read more in our [DHEA evidence check](/does-it-work/dhea/).

## When to get help

:::urgent
- **Any vaginal bleeding or spotting after menopause** should be checked by a doctor, whether or not you use vaginal estrogen [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Call 911** for chest pain with breathlessness, or sudden trouble speaking, weakness in an arm or leg, or a drooping face [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-side-effects-2023].
- **See a doctor promptly** for pain, redness or swelling in one leg, or a new breast lump or skin or nipple change [@nhs-vaginal-oestrogen-side-effects-2023].
:::
