The main menopause treatment options are hormone therapy, non-hormonal prescription medicines, and talking therapies such as CBT and clinical hypnosis [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Hormone therapy works best for hot flashes and vaginal dryness. If you can't or don't want to take hormones, several non-hormonal options have strong trial evidence.

Which one suits you depends on your worst symptoms, your age and time since your last period, your health history and what you prefer. This page compares the options and points you to a full guide for each one.

## What is the best treatment for menopause?

For hot flashes and night sweats, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment, and it also treats vaginal dryness and prevents bone loss [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. Its evidence for hot flashes: :grade{id="mht-vms"}. The benefits most clearly outweigh the risks if you start before age 60 or within 10 years of your last period and have no medical reason to avoid it [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. "Best" still depends on you: for someone with a past estrogen-sensitive cancer or blood clot, a non-hormonal option is usually the better fit [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## What can I take instead of HRT?

Three non-hormonal medicines are FDA-approved for moderate to severe hot flashes: low-dose paroxetine, fezolinetant and elinzanetant [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. Other SSRIs and SNRIs and gabapentin may be suggested too, but they aren't FDA-approved for this use [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. As a group, prescription non-hormonal treatments rate :grade{id="nonhormonal-rx-vms"}, and oxybutynin rates :grade{id="oxybutynin-vms"}.

Without medicine, CBT and clinical hypnosis rate :grade{id="cbt-hypnosis-vms"} [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Over-the-counter supplements and herbal remedies aren't recommended for hot flashes (:grade{id="otc-supplements-vms"}) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. For vaginal dryness, regular moisturizers and lubricants come first, before or alongside vaginal estrogen [@acog-clinical-consensus-gsm].

## Is hormone therapy safe?

For most healthy people under 60 or within 10 years of their last period, the benefits of hormone therapy outweigh the risks [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy]. The risks that remain are small but real, and depend on the type:

- **Blood clots**: the risk rises with estrogen pills but not with patches or gels [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **Breast cancer**: combined HRT slightly raises the risk. NICE estimates 59 in 1,000 women who never use HRT develop breast cancer between 50 and 69, compared with 79 after 5 years of combined HRT from 50 [@nice-ng23-hrt-discussion-aid-2024].
- **Warning labels**: in February 2026 the FDA approved removing heart disease, breast cancer and dementia language from the boxed warning on several HRT products [@fda-mht-labeling-changes-2026].

## How long does menopause treatment take to work, and how long do you take it?

Lower doses of hormone therapy can take 6 to 8 weeks to ease hot flashes, while fezolinetant improved them within the first week in a trial [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy; @lederman-skylight1-2023]. NICE recommends reviewing any treatment at 3 months, then yearly [@nice-ng23-recommendations]. There's no fixed time limit for HRT, and stopping it brings hot flashes back for about half of women [@nhs-when-to-take-hrt; @nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## When to see a doctor

:::urgent
- **Bleeding after menopause**, even a small amount, needs a doctor's check [@nhs-postmenopausal-bleeding].
- **Unexpected bleeding on HRT** after the first 6 months, or more than 3 months after a dose change, should be checked promptly [@nice-ng23-recommendations].
- **On fezolinetant**: stop and get medical help for yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, pale stools, itching, nausea or belly pain [@veozah-prescribing-information].
:::
