**Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)** and **clinical hypnosis** are both rated **Level I non-hormonal treatments** for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) by The Menopause Society's 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. NICE guideline NG23 also specifically recommends menopause-specific CBT as an option — alongside HRT, or for people who can't or prefer not to take it [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

## Why these are worth considering even alongside other treatments

The biggest practical advantage of CBT and clinical hypnosis: **no drug interactions, and no systemic side effects** [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. That makes either a reasonable option to combine with other treatments, or to use on its own if you have a complex medical history that limits other choices — see our [breast cancer survivors](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/) guide, where this matters particularly.

Menopause-specific CBT is also recommended by NICE for sleep problems associated with vasomotor symptoms, not just the hot flashes themselves [@nice-ng23-recommendations] — see our [insomnia](/symptoms/insomnia/) page for more on this overlap.

## What it actually involves

Menopause-focused CBT typically teaches specific strategies for managing the physical sensation of a hot flash, the thoughts and anxiety that can accompany it, and sleep disruption from night sweats — it's structured and short-term, not open-ended talk therapy. Clinical hypnosis for hot flashes similarly follows a structured, evidence-tested protocol rather than general relaxation.

## Finding a program

Look for programs or therapists specifically describing **menopause-focused CBT** or **clinical hypnosis for hot flashes**, rather than generic stress-management CBT, since the structured, symptom-specific protocols are what the evidence actually supports. By comparison, techniques like [acupuncture](/does-it-work/acupuncture/) and [paced breathing](/does-it-work/paced-breathing/) haven't held up as well in trials for hot flashes specifically — see our full [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/).
