On October 24, 2025, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved **Lynkuet (elinzanetant)** for the treatment of moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms ([hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/)) due to menopause [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]. It joins [Veozah (fezolinetant)](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/) in the class of targeted [non-hormonal prescription treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/) that act directly on temperature-regulating pathways in the brain [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## What the OASIS 1–3 trials evaluated

Unlike fezolinetant (which blocks only the NK3 receptor [@veozah-prescribing-information]), elinzanetant is a **dual neurokinin 1 (NK1) and neurokinin 3 (NK3) receptor antagonist** [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]. Its FDA approval was supported by three Phase 3 randomized clinical trials [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]:

- **OASIS 1 and OASIS 2 (efficacy):** 12-week randomized trials in **796 menopausal women** with moderate-to-severe hot flashes [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025].
- **OASIS 1–3 (safety):** Evaluated across **1,420 women** in total, including **627 women** followed for up to **52 weeks** in the long-term OASIS 3 study [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025].

*Source transparency note:* Our current registry entry for elinzanetant is verified against the October 24, 2025 approval announcement [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]; always review the full FDA-approved prescribing label with your clinician for contraindications, drug interactions, and laboratory monitoring requirements.
