**Lynkuet (elinzanetant)** is a non-hormonal capsule the FDA approved in October 2025 to treat moderate to severe hot flashes and night sweats caused by menopause [@lynkuet-prescribing-information; @lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]. Its most common side effects are headache, tiredness, dizziness and sleepiness, and it must not be used in pregnancy [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. Hormone therapy is still the most effective treatment for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## When did the FDA approve Lynkuet, and for what?

The FDA approved Lynkuet on October 24, 2025, to treat moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes and night sweats) due to menopause [@lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025; @lynkuet-prescribing-information]. That is its only approved use [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. It became the third non-hormonal medicine the FDA has approved for menopausal hot flashes, after low-dose paroxetine and fezolinetant [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

The approval rested on three trials. Two 12-week trials in 796 women tested whether it works, and safety was studied in 1,420 women in total, including a trial lasting up to 52 weeks [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

## How elinzanetant works

Elinzanetant works on brain cells that help control body temperature. It blocks two chemical messengers linked to [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/), substance P and neurokinin B, at the spots where they attach (the NK1 and NK3 receptors) [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. In contrast, [Veozah (fezolinetant)](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/) blocks only neurokinin B [@veozah-prescribing-information].

## What the OASIS 1–3 trials showed

The trials were randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **OASIS 1 and 2 (does it work?)**: 796 menopausal women aged 40 to 65 with at least 50 moderate to severe hot flashes a week took either Lynkuet 120 mg or a placebo at bedtime for 12 weeks [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **Fewer hot flashes**: Women started with about 13 to 16 moderate to severe hot flashes a day. By week 12, Lynkuet cut that by about 3.2 more hot flashes a day than placebo, a difference the label calls clinically meaningful [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **Milder hot flashes**: Severity also dropped more than with placebo at weeks 4 and 12 [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **OASIS 1–3 (is it safe?)**: Safety was studied in 1,420 women in total, including 627 women in OASIS 3, which lasted up to 52 weeks [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

## Lynkuet side effects

The most common Lynkuet side effects, each reported by at least 5% of women in the trials, are headache, tiredness, dizziness and sleepiness [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. The label's serious warnings cover daytime sleepiness, raised liver enzymes, pregnancy loss and seizure [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. Most were reported only a few percentage points more often than with placebo [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

### Common side effects

In the first 12 weeks of OASIS 1 and 2, the side effects reported by at least 2% of women and more often than with placebo were headache, tiredness, acid reflux, dizziness, nausea and sleepiness [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. The label gives exact rates from the 52-week OASIS 3 trial [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

| Side effect (OASIS 3, up to 52 weeks) | Lynkuet | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Headache | 9.6% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 7.0% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |
| Tiredness (fatigue) | 7.3% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 2.9% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |
| Dizziness | 6.1% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 1.9% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |
| Sleepiness | 5.1% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 1.3% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |
| Stomach pain | 4.5% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 2.5% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |
| Rash | 4.2% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 1.6% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |
| Diarrhea | 3.8% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 1.0% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |
| Muscle spasms | 3.2% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | 0.6% [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |

In OASIS 3, the side effects that led at least 1% of women to stop Lynkuet were stomach pain (1.6%), tiredness (1.6%), depression (1.6%) and headache (1.3%) [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. Mild to moderate skin reactions to sunlight happened in 0.5% of women on Lynkuet and 0.1% on placebo [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

### Serious side effects: the label's warnings

Lynkuet does not carry a boxed warning, but its label lists four warnings [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **Sleepiness and feeling foggy in the day**: Sleepiness, tiredness, dizziness, a spinning feeling (vertigo) or feeling faint happened in 11.9% of women on Lynkuet and 3.5% on placebo. In a driving-simulator study, some women's driving was impaired after a bedtime dose. Don't drive or do hazardous activities until these effects have passed.
- **Raised liver enzymes**: Blood tests check how well the liver is working before starting (ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin). Lynkuet is not started if liver enzymes or bilirubin are already twice the top of the normal range or higher. Liver enzymes are checked again 3 months after starting. In the trials, enzymes rose to 3 or more times normal in 0.6% of women on Lynkuet and 0.4% on placebo, up to 12 weeks.
- **Pregnancy loss**: Lynkuet must not be used in pregnancy, because animal studies suggest it can cause pregnancy loss or stillbirth. Pregnancy is ruled out before starting. Anyone who could still become pregnant should use effective birth control during treatment and for 2 weeks after stopping. See [birth control in perimenopause](/perimenopause/contraception/).
- **Seizure**: Seizures have been reported in people taking Lynkuet. It is used with caution in people with a history of seizures or conditions that make seizures more likely.

The label also says Lynkuet is not recommended for people with kidney failure or moderate to severe liver disease [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

## How it is taken, and drug interactions

The labeled dose is 120 mg (two 60 mg capsules) by mouth once a day at bedtime, with or without food. Capsules are swallowed whole with water [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. Lynkuet is broken down by a liver enzyme (CYP3A4), so medicines and foods that affect this enzyme can change the dose [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **Medicines that strongly slow the enzyme, and grapefruit**: Avoid using them with Lynkuet. This includes eating grapefruit or drinking grapefruit juice during treatment.
- **Medicines that moderately slow the enzyme**: The label lowers the dose to 60 mg (one capsule) once daily while these are used.
- **Medicines that speed up the enzyme**: Avoid using them with Lynkuet, because they may make it less effective.
- **Other medicines broken down by the same enzyme**: Lynkuet can raise their levels, so some combinations are avoided.

Tell your prescriber and pharmacist about every prescription, over-the-counter medicine and supplement you take. Your prescriber decides whether Lynkuet suits you and at what dose.

## Which medicines are FDA-approved for hot flashes?

Hormone therapy and three non-hormonal medicines, low-dose paroxetine (Brisdelle), fezolinetant (Veozah) and elinzanetant (Lynkuet), are FDA-approved to treat moderate to severe menopausal hot flashes [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. Their labels differ in what else they treat and what they warn about:

| Medicine | Type | FDA-approved use (from the label) | Label points to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hormone therapy (estrogen, such as Premarin tablets) | Hormone [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026] | Moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause; also vaginal dryness symptoms and preventing bone loss after menopause [@premarin-prescribing-information; @owh-menopause-treatment-2026] | With a uterus, a progestogen is needed too; labels describe risks such as blood clots, stroke and certain cancers [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026] |
| Paroxetine 7.5 mg (Brisdelle) | Low-dose SSRI, non-hormonal [@brisdelle-prescribing-information] | Moderate to severe hot flashes of menopause; not approved for any mental health condition [@brisdelle-prescribing-information] | One 7.5 mg capsule at bedtime; boxed warning about suicidal thoughts and behaviors [@brisdelle-prescribing-information] |
| Fezolinetant (Veozah) | NK3 blocker, non-hormonal [@veozah-prescribing-information] | Moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause [@veozah-prescribing-information] | One 45 mg tablet daily; boxed warning for liver injury, with regular liver tests [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Elinzanetant (Lynkuet) | NK1 and NK3 blocker, non-hormonal [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Two 60 mg capsules at bedtime; no boxed warning; not for use in pregnancy [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] |

Other medicines that can ease hot flashes, such as other SSRIs and SNRIs, gabapentin and oxybutynin, are not FDA-approved for this use, though guidelines recommend several of them [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Paroxetine can make tamoxifen less effective, which matters after breast cancer [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. For hormone options, see [hormone therapy for menopause](/treatments/hormone-therapy/) and [the FDA boxed warning change](/treatments/hormone-therapy/boxed-warning/).

## Lynkuet vs. Veozah at a glance

Both drugs are non-hormonal pills for moderate to severe menopausal hot flashes, but their labels differ in important ways. Side effect rates from different drugs' trials can't be compared directly [@veozah-prescribing-information].

| Feature | Lynkuet (elinzanetant) | Veozah (fezolinetant) |
|---|---|---|
| What it blocks | Substance P and neurokinin B (NK1 and NK3 receptors) [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Neurokinin B only (NK3 receptor) [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Labeled dose | Two 60 mg capsules once daily at bedtime [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | One 45 mg tablet once daily [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Boxed warning | None [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Yes, for liver injury [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Liver tests on the label | Before starting and at 3 months [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Before starting, monthly for 3 months, then at 6 and 9 months [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Key drug interactions | Medicines affecting the CYP3A4 liver enzyme: some avoided, some need a lower dose [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Must not be used with medicines that block the CYP1A2 liver enzyme [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Pregnancy | Must not be used [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | No human data [@veozah-prescribing-information] |

## Where it fits among treatment options

On our scale, elinzanetant for hot flashes and night sweats is graded :grade{id="elinzanetant-vms"}. When The Menopause Society published its 2023 nonhormone statement, elinzanetant was still in development. The statement noted early trials found it worked better than placebo within 2 weeks [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

That statement recommends CBT, clinical hypnosis, certain SSRIs and SNRIs, gabapentin and fezolinetant for hot flashes, at Level I. It also says hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment, and that non-hormonal options matter for people who can't or prefer not to use hormones [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. ## When to see a doctor

:::urgent
**Get medical care right away if you have any of these while taking Lynkuet** [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **Possible liver injury**: New tiredness, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, itching, yellowing of the skin or eyes, pale stools, dark urine, or stomach pain. Stop Lynkuet and ask for liver blood tests.
- **Seizure or loss of consciousness**: Seek emergency care. In the US, call 911.
- **Possible pregnancy**: Stop Lynkuet and tell your prescriber right away.
:::

Tell your prescriber if sleepiness, dizziness or other side effects bother you or don't go away [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
