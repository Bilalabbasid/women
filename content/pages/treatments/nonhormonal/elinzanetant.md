**Lynkuet (elinzanetant)** is a non-hormonal prescription capsule, approved by the FDA in October 2025, for moderate to severe hot flashes and night sweats caused by menopause [@lynkuet-prescribing-information; @lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]. It is taken at bedtime, can cause sleepiness, and must not be used in pregnancy [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. Hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## How elinzanetant works

Elinzanetant works on brain cells that help control body temperature. It blocks two chemical messengers linked to [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/), substance P and neurokinin B, at the spots where they attach (the NK1 and NK3 receptors) [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. In contrast, [Veozah (fezolinetant)](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/) blocks only neurokinin B [@veozah-prescribing-information]. Both belong to the newer group of [non-hormonal prescription treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/) that act directly on these brain signals [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## What the OASIS 1–3 trials showed

The FDA approval rested on three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **OASIS 1 and 2 (does it work?)**: 796 menopausal women with at least 50 moderate to severe hot flashes a week took either Lynkuet 120 mg or a placebo at bedtime for 12 weeks [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **Fewer hot flashes**: Women started with about 13 to 16 moderate to severe hot flashes a day. By week 12, Lynkuet cut that by about 3.2 more hot flashes a day than placebo, a difference the label calls clinically meaningful [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **Milder hot flashes**: Severity also dropped more than with placebo at weeks 4 and 12 [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **OASIS 1–3 (is it safe?)**: Safety was studied in 1,420 women in total, including 627 women in OASIS 3, which lasted up to 52 weeks [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

## Where it fits among treatment options

On our scale, elinzanetant for hot flashes and night sweats is graded :grade{id="elinzanetant-vms"}. When The Menopause Society published its 2023 nonhormone statement, elinzanetant was still in development. The statement noted early trials found it worked better than placebo within 2 weeks [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

That statement recommends CBT, clinical hypnosis, certain SSRIs and SNRIs, gabapentin and fezolinetant for hot flashes, at Level I. It also says hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment, and that non-hormonal options matter for people who can't or prefer not to use hormones [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## How it is taken, according to the label

The labeled dose is 120 mg (two 60 mg capsules) by mouth once a day at bedtime, with or without food. Capsules are swallowed whole with water [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. Lynkuet is broken down by a liver enzyme (CYP3A4), so medicines and foods that affect this enzyme can change the dose [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **Medicines that strongly slow the enzyme, and grapefruit**: Avoid using them with Lynkuet. This includes eating grapefruit or drinking grapefruit juice during treatment.
- **Medicines that moderately slow the enzyme**: The label lowers the dose to 60 mg (one capsule) once daily while these are used.
- **Medicines that speed up the enzyme**: Avoid using them with Lynkuet, because they may make it less effective.
- **Other medicines broken down by the same enzyme**: Lynkuet can raise their levels, so some combinations are avoided.

Tell your prescriber and pharmacist about every prescription, over-the-counter medicine and supplement you take. Your prescriber decides whether Lynkuet suits you and at what dose.

## Safety warnings on the label

Lynkuet does not carry a boxed warning, but its label lists four warnings [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **Sleepiness and feeling foggy in the day**: Sleepiness, tiredness, dizziness, a spinning feeling (vertigo) or feeling faint happened in 11.9% of women on Lynkuet and 3.5% on placebo. Don't drive or do hazardous activities until these effects have passed.
- **Raised liver enzymes**: Blood tests check how well the liver is working before starting (ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin). Lynkuet is not started if liver enzymes or bilirubin are already twice the top of the normal range or higher. Liver enzymes are checked again 3 months after starting. In the trials, enzymes rose to 3 or more times normal in 0.6% of women on Lynkuet and 0.4% on placebo, up to 12 weeks.
- **Pregnancy loss**: Lynkuet must not be used in pregnancy, because animal studies suggest it can cause pregnancy loss or stillbirth. Pregnancy is ruled out before starting. Anyone who could still become pregnant should use effective birth control during treatment and for 2 weeks after stopping. See [birth control in perimenopause](/perimenopause/contraception/).
- **Seizure**: Seizures have been reported in people taking Lynkuet. It is used with caution in people with a history of seizures or conditions that make seizures more likely.

The label also says Lynkuet is not recommended for people with kidney failure or moderate to severe liver disease [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

## Common side effects

The most common side effects, each in at least 5% of women, were headache, tiredness, dizziness and sleepiness [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]. In the 52-week OASIS 3 trial, abdominal pain, rash, diarrhea and muscle spasms were also more common than with placebo [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].

## Lynkuet vs. Veozah at a glance

Both drugs are non-hormonal pills for moderate to severe menopausal hot flashes, but their labels differ in important ways. Side effect rates from different drugs' trials can't be compared directly [@veozah-prescribing-information].

| Feature | Lynkuet (elinzanetant) | Veozah (fezolinetant) |
|---|---|---|
| What it blocks | Substance P and neurokinin B (NK1 and NK3 receptors) [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Neurokinin B only (NK3 receptor) [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Labeled dose | Two 60 mg capsules once daily at bedtime [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | One 45 mg tablet once daily [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Boxed warning | None [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Yes, for liver injury [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Liver tests on the label | Before starting and at 3 months [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Before starting, monthly for 3 months, then at 6 and 9 months [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Key drug interactions | Medicines affecting the CYP3A4 liver enzyme: some avoided, some need a lower dose [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | Must not be used with medicines that block the CYP1A2 liver enzyme [@veozah-prescribing-information] |
| Pregnancy | Must not be used [@lynkuet-prescribing-information] | No human data [@veozah-prescribing-information] |

## When to see a doctor

:::urgent
**Get medical care right away if you have any of these while taking Lynkuet** [@lynkuet-prescribing-information]:

- **Possible liver injury**: New tiredness, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, itching, yellowing of the skin or eyes, pale stools, dark urine, or stomach pain. Stop Lynkuet and ask for liver blood tests.
- **Seizure or loss of consciousness**: Seek emergency care. In the US, call 911.
- **Possible pregnancy**: Stop Lynkuet and tell your prescriber right away.
:::

Tell your prescriber if sleepiness, dizziness or other side effects bother you or don't go away [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
