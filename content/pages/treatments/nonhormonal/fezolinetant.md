**Veozah (fezolinetant)** is an FDA-approved, non-hormonal prescription medication for moderate-to-severe [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) due to menopause, supported by **Level I evidence** in The Menopause Society's 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## How fezolinetant works without hormones

During menopause, declining estrogen causes neurokinin B (NKB) signaling on KNDy neurons in the brain's thermoregulatory center (hypothalamus) to become overactive, triggering hot flashes. Fezolinetant blocks the **neurokinin 3 (NK3) receptor** directly rather than replacing estrogen [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @veozah-prescribing-information]. Compare it with the dual NK1/NK3 blocker in [Lynkuet (elinzanetant)](/treatments/nonhormonal/elinzanetant/) and other [non-hormonal treatments for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/).

## FDA boxed warning: liver blood test schedule and contraindications

:::urgent
**FDA Boxed Warning (Hepatotoxicity):** In December 2024 the FDA added a boxed warning for liver injury to the Veozah prescribing label [@veozah-prescribing-information]:

- **Required blood tests:** Your clinician must perform baseline hepatic laboratory tests before starting Veozah, **monthly for the first 3 months**, and then at **6 months and 9 months** [@veozah-prescribing-information]. Do not start treatment if ALT, AST, or total bilirubin is 2 or more times the upper limit of normal [@veozah-prescribing-information].
- **Stop and contact your doctor immediately** if you develop signs of liver injury such as unusual fatigue, nausea, vomiting, yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), dark urine, or right upper stomach pain [@veozah-prescribing-information].
:::

According to the FDA label, Veozah is contraindicated if you have known cirrhosis, severe kidney impairment or end-stage renal disease, or if you take medications that are **CYP1A2 inhibitors** [@veozah-prescribing-information].
