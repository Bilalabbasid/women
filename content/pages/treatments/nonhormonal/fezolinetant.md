**Veozah (fezolinetant)** is a non-hormonal prescription pill for moderate to severe hot flashes and night sweats caused by menopause [@veozah-prescribing-information]. It can harm the liver, so the label requires blood tests before starting and several times in the first 9 months [@veozah-prescribing-information]. Hormone therapy still works best, but Veozah has top-level evidence among non-hormonal options [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## How fezolinetant works without hormones

As estrogen falls during menopause, a group of brain cells that help set body temperature becomes overactive. These cells release too much of a chemical messenger called neurokinin B, which upsets the brain's temperature control and sets off [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Fezolinetant blocks the spot where neurokinin B attaches to these cells (the NK3 receptor) [@veozah-prescribing-information].

In studies, fezolinetant did not clearly change estrogen or other sex hormone levels [@veozah-prescribing-information]. Compare it with [Lynkuet (elinzanetant)](/treatments/nonhormonal/elinzanetant/), which blocks both NK1 and NK3 receptors, and with other [non-hormonal treatments for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/).

## What the trials showed

The FDA approval rested on two randomized, placebo-controlled trials. Together they enrolled 1,022 women who had at least 7 moderate to severe hot flashes a day on average [@veozah-prescribing-information]:

- **Fewer hot flashes**: Women started with about 10 to 12 moderate to severe hot flashes a day. At week 12, the 45 mg dose cut that by about 2.5 more hot flashes a day than placebo, a difference the label calls clinically meaningful [@veozah-prescribing-information].
- **Milder hot flashes**: Severity also dropped more than with placebo at weeks 4 and 12 [@veozah-prescribing-information].
- **Longer-term safety**: Safety was studied in three 52-week trials in which 1,100 women took Veozah [@veozah-prescribing-information].

## Where it fits among treatment options

The Menopause Society's 2023 nonhormone position statement recommends fezolinetant for hot flashes, at Level I, its top level of evidence [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. On our scale, fezolinetant for hot flashes and night sweats is graded :grade{id="fezolinetant-vms"}.

The same statement says hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment for hot flashes. Non-hormonal options matter most for people who can't take hormones, for example because of estrogen-dependent cancers or heart disease, or who prefer not to [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Other recommended non-hormonal options include CBT, clinical hypnosis, certain SSRIs and SNRIs, and gabapentin [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## How it is taken, according to the label

The labeled dose is one 45 mg tablet by mouth once a day, with or without food, at about the same time each day. Tablets are swallowed whole with liquid, not cut, crushed or chewed [@veozah-prescribing-information]. Your prescriber decides whether Veozah suits you.

## Liver safety: the FDA boxed warning

In December 2024 the FDA added a boxed warning to Veozah's label because liver injury has been reported in people taking it after it came on the market [@veozah-prescribing-information]. In those reports, liver injury appeared within 40 days of starting Veozah, and symptoms gradually got better after people stopped it [@veozah-prescribing-information].

The label sets out these liver steps [@veozah-prescribing-information]:

- **Before starting**: Blood tests check how well the liver is working (ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin). Veozah should not be started if liver enzymes or bilirubin are already twice the top of the normal range or higher.
- **Follow-up tests**: Liver tests are repeated monthly for the first 3 months, then at 6 months and 9 months, and any time symptoms suggest liver injury.
- **When to stop**: Veozah is stopped if liver enzymes rise above 5 times the top of the normal range, or above 3 times while bilirubin is above 2 times. If enzymes rise above 3 times normal, testing is done more often until they settle.

In the clinical trials, liver enzymes rose above 3 times the top of the normal range in 2.3% of women taking Veozah and 0.9% taking placebo. Most of these women had no symptoms, and levels returned to or near normal [@veozah-prescribing-information].

## Who should not take Veozah

According to the label, Veozah should not be used by people with [@veozah-prescribing-information]:

- **Known cirrhosis**: Scarring of the liver.
- **Severe kidney problems**: Severe kidney disease or kidney failure.
- **Certain other medicines**: Some medicines block a liver enzyme that breaks down Veozah (CYP1A2), which raises the amount of Veozah in the blood. In the label's studies, these included fluvoxamine, mexiletine and cimetidine. Tell your prescriber and pharmacist about every prescription, over-the-counter medicine and supplement you take.

Safety and effectiveness have not been established in people under 18. The trials also didn't include enough women over 65 to know whether they respond differently [@veozah-prescribing-information].

## Common side effects

In the 52-week placebo-controlled trial, the side effects seen in at least 2% of women on Veozah, and more often than with placebo, were [@veozah-prescribing-information]:

- **Stomach and gut**: Abdominal pain and diarrhea.
- **Sleep**: Trouble sleeping (insomnia).
- **Other**: Back pain, hot flushes, and raised liver enzymes on blood tests.

## When to see a doctor

:::urgent
**Stop taking Veozah and get medical care right away, including liver blood tests, if you notice any possible sign of liver injury** [@veozah-prescribing-information]:

- New tiredness or loss of appetite
- Nausea or vomiting
- Itching
- Yellowing of the skin or the whites of the eyes (jaundice)
- Pale stools or dark urine
- Stomach (abdominal) pain
:::

Also talk to your prescriber before starting or stopping any other medicine, because some can interact with Veozah [@veozah-prescribing-information]. Keep every scheduled liver test, even if you feel well, since most enzyme rises in the trials caused no symptoms [@veozah-prescribing-information].
