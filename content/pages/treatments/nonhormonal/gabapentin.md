**Gabapentin** is a seizure and nerve-pain medicine that also reduces hot flashes and night sweats, although it isn't FDA-approved for that use [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026]. The Menopause Society recommends it at Level I, its strongest rating for non-hormonal options [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Hormone therapy still works best for hot flashes [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## Does gabapentin work for hot flashes?

Yes. In randomized trials it made hot flashes less frequent and less severe than a placebo did, though it rarely stopped them completely [@guttuso-gabapentin-hot-flashes-rct-2003; @pandya-gabapentin-breast-cancer-rct-2005]. Placebo also helped noticeably in these trials, so the real benefit of the drug is the gap between the two numbers:

- **Postmenopausal women, 12 weeks**: In 59 women with at least 7 hot flashes a day, 900 mg a day cut hot flash frequency by 45%, against 29% on placebo [@guttuso-gabapentin-hot-flashes-rct-2003].
- **Women with breast cancer, 8 weeks**: In 420 women, 900 mg a day lowered hot flash severity scores by 46%, against 15% on placebo. A lower dose of 300 mg a day was not clearly better than placebo [@pandya-gabapentin-breast-cancer-rct-2005].
- **Higher dose compared with estrogen**: In a small trial of 60 women, gabapentin built up to 2,400 mg a day reduced a combined frequency-and-severity score by 71%, close to estrogen's 72% and above placebo's 54% [@reddy-gabapentin-estrogen-rct-2006]. Headache, dizziness and disorientation were more common at this dose, which limits its use [@reddy-gabapentin-estrogen-rct-2006; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

On our scale, gabapentin for hot flashes and night sweats is graded :grade{id="gabapentin-vms"}.

In the US, gabapentin is approved for nerve pain after shingles and, alongside other medicines, for some types of seizures [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]. Using it for hot flashes is off-label, as it is for SSRIs and SNRIs [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].

## How long does gabapentin take to work for hot flashes?

Often within a month. In the breast cancer trial, which checked early, severity scores at 900 mg a day were already 49% lower by week 4 [@pandya-gabapentin-breast-cancer-rct-2005]. Side effects such as dizziness, unsteadiness and drowsiness usually show up in the first week, ease in the second and fade by week 4 [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. The main trials lasted 8 to 12 weeks, so ask your prescriber how long to give it before deciding whether it helps [@guttuso-gabapentin-hot-flashes-rct-2003; @pandya-gabapentin-breast-cancer-rct-2005].

## How much gabapentin is used for hot flashes?

Guidelines describe a range, not a dose for you. The Menopause Society lists 900 to 2,400 mg a day split into several doses, reached step by step from a small dose at bedtime [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Several trials used 900 mg a day, taken as 300 mg three times a day [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

The FDA label says doses need to be lower when the kidneys don't work as well, which is more common with age [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]. Your prescriber sets the dose and schedule.

## Why gabapentin is often taken at bedtime

Drowsiness is a side effect and gabapentin leaves the body fairly quickly, so The Menopause Society says a bedtime dose may suit women whose sleep is broken by hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. That makes it worth asking about if [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) and [poor sleep](/symptoms/insomnia/) bother you most. Whether to take it only at night or through the day is your prescriber's call.

## Gabapentin side effects

The most common side effects are dizziness, sleepiness and feeling unsteady [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]. In the label's nerve-pain trials, more people on gabapentin than on placebo had [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]:

- **Dizziness**: 28%, against 8% on placebo.
- **Sleepiness**: 21%, against 5% on placebo.
- **Swelling of the legs or feet (peripheral edema)**: 8%, against 2% on placebo.

In the 12-week hot flash trial, half the women on gabapentin reported at least one side effect, against 28% on placebo, and 13% stopped because of side effects, against 3% [@guttuso-gabapentin-hot-flashes-rct-2003].

### Does gabapentin cause weight gain?

It can, but in few people. In the label's nerve-pain trials, 2% of people on gabapentin gained weight against none on placebo, and in its epilepsy trials the figures were 3% and 2% [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].

### Rare but serious risks

The label lists these less common risks [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]:

- **Slowed breathing**: Serious or even fatal breathing problems have been linked to gabapentin taken with opioids or other sedating medicines, or by people with lung problems.
- **Mood changes**: Like other seizure medicines, it may cause suicidal thoughts or actions in about 1 in 500 people.
- **Allergic reactions**: A rash with fever or swollen glands, or swelling of the face, lips or throat, needs medical care right away.
- **Driving**: It may impair driving, so don't drive until you know how it affects you.
- **Misuse**: Misuse and abuse have been reported, mostly in people with a history of misusing several substances.

## Does gabapentin interact with other medicines?

Yes. Opioid pain medicines, other sedating medicines and alcohol can make drowsiness and dizziness worse and add to the breathing risk [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]. Antacids containing aluminum and magnesium, such as Maalox, cut how much gabapentin the body absorbs by about 20%, so the label advises taking gabapentin at least 2 hours after them [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]. Give your prescriber and pharmacist a full list of your medicines and supplements.

## Can you stop gabapentin suddenly?

No. The label says not to stop without talking to your provider, because withdrawal symptoms have been reported, usually within 48 hours of stopping [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026]. When the dose is lowered or stopped, the label advises doing it gradually over at least 1 week [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].

## Who should be careful with gabapentin?

- **Older adults and people with kidney problems**: The body clears gabapentin more slowly, so doses are usually adjusted [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].
- **People who take opioids or have lung disease**: The risk of dangerously slow breathing is highest in these groups [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].
- **People with depression**: The label asks patients and families to watch for new or worse low mood or suicidal thoughts [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].
- **People who have had breast cancer**: Gabapentin is one of the non-hormonal hot flash options the American Cancer Society lists after breast cancer [@acs-mht-after-breast-cancer-2025], and it was tested in women with breast cancer [@pandya-gabapentin-breast-cancer-rct-2005].

## Gabapentin vs. other non-hormonal options

No single non-hormonal medicine suits everyone. Here is how gabapentin sits next to the main alternatives:

| Option | Menopause Society 2023 rating | Common side effects |
|---|---|---|
| Gabapentin | Level I [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] | Dizziness, unsteadiness, drowsiness [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |
| SSRIs and SNRIs | Level I [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] | Nausea or dizziness that usually eases after 1 to 2 weeks [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |

## When to get help

:::urgent
- **Call 911** if you or someone taking gabapentin is breathing slowly or shallowly, is very hard to wake, or has swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].
- **Call or text 988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) if you have thoughts of harming yourself [@lifeline-988]. The label asks people on gabapentin to report new or worse mood changes promptly [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].
- **Contact your prescriber soon** about a rash with fever or swollen glands, or unusual tiredness, confusion or shortness of breath [@dailymed-neurontin-label-2026].
:::
