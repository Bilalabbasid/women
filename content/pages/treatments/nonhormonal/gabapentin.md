**Gabapentin** — originally developed as an anti-seizure and nerve-pain medication — is rated a **Level I non-hormonal option** for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) by The Menopause Society's 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. It works through a different mechanism than hormone therapy, which is why it's an option for people who can't or prefer not to take estrogen.

## Why it's sometimes chosen for nighttime symptoms specifically

Because gabapentin can have a sedating effect and is sometimes dosed at bedtime, it's a treatment some prescribers favor for women whose [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) and [disrupted sleep](/symptoms/insomnia/) are the most disruptive part of their symptom pattern. The right timing and dose is a decision for your prescriber, based on your specific pattern of symptoms and any other medications you take.

## What to know before starting

Gabapentin can interact with other medications and cause side effects such as drowsiness or dizziness, which is part of why dose adjustments are typically made gradually under medical supervision rather than self-directed. It's not a decision to make without a clinician who knows your full medication list.
