Non-hormonal treatments for menopause hot flashes include three FDA-approved medicines (low-dose paroxetine, fezolinetant and elinzanetant), other prescriptions such as certain antidepressants and gabapentin, and talk therapies like CBT and clinical hypnosis [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Supplements and herbal remedies are not recommended for hot flashes, and hormone therapy is still the most effective treatment [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Which medications are approved for hot flashes?

The FDA has approved hormone therapy and three non-hormonal medicines for moderate to severe menopausal hot flashes: low-dose paroxetine 7.5 mg (Brisdelle), fezolinetant (Veozah) and elinzanetant (Lynkuet) [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026; @brisdelle-prescribing-information; @veozah-prescribing-information; @lynkuet-prescribing-information]. Other SSRIs, SNRIs and gabapentin can help but are not FDA-approved for this use [@owh-menopause-treatment-2026].

For each medicine's approved use, dose and main label warnings side by side, see the [table of FDA-approved hot flash medicines](/treatments/nonhormonal/elinzanetant/#which-medicines-are-fda-approved-for-hot-flashes) in our Lynkuet guide.

## Which non-hormonal treatments work for hot flashes?

The Menopause Society's 2023 position statement rates these as recommended for hot flashes, based on randomized trials [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]:

- **[Veozah (fezolinetant)](/treatments/nonhormonal/fezolinetant/)**: Blocks a brain signal (neurokinin B) linked to hot flashes. Rated Level I [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Its label has a boxed warning for liver injury and calls for scheduled liver blood tests [@veozah-prescribing-information].
- **[Lynkuet (elinzanetant)](/treatments/nonhormonal/elinzanetant/)**: Blocks two related brain signals. It was still in development in 2023 and was FDA-approved in October 2025 [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @lynkuet-approval-bayer-2025]. It can cause sleepiness and must not be used in pregnancy [@lynkuet-prescribing-information].
- **[SSRIs and SNRIs](/treatments/nonhormonal/ssris-snris/)**: Low doses of certain antidepressants, including paroxetine, escitalopram, citalopram, venlafaxine and desvenlafaxine, reduce hot flashes. Rated Level I [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **[Gabapentin](/treatments/nonhormonal/gabapentin/)**: A seizure and nerve-pain medicine that reduces hot flashes. Because it causes drowsiness, a bedtime dose may suit people whose sleep is broken by night sweats. Rated Level I [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **[Oxybutynin](/treatments/nonhormonal/oxybutynin/)**: A bladder medicine that reduces moderate to severe hot flashes. In older adults, long-term use may be linked to a decline in memory and thinking. Rated Levels I–II [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **[CBT and clinical hypnosis](/treatments/nonhormonal/cbt-and-hypnosis/)**: Talk-based treatments that don't use medicine. CBT reduces how much hot flashes bother you, and hypnosis reduces how often they happen and how severe they are. Rated Level I [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Weight loss**: May be considered to improve hot flashes (Levels II–III) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## What are the options after breast cancer?

Non-hormonal options are often used after [breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/), because hormone therapy may not be suitable after an estrogen-dependent cancer [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. CBT and clinical hypnosis have both been tested in breast cancer survivors [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

If you take tamoxifen, paroxetine and fluoxetine can make it less effective. The Menopause Society lists venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, escitalopram and citalopram as safer choices for people on tamoxifen [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Your cancer care team can say which options fit your treatment.
