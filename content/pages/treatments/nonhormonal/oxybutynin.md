**Oxybutynin**, a medicine for overactive bladder, also reduces hot flashes and night sweats, and The Menopause Society recommends it for them at Levels I–II [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Dry mouth is the most common side effect [@simon-oxybutynin-er-vms-rct-2016]. Long-term use may be linked to memory decline in older people, which matters when choosing it [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## What is oxybutynin, and why is it used for hot flashes?

Oxybutynin belongs to a group of medicines called anticholinergics (or antimuscarinics), which block a chemical messenger that tells muscles and glands what to do [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Its FDA label covers bladder symptoms such as urgency, frequent peeing, leaks and urge incontinence [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]. One of its known effects is less sweating [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026], and trials have shown it also eases [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

Using it for hot flashes is off-label, meaning it isn't the use the label was approved for [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]. On our scale, oxybutynin for hot flashes and night sweats is graded :grade{id="oxybutynin-vms"}.

## Does oxybutynin work for hot flashes?

Yes. The Menopause Society points to one prospective study and two randomized, placebo-controlled trials in which oxybutynin clearly improved moderate to severe hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. The two trials:

- **Postmenopausal women, 12 weeks**: In 148 women with at least 7 moderate to severe hot flashes a day, extended-release oxybutynin 15 mg once a day cut them by about 9.5 a day, against 4.7 on placebo. By the end, 73% on oxybutynin rated themselves "much better", against 26% on placebo [@simon-oxybutynin-er-vms-rct-2016].
- **Women with and without breast cancer, 6 weeks**: In 150 women who couldn't use estrogen, 2.5 mg or 5 mg twice a day reduced hot flash scores and frequency more than placebo, with the larger dose doing more. Both doses also improved quality of life [@leon-ferre-oxybutynin-rct-2020].

Fewer studies have tested it than gabapentin or the SSRIs and SNRIs, and its rating is a step below theirs [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment for hot flashes [@nams-2022-hormone-therapy].

## How quickly does oxybutynin work?

Within the first weeks. In the 12-week trial, women taking oxybutynin had fewer and milder hot flashes than women on placebo at every weekly check through the end of the study [@simon-oxybutynin-er-vms-rct-2016]. The other main trial measured results at 6 weeks [@leon-ferre-oxybutynin-rct-2020]. Your prescriber can tell you when to review whether it's helping.

## What doses of oxybutynin have been studied for hot flashes?

Studies used 2.5 mg or 5 mg twice a day, up to 15 mg of the extended-release form once a day [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Side effects tend to rise with the dose [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. For frail older adults, the label notes a lower starting dose because the drug stays in the body longer [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]. These are study and label figures, not a dose for you: your prescriber decides.

## Oxybutynin side effects

Dry mouth is by far the most common. In the 12-week hot flash trial, 52% of women on oxybutynin had a dry mouth, against 5% on placebo, and about 7% stopped the drug because of it [@simon-oxybutynin-er-vms-rct-2016]. In the 6-week trial, dry mouth, trouble passing urine and stomach pain were more common than with placebo, but most side effects were mild and no more women stopped than on placebo [@leon-ferre-oxybutynin-rct-2020].

In the label's bladder trials, which used 5 to 20 mg a day, the most common side effects were [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]:

- **Dry mouth**: 71%.
- **Dizziness**: 17%.
- **Constipation**: 15%.
- **Sleepiness**: 14%.
- **Nausea**: 12%.
- **Blurred vision**: 10%.

The label also reports confusion, agitation and hallucinations in some people, and asks for extra watchfulness in the first few months and after any dose increase [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026].

### Can oxybutynin make you overheat?

It can in hot conditions. Because oxybutynin reduces sweating, the label warns that fever and heat stroke can happen in high temperatures [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]. Take extra care in hot weather or during hard exercise.

## Does oxybutynin cause dementia?

It hasn't been proven to, but there is a real concern. The Menopause Society notes that long-term use of anticholinergic medicines may be linked to cognitive decline, especially in older people [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. A large English study of about 284,000 people aged 55 and older found that heavy long-term use of bladder antimuscarinic medicines was linked to a 65% higher chance of a dementia diagnosis [@coupland-anticholinergic-dementia-2019]. That kind of study shows a link, not proof of cause [@coupland-anticholinergic-dementia-2019].

The label also asks for caution in people who already have dementia and take cholinesterase inhibitors, a type of dementia medicine, because symptoms can get worse [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026].

## Who should not take oxybutynin?

The label says oxybutynin must not be used by people with [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]:

- **Urinary retention**: Trouble emptying the bladder.
- **Slow stomach or bowel emptying**: Including gastric retention.
- **Uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma**: A type of glaucoma that can flare suddenly.

It also needs caution in frail older adults, people with liver or kidney problems, myasthenia gravis, acid reflux, or conditions such as ulcerative colitis [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]. People who have had breast cancer may be able to use it: the American Cancer Society lists it among non-hormonal hot flash options [@acs-mht-after-breast-cancer-2025].

## Does oxybutynin interact with other medicines?

Yes. Taking it with other anticholinergic medicines, or anything else that causes dry mouth, constipation or drowsiness, can make those effects stronger [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]. Alcohol can add to the drowsiness [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026]. It may also change how well some medicines are absorbed, and it needs care with bisphosphonate bone medicines, which can irritate the food pipe [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026].

## If you have hot flashes and bladder symptoms

Oxybutynin is approved for overactive bladder [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026] and recommended for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy], so it may be worth asking about if you have both [bladder urgency](/symptoms/bladder-urgency/) and hot flashes. That overlap doesn't make it the best choice for you by default, especially given the memory question above.

## Oxybutynin vs. gabapentin

| Most common side effects | Dry mouth, trouble passing urine [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] | Dizziness, unsteadiness, drowsiness [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy] |

## When to get help

:::urgent
- **Call 911** for swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing. The label warns this reaction can block the airway, sometimes after the first dose [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026].
- **Get medical help quickly** if you feel very hot with a fever in hot weather, since oxybutynin reduces sweating [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026].
- **Contact your prescriber soon** about confusion, hallucinations, agitation, trouble passing urine or blurred vision [@dailymed-oxybutynin-label-2026].
:::
