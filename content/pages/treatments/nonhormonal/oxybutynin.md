**Oxybutynin**, more commonly known as a treatment for overactive bladder, is also rated a **Levels I–II non-hormonal option** for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) by The Menopause Society's 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. That "Levels I–II" rating sits slightly below the clearest Level I options (like gabapentin or certain SSRIs/SNRIs), reflecting somewhat less consistent trial evidence.

## Why it might come up for someone with both symptoms

If you have both [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [bladder urgency](/symptoms/bladder-urgency/) or overactive bladder symptoms, oxybutynin is sometimes considered because it may address both at once. That overlap is a reasonable thing to raise with your prescriber, not a reason to assume it's automatically the best fit.

## Who needs extra caution

Oxybutynin belongs to a class of medications called anticholinergics. These need particular caution in older adults and in people with certain eye, urinary, or digestive conditions, and commonly cause side effects like dry mouth and constipation. This is a decision to make directly with a clinician who knows your full medical history — not one to start based on this page alone.
