Selected SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) and SNRIs (serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors) are rated a **Level I non-hormonal option** for [hot flashes](/symptoms/hot-flashes/) and [night sweats](/symptoms/night-sweats/) by The Menopause Society's 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. They work independently of their effect on mood, which is why they're a reasonable option even if you aren't experiencing depression or anxiety.

## The tamoxifen interaction that actually matters

This is the single most important safety detail for this drug class: **certain SSRIs — specifically paroxetine and fluoxetine — inhibit the CYP2D6 enzyme that your body uses to activate tamoxifen** into its effective form [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. If you take tamoxifen for breast cancer treatment or prevention, taking one of these specific SSRIs alongside it can blunt how well tamoxifen works.

:::urgent
If you take tamoxifen and are also taking or considering an SSRI for hot flashes, make sure both your oncologist and the prescriber managing your hot flashes know about the other medication before you start. See our [menopause after breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/) guide for the fuller picture.
:::

This doesn't rule out the SSRI/SNRI class entirely — it means the **specific choice within the class** matters. Options that don't significantly inhibit CYP2D6 are generally preferred for people on tamoxifen, which is exactly the kind of decision to make with your prescribing clinician rather than alone.

## Who else should be cautious

These medications can interact with other drugs and have their own side-effect profile, which is why starting or adjusting one should always go through a clinician rather than self-selecting. Compare this option against other [Level I non-hormonal treatments](/treatments/nonhormonal/), including [gabapentin](/treatments/nonhormonal/gabapentin/) and [CBT and clinical hypnosis](/treatments/nonhormonal/cbt-and-hypnosis/), which don't carry the same drug-interaction profile.

If you're weighing this against unproven supplements, our [Does It Work? evidence directory](/does-it-work/) grades those honestly too.
