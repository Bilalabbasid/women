Some antidepressants, taken at low doses, ease hot flashes even if you aren't depressed. Low-dose paroxetine (Brisdelle) is FDA-approved for hot flashes, and escitalopram, citalopram, venlafaxine and desvenlafaxine also worked in large trials [@brisdelle-prescribing-information; @nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Sertraline (Zoloft) and fluoxetine (Prozac) haven't shown a clear benefit [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

These medicines are SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) and SNRIs (serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors). The Menopause Society recommends them for hot flashes and night sweats: :grade{id="ssri-snri-vms"} [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. In trials, they cut hot flashes by 25% to 69%, and hormone therapy is still the most effective treatment [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

## Which antidepressants work for hot flashes?

Five have good trial evidence: paroxetine, escitalopram, citalopram, venlafaxine and desvenlafaxine each reduced hot flashes in large, double-blind trials [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Duloxetine helped in smaller studies, while sertraline and fluoxetine did not do clearly better than placebo [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. There are no trials comparing them head to head [@aafp-ssri-snri-vms-2022].

## Does Zoloft (sertraline) help hot flashes?

Probably not much. Trials of sertraline showed only a trend toward fewer hot flashes that wasn't statistically significant, so The Menopause Society does not recommend it for hot flashes [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. The National Cancer Institute agrees that sertraline trials haven't given convincing evidence that it works [@nci-pdq-hot-flashes-hp].

The trials are small and mixed. Two (46 and 99 women) found no drop in hot flashes compared with placebo, while a third (97 women) found about five fewer hot flashes a week [@aafp-ssri-snri-vms-2022]. If you take tamoxifen, note that sertraline is not on The Menopause Society's list of safer choices [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

If you already take sertraline for depression or anxiety, don't stop it suddenly, because that can cause withdrawal symptoms; a doctor lowers the dose gradually over weeks or months [@nhs-sertraline]. Ask your prescriber whether it's worth staying on it, or switching, for your hot flashes.

## Is paroxetine good for hot flashes, and what dose is approved?

Yes, it's the best-studied option. A low-dose paroxetine capsule (7.5 mg, Brisdelle) was the first non-hormone medicine the FDA approved for moderate to severe hot flashes, and in studies it eased hot flashes and broken sleep for up to 24 months without weight gain or loss of sex drive [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. In one review, paroxetine gave the largest drop in hot flashes of the antidepressants studied [@aafp-ssri-snri-vms-2022].

The label dose is one 7.5 mg capsule at bedtime, which is lower than the doses used for depression; this product is not approved for any mental health condition [@brisdelle-prescribing-information]. Its most common side effects in trials were headache, tiredness and nausea or vomiting [@brisdelle-prescribing-information]. The big catch is tamoxifen (see below).

## What do guidelines say about antidepressants for hot flashes?

US and UK guidance differ. The Menopause Society (US) recommends SSRIs and SNRIs for hot flashes, based on good and consistent evidence [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. UK NICE guidance says not to routinely offer SSRIs, SNRIs or clonidine as the first treatment for hot flashes alone [@nice-ng23-recommendations].

The Menopause Society notes that non-hormone options matter most for women who aren't good candidates for hormone therapy [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. See whether [hormone therapy could suit you](/treatments/hormone-therapy/is-it-right-for-me/), and compare the other [non-hormonal treatments for hot flashes](/treatments/nonhormonal/).

## How fast do they work, and what are the side effects?

Non-hormone prescriptions for hot flashes usually start to work within 2 weeks [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Nausea or dizziness are common at first and usually ease after 1 to 2 weeks [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

- **Common, short-term effects**: nausea, sleepiness, dry mouth and appetite changes; at hot-flash doses, side effects are usually mild [@nci-pdq-hot-flashes-hp].
- **SNRIs**: these caused more side effects than SSRIs in trials; venlafaxine can cause nausea, dry mouth and constipation [@aafp-ssri-snri-vms-2022].
- **Bleeding**: SSRIs raise the risk of bleeding, more so with aspirin, ibuprofen-type painkillers (NSAIDs) or blood thinners [@brisdelle-prescribing-information].
- **Bones**: SSRIs have a possible link with broken bones, though this hasn't been seen with short-term use [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].
- **Night sweats**: some antidepressants can cause night sweats themselves [@nhs-night-sweats].
- **Boxed warning**: antidepressants carry a warning about uncommon suicidal thoughts in children, teens and young adults, mainly in the first few months [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @brisdelle-prescribing-information].

## Who should not take them?

These medicines are not used by people who have had serotonin syndrome or neuroleptic malignant syndrome, or who take an MAOI antidepressant [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]. Low-dose paroxetine is also not used in pregnancy [@brisdelle-prescribing-information].

Extra care is needed with [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy]:

- **Seizures** that aren't well controlled
- **Bipolar disorder**
- **Kidney or liver problems**
- **Low blood sodium** that isn't controlled
- **High blood pressure** that isn't well controlled
- **Another SSRI or SNRI** already in use
- **Gene variants** that change how your liver breaks down medicines

## If you take tamoxifen, the choice matters

Many SSRIs can block CYP2D6, the liver enzyme that turns tamoxifen into its most active form (endoxifen), and paroxetine and fluoxetine are the most likely to do this [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy; @nci-pdq-hot-flashes-hp]. The Menopause Society lists venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, escitalopram and citalopram as safer choices because they affect this enzyme less [@nams-2023-nonhormone-therapy].

Studies disagree on whether paroxetine with tamoxifen raises the chance of breast cancer coming back, and the paroxetine label says to weigh the benefit and consider avoiding the combination [@brisdelle-prescribing-information]. Make sure your oncologist and whoever treats your hot flashes both know every medicine you take. Our guide to [menopause after breast cancer](/groups/breast-cancer-survivors/) covers the other options.

:::urgent
Get help right away if you have thoughts of suicide or harming yourself after starting or changing an antidepressant. Call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), and call **911** if a life is at risk [@lifeline-988; @nimh-depression-booklet; @brisdelle-prescribing-information].

Serotonin syndrome is rare but can be life-threatening. Signs include agitation or confusion, a racing heartbeat, sweating, flushing, a high temperature, shaking, muscle twitching or stiffness, and diarrhea [@brisdelle-prescribing-information].

If you're in the UK: call 999 if a life is at risk, NHS 111 for urgent advice, or Samaritans on 116 123 [@nhs-urgent-help-mental-health].
:::
